## Why it’s blind {glyph="☽"}

In 1948 a psychologist, Bertram Forer, gave each of his students a personality profile “written just for them”. They rated it 4.26 out of 5 for accuracy. Every student had the same text, taken mostly from an astrology book. It’s known today as the [Barnum effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnum_effect).

That’s the weak spot of any reading handed to you with your name on it, mine included: it’s hard to tell a good fit from a flattering one. So on these calls I won’t hand you yours. I’ll hand you six, and you pick.

## Why I’m doing it {glyph="♄"}

I came to astrology to mock it, then [checked my own chart like an invoice](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/): a tick or a cross beside every placement. About a third held. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), [Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/) and [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) held far more often, which is why I [read the way I do](/how-i-read/).

But that was a notebook, not a test: the people I read knew the chart was theirs. This is the blind version. If my portraits are good, people should pick their own more often than one in six. If they’re not, I’d rather find out on a free call than after you’ve paid me for a reading.

## What a portrait looks like {glyph="☿"}

A few lines about how a person moves through a day, what they protect, what they keep circling back to. Every line that could fit anyone is cut: “you’re loyal but need your space” doesn’t make it. What stays is the line that fits one person in six, and not the other five.
