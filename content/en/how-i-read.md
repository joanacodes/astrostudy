## How it started

During the pandemic I was bored, and I found astrology on YouTube. I want to be honest about why I pressed play: I love understanding where people come from, how they arrive at the way they think — and I watched with the face you make at something you’ve already decided is nonsense.

It slapped me. I read my own chart, and it was too specific, too accurate. So I did what you do when you don’t understand something: I tested it.

## How I tested

I printed my chart and went through it like an invoice: every placement — Sun, Moon, rising, each planet in its sign and house — and beside each one, a tick or a cross. A tick if it described me in a way I couldn’t have written about anyone else. A cross if it was vague, wrong, or would have fitted my neighbour just as well.

Then I did the same, with their permission, for my family, my friends, and strangers online who told me nothing first.

## What held

Overall, about a third of what I checked held: **30 to 40%**. The Sun sign, the rising sign, Venus, Mercury — the placements everyone reads first. That isn’t nothing, but it’s close to what a flattering horoscope scores, so I hold those lightly.

Three were different. **Saturn, Lilith and Chiron held 75 to 90% of the time**, for me and for the people I tested. Where Saturn sits, the hardest lesson. Where Chiron sits, the wound a person keeps circling. Lilith, the part of them they were told to hide.

It’s a notebook, not a study: a count by hand, by me, mostly on people I knew. But it’s why I read the way I do.

## How I read because of it

- **Slowest where it matters most.** The tender placements get the most time and the softest words.
- **Honest about the rest.** When something rests on a placement that held less often in my tests, I tell you, so you can weigh it for yourself.
- **Tendencies, not fate.** A chart describes how you’re inclined to move, not where you must end up.
- **You stay in charge.** Take what fits and leave the rest. A reading is a conversation with yourself, not an instruction.

This isn’t prudishness. These are the subjects where a chart, even well read, can do harm. Astrology doesn’t diagnose, doesn’t plead, doesn’t invest, and doesn’t lift curses — and anyone offering you that last one for a fee has an excellent reason to: you.

## Not everything is in the stars

Sometimes what someone carries is psychological, sometimes medical. In those moments the right door isn’t mine, and I’ll tell you — plainly, and kindly. Seeing a therapist isn’t your chart failing. It’s the right tool for the right job.
