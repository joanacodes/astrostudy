When the other person's North Node lands in your 1st house, the direction they're growing in meets the front of your chart: your manner, your first reactions, the way you start things without waiting for permission. Their South Node sits exactly across, in your 7th, so this overlay always arrives as two ends of one line, and I read both.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) is the less practised end of that line: a capacity someone builds by repetition, awkward before it feels natural. [The 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) is the self you lead with, the body and manner people meet before they hear your opinions. Put together in a [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), the thing they're learning looks very like the thing you do when you simply begin.

For you, it can feel like being watched with affection. They notice how you walk in, how you decide, how you say no, and then you see them try it themselves, a little stiffly. You may act more decisively than usual with them around, the way anyone does once they realise someone is learning from them.

For them, you can look like the next step. Acting on their own say-so, before checking with anyone, is the stretch their chart describes, and next to you it starts to seem possible. The 1st is angular, and [Aries](/astrology/aries/), its natural sign, is initiative at its plainest. Within a few degrees of your [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), their North Node meets the degree your chart rises from while their South Node touches your Descendant, laying their whole nodal axis along your horizon.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Their [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) in [the 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) is where the friction usually starts. Partnering is their familiar ground: with you, they slip easily into the accommodating role, the one who adapts and agrees. You may enjoy being deferred to for a while, then tire of making every decision for two.

From their side, the stretch can feel exposing. Saying what they want in front of someone who says it so easily can make them shy, and when tension rises they may go back to keeping the peace just when you needed them to choose. On some days you are their example; on others, a reminder of what they haven't managed yet.

Some astrologers would call this a fated meeting, or a debt carried over from another life. I read a direction and a habit between two adults, neither of whom owes the other a lesson in self-assertion.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Leave them room to start things. In a couple, let them choose the plan and live with it; between friends, ask what they'd do before saying what you would; between colleagues, hand them a decision that is theirs alone; with an adult relative, tell them when you notice they've stopped deferring.

Turned round, this is your North Node in their 1st house, and their way of beginning becomes the one you study. On either side, [their North Node in your 12th house](/astrology/north-node-in-12th-house-synastry/) works in your private hours instead, and [their North Node in your 2nd house](/astrology/north-node-in-2nd-house-synastry/) around what you own and value.

Your Ascendant changes sign about every two hours on average, and in Placidus, the house system I use, your 1st house begins exactly there, so drawing it takes your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Their node lingers about 18 months in a sign, which is why their birth date alone places it, but whether it falls just before or just after your rising degree is a matter of minutes on your side of the pair. The mean node and the true node, a degree or two apart at most, can even straddle that degree, one in your 12th and one in your 1st: I look at both ([mean node and true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/)). And because this is about how someone learns to stand on their own feet, it belongs in a reading you've both agreed to, with both birth times on the table ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
