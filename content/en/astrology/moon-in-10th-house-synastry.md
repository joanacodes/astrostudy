Their Moon in your 10th house brings someone's feelings into the part of your life the world can see. Your work, your role and your reputation become something they care about, sometimes fiercely, and their care shows up in the place where the world can see it.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[The 10th house](/astrology/10th-house/) is your public direction: the role you play, the responsibilities you carry, the work you'd like to be known for. The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) is how a person attaches, cares and feels safe. In this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their attachment reaches your public life.

For you, it can feel like having someone in your corner. They ask how the presentation went and mean it, remember the name of the colleague who makes your days hard, and celebrate a small professional win as if it were their own. At work, they may be the person who notices how the team is feeling, and who makes your role more humane.

For them, your standing in the world can be part of what makes them feel secure. When your work goes well, they relax; when it's under pressure, they feel it too. It's an angular house, and what happens here is seen; [Capricorn](/astrology/capricorn/), steady and responsible, is the sign that belongs to it by nature. Just past your [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/), their Moon also meets the point from which your public life is oriented, and I take that contact into the reading.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Care that lives in your public life can blur into pressure. Their feelings about your career choices, their worry when you take a risk, their pride when you're praised: all of it can start to weigh on decisions that should be yours. You may find yourself explaining your work to protect their peace of mind, or keeping quiet about setbacks so as not to worry them.

From their side, your public role can be both reassuring and lonely. They may feel second to your job, or uneasy when your work makes you visible in ways that include them. Privacy matters to the Moon, and the 10th is the least private house of all.

Nothing here forecasts how your career will go. The overlay describes how their feelings meet your public direction, and what each of you feels there.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Let work be yours, and let them in on it. Share what's going on in terms that include them, and say which kind of support helps: listening, advice, or simply not worrying aloud. In a couple, talk about how each person's work affects the other's sense of security; between colleagues, warmth is a gift as long as feelings don't decide who gets what; between friends, this is the one who is proud of you without being asked; between adult relatives, notice when their hopes for your career are really about their own need to feel safe.

Turn the charts round and the overlay ties your feelings to how they do in the world, with your Moon in their 10th: the same need to support rather than steer. [Their Moon in your 9th house](/astrology/moon-in-9th-house-synastry/) finds comfort in your beliefs; [their Moon in your 11th house](/astrology/moon-in-11th-house-synastry/) among your friends.

Between a birth at dawn and one at dusk, their Moon covers several degrees, which can take it from one side of your Midheaven to the other. Where your Midheaven falls depends on your own time to the minute, and in Placidus, the house system I use, every other cusp follows from it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). If you work together, reading their feelings about your work needs their agreement as much as yours ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
