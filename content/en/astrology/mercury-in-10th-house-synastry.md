Their Mercury in your 10th house takes an interest in what you do in the world. Your work, your plans, the way you're seen in public: with them, these become subjects of conversation, and sometimes of strategy.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[The 10th house](/astrology/10th-house/) faces outward, towards the world: the role you play, the reputation you build, the authority you earn or push against, the work you'd like to have done. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) plans, explains and advises. As a [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), it brings their thinking to your working life.

For you, they can be the sounding board your career needed. They read the application before you send it, rehearse the presentation with you, ask where you want to be in five years and then hold you to the answer. They may also talk about you to other people, and well: your work travels further for having been put into their words.

For them, your ambitions give their ideas a stage. They learn from how you carry responsibility, enjoy thinking through your strategy, and may want a part in your projects. The 10th is angular and the most public of the houses, and its natural sign is [Capricorn](/astrology/capricorn/), Saturn's sign: patience, structure, a long climb.

Close to your [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/), their Mercury also touches the highest point of your chart, and what they say about your work can carry the weight of a public opinion.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Advice about your career can sound like an appraisal. A comment on how you handled a meeting, delivered over dinner, lands like a note from a manager, and you may feel your working life has become their favourite topic. If they speak about you in public, their words carry weight, which matters when they get something wrong.

From their side, the frustration is often about credit. If their suggestions turn up in your plans without their name, they may feel used; if you won't talk about work at home, they can feel kept away from what interests them most about you.

This overlay doesn't say whether you'll get the job, the promotion or the contract, and no chart can tell you that. It describes how the two of you think and talk about your public life.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Ask for feedback when you want it, and say so when you don't. Give credit out loud. In a couple, agree how much of the evening goes to work talk, and whose career leads this year; between friends, the one who reads your application, and celebrates when it's accepted; between colleagues, a manager who explains or a peer who briefs you, with clear lines about who speaks for whom; between adult relatives in a family business, separate meetings for the business and for the family.

Turn it round and your Mercury is in their 10th house: their career becomes something you think about and talk up. Earlier in your chart, [their Mercury in your 9th house](/astrology/mercury-in-9th-house-synastry/) deals in ideas and belief; later, [their Mercury in your 11th house](/astrology/mercury-in-11th-house-synastry/) moves the talk to friends, groups and plans.

Of everything on this page, your Midheaven depends most on the minute you were born: it moves through the whole zodiac in a day, and in Placidus, the house system I use, it is the cusp of your 10th. Their Mercury needs only their birth date unless it sits a degree or two from that cusp; I ask both people for their time all the same ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). A colleague's chart is still theirs, and I read the two of you only once they've agreed ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#what-i-ask)).
