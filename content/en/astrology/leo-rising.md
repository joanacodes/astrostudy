Leo was on the eastern horizon when you were born, and the result is a warm arrival: you come into a room as if you meant to. With [Leo](/astrology/leo/) rising, your chart ruler is the Sun itself, so the hour of your birth matters twice over.

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

Your [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) sits in a fixed fire sign: enthusiasm that holds steady rather than flaring and fading. I read Leo rising as warmth at the door. You meet new people by offering something of yourself first: a welcome, a story, an invitation. In Placidus, the house system I use, the Ascendant is the cusp of the [1st house](/astrology/1st-house/). Leo is the natural sign of the 5th, the house of play and of what you create, so with Leo rising, making something and enjoying it are part of how you introduce yourself.

Leo is one of the four slowest risers in the north. It takes about 170 minutes to rise at London, nearly three hours, then 165 at Paris and 153 at New York, and about 1 London birth in 8 has it, among the commonest rising signs there. Here a time rounded to the hour usually lands in the right sign: at London, half an hour moves the Ascendant less than a fifth of the way across Leo. Only near either end does the minute decide between [Cancer rising](/astrology/cancer-rising/) and Leo, or Leo and [Virgo rising](/astrology/virgo-rising/). I still want the minute, because the degree places your houses ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). And south of the equator the order flips: signs that rise fast in the northern hemisphere rise slowly in the southern, and Leo rises faster there.

## First impressions {#first-impressions}

People tend to notice you early, often before you've said much, because Leo rising gives warmth and presence to the way you arrive. You greet people as if they were expected. Generosity is usually what they remember: the compliment, the attention, the time.

The misreading is presence taken for pride, or warmth for a wish to be watched. Recognition isn't fame. What a Leo Ascendant wants is to be seen for what it brings, and it tends to give that same recognition to others without being asked.

A word from [my testing notes](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), which concerns you twice: the rising sign is among the placements everyone reads first, and it held about a third of the time; so did the Sun sign. That's why I read Leo rising through the whole chart.

## Its ruler {#its-ruler}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) rules Leo, so with Leo rising it is your [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/): the body that organises identity, vitality and purpose speaks for the whole chart. Its sign is simply your Sun sign, the one placement everyone knows. Its house is set by the hour. Born in daylight, you have the Sun above the horizon, in one of the houses from the 7th to the 12th; born at night, below it, from the 1st to the 6th. So the time of day you were born shows where your life tends to pull you.

In Leo, the Sun is at home: born in the hours around sunrise between about 23 July and 22 August, you have the ruler in its own sign, close to the Ascendant. In Aries, its exaltation, your sense of purpose comes out simple and direct; in Libra, its fall, it is worked out with and through other people. In Aquarius, the sign on your Descendant, the Sun is in detriment, and born with it there you came in the hours around sunset: the chart ruler sits in the sign of your partners, and much of who you become is found in company.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Close up, Leo rising is loyal and warm, and wants that warmth noticed. Being taken for granted tends to hurt more than an honest quarrel. In return you're quick to celebrate the people you love: the birthday remembered, the work praised in front of others.

Your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/) is in Aquarius, opposite: you're drawn to people who keep their independence and treat you as a friend as well as a love ([Aquarius on the 7th house cusp](/astrology/aquarius-on-7th-house-cusp/)).

Between your warmth and their independence, the lesson runs both ways. You show them how to be glad of someone out loud, and they show you that love can be steady without being on display.
