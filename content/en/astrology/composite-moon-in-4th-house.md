Some relationships are, above all, a home: the place both people come back to, the table where the day is put down, the family that forms around it. A composite Moon in the 4th house sets the bond's emotional centre at its foundations.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

The [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) puts its [Moon](/astrology/moon/) halfway between your two natal Moons, and in [the 4th house](/astrology/4th-house/) that Moon sits among home, roots, family and private life, in a house whose natural sign is Cancer, the sign the Moon rules. The house is angular and begins at the [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), the lowest point of the chart. With the Moon here, the relationship's sense of safety lives in what is private and lasting: a shared home, rituals, the people it treats as family, the history it keeps.

This home is the relationship's, not a copy of either family. A [Moon in the 4th house](/astrology/moon-in-4th-house/) of one birth chart describes where that person's roots are; [their Moon in your 4th house](/astrology/moon-in-4th-house-synastry/), how someone comes to feel like home to you. The composite 4th is the household two people make together, which can differ from both of the homes they came from and often borrows a little from each: a recipe from one side, a way of celebrating from the other. Since the house's natural sign is the Moon's own, the comfort here is close to instinct: the kettle on at the same hour, the chair that waits, the door that is always answered. The relationship often becomes the place where each of you mends after a hard day outside it.

Its difficulty is the pull inward. A bond that feels safe mainly at home can close its doors, lean on the past, or let old family patterns, from either side, settle into the new home unnoticed. Home isn't confinement, and family isn't dependency. A home open enough to let other people in helps, and so does asking which family customs the two of you actually want to keep.

The 4th is one place where the two methods of drawing composite houses agree on something. Both put the composite Midheaven at the midpoint of your two Midheavens, so both put the Imum Coeli, opposite it, in the same place; they part on the other cusps, so the far edge of the 4th can still move. All of it needs both birth times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)).

A family composite, between a parent and a grown-up child or two adult siblings, can put a Moon here too; it needs both people's consent, and no child's chart is read on this site ([children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)). On either side lie the [composite Moon in the 3rd house](/astrology/composite-moon-in-3rd-house/), comforted by daily talk, and the [composite Moon in the 5th house](/astrology/composite-moon-in-5th-house/), comforted by play. I set out the composite as a technique without drawing it; those who use it read this Moon with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). It describes a need for home, never whether two people will live together or start a family.
