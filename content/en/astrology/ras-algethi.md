Ras Algethi is the head of the Kneeler, the old figure that became Hercules, and a red giant whose light rises and falls. In the zodiac it stands at 16°31′ Sagittarius, though in the sky it is far to the north of the Sun's path.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Ras Algethi is α¹ Herculis, number 6406 in the Yale Bright Star Catalogue; the International Astronomical Union spells it Rasalgethi. For an alpha it is modest, only the fifth-brightest star of Hercules, and the catalogue's magnitude 3.48 is one moment of a moving figure.

It is a red giant near the end of its life, about 360 light-years away, so swollen that in the Sun's place it would reach past the Earth's orbit. It pulsates, and its brightness follows: the full range runs from magnitude 2.7 to 4.0, though it usually moves within about 0.6 of a magnitude, in cycles of a few weeks to several years. The strongest repeats every 128 days.

In the tropical zodiac it stood at 16°08′ [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/) in 2000 and stands at 16°31′ in 2026. Its latitude is 37.3° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), even further than its neighbour [Rasalhague](/astrology/rasalhague/), the head of Ophiuchus, 5° to the east-south-east: the two may be among the closest pairs of alpha stars in the whole sky. At that distance from the ecliptic, no planet ever passes near it; a chart can only line one up with its degree ([celestial latitude](/astrology/celestial-latitude/)).

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

The Arabic ra’s al-jāthī means the head of the kneeler. It is a head at the bottom: old star maps drew Hercules upside down, so his head sits at the southern end of the figure, right beside the Serpent-bearer's.

The Greeks didn't know at first who the figure was. Aratus's *Phaenomena* describes a phantom "like to a man that strives at a task", and admits: "That sign no man knows how to read clearly, nor what task he is bent, but men simply call him On His Knees." Only later writers named him Hercules. I like an old book of the sky that says plainly where its knowledge stops.

In Chinese astronomy the star is Dìzuò, the Emperor's Seat, standing alone at the centre of its figure inside the Heavenly Market enclosure, whose right-hand wall runs through [Unukalhai](/astrology/unukalhai/), the Serpent's neck.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In 2026 the degree either side of Ras Algethi runs from 15°31′ to 17°31′ Sagittarius ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)), and the Sun crosses it on about 7 and 8 December, a few days before it reaches Rasalhague: a [Sun in Sagittarius](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius/) birthday.

Among the slow planets, Neptune held that degree for births in parts of 1977 and 1978, Uranus on and off from December 1984 to late 1985 ([Uranus in Sagittarius](/astrology/uranus-in-sagittarius/)), and Pluto in parts of 2001 and much of 2002, so each of those stretches of births shares the contact.

A star whose light changes by more than a magnitude raises a question I haven't seen answered: whether a contact with it means the same in a bright season as in a faint one. I don't try to settle it. The system I built for my readings doesn't compute stars, so Ras Algethi is something I describe on this site and leave out of anyone's chart ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). Its lore here is a figure nobody could name, which seems the right note to leave it on ([fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/)).
