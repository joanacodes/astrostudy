Some pairs are known by what they say together in public: the talk they give, the shop they run, the cause they argue for. Composite Mercury in the 10th house describes a relationship whose mind works at its most visible point.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Through [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), the relationship gets its own [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), the midpoint of your two natal Mercurys, and the house it lands in shows what that mind is turned towards. [The 10th house](/astrology/10th-house/) is the top of the chart: public role, work, reputation, the aims a life is known by. Angular, with Capricorn as its natural sign, it opens at the composite [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/).

With the bond's thinking here, its conversation faces the world. The pair often works on something together, a business, a book, a campaign, a shared role in a community, and is known for its ideas. It talks about direction a great deal: where each of you is going, what the two of you are building, what you'd like to be remembered for. It also tends to speak for itself well in public, with one voice, and colleagues may know the pair's views long before they know anything of its private life. The natal [Mercury in the 10th house](/astrology/mercury-in-10th-house/) makes one person's voice part of their working life; [their Mercury in your 10th house](/astrology/mercury-in-10th-house-synastry/) brings one person's ideas into the other's ambitions. The composite gives the pair a public voice of its own, which outsiders may recognise before they know either of you well.

Since composite Mercury rarely strays more than about 28° from the composite Sun, the Sun often sits in the 10th as well, or close by. For about 1 pair in 26, the arithmetic of midpoints puts the composite Sun across the chart instead, around the 4th, so that the bond's public mind faces its private centre.

Here, unusually, the two ways of drawing composite houses agree on one thing: the composite Midheaven is the midpoint of your two natal Midheavens in both. What still needs both birth times is the Midheaven itself, which turns through the whole zodiac each day, and the cusps after it, where the methods differ again ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)).

What it risks is the public voice drowning out the private one. A bond that talks so much about aims and work can let shop talk fill every evening, or manage its image so carefully that it stops saying what it actually thinks. Recognition isn't fame, and ambition isn't the whole of a relationship. Agreeing what you say in public and what stays between you helps, and so does a rule about when work talk stops.

None of this forecasts a career, a success or a reputation: a composite describes manner, not results. [Composite Mercury in the 9th house](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-9th-house/) wonders what it all means; [composite Mercury in the 11th house](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-11th-house/) talks it over with friends. My readings leave composites out; astrologers who use them read them with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and for a working pair, as for any other, both people agree first ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
