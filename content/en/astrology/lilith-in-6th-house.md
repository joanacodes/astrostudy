Lilith in the 6th house lives in the ordinary day: the tasks, the timetable, the way the kettle gets filled. [Black Moon Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/), the point of the Moon's orbit farthest from Earth, was in [the 6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) when you were born, and the part you learned to hide shows in how you work, and in what you won't put up with in how you're asked to.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The 6th is where a life gets maintained: it's a cadent house, the second of the four, and its natural sign is Virgo. The old astrologers called it Bad Fortune and gave it toil; Mars, the planet of effort, had its joy here. It covers daily work — what's on the list rather than what's on the business card — routines and habits, the colleagues beside you, and the everyday care you give yourself. With Lilith in it, the hidden part is often your own method: a rhythm, an order, a way of doing ordinary things that worked for you and wasn't the house style.

What this Lilith refuses is being treated as help. It will work hard, and often well, but not as an extension of someone else's will, and not on a timetable that makes no sense. Its boundaries are practical: what's in your job and what isn't, which hours are yours, how a thing gets done once it's yours to do.

Tradition gave the 6th illness. I don't read illness in anyone's chart: a chart is no tool for diagnosis, and a worry about your health deserves a doctor, not an astrologer ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)). What I read there is how your days are built.

The house needs your birth time ([what the time decides](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)); in Placidus, the system I use, the 6th ends where the 7th begins, at the Descendant. On one side of it is play, [Lilith in the 5th house](/astrology/lilith-in-5th-house/); on the other, partnership, [Lilith in the 7th house](/astrology/lilith-in-7th-house/). Between them, this Lilith lives in the timetable.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Love, for this Lilith, is tested on ordinary weekdays. Who does the washing-up, who remembers the appointments, who carries the invisible work of a household: the hidden part notices, and refuses to become anyone's staff. It's often deeply caring in practical ways, and it wants that care noticed, shared and never assumed.

The relationships that suit it divide the everyday fairly, and leave each person a routine of their own.

## At work {#at-work}

This is where the placement lives. You may be excellent at the craft of a job and impatient with its procedures, especially the ones that exist to make someone else feel in control. Micromanagement lands badly; so does always being handed the dull half of every task. The hidden part does its best work with a clear brief and freedom over the method, and it often ends up changing how a whole team works without anyone having decided that it should.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The refusal can turn against every routine, including the ones that would help: the sleep, the meals, the order that would make a week easier. Or it goes underground, and you do everything you're asked, silently, while resentment builds up behind the efficiency.

Neither is a failing. Both are what happens to a method that was never given room.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Design your own routines, deliberately, and treat them as yours rather than as rules imposed on you. Negotiate how you work rather than whether: the method, the hours, the order of things. Say *that isn't my job* once, calmly, when it isn't.

And build rest into the day as firmly as work. A routine that includes you is the only kind this Lilith will keep.
