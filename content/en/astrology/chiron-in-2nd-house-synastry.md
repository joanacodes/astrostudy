What you own, what you charge and what you think you deserve: that's the ground their Chiron enters when it falls in your 2nd house. The other person's old sore spot, and what it taught them, settles among your belongings and your sense of value.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Chiron](/astrology/chiron/), in my reading, is the place where a person learned to function around something that never quite stopped stinging, and grew skilled because of it. [The 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) holds what you have and what you value: the money you earn and keep, the things you'd protect, the talents you could live from, and underneath them your sense of your own worth. Placed there as a [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their Chiron brings that hard-won skill to your resources.

With them, you may finally name a price. They tend to see the talent you give away for nothing, the rate you never raised, the purchase you keep apologising for. Someone who has had to work around a sore spot knows what a skill built the long way is worth, and they tend to say so, without fuss, when you price yours too low.

For them, your relationship with having can be moving, or tender. Your ease with comfort, or your careful thrift, may brush whatever they have had to work around, and money can carry more of their old feelings than they show; often they turn generous towards you in very practical ways. In the 2nd, a succedent house, what the 1st set moving comes to rest and is kept; [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), its natural sign, values what is solid and lasts.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Money is where old feelings hide in plain sight. Their advice on what you should charge, buy or save can start to sound like a judgement on your values, and you may feel your belongings are being weighed. If they help you out financially, gratitude can tangle with a sense of owing.

From their side, the sore spot can be touched by small things: a casual remark about what something cost, a gift received without much notice, salaries compared over dinner. They may also over-give, which is one way of proving to themselves that they have enough to offer.

An overlay doesn't predict earnings, losses or debts, and I never read one for that. It shows how two people value things, and themselves, in each other's company.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Keep money talk plain and specific: who pays for what, and why. Let their eye for your undervalued skills be a gift you choose to use. In a couple, a clear agreement about what's shared and what stays separate protects both people; between friends, a loan is better spelled out than assumed; between colleagues, they may help you set a fee, as long as the decision stays yours; between adult relatives, old family attitudes to money deserve a gentle look.

If the Chiron is yours and the 2nd house theirs, everything above runs the other way: your old feelings about worth would meet their possessions and pay. Next door, [their Chiron in your 1st house](/astrology/chiron-in-1st-house-synastry/) touches how you come across, and [their Chiron in your 3rd house](/astrology/chiron-in-3rd-house-synastry/) how you speak and learn.

Their birth date is enough to fix their Chiron, which hardly moves in a day. Your birth time draws the 2nd house, and in Placidus, the house system I use, how much of the zodiac it spans depends on when and where you were born, so I won't read the pair until I have both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Chiron was one of the three placements, with Saturn and Lilith, that held 75 to 90% of the time; those were single charts, and for an overlay I have no figure, only the reason I look here early. Worth is private, so this waits for both people's yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
