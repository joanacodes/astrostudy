Denebola is the tip of the Lion's tail, at the opposite end of the constellation from Regulus, and its oldest lore is about the weather: Arabic astronomers called it the Changer. In the zodiac it stands at 21°58′ Virgo.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Denebola is Beta Leonis, magnitude 2.14, the easternmost of the Lion's bright stars, 36 light-years away. It is a young white star, less than 400 million years old, spinning so fast that it bulges at the equator, and it is ringed by a disc of cold dust about 39 times the Earth's distance from the Sun, the kind of disc in which planets are thought to form. Its light wavers too, by a fortieth of a magnitude, some ten times a day.

Its tropical position was 20°38′ [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/) for a birth in 1930, 21°36′ in 2000 and 21°58′ in 2026: the Lion's tail in the sign of the Maiden, while [Regulus](/astrology/regulus/), at the Lion's other end, only crossed into Virgo between 2000 and 2026 ([constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/)). Its latitude, 12.3° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), is more than twice as far as the Moon ever strays; of the planets, only Pluto, on its tilted orbit, can pass north of it, as it did in 1966–68, about 2½° away.

With [Arcturus](/astrology/arcturus/) and [Spica](/astrology/spica/), Denebola makes a version of the Spring Triangle, more nearly equilateral than the usual one drawn with Regulus.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

Shortened from the Arabic ðanab al-asad, the tail of the lion, the name appears as Denebalezeth in the Alfonsine Tables and as Deneb Aleet on R. A. Proctor's star chart of 1871. The 15th-century astronomer Ulugh Beg recorded a title of its own, al-Ṣarfah, the Changer, meaning the change of the weather, and al-Biruni, four centuries earlier, explained it: "The heat turns away when it rises, and the cold turns away when it disappears." The same name marked one of the Arabic lunar mansions, as-Ṣarfa, just as [Sadalsuud](/astrology/sadalsuud/), the luck of lucks, marked another ([the 28 lunar mansions](/astrology/28-lunar-mansions/)); the mansion that follows as-Ṣarfa, the Barker, takes in [Vindemiatrix](/astrology/vindemiatrix/), the Maiden's grape-gatherer.

In India Denebola stands for Uttara Phalguni, the second reddish one, the 12th nakshatra, whose lord is the Sun ([Vedic astrology (Jyotish)](/astrology/vedic-astrology-jyotish/)); in China it was the first star of the Seat of the Five Emperors. The lore I could check is about the sky itself: a star whose rising and setting told people the season was turning.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

A planet or angle counts as conjunct Denebola within about a degree of its longitude: 20°58′ to 22°58′ Virgo for a birth in 2026, 19°38′ to 21°38′ for one in 1930 ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). The Sun is there on about 13 and 14 September, a [Sun in Virgo](/astrology/sun-in-virgo/) in the last third of the sign. In the late 1960s two slow planets took turns on it, Uranus in parts of 1966–67 and [Pluto in Virgo](/astrology/pluto-in-virgo/) at intervals from November 1966 to September 1968; Neptune had passed in 1937–39. For the Moon and the angles, the birth time settles it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

A star that once told the turn of the seasons can't tell the turn of a life, and I don't ask it to: stars play no part in the readings I give ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).
