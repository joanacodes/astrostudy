Criticise one of their friends and you'll find both of them at the door. That reflex is composite Mars in Cancer: a relationship whose drive protects, whose desire needs safety first, and whose anger tends to come out sideways.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) in a composite is the midpoint of two natal Marses, read as the bond's will to act and to defend itself ([the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/)). [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), the Moon's cardinal water sign, holds Mars's [fall](/astrology/fall/) in the tradition, opposite its exaltation in Capricorn. A fall says the planet's straight line bends here; the drive is no smaller, it travels through care. About 1 pair in 10 has this composite Mars, a little more than the average sign, since Mars spends about 47 days in Cancer on a usual visit.

Such a pair moves hardest when something it shelters is under threat: a friend in trouble, an ageing parent, the flat, each other. It may never compete with anyone on its own account and still take on a landlord, a school or a whole family to protect one of its people. Cancer is a cardinal sign, and it shows: this pair is quick to start looking after someone, however slow it is to start a fight. At home the energy goes into feeding, mending and gathering people round the table. Desire needs safety first, and tenderness opens doors that boldness would close. Natal [Mars in Cancer](/astrology/mars-in-cancer/) describes one person acting for others; the composite describes the loyalty you hold as a pair, a shared reflex to close ranks.

Arguments rarely arrive head-on. Hurt comes out sideways: a cupboard shut harder than it needs to be, a long silence over dinner, a remark about the other's family, while the real complaint, *I needed you to look after me*, stays unsaid. Old grievances are kept, like everything else this bond keeps. What helps is naming a hurt while it's small and saying the need behind it plainly. Protection isn't control, either: guarding each other shouldn't turn into deciding for each other. A pair this loyal also needs a way to disagree with its own circle without feeling it has betrayed anyone.

[Composite Mars in Gemini](/astrology/composite-mars-in-gemini/) argues everything out in words; [composite Mars in Leo](/astrology/composite-mars-in-leo/) acts with pride, in full view. Cancer works from inside the house.

Composites aren't among the charts I read, but the method is worth knowing. In it, this Mars is weighed against the composite [Moon](/astrology/moon/), Cancer's ruler, which shows what lets the bond rest easy, and against the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), where a contact like [Moon square Mars in synastry](/astrology/moon-square-mars-synastry/) shows whose actions stir whose feelings. Neither says anything about a wedding, a separation or a family to come.

This Mars is about defending a home, never about making anyone afraid in it. If being at home together ever feels unsafe, astrology has no part in that: in the UK, ring 999 in an emergency, or Samaritans, free, on 116 123. And a relationship this protective of its privacy would want both people's yes before anyone draws its chart ([Synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
