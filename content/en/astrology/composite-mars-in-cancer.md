Criticise one of their friends and you'll find both of them at the door. Composite Mars in Cancer gives the relationship's chart a protective drive: the bond acts to shelter its own, wants closeness that feels safe, and shows its anger sideways more often than head-on.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) sets [Mars](/astrology/mars/) at the midpoint of your two natal Marses and reads it as the bond's will to act and to defend itself. [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), cardinal water ruled by the Moon, is where tradition puts Mars's [fall](/astrology/fall/), opposite its exaltation in Capricorn. A fall says the planet's straight line bends here; the drive is no smaller, it travels through care. About 1 pair in 10 has this composite Mars, a little more than the average sign, since Mars spends about 47 days in Cancer on a usual visit.

Such a pair moves hardest when something it shelters is under threat: a friend in trouble, an ageing parent, the flat, each other. It may never compete with anyone on its own account and still take on a landlord, a school or a whole family to protect one of its people. At home the energy goes into feeding, mending and gathering people round the table. Desire needs safety first, and tenderness opens doors that boldness would close. Natal [Mars in Cancer](/astrology/mars-in-cancer/) describes one person acting for others; the composite describes the loyalty you hold as a pair, a shared reflex to close ranks.

Arguments rarely arrive head-on. Hurt comes out sideways: a cupboard shut harder than it needs to be, a long silence over dinner, a remark about the other's family, while the real complaint, *I didn't feel looked after*, stays unsaid. Old grievances are kept, like everything else this bond keeps. What helps is naming a hurt while it's small and saying the need behind it plainly. Protection isn't control, either: guarding each other shouldn't turn into deciding for each other. A pair this loyal also needs a way to disagree with its own circle without feeling it has betrayed anyone.

This Mars is about defending a home, never about making anyone afraid in it. If being at home together ever feels unsafe, astrology has no part in that: in the UK, ring 999 in an emergency, and Samaritans answer on 116 123, day and night.

[Composite Mars in Gemini](/astrology/composite-mars-in-gemini/) argues everything out in words; [composite Mars in Leo](/astrology/composite-mars-in-leo/) acts with pride, in full view. Cancer works from inside the house.

I describe composites here without drawing them in my readings. In the method, this Mars is read with the composite [Moon](/astrology/moon/), Cancer's ruler, for what the relationship needs in order to feel safe, and with the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), where one person's Mars touching the other's Moon ([Moon square Mars in synastry](/astrology/moon-square-mars-synastry/)) shows whose actions stir whose feelings. Neither says anything about a wedding, a separation or a family to come. The sign needs your two birth dates, and your times only within about half a degree of Gemini or Leo. And a relationship this protective of its privacy would want both people's yes before anyone draws its chart ([Synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
