Put Mercury in the 10th house and your mind goes to work in public. Whatever you do for a living, you tend to be known for how you think and how you explain, and your words have a way of becoming part of your reputation.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 10th house](/astrology/10th-house/) is your public life: work, vocation, reputation, the part you play in the world. Its cusp is the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/), which makes it the most visible of the four angular houses, and its natural sign is Capricorn. With [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) here, communication and ideas are part of your public role: you may be known as the clear one, the one who writes well, the one who can explain. The sign colours the reputation. A 10th-house Mercury in Taurus is trusted for good sense, in Gemini for range, in Scorpio for discretion, and in Capricorn, the 10th's own sign, for authority ([Mercury in Capricorn](/astrology/mercury-in-capricorn/)).

The Midheaven is where the zodiac crosses the meridian, not the point straight overhead, and a 10th-house Mercury was approaching that crossing, high in the sky, when you were born. Since Mercury travels close to the Sun, it usually means a daytime birth. In Placidus, the system I use, the Midheaven is the cusp of the 10th, and a birth time even half an hour out can move Mercury into the 9th or the 11th ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

On one side, [Mercury in the 9th house](/astrology/mercury-in-9th-house/) studies the world; on the other, [Mercury in the 11th house](/astrology/mercury-in-11th-house/) talks it over with friends. Here, what the mind says goes on record.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Your work voice can follow you home. You may find it easier to talk about projects than feelings, and a partner may know your views on everything except yourself. Shared ambitions can be a strong bond, as long as home doesn't turn into another meeting. Some of your best conversations may happen on the way home, when nobody is taking notes. A partner who respects your work and still asks how you are, not how it's going, helps this Mercury rest.

## At work {#at-work}

Here Mercury is at its most public: writing, speaking, teaching, broadcasting, management, consulting, public relations, any role where your ideas and words are the work. You often become the person others quote, or the one sent to represent the team. People may remember a sentence of yours for years, which is a responsibility as well as a compliment. Writing things down protects you here: emails, reports and notes keep your ideas attached to your name. Your reputation tends to rest on clarity, so it's worth protecting: say what you know, and say when you don't.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Becoming only your public voice: always articulate, always on, never allowed to think out loud or be unsure. Saying the official thing instead of the true one. Measuring a thought by how it will look rather than whether it's right. Criticism of your work can feel like criticism of your mind, because here the two are so closely tied. And a working identity built on words can make silence feel like failure.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Keep a private notebook where nothing is for an audience. Let a few people hear your unfinished thoughts. Choose the subjects you want to be known for, and let the rest of your thinking stay private for a while. When you speak in public, keep what you know apart from what you guess; it builds the kind of reputation that lasts. Practise saying *I don't know yet* in a meeting: for a mind this visible, it's a surprisingly strong sentence. And let your work voice rest at the end of the day, because the people at home need a different one.
