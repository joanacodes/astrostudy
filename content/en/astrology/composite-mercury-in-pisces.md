Some pairs can choose a film, a route home or a name for the cat with almost nothing said aloud. Composite Mercury in Pisces puts that kind of understanding at the centre of a relationship's thinking: a shared mind that works by image and impression, and often knows before it can explain.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), made of midpoints one planet at a time, composite [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is the way the relationship thinks, talks and makes sense of things. In [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/), mutable water, that sense-making runs through feeling and imagination. Of the two million pairs in the site's count, about 1 in 12 has it.

This bond communicates in more than words: a song that says *sorry*, a look across a room, a dream told over breakfast and remembered for years. It listens well, to what lies under a sentence as much as to the sentence, and it tends to be gentle with confusion. Imaginative projects suit it: a story written together, a garden planned by feel, music. The intuition is the relationship's: two people who need everything spelled out at work can understand each other here from half a sentence. Someone with a natal [Mercury in Pisces](/astrology/mercury-in-pisces/) understands this way alone; a composite Mercury in Pisces understands this way only in company, the company of the two of you.

Tradition gives Mercury both its [detriment](/astrology/detriment/) and its [fall](/astrology/fall/) in Pisces, opposite Virgo: a mind made for distinctions working in a sign that blurs them. For a relationship, that describes how understanding arrives, as impressions rather than lists, not how much of it there is. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) ruled Pisces in the tradition and [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) rules it in modern tropical astrology; in a birth chart I read both. In the composite, Jupiter's midpoint shows where the bond goes looking for meaning; Neptune, about fourteen years in each sign, mostly marks the generation you were born into.

Most pairs with this placement also have the composite Sun in Aquarius, Pisces or Aries, as a birth chart would. In about 1 pair in 18, though, composite Mercury drifts more than 28° from the composite Sun, and in about 1 pair in 26 the Sun sits around Virgo, facing it. Pisces, of all signs, is tempted to find a meaning in that. There isn't one here; it comes from the way midpoints are worked out.

Trouble starts with what was understood but never said. A bond that senses so much can assume it has agreed when each of you heard something different, and a plan made in a mood can dissolve when the mood does. Imagination isn't deception, and empathy isn't the absence of boundaries, but both work better with a little written down. Repeating arrangements back helps (*so, six o'clock at the station?*), and so does a shared calendar for the facts, which leaves the feelings free to stay unspoken when they want to.

[Composite Mercury in Aquarius](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-aquarius/) wants to know why; [composite Mercury in Aries](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-aries/), where the cycle begins again, says it first and plainly. Pisces already knows, and is still looking for the words.

I describe composites rather than draw them; astrologers who use them read them with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). However well you think you know what the other person would say, a composite is never something to draw on their behalf without asking ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
