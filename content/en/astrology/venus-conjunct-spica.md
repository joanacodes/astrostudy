Venus conjunct Spica puts Venus in one of its own signs, late in Libra, on a star that medieval magic assigned to Venus and Mercury. Only births between late August and early December can have it, about 1 person in 218, and in some years the two came close enough in the sky to be seen together.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) is, for me, the planet of preference and exchange: what you are drawn to, what you think beautiful or fair, and how you like giving and receiving to balance out. [Spica](/astrology/spica/) is one of the stars the old magic tied to Venus: the Behenian list of fifteen talisman stars names Venus for six of them, and gives Spica to Venus and Mercury together.

The star's degree, 22°51′ Libra in 1930 and 24°12′ in 2026, also puts Venus in a sign it rules ([Venus in Libra](/astrology/venus-in-libra/)): a Venus that values proportion and fair dealing, and likes agreement best when both people have had their say. Star and sign agree here, which is rare. The tradition would call it Venus at home on a star of its own nature; I'd call it Venus in Libra with an old compliment attached, and read the compliment as lightly as I would an insult.

Because Spica lies only 2° from the Sun's path, Venus can pass very close to it, and Venus's eight-year rhythm decides when. On 30 or 31 August of 1930, and of every eighth year after that until 2002, Venus was shining in the evening sky about as far from the Sun as it ever gets, and it went by within about half a degree of the star. Anyone looking west after sunset on those evenings could see the brightest planet with Spica beside it, about a full Moon's width away. A second eight-year series brings it past Spica in early November, as the star returns to the dawn sky: on 3 November 2009 Venus passed 3.5° north of it. The same eight-year return underlies the [Venus star point](/astrology/venus-star-point/).

The season is fixed by Venus's short leash to the Sun. From 1930 to 2009 the first birthday with Venus on Spica fell on 30 August and the last on 2 December, and within that span the date moves from year to year. Venus crosses the degree only once a year, quickly, so about 1 person in 218 born in those eighty years has the contact, fewer than for any other body on this star except Saturn.

Each body meets Spica on different terms, because Libra treats them differently in the tradition. The [Sun conjunct Spica](/astrology/sun-conjunct-spica/) falls in the Sun's fall, [Saturn conjunct Spica](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-spica/) in Saturn's exaltation, [Mars conjunct Spica](/astrology/mars-conjunct-spica/) in Mars's detriment, and the [Moon conjunct Spica](/astrology/moon-conjunct-spica/) in a sign where the Moon has no special standing. Venus alone is at home.

None of that makes a star part of my readings: I don't place Spica, or any other star, in a chart ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). What I'd read is Venus in Libra, its house and its aspects, and the tradition's rule for star contacts is set out under [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/).
