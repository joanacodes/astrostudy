Apollo was found in 1932, then lost for 41 years. It has since given its name to a whole class of asteroids, the ones whose orbits cross the Earth's, and it shares that name with a point in Uranian astrology that is something else entirely.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Apollo is a stony asteroid about 1.5 km across. Karl Reinmuth found it at Heidelberg on 24 April 1932, but it was lost and not recovered until 1973. Its orbit is stretched: at its closest it comes 0.65 times the Earth's distance from the Sun, inside the orbit of [Venus](/astrology/venus/); at its farthest, 2.3 times, beyond Mars. One circuit takes about 1.8 years. In 2005 radar from the Arecibo Observatory showed that it has a moon, about 80 metres across, circling only 3 km away.

It was the first of its kind to be recognised, and the whole group now carries its name. The Apollo asteroids are, on average, farther from the Sun than the Earth, but come in closer than the Earth's farthest point, so their paths cross ours; by January 2025 more than 21,000 were known, the largest group of near-Earth objects. Because Apollo was lost for so long, others were numbered before it: [Icarus](/astrology/icarus/), found in 1949, is number 1566.

Like Icarus, Apollo counts as potentially hazardous by the astronomers' definition, which is about size and orbital geometry. Its orbit is well determined for the next several hundred years, and one meeting already worked out is with Venus: on 17 May 2075 it will pass about 1.2 million km from that planet.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Apollo was the son of Zeus and Leto and the twin of Artemis: god of prophecy, through his oracle at Delphi, of music and poetry, of archery, and of healing, though his arrows could also bring plague. He was called the averter of evil and protected herds and the young, and from the fifth century BC he was often identified with Helios, the Sun. He is also the god who gave [Kassandra](/astrology/kassandra/) her prophecy and then made sure no one would believe her.

His name follows the rules. Near-Earth asteroids are meant to receive names from mythology, except names tied to creation or the underworld, and the Apollo group mostly takes Greek gods. Ordinary main-belt asteroids can carry almost any name the rules allow, an everyday first name included ([Personal-name asteroids](/astrology/personal-name-asteroids/)).

Astrology has a second Apollo, and the two are easy to confuse. Apollon is one of the eight hypothetical points beyond Neptune used in Uranian astrology ([The Hamburg School (Uranian astrology)](/astrology/hamburg-school-uranian-astrology/)), and it has a page of its own ([Apollon (Uranian point)](/astrology/apollon-uranian-point/)). No telescope has found it. The asteroid, by contrast, is small, real and close.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I don't read the asteroid Apollo. It was never on my list: my notebook checked the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign, each planet, Chiron and Lilith, and the system I built for my readings computes no asteroid ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

Two things about this name call for care. Apollo healed and brought plague, and a body named after him invites questions about health that no chart can answer; I don't read charts for illness, and a doctor is the person for those questions ([Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)). He was also identified with the Sun, which makes it tempting to treat the asteroid as a second [Sun](/astrology/sun/). It isn't one. The real Sun is in every reading I give, and even the Sun sign held only about a third of the time in my notebook ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)); a 1.5-km rock with no record at all can't stand in for it.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The asteroid Apollo is the Uranian Apollon.”** One is a real near-Earth asteroid; the other is a hypothetical point beyond Neptune that no one has observed.
- **“Apollo was the first Apollo asteroid to be numbered.”** It was the first found, but it was lost until 1973, so it carries the number 1862, after Icarus's 1566.
- **“Apollo was always the Sun god.”** He was identified with Helios, the Sun, from the fifth century BC; in older myth the Sun was Helios's own.
