Cassandra could see what was coming and could make no one believe her. Asteroid 114 carries her name in its Greek spelling, and on an astrology site her story deserves a straight answer: I don't predict, so her curse isn't one I can borrow, or wish on anyone.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Kassandra is a dark asteroid about 100 km across in the main belt, between 2.3 and 3.0 times the Earth's distance from the Sun, and one circuit takes about 4.4 years. It belongs to the rare T type: parts of its spectrum resemble the mineral troilite and carbonaceous meteorites, and the spectrum as a whole looks like that of fine grains from the Ornans meteorite, which fell in France in 1868. It turns once in about 10.8 hours, and radar from the Arecibo Observatory reached it in 2001.

C. H. F. Peters found it on 23 July 1871. Born in Schleswig and trained in Germany, he became director of the Litchfield Observatory at Hamilton College, in New York, and was a pioneer of finding asteroids by eye. Kassandra took the number 114, a measure of how fast the list had grown since [Astraea](/astrology/astraea/), number 5, broke the long pause. The names still came from Greek myth, the darker stories included: [Medusa](/astrology/medusa/) is number 149.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Cassandra was a daughter of Priam, king of Troy, and a priestess of Apollo. In the commonest telling, the god gave her true prophecy in exchange for a promise, and when she refused him he added a curse: no one would ever believe her. In Aeschylus's *Agamemnon* she admits it herself: she consented to the god, broke her word, and "ever since that fault I could persuade no one of anything." Another version, recorded by Tzetzes, has her and her brother Helenus left overnight in Apollo's temple and found in the morning with snakes licking their ears.

Her family took her for a liar or a madwoman, and Priam is said to have kept her shut away. Homer, who calls her the loveliest of Priam's daughters, has her announce to the city that Hector's body is being brought home, so that Troy can mourn. She survived the fall of the city, was taken to Mycenae by Agamemnon, and died there beside him. In English her name now stands for anyone whose accurate warnings go unheeded.

Her gift came from Apollo, who has an asteroid of his own ([Apollo (asteroid)](/astrology/apollo-asteroid/)). Placed in a chart, Kassandra gets what any main-belt asteroid gets: a sign shared with many people born around the same time, a house that needs a birth time ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and contacts that matter only when tight ([Orbs](/astrology/orbs/)). None of that can foretell anything, which is the one thing her name seems to promise.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I leave Kassandra out of readings, as I do every asteroid: the system I built doesn't compute them, and this one was never tested. My notebook went through the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign, each planet, Chiron and Lilith, and even the placements everyone reads first, the Sun sign, the rising sign, Venus and Mercury, held only about a third of the time ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). An asteroid with no line at all has nothing to show.

Her story is worth turning round, though. Cassandra's tragedy depends on prophecy being possible, and a reading of mine never says what will happen. A transit is weather, not a forecast: it describes the conditions of a stretch of time, not the events in it ([Transits](/astrology/transits/)). So if a reading, mine or anyone's, leaves you braced for a disaster, the reading has gone wrong, not you: no chart can foretell a death or a catastrophe ([Can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)), and no one should frighten you with one ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Cassandra's prophecies were false.”** They were true. The curse was that no one believed them.
- **“The asteroid Kassandra is heading for the Earth.”** That was the plot of a 2009 television miniseries, *Meteor*. The real Kassandra stays in the main belt, more than twice as far from the Sun as the Earth.
- **“Kassandra and Cassandra are two different women.”** They're one Trojan prophetess; Kassandra is the Greek spelling.
