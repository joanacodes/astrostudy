The first decan of Aries opens the zodiac: the ten degrees after the March equinox, where the count of signs, of faces and of decans all begin. Every scheme that hands these degrees to a planet hands them to Mars.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

By face, the ruler is [Mars](/astrology/mars/). William Lilly's table of [faces](/astrology/faces/), printed in *Christian Astrology* in 1647, starts the zodiac here and walks down the [Chaldean order](/astrology/chaldean-order/) from Mars, so the Sun takes the next ten degrees and Venus the last. By element, the modern decans give the first decan of every sign to that sign's own ruler, and the ruler of [Aries](/astrology/aries/) is Mars again.

So sign, face and decan answer to one planet. With most decans I keep two rulers in view and look at where each of them stands in the chart. Here there is only one to find: whatever sits in these degrees, the sign, house and aspects of your Mars describe how it gets used. [Mars in Aries](/astrology/mars-in-aries/) between 0° and 10° is in its own sign and its own face at once, five points and one in Lilly's scoring of dignities.

The Sun is exalted in Aries, but it has to wait: its exaltation degree and its face both lie in the next ten degrees ([Aries, second decan](/astrology/aries-second-decan/)).

## Its image {#its-image}

Egypt started counting somewhere else. Its sequence of decans opened with Sothis, the star Sirius, at the start of Cancer, which makes the zodiac's first decan Egypt's twenty-eighth: Khent-kheru, as E. A. Wallis Budge transliterated it in 1904. the Greco-Egyptian lists give Chont-har; Firmicus Maternus, in the fourth century, called it Senator; Athanasius Kircher, in 1652, gave it to Arueris, a form of Horus. In the list of Cosmas of Maiuma every decan carries the name of a god or a personification, and this one is Aidoneus, another name for Hades; Persephone, his queen, takes the next.

Medieval magic drew pictures as well: the *Picatrix* describes images of the decans for making talismans ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)). Those pictures reach me only through translations I haven't been able to check line by line, so I don't repeat them second-hand.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun stands here from about 21 to 30 March, the first ten days of spring in the northern hemisphere ([Sun in Aries](/astrology/sun-in-aries/)). In 2026 it reached 0° Aries, the [Aries Point](/astrology/aries-point/), on 20 March at 14:45 UT and left the decan on 30 March at 16:58 UT; in 2027 it does both about six hours later.

Seen from the northern hemisphere, this decan and the last one of Pisces are the quickest stretch of the zodiac to rise. At the latitude of London the whole decan rises in about seventeen minutes, so a birth time rounded to the half-hour can move the Ascendant into the next decan, or back into Pisces ([Aries rising](/astrology/aries-rising/)). South of the equator the same degrees rise slowly. That is a detail I won't guess at, and one reason I don't read a chart without its time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

The slow planets mark years. Saturn crossed these degrees in 1937–38, 1967–68 and 1996–97, and Uranus from 1927 to 1930, so whole school years carry one of them here, its house making it personal.

They are crossing again now. Neptune settled in on 26 January 2026 and stays until 2029, with a last visit from September 2029 to March 2030 ([Neptune in Aries, 2026–2039](/astrology/neptune-in-aries-2026-2039/)). Saturn met it at 0°45′ Aries on 20 February 2026 ([The Saturn–Neptune conjunction of 2026](/astrology/saturn-neptune-conjunction-of-2026/)), moved on in May and came back retrograde on 21 October, until 28 January 2027. If a planet or angle of yours sits in these degrees, that is weather over it, not a forecast.

Among the fixed stars, [Algenib](/astrology/algenib/), γ Pegasi, sits close to the decan's far edge, at 9°31′ Aries in 2026.
