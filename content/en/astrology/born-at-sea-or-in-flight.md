A chart is drawn for a time and a point on the Earth. When the point was moving, the chart still needs one: where the ship or the plane was at the minute you were born, read from a clock you can name.

## Step by step {#step-by-step}

1. **Copy the time exactly as it was written, with its source.** A birth record, a report from the ship or the crew, a family letter: keep the words around the time, and don't convert anything yet ([How to find your birth time](/astrology/how-to-find-your-birth-time/)).
2. **Find out which clock it was read from.** At sea, ships may put their clocks forward or back at a convenient moment, usually at night, rather than exactly when they cross a line of longitude, and some keep the time of the port they left for the whole voyage. On a plane, the time may come from a crew member or from a passenger's watch still set to the departure or the destination. Ask whoever wrote it down.
3. **Convert it to Universal Time.** Once you know the clock and its offset from Greenwich, the conversion is the same as for any birth, daylight saving included ([Time zones and daylight saving in charts](/astrology/time-zones-and-daylight-saving-in-charts/)). Convert the date with the time: it can change.
4. **Find the position at that minute.** You need the latitude and longitude of the ship or the aircraft when you were born. Ask the company that ran the voyage or the flight whether its records give the position at that time, and ask your family what they were told on board.
5. **Don't take the place on your papers for the position.** Records follow legal rules, not the sky. The US State Department's guidance on recording a place of birth, for instance, uses “AT SEA” for a birth in international waters, the country's name for one in its territorial waters, and “IN THE AIR” over a region no country claims. A place that has changed its name raises the same question of what the paper says against where you were ([Birthplaces that changed names](/astrology/birthplaces-that-changed-names/)).
6. **If you only know the route, bracket the position.** Work out the earliest and latest points the ship or plane could have reached, and draw the chart for both. One degree of longitude moves the angles as much as four minutes of clock time; latitude changes the size of the houses. If the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) changes sign or a planet changes house between the two, an exact position matters all the more. On a route near the poles, Placidus, the house system I use, may not place every house ([Houses at high latitudes](/astrology/houses-at-high-latitudes/)).
7. **Write the coordinates down with their source.** Note how each figure was found and how sure it is, then enter the coordinates in place of a town. If you're unsure whether what you have is enough for a [birth chart reading](/birth-chart/), write to me before you order.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes}

- **Using the port or the airport.** The place you left and the place you arrived are where the trip began and ended, not where you were born. Over a long passage they can be thousands of kilometres from the right point.
- **Using the flag or the paperwork.** A ship's country of registration and a record that says “at sea” answer legal questions. A chart needs coordinates.
- **Taking ship's time for the coast's time.** A ship's clocks follow the captain's choices, not the time zone of the nearest land, and they may have changed the night before.
- **Losing the date.** Converting to Universal Time can move the birth to the day before or after, especially far east or west of Greenwich. Convert the date and the time together.
- **Rounding the position to the nearest land.** Every degree of longitude you round away shifts the angles as if the clock had been four minutes out.
- **Filling the gap with a guess.** If no time can be found, there is nothing to draw: without the hour, the rising sign and the houses are missing, and I don't read a chart on the half that's left ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

For now I can only read with the time and place of birth, and for a birth on the move, the place is a pair of coordinates. Once you have them, with the clock behind the time, your chart is drawn like anyone else's.
