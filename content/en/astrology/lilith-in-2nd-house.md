Lilith in the 2nd house is about what you're worth, and who gets to say. [Black Moon Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/), the point of the Moon's orbit farthest from Earth, was in [the 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) when you were born, and it marks a sense of your own value — and of what is yours — that learned early to keep quiet.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The 2nd house is succedent: it follows the 1st, and what the 1st begins, the 2nd gathers and keeps. Its natural sign is Taurus. It covers your resources in the widest sense — money, belongings, skills, time — and the values that decide what they're for, including the value you place on yourself. With Lilith here, the hidden part is usually tied up with worth: wanting things for yourself, owning them outright, refusing to be paid in gratitude. If money or wanting was a loaded subject at home, the wanting may have gone quiet while the refusal didn't.

What this Lilith won't accept is being bought. A gift with strings, a salary that comes with silence, a security that costs you your say: the hidden part notices the price, and often pays it once and never again. Its boundaries are about ownership — what's yours, what isn't for sale — and they hold.

I don't read money outcomes in a chart, here or anywhere: the 2nd describes how you handle what you have and value, not what you'll earn or lose.

Lilith barely moves in a day, about a ninth of a degree, while the houses turn right round, so the house depends entirely on your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). I use Placidus, in which the 2nd begins at the cusp after the Ascendant ([which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)). Read along the houses, the hidden part moves outward: [Lilith in the 1st house](/astrology/lilith-in-1st-house/) shows in your face, Lilith in the 2nd in what you'll accept in exchange for your time, and [Lilith in the 3rd house](/astrology/lilith-in-3rd-house/) in what you'll say out loud.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In a couple, Lilith in the 2nd house wants its own: its own money, its own things, its own say in how shared resources are spent. Financial dependence can feel to it like a leash, even a kind one, and it may refuse help it needs rather than owe anyone. It also tends to show love in what it gives — time, attention, carefully chosen things — and to feel it in what it's allowed to keep.

The bonds that last respect the line between *ours* and *mine*, and talk about money plainly before it turns into a test.

## At work {#at-work}

Pay is where this Lilith becomes visible. You may find it hard to name your price, or impossible not to; either way, being underpaid lands as an insult to your worth rather than a line in a budget. The hidden part refuses work that goes against its values, even well-paid work, and does best where it owns something: a skill, a client list, a share in what it builds.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Worth can get tangled up with earnings, so that a bad month feels like a verdict on you. Or the opposite: having learned that wanting things was greedy, you keep almost nothing for yourself and are fierce about your independence — no help, no gifts, no needing. Both versions leave your worth on someone else's scales.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Separate what you're worth from what you're paid; write down, if it helps, what you value that money can't measure. Then name your price anyway, out loud, once, and see what happens. Own one thing outright — a tool, a corner, a sum kept for your own use — that answers to nobody.

And let receiving be allowed. A gift accepted isn't a debt; it can simply be a gift. This Lilith has spent a long time making sure nobody owns it. It can afford, now and then, to be given something.
