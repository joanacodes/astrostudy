Nessus is the centaur of the poisoned robe: in the myth, his blood killed Heracles years after Heracles had killed him. The small body named after him was found in 1993, a year after [Pholus](/astrology/pholus/), and some astrologers read it in the chart. It has no place in my readings, and its story is one reason why.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Nessus is a centaur, one of the small icy bodies that wander among the giant planets on orbits the giants will eventually break up. It is about 60 km across and very red. One circuit takes about 122 years: at its closest it comes 11.9 times the Earth's distance from the Sun, between the orbits of [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) and [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/); at its farthest, 37.4 times, well beyond [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/). For a centaur its path is settled: fifty computed copies of its orbit all stay more than 150 million km from every planet for at least 20,000 years.

David Rabinowitz of the Spacewatch survey found it at Kitt Peak, Arizona, on 26 April 1993, the third centaur after [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) and Pholus. Many more have turned up since, among them [Chariklo](/astrology/chariklo/), found in 1997: the largest centaur known, about 250 km across, and the first small body found to have rings.

In the myth, Nessus was a ferryman on the river Evenus. He carried Deianira, Heracles's wife, across and attacked her on the far bank, and Heracles shot him with an arrow dipped in the Hydra's blood. Dying, Nessus told Deianira that his blood would keep Heracles true to her for ever, knowing it was poisoned. Years later, afraid of losing her husband, she spread it on a robe and sent it to him. It burned him, and he died on a pyre. Ovid tells the story in the *Metamorphoses*, and Sophocles built a tragedy on it, *The Women of Trachis*.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

With no ancient tradition of its own, Nessus is read through that story, and the story is about harm that doesn't stay where it was done. A wrong at a river crossing returns years later dressed as a gift; a poison passes through an innocent pair of hands before it reaches its target. Astrologers who use Nessus look for where that kind of pattern might sit in a chart, through the usual three doors: its sign, its house, and its contacts with the personal planets and the angles.

The sign says the least. A body that takes 122 years to go round, faster through some signs than others, shares its sign with everyone born over a span of years. The house depends on the birth time; in Placidus, the house system I use, a few minutes can carry a slow body across a cusp. So for anyone who reads Nessus, it's the close contacts, within a degree or two of the Sun, the Moon, a personal planet or an angle, that carry the weight.

Some also follow Nessus by transit. My forecasts use the slow planets, Chiron and the true node, never another centaur ([Transits](/astrology/transits/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I leave Nessus out, for the same two reasons as Pholus: the system behind my readings doesn't compute it, and my notebook never tested it. That notebook was a tick or a cross beside every line I [checked like an invoice](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/): the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign, the planets, Chiron and Lilith. About a third of it held overall, and Saturn, Chiron and Lilith held far more often. A centaur I never put on the page has no figure, and I won't invent one ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

With Nessus there's a third reason. Its myth is about an assault and a poisoning, and it's too easy to turn a chart point into an accusation: to tell someone their Nessus shows they were harmed, or will be, or will harm others. No placement can show any of that. If a reading, mine or anyone's, brings up harm someone did to you, the person to talk to is a qualified professional, not an astrologer ([When to see a professional instead](/astrology/when-to-see-a-professional-instead/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Nessus in your chart shows who will hurt you.”** No placement names a person or predicts harm. A myth describes a pattern; it accuses no one.
- **“The robe was a love charm gone wrong.”** In the myth it was never a charm. Nessus knew his blood carried the Hydra's poison and lied as he died; Deianira was deceived, not careless.
- **“A Nessus transit means something terrible is coming.”** No transit makes anything happen, and a body nobody has tested can't carry a warning.
