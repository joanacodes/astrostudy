Alone, an idea stays half-formed; said to someone who answers, it takes shape. That is Mercury in the 7th house: a mind that finds out what it thinks across a table, where the other person's reply becomes part of your own thinking.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) is the house of the other: partners, close one-to-one relationships, agreements, and the people you face, in love or in business. The 7th is angular, its cusp the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), and Libra is its natural sign. It sits opposite the 1st, where tradition says Mercury rejoices, so here the planet of thought works facing someone rather than through your own manner. With [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) in this house, dialogue is a method. You reason best in conversation, you can usually hear the other side, and the people closest to you tend to be the ones you talk with most. Its sign describes the conversation you go looking for: quick and frank in Aries, tender and remembering in Cancer, measured and practical in Capricorn, intuitive and half-spoken in Pisces. Mercury in Libra, in this house, makes the other side of every argument almost as real to you as your own ([Mercury in Libra](/astrology/mercury-in-libra/)).

In Placidus, the system I work with, the Descendant is the cusp of the 7th, on the western horizon, and a 7th-house Mercury was just above it when you were born, about to set. Mercury travels close to the Sun, so this usually goes with a birth in the hours around sunset. A few minutes either way can carry Mercury across the Descendant into the 6th, which is why the time matters so much here ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Before it, [Mercury in the 6th house](/astrology/mercury-in-6th-house/) thinks through work and routine; after it, [Mercury in the 8th house](/astrology/mercury-in-8th-house/) digs for what's hidden. The 7th sits between them, in the conversation itself.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

This is the house where partnership lives, so Mercury here is at the centre of it. You need a partner you can think out loud with, and conversation may be how you judge a relationship's health: when the talk goes quiet, something is wrong. You're often the one who names the problem and suggests the talk. Distance is less of a problem for you than silence: a relationship can survive the miles as long as the conversation keeps going. The pull to watch is letting the other person do your thinking, picking up their views, their phrases, their certainties, until you're not sure which opinions began as yours.

## At work {#at-work}

Consulting, counselling, negotiation, mediation, coaching, interviewing, client work: anything done one to one. You may work best in a pair, a co-writer, a business partner, a colleague to think aloud with. You're often the one who notices what the other side really wants, and agreements benefit from your careful reading of the words. Written agreements suit you better than handshakes, because words on paper let both sides think twice.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Not knowing what you think until someone asks. Taking on a partner's opinions without noticing the moment you did. Treating every disagreement either as a debate to win or as a reason to give way. And a reliance on conversation that can make time alone feel like a silence you have to fill.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Keep a journal, so that you hear your own voice without an echo. Before an important conversation, write down what you think, and check afterwards whether you still do. Choose partners, in love and at work, who argue well and kindly. If you'd like to compare your chart with someone else's, ask them first ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)). The ticks for Mercury in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) came to about one in three, so I'd test this placement the way it works best: in conversation with you.
