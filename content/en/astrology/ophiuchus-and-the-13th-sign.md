Every few years the same headline comes round: there's a thirteenth sign, and yours has changed. The constellation behind it, Ophiuchus, is real, and the Sun does pass in front of it every December. What the headline leaves out is that the zodiac's signs were never the constellations.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Ophiuchus is the Serpent-bearer, a large constellation of the northern summer sky, drawn as a figure holding a snake. It's one of the 88 constellations astronomers recognise, and like all of them it has official borders: lines across the sky drawn by the Belgian astronomer Eugène Delporte for the International Astronomical Union and published in 1930. Within those borders, the Sun's path, the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), runs through the southern part of Ophiuchus, between Scorpius and Sagittarius, so each year the Sun stands in front of it from about 30 November to about 18 December.

In 2016, an explainer on NASA's Space Place, its website for children, set out the astronomy: the Sun passes in front of thirteen constellations, not twelve, and the slow wobble of the Earth's axis has shifted them since the zodiac was first drawn up. Headlines turned it into "NASA adds a thirteenth sign". NASA answered that it hadn't changed any signs: it studies astronomy, and it had only done the arithmetic.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The tropical zodiac, the one I use, isn't measured against the stars at all. It's defined by the seasons. Its first sign, Aries, begins at the March equinox, when the Sun crosses the celestial equator heading north and day and night are about equal everywhere; from there, the Sun's yearly path is cut into twelve equal signs of 30° ([tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/)). Cancer begins at the June solstice, Libra at the September equinox, Capricorn at the December solstice, every year.

Seen that way, the thirteenth sign is a mix-up between two maps. The constellations are star patterns of very different sizes, so the Sun spends very different lengths of time in front of each. The signs are equal slices of the seasons. And the two have drifted apart over the centuries, as the equinox creeps backwards against the stars ([precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/)): that's why the Sun is in front of Ophiuchus while it's in the sign of Sagittarius. [Constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/) goes through the difference in full.

Making Ophiuchus a sign would mean giving up the equal signs, and the seasons that define them, and reading the constellations' borders instead. That's a different system altogether, drawn in 1930 so that astronomers could say which constellation any point of the sky belongs to, not to cast charts.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read the tropical zodiac, and Ophiuchus doesn't appear in my readings, any more than the other constellations do. If you were born between about 30 November and 18 December, two things are true at once: the Sun stood in front of the constellation Ophiuchus, and it was in the sign of [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/). One is a fact about the stars behind the Sun, the other about the season, and modern tropical astrology reads the second ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

Nothing in your chart changes when the headline comes round again. The Sun is where your chart says, to the degree; the rising sign and the houses still depend on your birth time; and what Sagittarius means hasn't moved. If you were born near the Sun's change into Sagittarius or out of it, the date alone isn't enough, but that's a different question, with a different answer ([sign dates, and why they move](/astrology/sign-dates-and-why-they-move/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“NASA added a thirteenth sign.”** NASA explained where the constellations are and changed nothing. It says plainly that it studies astronomy, not astrology.
- **“Your real sign is the constellation behind the Sun.”** That's a map of the stars, not the zodiac astrologers use. In the tropical zodiac, your sign is set by the season of your birth.
- **“Ophiuchus is a secret sign the ancients hid.”** There was nothing to hide: the tropical zodiac has always had twelve equal signs, and Ophiuchus's borders, as astronomers use them, were drawn in 1930.
- **“Born in early December, you're an Ophiuchus, not a Sagittarius.”** In the tropical zodiac you're a Sagittarius; the constellation behind the Sun is a fact of another kind.
