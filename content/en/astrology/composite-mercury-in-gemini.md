A relationship with composite Mercury in Gemini rarely runs out of things to say: a link to forward, a question to chase, a friend to introduce to another friend. Its shared mind sits in one of Mercury's own signs, and it thinks best in exchange.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) takes the midpoint of each planet's two natal positions, and composite [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) describes how the relationship gathers information, sorts it and passes it on. [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/), mutable air, is one of the two signs Mercury rules, its [domicile](/astrology/domicile/) in tradition: the place where a planet works on its own terms. Here the bond's thinking needs no translating. Comparing notes, asking the second question, swapping news over breakfast, inventing nicknames for half the people you know: this pair does it without trying.

That ease belongs to the relationship, not to the two people in it. The talkativeness lives in the relationship, not in either of you: it can go quiet while you're apart and start up again the moment you meet. A natal [Mercury in Gemini](/astrology/mercury-in-gemini/) would be one person's quickness; here the quickness is shared, and it lives in the conversation itself. It's often a connecting one, too: the pair friends ask for a recommendation, the two who know who should meet whom. For all that, the placement is less common than it sounds: Gemini is, with Taurus, one of the two signs composite Mercury reaches least often, about 1 pair in 14 in the site's count.

In a birth chart, Mercury in Gemini comes with the Sun in Taurus, Gemini or Cancer, and most composites keep to that. The exceptions belong to pairs born far apart in the year: each midpoint is taken the short way round on its own, so when two Suns sit almost across the circle from each other, the Suns' midpoint can fall on one side and the Mercurys' on the other. About 1 pair in 26 ends up with the composite Sun near Sagittarius, facing this Mercury; the [composite Sun in Gemini](/astrology/composite-sun-in-gemini/) meets the same quirk from the other end. Either way it's a by-product of the method.

The difficulty is that the interesting conversation can crowd out the necessary one. With so many threads, a decision gets talked around rather than made, and the subject that needs an evening of its own keeps being postponed for a livelier one. Variety isn't indecision, but it does need an ending now and then. A written list of what the two of you have actually decided helps more than it sounds, and so does a simple rule: a subject that comes up three times gets a proper evening.

Before it, [composite Mercury in Taurus](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-taurus/) thinks slowly and keeps what it decides; after it, [composite Mercury in Cancer](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-cancer/) thinks through memory and feeling. Gemini is the one asking what happens next.

I don't draw composites in my readings; this page describes the technique, which astrologers read beside both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). Asking the other person first is the most Gemini move there is anyway: one more question, and the one that matters most ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
