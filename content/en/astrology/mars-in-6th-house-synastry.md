Their Mars in your 6th house sends another person's drive to the place tradition said Mars likes best: work, effort and the jobs of every day. Your routines, methods and lists pick up speed around them, and so, now and then, does your impatience.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is effort put to use, the energy that gets a task from started to finished. [The 6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) holds the work you actually do from one day to the next, your habits and methods, the colleagues beside you and the care you give your body. Through this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their energy goes to work on your ordinary days.

With them, things get done. You may start the training plan, clear the backlog, fix the system that's been slowing you down for months, or simply work harder and better when they're in the room. Their push gives your routines a pulse, and effort can feel satisfying rather than grim.

For them, your working life is a place where their drive feels useful. They like tackling a job beside you, and your method gives their energy a channel, so they may feel at their most effective in your company. This is the house where tradition said Mars rejoices ([planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/)), odd as that sounds for a house it also called Bad Fortune: work was where Mars was thought happiest. Cadent, the 6th maintains and adjusts, and [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/), its natural sign, brings method and precision. Towards the end of the house their Mars reaches your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), and the person across the desk starts to feel like a counterpart, with all the spark and friction of a partnership.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Effort has a pace, and theirs may not be yours. You may feel driven too hard, watched while you work, or criticised for the way you do a job you've done well for years. Their impatience with slowness can turn an ordinary Tuesday into a race.

From their side, your method can feel like a brake. They want to get on with it and you want to get it right, so they may start doing your tasks for you, then resent having taken them on.

Tradition also gave the 6th illness. I don't read that, for a single chart or a pair ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)): their Mars here describes effort, pace and habits, and questions about your body are for a doctor, never for a synastry.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Agree on the pace before the work starts. Who sets the deadline, how fast is fast enough, when a break is part of the job: settled early, these turn their drive into momentum rather than pressure. In a couple, divide the household jobs so that each of you owns some outright; between friends, they may be the training partner who gets you out of the door on a cold morning; between colleagues, give their energy the urgent tasks and your method the careful ones; between adult relatives sharing a move or a renovation, decide who leads which part.

Read from their side, the same contact is your Mars in their 6th house, and the impatience may be yours. Before it, [their Mars in your 5th house](/astrology/mars-in-5th-house-synastry/) brings that drive to play; after it, [their Mars in your 7th house](/astrology/mars-in-7th-house-synastry/) sets it across the table, partner to partner.

The far edge of your 6th is your Descendant, always exactly opposite your Ascendant, so the clock time of your birth draws that edge; in Placidus, the house system I use, it draws every other cusp as well. Twelve hours either way move their Mars by about a quarter of a degree, so their time only places a Mars sitting on one of your cusps, while the rest of the reading needs it regardless ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). A colleague's chart is still a person's chart: they need to know, and to agree ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#the-question)).
