Decennials take their name from the decade. They belong to the time-lord techniques, where the planets take turns in charge of a life, and they are the member of that family I can tell you least about with certainty. Here is what I can stand behind, and what to ask before you trust a table of them.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Every time-lord technique answers the same question: which planet is in charge of this stretch of life? [Annual profections](/astrology/annual-profections/) answer it year by year, counting from the rising sign. [Firdaria](/astrology/firdaria/) answer it with a fixed table of planetary periods, best documented by [Abu Ma'shar](/astrology/abu-mashar/) in the ninth century. [Zodiacal releasing](/astrology/zodiacal-releasing/) answers it with chapters counted from a lot. Decennials are another answer of the same kind, and so are [circumambulations](/astrology/circumambulations/).

*Decennial* is the Latin-derived word for a span of ten years, the same word that names a tenth anniversary. French calls the technique *décennies*, decades.

The idea behind the family is old. In the first century, the Roman poet Manilius already explained, in the third book of his *Astronomica*, how to work out the chronocrators, the celestial rulers of the successive sections of a life. In the second century, Ptolemy ended the *Tetrabiblos* with a short discussion of the timing methods of his day ([Ptolemy’s Tetrabiblos](/astrology/ptolemys-tetrabiblos/)), while his contemporary Vettius Valens set down the practical techniques of the time in his *Anthology* ([Hellenistic astrology](/astrology/hellenistic-astrology/)).

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Here I have to stop short. Which planet opens the sequence, how long each period lasts, how a period divides: I couldn't check any of it against a source I trust, so I won't print a table. A table I can't check would be a guess with numbers on it, and you'd have no way to tell.

What I can describe is how a period of this kind is read, because every time-lord is read the same way. The planet in charge is taken as it stands in your birth chart: its sign, its house, its aspects, how comfortable it is there. The tradition read its time-lords this way, and one author, writing about the firdaria, warned that judging a year from the table alone gives an excessively general result.

If you meet decennials in a program or a reading, four questions sort a careful version from a careless one:

- **Whose rules?** A named author, and a named translation.
- **Who goes first?** How the opening planet is chosen, and whether day and night births differ.
- **How long, and how divided?** The length of each period and of its parts.
- **What else is read with it?** A period read without the birth chart and the rest of the timing says very little.

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

Like any time-lord period: which planet's function is in front for a while. A Saturn period invites a look at structure and responsibility as your natal Saturn shapes them; a Venus period, at what you value and are drawn to. Nothing more precise than that, and nothing about events.

Different time-lord methods often name different planets for the same birthday. Take a day birth at 35. The firdaria put it in the Moon's period, in a Mars sub-period. Profections put the year on the 12th house, the twelfth sign from the rising sign, whose ruler becomes the lord of the year. Zodiacal releasing names the ruler of whichever chapter is running. Three methods, possibly three planets: adding a fourth doesn't make any of them more certain.

That's why my own forecasts keep to two time-lords, the lord of the profection year and the ruler of the releasing chapter, and give them weight only when [transits](/astrology/transits/) or progressions reach the same planet.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

Beyond the gap in its rules, decennials share the limits of every time-lord technique. The periods are symbolic: nothing moves in the sky when one begins. A period names a planet, never an outcome. And no period, in any scheme, can say whether you'll fall ill, earn money or lose someone; those are questions I never read a chart for ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).
