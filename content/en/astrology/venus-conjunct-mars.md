Venus conjunct Mars sets the planet of what you like beside the planet of what you go after, usually in the same sign. Liking and pursuing then tend to come as one movement: by the time something pleases you, you're already on your way to it.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) evaluates: what attracts you, what you prefer, what feels fair between people. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) initiates: effort, assertion, the push that gets something started. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) binds the two into one active process while each keeps its nature. Venus still weighs and Mars still acts, but they do it at the same moment.

In plain words, you rarely want something halfway. Affection becomes effort, and effort becomes affection: the people and projects you put energy into become the ones you love, and the ones you love get your energy. That can make you warm and direct at once, someone whose interest is easy to read. The task, as with any conjunction, is noticing which partner is leading when it matters: whether you want something because you value it, or value it because you're already chasing it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The sign matters more than usual, because it styles both planets at once, and it can favour one of them. Venus rules Taurus and Libra; Mars rules Aries and, traditionally, Scorpio. So in Aries or Scorpio, the pursuit tends to set the tone, with Venus opposite one of its own signs; in Taurus or Libra, liking leads, and Mars works on unfamiliar ground. Elsewhere they share the sign more evenly: in Leo, generous and wholehearted; in Virgo, attentive and practical. The house shows where it lives: in the 10th, you may come to love the work you push hardest at; in the 5th, play and romance get the same energy.

About 1 person in 15 is born with Venus and Mars within 6° of each other. Within a degree or two, liking and going after are hard to tell apart, even for you; at 5° or 6°, they feel more like close neighbours, and the difference is easier to hear. Of the two, Venus usually moves faster and does the closing in or pulling away. Near the end of a sign, the pair can fall either side of the boundary ([out-of-sign aspects](/astrology/out-of-sign-aspects/)): then the fusion speaks with two accents.

The two can meet at every angle, and each one gives liking and doing a different relationship:

- **[Venus sextile Mars](/astrology/venus-sextile-mars/):** the two help each other through small openings: an invitation, a suggestion, a first step.
- **[Venus square Mars](/astrology/venus-square-mars/):** liking and pursuing out of step, learning each other's timing.
- **[Venus trine Mars](/astrology/venus-trine-mars/):** liking and doing keep pace, usually in one element; an ease worth using deliberately.
- **[Venus opposite Mars](/astrology/venus-opposite-mars/):** the two face each other across the chart, and are often met in other people before you hold both.

Between two charts, [Venus conjunct Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-conjunct-mars-synastry/) puts one person's taste on the very spot where the other's drive sits, the contact astrologers look at first for attraction. Being desired and desiring don't feel the same, so I read both charts, and only with both people's agreement ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

## In transits {#in-transits}

In [transits](/astrology/transits/), the pair in your chart gets visited as one. Venus in the sky crosses it about once a year, Mars about every two years, and because your Venus and Mars sit together, whatever touches one touches the other within days: a few days when affection and effort get the same push. In a year when Mars turns retrograde close to the pair, it can cross it three times in a few months, and the push comes in waves.

Weather, not a forecast: a short season of warmth and drive in the same place, with no promise of who or what it's for.
