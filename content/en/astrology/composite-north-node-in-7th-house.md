Some couples are a closed company of two: they decide fast, move fast, and rarely consult anyone, sometimes not even each other. Composite North Node in the 7th house asks a relationship like that to open the door to partnership, with each other and with the people around it.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

I don't work with composites myself; I describe the technique because so many astrologers use it. [The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) builds one chart from the midpoints of two, read as the relationship's own. Its [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) points the way the bond grows, and its [South Node](/astrology/south-node/), exactly opposite, shows the reflex it already has. With the South Node in [the 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/), the reflex is self-sufficiency: the two of you as a unit that acts on its own impulse and answers to nobody. The North Node sits in [the 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/), angular, naturally Libra's, the house of the other party.

In a birth chart the 7th is the person across the table. In a composite, whose subject is already a pair, the 7th covers the others the relationship meets as a unit: other couples, clients, collaborators, sometimes open rivals. It also covers the partnership inside the pair, two people treating each other as equals rather than as halves of one will. The direction is cooperation on both fronts: consulting before acting, negotiating terms, letting other people's views improve the plan.

For one person, [North Node in the 7th house](/astrology/north-node-in-7th-house/) is growth through the person facing you; [their North Node in your 7th house](/astrology/north-node-in-7th-house-synastry/) can make the relationship feel like part of someone's path. The composite node puts partnership on the relationship's own path, so neither person has to carry it alone.

The house begins at the composite [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), always exactly opposite the composite Ascendant, so it inherits every uncertainty of that angle: one method halves the distance between your natal Descendants, the other derives the angle from the composite Midheaven for the latitude where you live, and both need two birth times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). A node within a few degrees of the Descendant can fall in [composite North Node in the 6th house](/astrology/composite-north-node-in-6th-house/) by another reckoning, one deeper in may reach [composite North Node in the 8th house](/astrology/composite-north-node-in-8th-house/), and the mean and true node can decide it too. I look at both.

The 7th-house trap is losing the outline you had: a pair so busy accommodating everyone else that it forgets what it wanted, or contracts so careful that nothing spontaneous survives. The 1st-house end stays a strength. A relationship that knows its own mind negotiates better than one that has given it away. One habit helps on both fronts: before saying yes to anyone else, check with each other, and before saying no, ask what the offer might teach you.

None of this predicts a wedding, a contract signed or a separation, and its practitioners read it only beside both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/); it shows a direction, never a sentence. Partnership means both partners sign, and the chart itself is no exception: it is drawn only with both people's agreement ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
