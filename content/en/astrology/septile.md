The septile divides the circle into seven: two planets about 51°26′ apart. Seven doesn't go into 360 evenly, so the angle never lands on a whole degree, and the aspect has a matching quality: it describes patterns that don't line up neatly, and show themselves only when they come round again.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

Three hundred and sixty divided by seven is 51.43°, written 51°26′: one sign and about 21½ degrees. A septile joins neighbouring signs only when the first planet sits in roughly the first 8½ degrees of its sign; otherwise it reaches two signs on.

I read a septile as a pattern that emerges by repetition. Two planets organise each other in a way no single moment reveals, but over cycles of experience the same shape keeps forming, and you start to recognise it. It isn't fate, destiny or magic, and it promises nothing good or bad: what emerges is a pattern, and a pattern can be noticed, understood and worked with.

Astrologers who read septiles keep them within about a degree (see [orbs](/astrology/orbs/)), and so do I: a seventh is a fine division, and I'd rather miss one than invent one. The sevens also have a chart of their own among [harmonic charts](/astrology/harmonic-charts/): the seventh harmonic, which gathers all of a chart's sevens into one picture.

The septile falls between the semi-square and the [sextile](/astrology/sextile/): 6°26′ past one, 8°34′ short of the other. Its two siblings lie further out: the biseptile between the [square](/astrology/square/) and the [trine](/astrology/trine/), the triseptile between the quincunx and the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/). None of the three lands on a major aspect, and none of Ptolemy's four divides the circle by seven.

## Between which planets {#between-which-planets}

Septiles don't come pair by pair on this site. Where I find one, I read it by speed, with one fact in mind: the Sun can't make a septile with Mercury or Venus, since neither ever strays that far from it.

Between two personal planets, the pattern shows in ordinary life: the same kind of conversation, the same kind of choice, coming round until you notice its shape. With a slow planet involved, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune or Pluto, the cycle is longer, a theme that returns over years, and that's where I look most carefully. Between two slow planets it's woven into a generation, and the houses show where the pattern meets your own life.

Among the minor aspects, the [biquintile](/astrology/biquintile/), 144°, two-fifths of the circle, which Johannes Kepler counted among his additions to Ptolemy's aspects in *Harmonices Mundi* (Linz, 1619), is intricate craft you build; the septile is pattern you recognise. The [biseptile](/astrology/biseptile/), at 102°51′, doubles the septile, and there the pattern starts to branch.

## The septile in synastry {#in-synastry}

Between your chart and someone else's, a septile tends to show as something that recurs: the same kind of meeting, parting or conversation, coming round at intervals, a little different each time. It's tempting to read that as a sign two people were meant to meet. I don't: a pattern that repeats is something you can see together and choose what to do with. It's quiet next to a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) or an opposition between you, and I bring it up in [synastry readings](/synastry/) only with personal planets and a tight orb, read from both sides.

## The septile in transits {#in-transits}

A septile transit is easy to miss: kept to a degree, it's gone within hours from the Moon, and even Saturn holds it for weeks, not seasons, unless a retrograde brings it back. I notice septile transits most when they rhyme with something, a situation that echoes an earlier one, as if a cycle had come round. Weather, not a forecast: a septile transit may show you a pattern, never a fate.
