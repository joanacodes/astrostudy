Mars opposite Uranus sets your drive across the chart from your need for freedom. One end wants to commit effort and see something through; the other wants room to change course. For a while, the second end often seems to belong to other people.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the part of you that acts on what you want: initiative, assertion, effort and the nerve to compete. [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) opens the way to the new: it sets you apart, tries things differently and reforms whatever fits too tightly. In an [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) the two face each other, a polarity in which each sharpens your awareness of the other.

In plain words, you may swing between pouring yourself into something and suddenly needing out, or between steady effort and a restlessness that wants a new start. Often the swing arrives from outside first: plans that change at the last minute, partners or colleagues who need a lot of room, people whose independence both attracts and unsettles you. None of that is a sign of failure. The opposition is teaching you to hold commitment and freedom at once.

Autonomy is not isolation, and the aspect's mature form shows exactly that: you can keep your independence without walking away, and keep your commitments without feeling caged.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The two ends of an opposition fall in signs of the same modality, with complementary elements ([polarity](/astrology/polarity/)). With Mars in Aries opposite Uranus in Libra, Mars is in its own sign and the need for room tends to show in partnerships; with Uranus in Aquarius and Mars in Leo, Uranus is in its modern sign and the pull runs between self-expression and the group. The houses name the two arenas: Mars in the 10th opposite Uranus in the 4th, for instance, could set a career push against a home life that keeps changing.

Within 6°, about 1 person in 33 has this opposition, about half as many as have the pair's square or trine, since an opposition comes once in each cycle and those come twice. Tight, the swing between effort and freedom runs through your relationships and your work; near 6°, it shows mostly when someone tries to pin you down.

Mars, the quick one, forms the opposition once in each two-year cycle and holds it for a few weeks of births, while Uranus keeps a sign for some seven years. How I weigh an aspect like this, against the rest of your chart and against your own account of your life, is set out in [How I read](/how-i-read/).

Set against the pair's other aspects:

- **[Mars conjunct Uranus](/astrology/mars-conjunct-uranus/):** effort and freedom in one place, so the push for change comes from you rather than from across the room.
- **[Mars sextile Uranus](/astrology/mars-sextile-uranus/):** the two cooperate at a short distance, making change an option rather than a pull.
- **[Mars square Uranus](/astrology/mars-square-uranus/):** friction at a corner instead of a face-off, felt more inside than through others.
- **[Mars trine Uranus](/astrology/mars-trine-uranus/):** independence and effort in one element, at ease where the opposition has to balance.

Between two charts, [Mars opposite Uranus in synastry](/astrology/mars-opposite-uranus-synastry/) can be electric: one person's drive meets the other's need for room head-on. I read it for each person, since the one who pushes and the one who needs space feel it differently.

## In transits {#in-transits}

Uranus opposes your natal Mars at most once in a life and stays near the point for a year or so, often in three passes: [Transiting Uranus opposite natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-uranus-opposite-natal-mars/), a season when other people's changes, or your own need for room, ask your effort to adapt. For those born with the opposition, 42 or so is the turning point: at the [Uranus opposition](/astrology/uranus-opposition/), Uranus in the sky arrives on your Mars itself.

Mars in the sky opposes your natal Uranus about every two years, for a few days: a short spell of restlessness, or of energy for breaking a habit.

Neither transit is a forecast. Both are weather, describing a season in which commitment and freedom get renegotiated.
