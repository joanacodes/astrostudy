Alphecca is the jewel of the Northern Crown, the brightest star in the half-ring of stars that the Greeks read as Ariadne's wedding diadem. The tropical zodiac gives it 12°39′ Scorpio in 2026.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Alphecca, α Coronae Borealis, shines at magnitude 2.23 from about 75 light-years away. Like [Algol](/astrology/algol/) it is an eclipsing pair, a white star and a smaller yellow one that pass in front of each other every 17.36 days, but its dimming is slight, from 2.21 to 2.32, hardly noticeable to the eye. The white star has 2.6 times the Sun's mass; its yellow partner, slightly lighter than the Sun, is young and active, thirty times brighter in X-rays than the Sun at its peak. Around the white star lies a wide disc of dust, which has made astronomers wonder whether planets are forming there, and the whole system shares its motion through space with most of the Plough's stars, as a member of the Ursa Major moving group.

Its 2026 degree, 12°39′ [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/), is only twenty-two minutes on from the 12°17′ of 2000 ([precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/)). Of the fifteen Behenian stars, only [Vega](/astrology/vega/) and Alkaid lie further from the Sun's path than Alphecca, 44.3° north of it.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

Its names circle round the crown and its jewel. Alphecca is Arabic, short for nayyir al-fakka, the bright one of the broken ring, for the open circle of stars it sits in. Its Latin name, Gemma, simply means jewel. A third name, Gnosia Stella Coronae, the Cretan star of the Crown, comes from Vergil's *Georgics* and points to Knossos, birthplace of Ariadne: in some versions of the myth, the diadem she wore at her wedding to Bacchus was set in the sky as this crown.

The brightest star of the Northern Crown also lent its name to the brightest of the Southern one, Alphekka Meridiana, and in Chinese astronomy it is the Fourth Star of the Coiled Thong. Medieval magic gave the jewel a jewel of its own, topaz, in the Behenian list, with rosemary for its plant and Venus and Mars for its planets.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun comes to Alphecca's degree in the first week of November, on about the 4th or 5th, a [Sun in Scorpio](/astrology/sun-in-scorpio/) in its second decan; in a recent chart, so does any planet from about 11°39′ to 13°39′ Scorpio. That shared longitude is the whole of the contact in the tradition ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). [Arcturus](/astrology/arcturus/), the bright star that precedes it along the zodiac by eighteen degrees, is another northern star met only by degree.

An eclipsing star that barely dims, a crown made from a broken ring of stars, a wedding gift: Alphecca's tradition is one of the gentlest among the Behenian stars.
