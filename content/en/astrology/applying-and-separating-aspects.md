Two planets in aspect are never standing still: one of them is catching up with the exact angle, or leaving it behind. That direction, applying or separating, is one of the finer details astrologers read, and one of the easiest to check.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Every aspect has an exact point, 90° for a square, 120° for a trine, and an orb around it, the leeway astrologers allow ([orbs](/astrology/orbs/)). Within that orb, the faster of the two planets is either moving towards the exact angle or away from it:

- **Applying**: still closing in. With the Moon at 10° Aries and Saturn at 15° Capricorn, the Moon is 5° short of an exact square and moving forward at about 13° a day, so the square is applying and will be exact in about nine hours.
- **Separating**: past exact and moving away. With the Moon at 20° Aries, the same square is 5° past, and separating.

The planet doing the moving is the faster one. On average the order runs from the Moon, through Mercury, Venus and the Sun at about a degree a day, to Mars, Jupiter, Saturn and the outer planets. At the moment in between, the aspect is [exact](/astrology/exact-aspects/), the point where astrologers read it at full strength.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Retrograde motion complicates the picture. A planet that turns retrograde can back away from an aspect it was applying to, or return to one it had left, so an aspect can apply, separate and apply again ([retrograde, explained](/astrology/retrograde-explained/)). In transits, that's why a slow planet often makes the same aspect to a point in your chart three times: once moving forward, once retrograde, once forward again. When one planet is direct and the other retrograde and they're moving towards each other, the aspect applies from both sides at once.

Direction works the same way for every aspect, hard or soft ([hard and soft aspects](/astrology/hard-and-soft-aspects/)): an applying square is friction gathering, an applying trine is ease gathering.

Tradition watched it most closely with the Moon. Dorotheus, choosing times to begin things, looked at which planets the Moon was separating from and which it was applying to. The Moon that has no applying aspects left before it changes sign has a name of its own: [void of course](/astrology/void-of-course-moon/).

Direction also carries forward in time. In [secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/), where each day after birth stands for a year of life, an aspect that was applying at birth goes on closing: with the progressed Sun, which moves about a degree a year, an aspect 3° short of exact perfects around the age of three.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read applying and separating as tone and timing, not good and bad. An applying aspect at birth tends to feel like something still in motion, a question you keep working on; a separating one like something already absorbed, familiar enough to go unnoticed. The orb still matters more: a separating aspect a degree past exact is closer, and usually louder, than an applying one at the edge of its orb.

With the Moon, the birth time decides it. The Moon moves about 13° a day, so a few hours can turn an applying aspect into a separating one, or take it out of orb altogether. Aspects with the Moon are among the things the time decides, and part of why there's no reading without it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

In transits, applying is the weather coming in: a transit is often felt gathering before it's exact, and fading as it separates. Weather, not a forecast.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Separating aspects don't count.”** A separating aspect is still an aspect while it's in orb, and close to exact it can be as loud as any.
- **“Applying means good, separating means bad.”** Direction says whether an aspect is building or easing. It doesn't make it kinder or harder.
- **“A birth chart doesn't move, so direction can't matter.”** The chart is a still frame, but the planets in it were moving, and the frame records which way.
- **“An aspect is either applying or separating for ever.”** Only in a birth chart, as a record of one moment. In the sky, every aspect applies, perfects and separates in turn.
