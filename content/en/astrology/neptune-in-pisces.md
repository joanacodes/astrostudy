Neptune in Pisces is Neptune at home, as modern tropical astrology sees it, and the placement of a generation that is still growing up. Everyone born with it arrived between April 2011 and January 2026, so this page reads the sign as the sky of those years, never as anyone's child.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/), mutable water, [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) has nothing to translate. Sign and planet speak the same language: imagination, compassion, the feeling that everything is connected to everything else. Modern tropical astrology calls this Neptune's [domicile](/astrology/domicile/), its home. The edges between people soften, art and belief run into each other, and feeling for others comes before argument. Because the sign is mutable, the dream doesn't harden into a doctrine. It keeps moving, the way water finds its level.

Pisces has two rulers, and I read both. In the modern scheme Neptune rules it, so this Neptune answers to no other planet. The tradition, which never knew Neptune, gave Pisces to [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/), and Jupiter keeps a say: where it sits in a chart shows how such open-ended compassion might grow into something lived, with a shape and a direction.

Neptune was in Pisces from April 2011 to January 2026. Until February 2012 it was still crossing back and forth with Aquarius, and from March 2025 with Aries, so births in those months may have [Neptune in Aquarius](/astrology/neptune-in-aquarius/) or [Neptune in Aries](/astrology/neptune-in-aries/) instead. The stay before ran from April 1847 to February 1862: Pisces was the first sign Neptune entered after its discovery in 1846. Nobody born from 1930 to 2009 has it, and no adult alive today does.

Everyone born with it is still a child, and I don't read [children’s charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/). So I read Neptune in Pisces the way I'd read a sky: as the dream of a time, shared by everyone born in it. The house, once each of them is old enough to ask, will be what makes it theirs.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

As a generation's placement, Neptune in Pisces describes an ideal of closeness without walls: love that forgives easily, sees the best in people, and takes in another person's mood before a word is said. I won't say how any one child will love; that belongs to their own life, and to a chart they can choose to look at later. What the sign describes is the dream in the air of those years: tenderness prized above toughness, and a longing to be understood without having to explain.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, the same dream points to callings of imagination and care: music, film, painting, poetry, caring work, any role that eases suffering or makes something beautiful out of very little. In its own sign, Neptune values work for what it gives more than for what it earns. When the people born with it are grown, their own charts, and Neptune's house above all, will say where that dream meets a working life.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The shadow of this placement is the shadow of its gift. Compassion with no edges can end up giving too much of oneself away, and an imagination this open can make it hard to tell one's own feelings from the feelings in the room. Idealisation here isn't delusion; it's a love of what could be, which still needs a look at what is. And the adults around this generation have a hard part of their own: the temptation to read the sign into a child, to call a dreamy six-year-old “very Neptune in Pisces”. A child is not a placement.

## What helps {#what-helps}

If you're an adult reading this, you met Neptune in Pisces as a transit. For those years it was crossing part of your own chart, and the house it moved through is where edges may have softened for a while. That one is yours to look at.

In my [notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Neptune was checked like every placement, and it wasn't one of the three that held far more often, Saturn, Chiron and Lilith; overall about a third held. What helps most with this sign is patience: a chart can wait until the person it belongs to wants it.
