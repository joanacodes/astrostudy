Put the same birth details into two astrology apps and the North Node can come out a degree apart. Neither app has made a mistake: one is showing you the mean node, the other the true node, and they are two answers to the same question.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The lunar nodes are the two places where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's: the North Node where the Moon crosses going north, the South Node exactly opposite. Read together they make the [nodal axis](/astrology/nodal-axis/). Nothing is there to see, so the nodes have to be calculated, and there are two ways of doing it.

- **The mean node** is an average. It places the crossing point where it would be if it slid backwards through the zodiac at a perfectly even pace. It always moves backwards, steadily, and goes all the way round in about 18.6 years.
- **The true node** is the crossing point as it actually was at the moment of birth, worked out from the Moon's orbit on that day. It goes round in the same 18.6 years, but not smoothly: it runs a little ahead of the mean node, then falls a little behind.

The gap between them is a degree or two at most. That's small enough that most charts give the same sign and the same house either way.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The Moon's orbit is tilted to the Sun's path, so the Moon crosses that path twice a month. The Sun's pull slowly swings the whole orbit round, and the crossing points slide backwards. That slide isn't even, though: the pull changes as the Sun and Moon change places, so the real crossing point wobbles around its average. The mean node smooths the wobble out; the true node keeps it. Jean Meeus's *Astronomical Algorithms* sets out how to calculate both.

What the difference can change:

- **The sign.** The nodes spend about 18 months in each sign. Only when your node sits within a degree or two of a sign's edge can the mean and true node land on either side of it.
- **The house.** The same at a house cusp. Where a cusp falls depends on the birth time, so the time decides whether the question arises at all; [without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/), there are no houses to place the nodes in, which is why I don't read a chart without one.
- **The aspects.** A degree can bring a close aspect into orb or take it out.
- **The retrograde symbol.** The mean node always shows as retrograde. The true node sometimes stands still, or moves forwards for a while, and a program may mark it direct. Nothing in the node's meaning turns on that ([retrograde, explained](/astrology/retrograde-explained/)).
- **Timing.** Over the years, the mean node crosses a degree once. The true node, wobbling, can pass the same degree more than once before it moves on. That's worth knowing for the [nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/), and for any transit of the nodes.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I look at both. In most charts they agree on sign and house, and the [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) and [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) read the same whichever you take. When they don't, I tell you: here is the sign the mean node gives, here is the one the true node gives, and here is what the reading would say on each side. I don't pick one because it sounds nicer.

In a forecast, the node whose transits I follow is the true node, alongside Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto and Chiron. Because it wobbles, its crossing of a natal point can stretch into a longer season than a single date suggests, and I read it as weather, never as an appointment with an event.

If your chart came from an app, check which node it draws before you compare it with anything you've read. The same caution applies to Lilith, where the gap is far larger ([which Lilith is mine?](/astrology/which-lilith-is-mine/)). [How I read](/how-i-read/) sets out the rest of what I use.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The true node is the real one, so the mean node is wrong.”** Both are calculations of a point nobody can see. One smooths the motion and one doesn't; the choice is a matter of method, not of truth.
- **“They put the node in different signs for months at a time.”** They're a degree or two apart at most. The sign only differs near its edge, and then usually for a short stretch.
- **“A direct North Node means something special.”** The true node's wobble sometimes carries it forwards; the node means what it always means.
- **“Your birth date settles everything about the nodes.”** It nearly always settles their sign. Their house, and the angles they touch, need the birth time.
