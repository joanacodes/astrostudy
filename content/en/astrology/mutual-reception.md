Venus in Capricorn reads one way on its own. Find Saturn in Taurus in the same chart and the picture changes: each planet stands in a sign the other rules, a guest in the other's house and its host at the same time. The tradition's name for that is mutual reception.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Every planet stands in a sign, and every sign has a ruler, the planet at home there ([domicile](/astrology/domicile/)). The old texts describe the ruler as receiving the planet in its sign, the way a host receives a guest, and call it that planet's dispositor; following the link from planet to ruler, and on again, is the subject of [dispositors and the final dispositor](/astrology/dispositors-and-the-final-dispositor/).

Most receptions run one way. Mutual reception is the case where they run both ways: Mercury in Aquarius, a sign Saturn rules, while Saturn stands in Gemini, a sign Mercury rules. Ancient astrologers called it an exchange of signs.

Signs aren't the only currency. Ancient and medieval astrologers gave the same weight to an exchange of exaltations, and also counted receptions by triplicity and by term ([triplicity rulers](/astrology/triplicity-rulers/), [terms (bounds)](/astrology/terms-bounds/)), by face, though that one counted for very little, and mixed ones, where one planet stands in the other's sign and the other in the first one's exaltation. When astrologers say mutual reception and nothing more, they mean the exchange of signs.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Tradition read the exchange as help: each planet, at home where the other stands, can lend it support. How much depends on three things.

- **The aspect between the two signs.** Venus in Capricorn and Saturn in Taurus stand in two earth signs a trine apart, so the planets can see each other and the help has a channel. Signs side by side, or 150° apart, make no aspect at all ([aversion](/astrology/aversion/)), and the exchange is reckoned much weaker.
- **Opposite signs.** A planet in the sign opposite its own is in [detriment](/astrology/detriment/). When the two signs of an exchange face each other, both planets stand in detriment, and each has little to lend.
- **Each planet's own state.** One in its fall, retrograde or lost in the Sun's light has less to give, and a reception doesn't make up for that.

The table of rulerships decides more than people expect. The Sun and the Moon can only exchange from Cancer and Leo, two neighbouring signs, so the lights' reception is always in aversion. Every exchange between Venus and Mars falls in neighbouring signs or in opposite ones, because Venus's two signs sit beside Mars's or across from them. And Mercury and Venus, never much more than 75° apart in the sky, can only trade places from neighbouring signs: Mercury in Taurus with Venus in Gemini, or Mercury in Libra with Venus in Virgo.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Reception is one of the foundations of [horary astrology](/astrology/horary-astrology/), the old art of answering a question from the chart of the moment it is asked. There, whether the planets standing for the people or things in a question receive each other shows whether they can work together, a test that sits beside others such as the [void-of-course Moon](/astrology/void-of-course-moon/). With the Moon in Taurus and Venus in Cancer, the two receive each other favourably.

In a birth chart, I read it through the grammar my readings are built on: a planet is a function, its sign the manner in which it works. In a mutual reception, each function works in the other's manner. Venus in Capricorn weighs what is worth having the way Saturn would, by what will last; Saturn in Taurus builds the way Venus would, slowly, keeping what it values. The two placements describe one way of working, so I read them as a pair rather than as two separate judgements.

Because I read both rulers of Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)), I notice exchanges in both schemes. Some modern ones belong to whole cohorts: everyone born between February 1991 and January 1994 has Uranus in Capricorn with Saturn in Aquarius. Shared that widely, it describes a birth year more than a person, and the houses the two planets occupy, which need the birth time, are what make it personal.

A reception is one strand among many. It doesn't override a planet's sign, house or aspects, and it never turns a demanding placement into an easy one by decree; that's [how I read](/how-i-read/) every factor in a chart.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Mutual reception makes both planets strong.”** It opens a channel for help, and the signs decide how much gets through: across opposite signs both planets stand in detriment, and between neighbouring signs they don't see each other.
- **“Venus and Mars in mutual reception make the perfect match.”** In the traditional scheme their exchange always falls between neighbouring or opposite signs. It describes attraction and action borrowing each other's manner inside one person, never a promise about a relationship.
- **“Only rulers can receive each other.”** Tradition counted exchanges by exaltation, triplicity, term and face as well, and mixed ones.
- **“Mutual reception is a kind of conjunction.”** The two planets can stand anywhere in the chart. The exchange depends on the signs they occupy, not on how close they are.
