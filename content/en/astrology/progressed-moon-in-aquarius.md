From the climb of Capricorn, the progressed Moon moves into Aquarius, and the chapter opens onto the wider world. For two years or so, what steadies you is belonging on your own terms: friends who let you be yourself, a group with a purpose, room to think differently.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

In a progressed chart, where [secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/) turn days into years, [the progressed Moon](/astrology/progressed-moon/) tells you what each chapter feels like. Fixed air, [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/) answers to two rulers, Saturn in the old scheme and Uranus in the modern one, and I read both. In this sign the Moon’s needs turn towards freedom and fellowship at once: to be part of something larger than yourself, and to stay yourself inside it. Feelings are often understood best with a little perspective, by thinking them through or talking them over with a friend rather than in the heat of the moment, and comfort comes from people who share your ideas as much as your history.

Perspective isn’t coldness. The chapter’s need is for room, the kind that lets a relationship breathe, and for honesty about what you want for the future. It can show as a new circle of friends, a cause you join, a change to the way you live that makes more sense to you than to anyone else, or a sudden clarity about what no longer fits. Friends may also become where you go for the comfort family once gave, and a chosen circle can feel as safe as a home. Aquarius is fixed, too: once an idea about how to live takes hold in these years, it tends to stay.

Which part of life this need for room and fellowship gathers in depends on the house the progressed Moon crosses, and the houses come from [your birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/); in Placidus, the system I use, an exact time places them, and without it I don’t read a chart. Reading both rulers adds two more houses to look at, those of your natal Saturn and your natal Uranus.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Counted over the births from 1930 to 2009, the Aquarius chapter ran from two to two and a half years, usually about two years and four months, one year for each day the real Moon needed to cross the sign after the birth. Two years is long enough for a new way of living to stop feeling new, which suits a fixed sign that likes its changes to last.

It follows the [progressed Moon in Capricorn](/astrology/progressed-moon-in-capricorn/) and leads into the [progressed Moon in Pisces](/astrology/progressed-moon-in-pisces/), the last chapter of the round. Aquarius is the eleventh sign, so by the time the progressed Moon reaches it, most of its round of about 27 years lies behind: a good vantage point for looking back over what the round has brought, and ahead to what you would like the next one to hold.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Find your people. An Aquarius chapter tends to steady itself through friendship and shared purpose: a group, a project, a cause, or simply a few friends with whom you can be entirely yourself. Give the relationships you have the room the chapter asks for, and ask for the same in return. Join, gather, volunteer if it suits you: these years often feel most like themselves when what you do is useful to more than one person.

Let an unusual idea have a trial run before you judge it; Aquarius learns by experiment. If you notice yourself explaining your feelings rather than feeling them, that is fine for a while, but let someone close hear the feeling too. And keep some quiet in the plan: Pisces, next, will ask you to soften what Aquarius has built.
