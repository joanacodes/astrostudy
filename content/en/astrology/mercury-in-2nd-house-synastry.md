With their Mercury in your 2nd house, someone else's reasoning goes to work on what you own, earn and value. Conversations with them drift towards what things are worth, and now and then towards what you think you're worth.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[The 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) holds what you can count on: savings, possessions, the talents that could earn you a living, and under all of it the sense of your own worth. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) weighs, compares and names. As a [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), this one points their thinking at your resources.

With them, practical questions get clearer. They help you price a piece of work, compare two offers, read the small print, or say out loud what you'd never sell. Money stops being an awkward subject because they treat it as information. You may also hear your skills named for the first time: "you know you could charge for that?"

For them, your steadiness gives their ideas somewhere to land. They learn from the way you decide what's worth keeping, and your values become a reference point when they're making up their own mind. Succedent houses gather and consolidate, and [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), the 2nd's natural sign, takes its time: a quick mind visiting slow ground may have to learn patience.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Opinions about your spending can feel like opinions about you. A passing remark on a purchase, a raised eyebrow at a price, a habit of reviewing your choices, and the 2nd house's deeper question, what you're worth, gets tangled up in a conversation about money.

From their side, your values may seem immovable. They bring a clever argument and you simply don't change your mind, because what you value isn't up for debate. They can feel that their ideas are being priced, accepted only once they've proved useful.

This overlay doesn't say whether you'll earn or lose with them, or whether a plan you make together will pay. No chart can, and I don't read one for that ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)); what it shows is how the two of you talk about what you have.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Separate the figures from the feelings. Ask for their thinking on the practical question, and keep any verdict on your worth out of it. In a couple, a regular money talk with a short agenda beats a running commentary; between friends, say plainly how you split a bill or a loan; between colleagues, they may be the one who helps you prepare a pay negotiation, though the decision stays yours; between adult siblings, writing things down saves arguments about who said what.

When it's your Mercury in their 2nd house, the questions about cost and keeping come from you, and the patience is yours to find. A house earlier, [their Mercury in your 1st house](/astrology/mercury-in-1st-house-synastry/) has your manner to talk about; a house later, [their Mercury in your 3rd house](/astrology/mercury-in-3rd-house-synastry/) has the whole daily traffic of your messages and errands.

The 2nd begins at its own cusp rather than at an angle, and in Placidus, the house system I use, that cusp depends on your birth time and birthplace: some charts have a narrow 2nd, others a wide one. Mercury rarely covers more than two degrees in a day, so their birthday is enough to put it in your 2nd unless it sits right at the edge, where their time decides. I still wait for both times before reading ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and two people's money habits are read only with both people's agreement ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#a-chart-is-personal)).
