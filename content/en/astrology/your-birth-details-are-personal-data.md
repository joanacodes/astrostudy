Three small facts, a date, a time and a place, are all an astrologer needs to draw your chart. That's exactly why they're worth looking after.

## The short answer {#the-short-answer}

Yes, they're personal data. Under the GDPR, which applies across the European Union, and the United Kingdom's own version of it, personal data is information that relates to an identified or identifiable person. Your date, time and place of birth, with your name or email address beside them, relate to you.

They're also more than an identifier. From those three facts anyone can draw your whole chart, down to the houses of your Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, the three placements that held most often in my notebook ([The three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)). Give them where you trust the use, and ask what happens to them.

## Why it matters {#why-it-matters}

- **They travel.** A free chart, a compatibility quiz or a horoscope sign-up may pass your details on to the services it runs on, for calculation, hosting, analytics and sometimes advertising. A careful privacy notice names every one of them, and says how long your details are kept.
- **They can't be changed.** A leaked password can be replaced. A date, time and place of birth can't, so once they're out, they stay out.
- **The time says the most.** It decides your rising sign and your houses, what sets your chart apart from those of everyone else born the same day ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
- **They're often someone else's.** A synastry needs another person's details, and a parent holds a child's. Those belong to the other person, who should know before they're shared ([why I won’t read a synastry behind someone’s back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)), and a child can't yet say yes or no ([Children’s charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)).
- **Posted publicly, they're readable by anyone.** A birth time shared on social media beside your name lets strangers read the chart you'd only show a friend. It's the same reason I leave alone the charts of people who never asked me to read them ([Reading public figures](/astrology/reading-public-figures/)).

When you order a reading here, your birth details reach me with the payment, and they're used to prepare and deliver that reading and nothing else: never published, never sold, never used for marketing. The site itself sets no cookies and runs no analytics or advertising trackers. The privacy note names every service that touches them, and I keep what a reading needs for no longer than two years, unless you ask me to delete it sooner.

## What to do instead {#what-to-do-instead}

- **Read the privacy notice first.** Before typing your birth details anywhere, look for who receives them, why, for how long, and whether they're used for advertising or sold. No notice, or no answers, is an answer.
- **Give only what's needed.** A sun-sign horoscope doesn't need your birth time. A full reading does, and that's where it's worth giving it.
- **Share someone else's details only with their knowledge.** For a synastry on this site, the order form asks you to confirm the other person knows, and for a colleague, that they agreed ([synastry readings](/synastry/)).
- **Use your rights.** In the European Union and the United Kingdom, you can ask an organisation that holds your data to show you what it holds, have it corrected, and ask for it to be erased on a number of grounds. Write to the contact named in its privacy notice.
- **Complain if you get nowhere.** Each EU country has an independent data protection authority that hears complaints, the CNIL in France among them; in the UK, it's the Information Commissioner's Office.
- **If a subscription or a scam has your details,** cancel, ask for erasure, and report what happened ([Subscription traps](/astrology/subscription-traps/), [How to report a scam](/astrology/how-to-report-a-scam/)).
