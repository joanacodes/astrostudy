Two questions sit behind every traditional judgement of a planet. The first is about its sign: does the planet suit the place where it stands? The second is about its place in the chart: is it somewhere it can act? Angularity answers the second.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The tradition measures a planet twice. Essential dignity comes from the zodiac, the sign and degree, and describes the planet's quality: domicile, exaltation and the rest of the [table of dignities](/astrology/table-of-dignities/). Accidental dignity comes from everything else about its situation, and describes its ability to act. Its main part is angularity: where the planet stands in relation to the four angles, the Ascendant, the IC, the Descendant and the Midheaven.

The scale follows the houses. A planet in an angular house, the 1st, 4th, 7th or 10th, was counted most able to act; one in a succedent house, the 2nd, 5th, 8th or 11th, moderately able; one in a cadent house, the 3rd, 6th, 9th or 12th, least. A well-dignified Moon in Cancer, placed in the 12th house, was said to have little scope to show its good nature; the same Moon in the 1st, 4th, 7th or 10th, far more. The houses themselves, and why their names come from rising and falling, have their own page ([Angular, succedent and cadent houses](/astrology/angular-succedent-and-cadent-houses/)), and so does the narrow case of a planet right on an angle ([Angular planets](/astrology/angular-planets/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The two measures don't have to agree. Mars at 27° Capricorn is exalted, about as dignified by sign as Mars gets; with Aquarius rising and Capricorn filling the 12th house, the same Mars stands there, and a traditional astrologer would call it strong in kind and weak in position. Neither verdict cancels the other. The sign describes how well the function works; the house, how much room it has to work in.

Angularity is the main accidental condition, not the only one. The others are measured by the planet's motion and its relation to the Sun: its [speed](/astrology/planetary-speed/), slow or fast for that planet; whether it is [retrograde](/astrology/retrograde-explained/); whether it is hidden in the Sun's light, as [combustion](/astrology/combustion/) and being under the beams describe; and which side of the Sun it rises on, the question of [oriental and occidental](/astrology/oriental-and-occidental/) planets.

The old books also ranked houses by their nature, and called the 6th, 8th and 12th bad houses, the places of illness, death and confinement. Those are the tradition's words. Read in an adult's life, they are the houses of work and routine, of what is shared and intimate, of rest and solitude, and none of them predicts an illness or a death ([Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

In my readings a house is context: where a function works, never the function itself. So I read angularity as exposure: how much a function shows in a life, and how often other people meet it. Put that together with dignity and four pictures appear.

- **Dignified and angular:** a function that is comfortable and seen, often the thing people know you for.
- **Dignified and cadent:** comfortable and quiet, a strength used in the background.
- **Debilitated and angular:** awkward and seen, a function practised in public, which, worked at, can become a visible skill.
- **Debilitated and cadent:** awkward and out of sight, a function that develops privately, at its own pace.

None of the four is a better life than another. Each describes where effort and ease are likely to show.

All of it rests on the houses, and the houses rest on the birth time. In Placidus, the house system I use, a few minutes can carry a planet across an angle, so I don't judge angularity from a birth date; there's always a birth time, or no reading ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Angularity and dignity are the same thing.”** Dignity comes from the sign; angularity from the house. A planet can be strong in one and weak in the other.
- **“A strong position means an easy life.”** It means a visible function. What it shows can be ease or effort.
- **“You can judge angularity from your birth date.”** It depends on the houses, and the houses need the time and place of birth.
- **“A planet's house is the same in every system.”** House systems draw the cusps differently, so a planet near a cusp can change house, and with it its angularity.
