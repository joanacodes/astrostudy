Mars is the planet whose backward loop you can follow with your own eyes, no telescope needed. Every two years or so, as Earth catches up with it, Mars brightens, slows against the stars, backs up for two months or more, and moves on.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) takes 687 days to go round the Sun, nearly twice as long as we do, so Earth, on the inside track, catches up with it about every two years. As we pass, Mars seems from here to slide backwards against the stars: that is Mars retrograde. Halfway through, Mars is opposite the Sun, rising at sunset and up all night, at its closest and brightest for that round.

In the dates I work from, it happens 19 times between 2000 and 2040, every 764 to 810 days, and each time it lasts 60 to 81 days, the longest of the three personal planets’ retrogrades. Each loop lands a sign or two further on than the last:

- **6 December 2024 to 24 February 2025**, from 6°10′ Leo back to 17°00′ Cancer;
- **10 January to 1 April 2027**, from 10°25′ Virgo back to 20°55′ Leo ([Mars retrograde in Virgo](/astrology/mars-retrograde-in-virgo/));
- **14 February to 5 May 2029**, from 13°55′ Libra back to 24°55′ Virgo;
- **29 March to 13 June 2031**, wholly in Scorpio, from 21°38′ to 4°26′.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Mars’s orbit is more stretched than ours, and its loops show it. In the dates I use, the shortest, 60 to 65 days, fall in the signs from Capricorn to Aries, in years when Mars comes closest to us, as in 2003 and 2018. The longest, about 80 days, fall between Cancer and Libra, as in 2027 and 2029. The arc changes too: from about 10° in the short loops to 19½° in the long ones.

Because it lasts so long, Mars retrograde is the one most likely to stay with one part of your chart for a whole season. Two months or more backing through the same house, and every degree of the loop crossed three times, keep Mars on the same stretch of your chart for months: astrologers count those passes either side as [the shadow period](/astrology/shadow-period/).

Its rhythm sits between its neighbours’. [Venus retrograde](/astrology/venus-retrograde/) is shorter and comes more often, about six weeks every year and a half or so. [Jupiter retrograde](/astrology/jupiter-retrograde/), the next planet out, comes every year and lasts about four months.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Mars is retrograde about one day in eleven, so about one person in eleven is born with it. I read Mars as the way you act on what you want: initiative, effort, the courage to begin, the capacity to hold a boundary. Retrograde, that drive gathers inside before it shows. Plans are rehearsed, a decision is tested in private, and when the move comes it is often the considered one. I wrote about that inward rhythm in [Natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/). In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Mars was checked like every placement and wasn’t one of the three that held far more often, so I read its retrograde as part of the picture, never the whole of it.

As a transit, Mars retrograde backs through one house of your chart for two months or more, and your birth time says which. I read it as a season when effort turns back on itself: a plan reworked, a disagreement reconsidered, energy gathered for the next push. It’s weather, not a forecast: it never decides how a quarrel ends or whether a purchase was wise.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Mars retrograde makes people quarrel.”** Tempers belong to people, not to the sky. A transit describes weather; it doesn’t hand anyone a temper.
- **“Nothing begun under Mars retrograde will succeed.”** No transit decides that. What you start in those weeks stands or falls on what it is and how you go about it.
- **“Mars retrograde is a time to do nothing.”** Rethinking is doing something. The season favours reviewing how effort is spent, not giving it up.
- **“Mars always turns back in the same sign.”** Each loop lands a sign or two further on: Leo and Cancer in 2024–25, Virgo and Leo in 2027, Libra and Virgo in 2029, Scorpio in 2031.
