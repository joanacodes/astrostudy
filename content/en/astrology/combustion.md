Look for [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) in the evening sky and, for weeks at a time, you won't find it: it sits too close to the Sun, drowned in the glare. Tradition has a word for a planet in that state. It calls it combust, which simply means burnt, and counts it among the troubles a planet can meet.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A planet is combust when it stands so close to the Sun in the zodiac that it can't be seen with the naked eye: the Sun's light hides it. How close is close enough is not fixed. Authors set the limit differently, and some give each planet its own. What is settled is the frame around it: within about 15° of the Sun, a planet is [under the beams](/astrology/under-the-beams/), hidden but less deeply; combustion is the tighter zone inside that; and at the very centre, within 17 minutes of arc, the planet is [cazimi](/astrology/cazimi/), which tradition turns into a strength.

Combustion is one of the accidental debilities, the weaknesses of situation rather than of sign. Horary astrologers list it with retrograde motion, a place in a difficult house and an aspect from Mars or Saturn, the tradition's malefics, as reasons a planet is hindered in what it can do.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Every planet becomes combust when it passes the Sun, but not equally often. Mercury never strays more than about 28° from the Sun and Venus about 47°, so both meet it twice in each of their cycles: once on the far side, and once on our side, in the middle of each [retrograde](/astrology/retrograde-explained/). Mars passes the Sun about every two years, Jupiter every thirteen months or so, Saturn every year and two weeks, and the outer planets each year as well. The Moon meets the Sun every month: a [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/) is the Moon combust.

A combust planet is usually at the quick point of its cycle ([Planetary speed](/astrology/planetary-speed/)). The exception is Mercury or Venus passing between us and the Sun, in the middle of a retrograde, when the planet is combust while moving backwards.

Distance from the Sun is counted along the zodiac alone. A planet can pass well above or below the Sun in the sky and still be combust, which is why the chart shows a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) even when the two never meet overhead.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

In my readings the Sun is the function that holds identity, purpose and self-expression together, and a planet hidden in its light isn't broken: it is absorbed. I read combustion as a merger: the planet's function works inside your sense of who you are, less separately visible, more personally held. A combust Venus values what is bound up with identity, so what you love and who you are can be hard to tell apart; a combust Mars acts from purpose, and effort feels like self-expression. With Mercury, thought and identity come close enough to share a voice ([Sun conjunct Mercury](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mercury/)).

The cost, when there is one, is distance. A function this close to the self can be hard to look at from outside: you may find it easier to know what you think than to see why you think it. That's worth knowing, and it's never a verdict.

A combust Moon means a birth close to a new moon, and whether the Moon was inside the zone or just beyond it can turn on a few hours, which only the birth time settles ([The Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A combust planet is burnt out.”** The word is old and dramatic. The planet keeps its whole function; it works from inside your sense of self rather than beside it.
- **“Only Mercury gets combust.”** Every planet passes the Sun. Mercury and Venus simply do it most often, and the Moon does it every month.
- **“Every conjunction with the Sun is combust.”** A wide conjunction may fall under the beams instead, and the tightest is cazimi, which tradition reads the other way.
- **“Combustion has one agreed orb.”** Authors disagree, and some vary it by planet, so it's always worth saying which limit is being used.
