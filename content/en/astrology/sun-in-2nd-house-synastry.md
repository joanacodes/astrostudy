Their Sun in your 2nd house brings someone else's sense of purpose into the part of your life that asks *what can I count on?* Your values, the skills and things you keep, and the worth you quietly assign yourself all come under a warmer light, sometimes a stronger one than you'd choose.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is where a person's vitality and direction gather; [the 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) is where yours settle into something you can hold: resources, possessions, talents you could live from, and the self-worth underneath them. In this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their direction falls across that ground.

What you notice first may be a change in how you rate your own abilities. A remark of theirs makes a skill you'd shrugged off look worth building on; a question about what you'd never sell tells you what you value. People with this overlay often say the other person made them take their own resources seriously, not by giving them anything, but by treating what they already had as real.

From where they stand, your steadiness can look like firm ground. The way you look after your things, keep your habits and use your hands gives their own purpose somewhere practical to land, and they may feel unusually settled in the tangible world you've made. The 2nd is a succedent house, where what the 1st set in motion accumulates, and its natural sign is [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), patient and fond of what lasts: this overlay tends to add weight rather than stir things up.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

The friction comes when their purpose starts setting your priorities. You can find time, energy or savings flowing towards what matters to them, and only later ask whether you'd have chosen it. Worth can drift the same way: if their approval becomes the measure of what you're worth, a distant week from them feels like a drop in value.

For them, your need to be sure before you move can feel like a brake. Their Sun wants to go; your 2nd house wants to know what it can rely on first, and they may take your care for doubt about them. Neither reading is about money as such, and this overlay doesn't say who will gain or lose by the other: I don't read a chart that way ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)). It shows how two people handle what matters to them.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Talk about values before they turn into bills. What would each of you protect if you had to choose? What are you glad to share, and what stays yours? In a couple, that might mean a shared pot and two private ones; between friends, clear words about lending; between colleagues, saying out loud whose skills a project rests on and how they're credited. And if your sense of your own worth rises and falls with their mood, give some time to the things that steady you without them.

Turn the charts round and the same reading serves for your Sun in their 2nd house, your direction shining on what they value. A house earlier, [their Sun in your 1st house](/astrology/sun-in-1st-house-synastry/) lands on how you come across; a house later, [their Sun in your 3rd house](/astrology/sun-in-3rd-house-synastry/) lands on your everyday words and your close circle.

In Placidus, the house system I use, a 2nd house can be much wider or narrower than a sign, and only your birth time says where yours begins and ends. Their Sun, moving about a degree a day, needs their time only when it sits at one of those two edges, and both times are known before I begin ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Values are personal, so this is a reading for two people who both know it's happening ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
