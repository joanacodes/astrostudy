A birthday gives you a Sun sign; it can't give you a rising sign. That depends on the minute and the place you were born, so finding it takes a few careful steps, most of them before you ever look at a chart.

## Step by step {#step-by-step}

1. **Find your birth time.** As exact as you can, from a record rather than a memory if possible. The rising sign changes about every two hours on average, about one degree every four minutes, so a rounded time can hand you the wrong one. [How to find your birth time](/astrology/how-to-find-your-birth-time/) lists where to look, and [what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/) explains why it matters.
2. **Note where you were born.** The town of your birth, not the one you grew up in: the program needs its latitude and longitude. The same minute gives a different Ascendant in another town, and how quickly the signs rise changes with how far from the equator you were born.
3. **Check the clock.** Note whether daylight saving was in force that day and, for an older birth, which time the clocks kept: they're where a birth time most often goes wrong ([Time zones and daylight saving in charts](/astrology/time-zones-and-daylight-saving-in-charts/), [Local mean time for old births](/astrology/local-mean-time-for-old-births/)). Enter the time exactly as it was recorded, then check which time zone and offset the program applied.
4. **Choose the tropical zodiac.** It's the zodiac I read in ([Tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/)). A program set to the [sidereal zodiac](/astrology/sidereal-zodiac/) puts your Ascendant about 24° earlier, often in the sign before. The house system, on the other hand, doesn't move the Ascendant: in Placidus, the system I use, it is the cusp of the 1st house, and other systems divide the houses differently around the same rising degree ([Which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)).
5. **Find the Ascendant on the wheel.** It's the left-hand end of the horizontal line across the chart, marked AC or ASC ([Abbreviations in astrology](/astrology/abbreviations-in-astrology/)). The sign it falls in is your rising sign, and the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) is the degree itself.
6. **Read its degree.** A sign is 30° wide. If your Ascendant sits within a degree or two of either edge, a few minutes either way change the sign. Some signs also rise faster than others: at London, Aries and Pisces take about 52 minutes each, Leo to Scorpio nearly three hours each. Signs that rise fast north of the equator rise slowly south of it.
7. **Check it against the Sun.** The Sun's place is a quick test of the time. If you were born around sunrise, it sits close to the Ascendant, and your rising sign is likely to be your Sun sign; around the middle of the day, by the sun rather than the clock, near the top of the wheel; at night, below the horizon line. If the picture contradicts what you know of your birth, look again at the time zone, and at a.m. and p.m.
8. **Find your chart ruler.** The planet that rules your rising sign is your [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/): Mars for Aries rising, Venus for Taurus and Libra, and so on round the zodiac. Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces rising each have two, a modern ruler and a traditional one, and I read both ([Traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)).
9. **Then read it, lightly.** Each rising sign has its own page: [Aries rising](/astrology/aries-rising/), [Taurus rising](/astrology/taurus-rising/), [Gemini rising](/astrology/gemini-rising/), [Cancer rising](/astrology/cancer-rising/), [Leo rising](/astrology/leo-rising/), [Virgo rising](/astrology/virgo-rising/), [Libra rising](/astrology/libra-rising/), [Scorpio rising](/astrology/scorpio-rising/), [Sagittarius rising](/astrology/sagittarius-rising/), [Capricorn rising](/astrology/capricorn-rising/), [Aquarius rising](/astrology/aquarius-rising/), [Pisces rising](/astrology/pisces-rising/). In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the rising sign held about a third of the time, so I read it as a first sketch of how you meet the world, never on its own ([How to read your birth chart, step by step](/astrology/how-to-read-your-birth-chart-step-by-step/)).

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes}

- **Using a birthday-only calculator.** A date can give you a Sun sign, and usually a Moon sign, but never a rising sign.
- **Taking your Sun sign for your rising sign.** The two usually match only for births around sunrise.
- **Counting daylight saving twice.** If the program asks for local time and applies the time zone itself, taking an hour off beforehand moves your birth by an hour, and your Ascendant with it.
- **Mixing up a.m. and p.m.** Twelve hours out is a different chart altogether, houses and all.
- **Reading a sidereal chart with tropical descriptions.** The two zodiacs sit about 24° apart, so a sign read in one isn't the same sign in the other.
- **Blaming the house system.** If two programs disagree about your rising sign, look at the time, the place, the time zone and the zodiac: switching house system changes the houses, not the Ascendant.
