In a chart that already holds both of you, the house of the other person has to mean something new. A composite Moon in the 7th house puts the relationship's comfort in partnership itself: in commitment, in fairness between you, and in the way the two of you meet other people as a pair.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

The [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) places its [Moon](/astrology/moon/) at the midpoint of your two natal Moons. [The 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/), angular, begins at the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), has Libra as its natural sign, and covers partnership, agreements, cooperation and the people who stand opposite us. With the Moon here, the bond feels safest when the partnership is explicit: things agreed rather than assumed, a clear sense that the two of you are in this together, other couples, clients or collaborators met side by side. Libra, the house's natural sign, adds a taste for evenness: the pair is calmest when neither person feels they give more than they get, and when disagreements are settled rather than buried.

Since both people are already inside the composite, its 7th isn't one of you looking at the other. It describes the relationship as a partnership: the commitment as a shared project, and the way the pair relates outward, to friends who are couples, to business contacts, to anyone it has to negotiate with. That is the difference from [their Moon in your 7th house](/astrology/moon-in-7th-house-synastry/), where one person's feelings walk into the other's idea of partnership, and from a [Moon in the 7th house](/astrology/moon-in-7th-house/) of one birth chart, which describes a single person's need for a partner.

Its difficulty is leaning on the agreement. A bond that settles through partnership can find it hard when one of you wants something alone, or can rely on other people's view of the couple to feel secure. Partnership isn't dependence, and balance isn't people-pleasing. Agreements revisited as life changes serve this Moon better than ones kept by habit, and a little independence on each side makes the shared part feel chosen rather than required.

No composite can say whether two people will marry, stay together or part, and this placement is no exception: it describes how a relationship lives its partnership, between friends or colleagues as much as between lovers. Where the 7th begins depends on method as well as on both birth times. In either method the composite Descendant sits opposite the composite Ascendant, but one method takes that Ascendant as the midpoint of your two Ascendants and the other derives it from the composite Midheaven for the place where you live, so the two Descendants can differ ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)).

Before it comes the [composite Moon in the 6th house](/astrology/composite-moon-in-6th-house/), settled by shared routines; after it, the [composite Moon in the 8th house](/astrology/composite-moon-in-8th-house/), which needs depth and trust. I describe the composite here as a technique and don't use it in my readings; the astrologers who do set it beside the two natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), which is where each person's own needs in partnership show.
