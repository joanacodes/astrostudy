Faced with a landlord, a contractor or a rival team, this couple closes ranks and bargains hard, and sometimes it bargains just as hard with itself. Encounters are where composite Mars in the 7th house comes alive.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Opposite the composite Ascendant lies the composite [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), and [the 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) that starts there is the house of partners, agreements and the people you meet face to face. Tradition gave it marriage and contracts, and open enemies too, the opponent who confronts you directly. In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), where the whole chart already belongs to a partnership, the 7th shows how the relationship meets other people one to one and how it behaves as a partnership. [Mars](/astrology/mars/), which tradition counted a [malefic](/astrology/benefics-and-malefics/), a planet of conflict, brings energy and friction to those meetings. The house is angular, and Libra, its natural sign, is one of the two signs of Mars's detriment.

As a team, this couple is formidable. It negotiates the price of the flat, takes on the council over a planning dispute, plays other pairs at bridge or tennis to win, and rarely backs down in public. Encounters wake it up; a relationship with an outside challenge to meet feels most alive. Desire carries an edge of challenge too, the pleasure of being met by an equal. [Mars in the 7th house](/astrology/mars-in-7th-house/) in a birth chart describes someone drawn to strong partners; [their Mars in your 7th house](/astrology/mars-in-7th-house-synastry/) is a person who brings spark and friction to partnership with you; the composite makes the meeting of equals the relationship's own theme.

The risk is that the bargaining turns inwards. Two people can start treating each other as the other side: keeping score, arguing to win, hearing every request as a demand. Competition isn't hostility, and a partner isn't an opponent. What helps is a rule that you're allies first, with disagreements fought by agreed rules: no point-scoring, no old grievances, a pause when it heats up.

Fighting like opponents is friction; feeling unsafe with a partner is something else entirely, and no placement explains it. In the UK, ring 999 in danger; the Samaritans' line, 116 123, is open day and night.

The Descendant, like the Ascendant it faces, moves with both birth times and with the method: averaged from your two charts, or derived from the composite Midheaven where you live, it can shift by degrees, enough to move a Mars near the boundary between the 6th and the 7th ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)).

Composites aren't in my toolkit; I lay out the method so that one found elsewhere can be weighed properly. It belongs beside the two natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and no marriage, divorce or verdict in a dispute can be read from it. [Composite Mars in the 6th house](/astrology/composite-mars-in-6th-house/) gets on with the work; [composite Mars in the 8th house](/astrology/composite-mars-in-8th-house/) goes down into trust and shared stakes; the 7th meets the world head to head. A chart drawn for two people needs two yeses ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
