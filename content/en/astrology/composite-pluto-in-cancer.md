Pluto was in Cancer when it was discovered in 1930, and the pairs who carry it in their composite chart were born around those years. Composite Pluto in Cancer needs at least one person born before June 1939, so every pair who has it today includes someone aged 87 or more.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), cardinal water ruled by the Moon, protects, shelters and makes room for belonging. When [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) puts the relationship's [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) in Cancer, the bond's deepest changes happen at home: a household rebuilt after a loss, the families each person came from slowly rewritten into one of their own, a way of caring for each other that has changed more than once over the decades. Astrologers read a relationship that regenerates by sheltering: it takes in, holds and outlasts, and what it protects, it protects completely.

Protection isn't control, and that's where this placement asks for care. The danger is a shelter that tightens: a pair so busy keeping each other safe that friends drift away, decisions get made for the other one *for their own good*, and the walls that once kept the weather out start keeping the two of you in. Cancer belongs to the [Moon](/astrology/moon/), so the composite Moon's sign and house say what all this shelter is for: what the relationship needs in order to feel secure.

It's the composite Pluto of the oldest couples alive. Pluto stays 12 to 31 years in a sign, so two people of about the same age share their generation's Pluto sign, and an age gap moves the composite to the midpoint. Pluto was in Cancer from September 1912 to June 1939; if both of you were born after it left, both your Plutos lie in Leo or beyond, and so does the midpoint. Among two million pairs drawn from the births of 1930 to 2009, it came up for about 1 pair in 43, every one of them born between 1930 and 1948. The count starts in 1930, so the pairs born earlier who share it aren't in that figure. When both were born before mid-1939, each partner also carries [Pluto in Cancer](/astrology/pluto-in-cancer/) in their own chart.

Shared by so many pairs of the same age, the sign mostly describes the era the two of you grew up in. What makes it particular is the house, which needs both birth times: with [composite Pluto in the 4th house](/astrology/composite-pluto-in-4th-house/), say, the sign of home would sit in the house of home. Either side of it, [composite Pluto in Gemini](/astrology/composite-pluto-in-gemini/) needs a partner born before 1914, and [composite Pluto in Leo](/astrology/composite-pluto-in-leo/) is the composite of many pairs born between the late 1930s and the late 1950s.

Care can be misused like anything else. When one of you is being kept from friends, money, a doctor or the telephone, the chart has nothing to say about it, and help comes first: 999 for an emergency, Samaritans on 116 123, and the other lines on my Disclaimers page ([when to see a professional instead](/astrology/when-to-see-a-professional-instead/)). And a composite drawn as a family keepsake, by grandchildren as a gift, still needs the two people it describes to have agreed to it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
