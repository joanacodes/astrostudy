Some people's difference takes a while to notice. With Uranus in the 1st house it's the first thing others meet: a manner, a look, a way of starting things that doesn't quite follow the script, and works best once you stop trying to make it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) is the self you lead with: your body, your manner, how you begin things and how people first meet you. It's angular, starting at the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), and its natural sign is Aries, ruled by Mars. With [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) here, the function of originality and independence works in the most visible place in the chart. Differentiation isn't something you arrive at later in life; it's in how you walk into a room.

In Placidus, the house system I use, this means Uranus was just below the eastern horizon at your birth, due to rise within the next couple of hours. The Ascendant runs through the whole zodiac in a day, so a few minutes can carry Uranus over the line ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Uranus's sign is shared by everyone born over about seven years; its house is set by the hour, which is why this is the part of the placement that's yours. Your Uranus sign says how the difference shows: through method with Uranus in Virgo, say, or through outspoken beliefs with Uranus in Sagittarius. The neighbours read differently: [Uranus in the 12th house](/astrology/uranus-in-12th-house/) keeps its independence out of sight, while [Uranus in the 2nd house](/astrology/uranus-in-2nd-house/) puts it into what you value.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, a 1st-house Uranus needs to stay recognisably itself. You may attract people with what's unexpected about you, and then find out whether they meant it when your style, plans or opinions shift. The 7th house, opposite, is where partnership lives, and Uranus here asks that a partner meet you as you are rather than as a project to settle down. You tend to do well with someone who has a strong sense of self of their own, and who finds your independence restful rather than threatening. First impressions matter here in both directions: people often decide quickly what they think of you, and the bond deepens when they let you surprise them twice.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, you're often the one who does it differently in plain view: the first to try the new tool, the person whose approach others notice before they adopt it. Independent roles suit you, as do jobs where your personal way of doing things is part of what's being offered. Being asked to blend in, follow a uniform script or wait years for your turn wears on this placement more than hard work does.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The hard part is being defined by your difference. If you've been called the odd one out often enough, you may start performing it, or reinvent yourself so often that people can't find you twice. Some people with this placement feel they must keep proving their independence, especially at the start of anything new, and treat every suggestion as an attempt to change them. Differentiation is not alienation: you can be distinct and still close to people.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Let your difference become ordinary to you, so it doesn't need defending every time. Check, now and then, whether a change of look or direction comes from you or from the need to surprise. Mars, natural ruler of this house, and your [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/), the planet that rules your rising sign, say more about how you start things; read them alongside Uranus. Uranus's house went through the same tick-or-cross as every other placement in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/): not one of the three that held far more often, with about a third holding overall.
