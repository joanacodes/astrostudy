Some couples pool everything; some keep separate pots; some lend, share and swap in ways their families never did. With composite Uranus in the 2nd house, the relationship invents its own arrangement for what it owns and values, and changes it when its values change.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

The 2nd house has no angle at either edge. Both its cusps are intermediate ones, and [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) can set them in two ways: at the midpoints of your two natal cusps, or rebuilt from the composite Midheaven for the place where you live, in whatever house system the astrologer uses. Mine, for birth charts, is [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/). A Uranus near either edge of [the 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) can therefore change house with the method, and both methods need both birth times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

The 2nd is succedent, with Taurus as its natural sign: the house of resources, possessions, values and the security a bond builds from them. [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) there makes the pair's relationship to all of these its own invention. It might keep finances separate within a shared life, own little and borrow much, run a household on swaps and repairs, or put its resources into something its families would call unwise. Its values move as the two of you change, and its sense of security comes from freedom more than from accumulation. As a third thing, the bond may be more relaxed about possessions than either person alone, or more inventive with them. The 2nd also covers self-worth, and here the bond's sense of its own value hangs on nobody's approval: it can be proud of an arrangement no one else would choose.

[Uranus in the 2nd house](/astrology/uranus-in-2nd-house/) of a birth chart is one person's unconventional idea of worth; [their Uranus in your 2nd house](/astrology/uranus-in-2nd-house-synastry/) describes someone who unsettles what you thought you valued. In the composite, the unconventional idea is shared, and has to be agreed. [Composite Uranus in Taurus](/astrology/composite-uranus-in-taurus/), the sign of this house, belongs mostly to couples over eighty; the house, unlike the sign, can turn up in a composite at any age.

No composite predicts money gained or lost, and this one says nothing about either. The difficulty it describes is subtler: arrangements that change faster than both people can follow, or a freedom so prized that nobody decides what is shared. What helps is to put the arrangement into words, separate, pooled or mixed, so that each of you knows what is yours, what is the pair's, and how either can be changed.

On one side, [composite Uranus in the 1st house](/astrology/composite-uranus-in-1st-house/) shows the bond's difference to the world; on the other, [composite Uranus in the 3rd house](/astrology/composite-uranus-in-3rd-house/) puts it into daily talk. I set composites out as a method, not as part of my practice: a composite is read beside both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and it is drawn from two people's birth data only when both have agreed ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
