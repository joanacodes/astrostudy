Every tropical chart shares one degree with every other: 0° Aries. It is where the zodiac starts counting, the place the Sun reaches at the March equinox, and astrologers call it the Aries Point. What sits on it, or aims at it, is what makes it yours.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The Sun's yearly path, the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), is tilted against the [celestial equator](/astrology/celestial-equator/), Earth's equator projected onto the sky, and the two circles cross at two points. At one of them the Sun passes from south to north, around 20 March: that crossing is 0° Aries. Astronomers call it the First Point of Aries and measure from it both celestial longitude and [right ascension](/astrology/right-ascension/). The other crossing, half a circle away, is 0° Libra.

The name is older than the sky it describes. The point lay near the western edge of the constellation of Aries when it was named, in the time of Hipparchus. The equinox has slid back against the stars by about a degree every 72 years since ([precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/)), and it now lies in front of the stars of Pisces. The [tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/) follows the equinox, not the stars, so Aries still begins there.

In astrology, the Aries Point often stands for a group of four: 0° Aries and 0° Libra, the equinoxes, and 0° Cancer and 0° Capricorn, the solstices, where the Sun is farthest north and farthest south of the equator ([solstices and equinoxes](/astrology/solstices-and-equinoxes/)). Unlike the [East Point](/astrology/east-point/) or the [Polar Ascendant](/astrology/polar-ascendant/), which are worked out from the birth time and place, these four degrees are fixed: only their houses and their contacts change from one chart to the next.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

On the Uranian 90° dial, which folds the zodiac into quarters, 0° Aries, 0° Cancer, 0° Libra and 0° Capricorn all land on the dial's zero, and astrologers who use the dial read the four as a single point ([the Uranian 90° dial](/astrology/uranian-90-dial/)). A planet or angle on any of them, or in hard aspect to them, counts as on the Aries Point.

How often that happens is plain arithmetic. Each cardinal sign's opening degree holds the Sun for about a day, so the four together hold it about four days a year: about 1 birth in 90. The Moon covers a degree in about two hours, and over a month gives the same share. The slow planets sit on these degrees for weeks or months, for everyone born then. The Ascendant and the Midheaven cross a degree in a few minutes, so whether an angle sits on the Aries Point depends entirely on the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

The same degree has a public use. In [mundane astrology](/astrology/mundane-astrology/), astrologers cast a chart for the moment the Sun reaches 0° Aries, the Aries ingress, for a capital or a country, and read it for the year; Richard Trewythian did it for some thirty years in 15th-century England. The four cardinal degrees also open the usual lists of [critical degrees](/astrology/critical-degrees/).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I don't read the Aries Point as a separate point, and I don't promise anyone a public life for having a planet on it. What I do notice is simpler. A planet at 0° of a cardinal sign has only just entered it, and belongs to that sign as fully as it would at 15° ([0° of a sign](/astrology/0-of-a-sign/)); I read it, as any planet, by its sign, its house and its aspects. With the Moon, or with an angle, the birth time comes first: a few minutes, or a couple of hours for the Moon, can move it into the sign before.

The idea that a planet there links your life to the wider world is one some astrologers find useful, and you may too. I'd hold it as a question to try on, not as a finding ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A planet on the Aries Point makes you famous.”** No degree can promise fame. The planet describes a function in you, read by sign, house and aspect.
- **“The Aries Point is in the constellation of Aries.”** It hasn't been for about two thousand years; it now lies in front of Pisces.
- **“The Aries Point is calculated from your birth time.”** It is always 0° Aries. The birth time only decides which of your houses it falls in.
- **“Only 0° Aries counts.”** Many astrologers count all four cardinal degrees, and a hard aspect to any of them.
