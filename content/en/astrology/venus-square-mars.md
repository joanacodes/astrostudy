Venus square Mars sets liking and pursuing a quarter of the circle apart. The two don't keep the same schedule: desire may come before affection, or affection without the nerve to act, and much of this square's work is learning to time them.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) weighs and chooses: what attracts you, what you'd like to keep, how to keep an exchange fair. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) pushes: it asserts, competes, makes the effort, draws the line. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates them through friction, the kind that makes you adapt: neither function gets its way for long, and each keeps testing the other.

In plain words, wanting and liking can pull in slightly different directions. You may go after what you're not sure you value, or hold back from what you plainly love; you may soften an ask until it disappears, or push it so hard the exchange tilts. Neither is a flaw. This square tends to teach two lessons at once: that keeping the peace isn't the same as harmony, and that asserting yourself doesn't have to cost the relationship. Lived with, it tends to make you good at both, clear about what you want and fair in how you ask.

There's spark in it too. Friction between wanting and liking is lively, which is why astrologers often read this square as attraction that stays awake. I don't read it as conflict: the rub is between two parts of you, learning each other's timing.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In a chart, the two planets usually share a modality, cardinal, fixed or mutable, and that sets the rhythm of the rub. With Venus in Cancer and Mars in Aries, both cardinal and quick to begin, care wants to shelter while drive wants to charge. With Venus in Taurus and Mars in Leo, both fixed, comfort and pride are each hard to move. The houses show where the friction is felt: Venus in the 7th square Mars in the 10th might set partnership and ambition asking for the same evenings.

About 1 person in 15 is born with Venus and Mars within 6° of a square. Exact or close to it, the rub is part of most days; at 5° or 6°, it tends to surface under pressure, when you want something badly. Venus held about a third of the time in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), so I offer this square as a tendency to test against your own life, not as a verdict.

The pair's other meetings, for comparison:

- **[Venus conjunct Mars](/astrology/venus-conjunct-mars/):** liking and pursuing in one place; no gap between them to rub, only the question of which leads.
- **[Venus sextile Mars](/astrology/venus-sextile-mars/):** an easy working arrangement; the first step comes naturally once you take it.
- **[Venus trine Mars](/astrology/venus-trine-mars/):** the two keep pace in one element, so wanting rarely argues with liking.
- **[Venus opposite Mars](/astrology/venus-opposite-mars/):** liking and pursuing at the two ends of the chart, a seesaw that often tips towards other people before it balances.

Between two charts, [Venus square Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-square-mars-synastry/) is the spark and the snag in one contact: one person's taste rubbing against the other's drive, which asks both people to name what they want plainly; since each feels a different side of the snag, I read both charts.

## In transits {#in-transits}

In [transits](/astrology/transits/), Venus moving through the sky squares your natal Mars twice a year, a day or two each time: a moment when what you'd enjoy and what you're pushing for don't line up. About once a year Mars returns the angle, squaring your natal Venus for several days, longer if it turns retrograde nearby: drive that tests what you value, or a pleasure that asks for more patience than you'd like.

Weather, not a forecast: a few days of friction worth noticing, never a sign that something will go wrong.
