Sun conjunct Pluto sets the two side by side, so the most intense function in a chart sits right at its centre. It describes a self that rarely does anything by halves, and that can, more than most, close a chapter and begin again.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) organises your identity and vitality: who you are, what you're for, the energy you bring to it. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) transforms: it clears out what has outlived its use, regenerates, and hands back a deeper kind of strength. In a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) the two work as one while each keeps its identity, so transformation isn't something that happens to you from outside. It's part of how you become yourself.

In plain words, you feel fully and commit fully. Surface answers frustrate you; you'd rather know what's underneath, in a situation and in yourself. Your life may hold more than one ending and more than one rebuilding, and each tends to leave you more yourself rather than less. People sense the intensity, sometimes before you've said a word. The one thing I'd add, gently: empowerment isn't domination. This strength is yours to hold, not to press on others, and it's at its best turned towards what you yourself can change. I read Pluto slowly, and never as a verdict ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The two usually share a sign, and Pluto's sign belongs to everyone born over twelve to thirty-one years. The Sun beside it makes that generation's theme personal: with both in Scorpio, depth and intimacy are part of who you are; with both in Libra, the way you relate is where your deepest changes happen. The house of the conjunction, which needs a birth time, shows where you go all in: in the 2nd, what you own and value; in the 10th, your work and the part you play in public.

Within 8°, this conjunction belongs to about 1 person in 22. Pluto's pace varies a great deal, so the date the Sun meets it moves on by about a day a year in some decades and by two or more in others; either way, the two stay in orb for a little over two weeks. At this aspect Pluto is never retrograde: it lies beyond the Sun, moving forward. The closer the two, the more concentrated the intensity.

Each of the pair's other aspects gives the same depth a different place:

- **[Sun sextile Pluto](/astrology/sun-sextile-pluto/):** depth on offer rather than built in; a quiet strength to call on when something ends.
- **[Sun square Pluto](/astrology/sun-square-pluto/):** depth rubbing against who you've decided to be, so strength is claimed rather than given.
- **[Sun trine Pluto](/astrology/sun-trine-pluto/):** intensity that flows, showing mostly when things get hard.
- **[Sun opposite Pluto](/astrology/sun-opposite-pluto/):** the intensity faces you across the chart, often in someone else's power.

In [Sun conjunct Pluto in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-pluto-synastry/), one person's Sun lands on the other's Pluto: magnetic, and a real question of who changes whom, asked from both sides.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Pluto conjunct natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-pluto-conjunct-natal-sun/) is rare and slow. Pluto takes about 248 years to go round, so a Sun meets it once at most, and the transit stays within range for two years or more.

Born with the conjunction, which came first decides it. If your Sun was a few degrees ahead of Pluto, the transit falls in your first years; if it was behind, Pluto had already passed and won't return. Later, the [Pluto square](/astrology/pluto-square/) to its own birth place squares your Sun as well, at an age only your chart can date. Weather, not a forecast, and the slowest kind: it describes a deep season, never an event.
