Their Sun in your 6th house is the overlay of the ordinary day. Another person's sense of purpose walks into your routines, your work and the small habits that keep a week running, and the daily round starts to feel as if it's going somewhere.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[The 6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) covers the shape of your days: the jobs themselves rather than your job title, the habits that keep you upright, the people you work beside, the care you give your body. The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is the drive in a person's chart to become someone in particular, and the energy that goes with it. Their Sun in this house makes a [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/) where that drive meets your daily round.

With them around, the ordinary can stop feeling ordinary. A shared morning routine, a project at work, a kitchen run smoothly by two: they bring a sense of purpose to the jobs you'd normally just get through, and you may find yourself doing them better. Plenty of good working partnerships have this overlay somewhere, though it isn't confined to work.

They, for their part, often admire the way you do things. Your method, your reliability and your skill give their purpose something practical to work with, and around you they may feel more capable, as if their plans had finally found hands. Cadent houses adjust and maintain, and the 6th is the one that keeps a life running; [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/) is its sign in the natural zodiac, with its care for detail and usefulness, and tradition gave Mars its joy here.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

When two people meet mostly in the house of tasks, the relationship can turn into a list. Conversations become logistics, evenings become planning, and you may notice that helping them has quietly become part of your job description: their projects in your diary, their errands on your way home. Being useful is a gift; getting used to being used is something else.

From their side, the risk is a shrinking. Their larger aims arrive in a house of chores and method, and with you they may feel their direction reduced to next steps, or measured against your standards for how things should be done.

Tradition gave the 6th illness and the name Bad Fortune. I don't read health in a chart, in synastry or anywhere else ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)); what I read here is how the two of you work and look after yourselves, side by side.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Make the division of labour visible. Who does what, and does it still feel fair? In a couple, a weekly look at the chores keeps resentment from settling in; between friends, a shared habit such as a walk, a class or a Sunday cook gives the overlay its best form; between colleagues, clear roles let their direction and your method work together instead of one swallowing the other.

Late in your 6th, their Sun nears your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), the edge of the house of partners, and a working bond can then start to feel like a partnership; I read the contact with that angle too. Reverse the charts and this page is about your Sun in their 6th house. Beside it, [their Sun in your 5th house](/astrology/sun-in-5th-house-synastry/) is about play, and [their Sun in your 7th house](/astrology/sun-in-7th-house-synastry/) about partnership.

Because the 6th ends exactly at your Descendant, a few minutes of your birth time can move a Sun near that line from one house to the other, and in Placidus, the house system I use, every cusp shifts with it. Their own time enters only when their Sun and one of your cusps are within a degree, and I need it before reading anyway ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). If the two of you work together, a reading still needs their agreement, not just their birth details ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#what-i-ask)).
