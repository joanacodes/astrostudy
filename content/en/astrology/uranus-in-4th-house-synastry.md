Furniture moves when someone's Uranus falls in your 4th house, sometimes literally. The way your household runs, who counts as family, what home is for: with them, all of it can become a question, and some of the answers surprise you.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) reconfigures systems: it asks why things are arranged as they are, then tries another arrangement. [The 4th house](/astrology/4th-house/) is the house of roots: where you come home to, where you come from, and what you show only to the people nearest you. This [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/) carries change down to your foundations.

For you, it can be a breath of air at home. You may set up house differently, open your door to people who don't fit the usual picture of family, or finally drop a household rule you'd inherited without choosing it. Home can become a place you design rather than one you repeat.

For them, your home may be where they feel most free, an unexpected base from which to be themselves. They may bring unusual ideas about how to live, and light up at the thought of rearranging. Of the four angular houses, the 4th is the one turned away from the street; its natural sign is [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), the sign of shelter and memory, so change arrives in the house that keeps the past. Where their Uranus touches your [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), the very root of your chart, the contact counts for more than the house alone.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Home needs some continuity, and Uranus doesn't always notice. You may feel the ground move: plans changed at short notice, traditions questioned, a sense that nothing at home stays put. If your feeling of safety rests on routine, their restlessness can be a draught through the house.

From their side, your attachment to the way things have always been done can feel like a cage, and they may change a system before understanding what still works in it. Your need for a fixed base isn't resistance; it's what lets you rest.

No move, separation or family rift can be read here. Uranus describes change and freedom at home, never where they lead, and the 4th says nothing certain about anyone's parents.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Change one room at a time. Agree what stays as it is, the Sunday meal, the photographs, the drawer where things always go, and experiment with the rest. In a couple, decide together before anything structural changes at home; between flatmates or friends, this may be the person who shows you another way to live, best taken in small doses; between adult relatives, it can be the one who breaks an old family pattern, sometimes to everyone's relief; between colleagues who work from home, keep the domestic experiments out of the shared calendar.

From where they sit, it's your Uranus in their 4th house: you're the one rearranging their home, and the one to ask what's sacred before moving it. [Their Uranus in your 3rd house](/astrology/uranus-in-3rd-house-synastry/) changes how you talk; [their Uranus in your 5th house](/astrology/uranus-in-5th-house-synastry/), how you play.

Your IC turns with the sky like every angle, a full circuit each day, so your birth time places this house; in Placidus, the house system I use, a few minutes can decide whether their Uranus sits in your 3rd or your 4th. Their own time barely moves Uranus, though I need it as well ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). With someone a generation older or younger, their Uranus usually stands in another sign from yours: two eras meeting more than two people, one generation's idea of home walking into another's. If you're close in age instead, your own Uranus may be in your 4th too ([Uranus in the 4th house](/astrology/uranus-in-4th-house/)). Nothing is more private than a home, so I read none of this without both people's consent ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
