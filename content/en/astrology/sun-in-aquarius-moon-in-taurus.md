Of all the pairs of Sun and Moon signs, only this one sets the Sun in its detriment and the Moon in its exaltation. Your sense of self is at work in Aquarius, a sign that asks it to stand among others, and your feelings rest on Taurus, the ground the old books liked best for the Moon. You were born when the Moon was close to half, high in the sky at nightfall.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Aquarius and Taurus are both fixed, one air and one earth, with Taurus three signs on: a [square](/astrology/square/) by sign, two steady signs holding on to different things. The [Sun in Aquarius](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius/) holds to an idea of how people could live, work or organise themselves better, and keeps hold of it for years; the [Moon in Taurus](/astrology/moon-in-taurus/) holds to what already works, and is calmest when a day has a shape it recognises. You can argue for change at a meeting and go home to the same route, the same supper, the same few people, and see no contradiction, because there isn't one: the reform is for the world, and the routine is for you.

Taurus is the Moon's [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/) in Ptolemy's scheme, while Aquarius, the sign across from Leo, is the Sun's [detriment](/astrology/detriment/). I read the first as feelings that settle easily and hold steady, the second as a self that works best among others, and neither as a ranking of you. [Venus](/astrology/venus/) rules Taurus and shows what your Moon values and keeps. There are two rulers for Aquarius, and I read both: [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), the planet modern astrology added, points to the habit your Sun most wants to break; [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), the ruler Aquarius had before Uranus was found in 1781, to the structure it is willing to maintain. Of the three, Saturn and Venus speak your Moon's language of keeping; Uranus is the one that asks it to move.

The Moon had pulled 61° to 119° ahead of the Sun, five to almost ten days after the new moon, and these births fall evenly into two phases, the [waxing crescent](/astrology/waxing-crescent/) and the [first quarter moon](/astrology/first-quarter-moon/): the hour can decide yours. Near 90° comes a waxing [Sun square Moon](/astrology/sun-square-moon/) between two fixed signs, friction that asks you to act rather than wait for it to pass. [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) goes through the boundary between the two.

An exalted Moon under a Sun in its detriment, as here, belongs to about 1 person in 145. The Moon reaches Taurus from Aries and has gone on into Gemini some two and a half days later; on the day of either crossing, the [Sun in Aquarius, Moon in Aries](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius-moon-in-aries/) and the [Sun in Aquarius, Moon in Gemini](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius-moon-in-gemini/) are still possible, and the time decides ([the Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). It decides the houses too, and so the part of your life where the reform happens and the part where the routine is kept.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You're loyal, physically present and slow to change your mind about the people you love. The Taurus Moon shows care by turning up and keeping the rituals going; the Aquarius Sun wants friendship at the heart of the couple, with room for each of you to think differently. A partner may be puzzled that someone so open to new ideas needs home to stay so constant. Say it plainly: the ideas can change daily, the bond isn't up for revision.

## At work {#at-work}

You're good at making a new idea last. The Aquarius Sun sees what could be improved in a system, a service or a community; the Taurus Moon wants it built properly and kept running, with the resources it needs. Work that asks for both suits you: infrastructure, cooperatives, long research projects, design meant to be used for decades. Restructuring that serves no one tires the Moon, even when the Sun approves of the principle behind it.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Fixed against fixed, at a right angle, both lights can refuse to budge. When a change you argued for arrives at your own door, the Moon may need far longer to settle into it than you expected, and when someone challenges your principles, the Sun can hold its ground past the point of listening. What helps is pace: change one thing at a time, keep one routine untouched while you experiment elsewhere, and count the Moon's need for continuity as a need rather than a failing.
