Venus conjunct Jupiter places the two planets tradition called benefics side by side. In a chart it reads less like luck than like generosity of taste: what you like, you want more of, and want to share.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) evaluates: attraction, preference, pleasure, and the give and take that keeps a bond fair. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) expands: it looks for meaning, learns, and helps things grow. In a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/), the two work together as one, each keeping its own nature, so valuing and growing tend to happen in the same breath.

In plain words, liking something makes you want to understand it, widen it, pass it on. A meal becomes a tradition, a song becomes a subject, a friend becomes a circle. You tend to give credit, praise and encouragement freely, and people often feel larger for it. That's the warmth tradition had in mind when it called these two the [benefics](/astrology/benefics-and-malefics/), the planets that bring good things.

If this conjunction has a risk, it's too much of a good thing. Growth isn't excess, but when pleasure and growth sit together, neither is in a hurry to ask when to stop. The quiet work here is learning what enough feels like, so that generosity stays a pleasure rather than a reflex.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The sign styles both planets at once. In Pisces, where Venus is exalted and Jupiter traditionally at home, the two are at their most welcome: tenderness that widens into kindness. In Sagittarius, Jupiter's own sign, taste turns towards the far and the new; in Taurus or Libra, Venus's signs, growth comes through comfort, beauty or fairness. In Virgo, where tradition puts Venus in its fall and Jupiter opposite one of its signs, the generosity is careful and practical, and no smaller for it. The house shows where the abundance lives: in the 2nd, what you value and keep; in the 11th, friendships and groups.

About 1 person in 26 is born with Venus and Jupiter within 6° of a conjunction. Venus does the moving: it crosses a sign in four or five weeks, Jupiter in about a year, so Venus catches up with Jupiter roughly once a year, and the conjunction belongs to the people born in that short window, not to everyone who shares Jupiter's sign. Within a degree or two, generosity is a theme of the chart; near 6°, a warm undertone.

Venus and Jupiter can meet at any angle, and each changes how taste and growth get along:

- **[Venus sextile Jupiter](/astrology/venus-sextile-jupiter/):** openings for generosity that you take: friends, study or travel that widen what you love.
- **[Venus square Jupiter](/astrology/venus-square-jupiter/):** generosity that outruns its measure; the friction teaches proportion and honest promises.
- **[Venus trine Jupiter](/astrology/venus-trine-jupiter/):** appreciation and good will flowing in one element; real ease, with a little discernment needed.
- **[Venus opposite Jupiter](/astrology/venus-opposite-jupiter/):** the near and the far across the chart; a balance often found through other people.

Between two charts, [Venus conjunct Jupiter in synastry](/astrology/venus-conjunct-jupiter-synastry/) sets one person's taste on the other's urge to grow: the kind of contact where each makes the other feel more generous. The warmth rarely flows evenly, which is why I read each chart.

## In transits {#in-transits}

In [transits](/astrology/transits/), Venus passes over your natal Jupiter about once a year, for a day or two of easy warmth. The longer season is [Transiting Jupiter conjunct natal Venus](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-conjunct-natal-venus/), about every twelve years as Jupiter completes its round: it stays near your Venus for a few weeks, or passes three times over several months when its yearly retrograde falls there. If the conjunction is yours, that transit comes round with each [Jupiter return](/astrology/jupiter-return/), since your Jupiter and Venus sit together.

Weather, not a forecast: a season of open-handedness that promises neither a love story nor a windfall ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).
