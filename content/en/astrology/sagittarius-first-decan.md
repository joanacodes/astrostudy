Sagittarius opens with a contradiction the old tables seem to enjoy: Mercury, a stranger in the sign, rules its first face. And the Egyptian decan behind these degrees is one of the very few whose name can still be read.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

The Chaldean order runs on from Venus to Mercury, so the first face of [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/) is Mercury's ([faces](/astrology/faces/)), and from here the sign repeats the faces of Taurus: Mercury, the Moon ([Sagittarius, second decan](/astrology/sagittarius-second-decan/)), then Saturn. That gives Mercury a foothold in a sign that otherwise keeps it at arm's length. Sagittarius is Mercury's [detriment](/astrology/detriment/), the sign opposite its home in Gemini, so [Mercury in Sagittarius](/astrology/mercury-in-sagittarius/) between 0° and 10° holds the slightest of dignities in the sign of its exile.

The modern decans give the first ten degrees to the sign's own ruler, and Sagittarius has only one, Jupiter, which no modern planet has displaced. A planet here works under Jupiter twice, through the sign and through the decan, with Mercury's face as the voice of the small print: the facts, checked, before the conclusion.

## Its image {#its-image}

This is one of the rare decans whose Egyptian name can be translated. Wallis Budge wrote it Ḥer-ab-uȧa; in the transliteration Egyptologists use today it is ḥry-ib wiꜣ, which the Egyptologist Sarah Symons gives as her example of a decan name we can read: "in the centre of the boat". Which stars made up that figure, she adds, is not known ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

The later lists give other forms of the name, without its meaning: Hre-ua in the Greek-Egyptian lists, Eregbuo or Ergbuo in [Firmicus Maternus](/astrology/firmicus-maternus/), Eregbuo again in Joseph Scaliger's sixteenth-century list. Few of the decans' names can be read as a picture at all; this one I can give you with a translation behind it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

A [Sun in Sagittarius](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius/) born from about 22 November to 1 December has its Sun in these degrees. For a birth on 21 or 22 November, only the time decides whether the Sun had left Scorpio ([Scorpio, third decan](/astrology/scorpio-third-decan/)). Under Jupiter twice and Mercury's face, I read the decan as the sign's search for meaning, with the facts still checked along the way.

[Sagittarius rising](/astrology/sagittarius-rising/) in its first decan takes about 53 minutes to cross the horizon in Paris and 54 in London, and makes Jupiter the chart ruler.

For anyone born before about 2017, the red star [Antares](/astrology/antares/), the heart of the Scorpion, sits in the last degree of this decan: at 8°46′ Sagittarius in 1930 and 9°45′ in 2000. Precession has since nudged it to 10°07′. The Sun passes it about 1 or 2 December ([Sun conjunct Antares](/astrology/sun-conjunct-antares/)).

Three slow planets have crossed this decan within living memory: [Neptune in Sagittarius](/astrology/neptune-in-sagittarius/) from 1970 to 1975, Uranus from 1981 to 1984 and Pluto from 1995 to 1999, each with retrograde loops at the edges. A whole generation shares each one; the house is what makes it yours, and the house needs the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
