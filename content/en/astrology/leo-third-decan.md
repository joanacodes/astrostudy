Leo ends under Mars. The old and modern lists of decan rulers agree on Mars for the sign's last ten degrees, 20° to 30°, and until about 2012 these degrees also held Regulus, the star at the Lion's heart.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

Saturn, Jupiter, then [Mars](/astrology/mars/): Lilly's [faces](/astrology/faces/) for [Leo](/astrology/leo/) follow the Chaldean order down to its third planet. By element the decan goes to Aries, the third fire sign, whose ruler is Mars as well, so the two lists meet again, as they did on Jupiter one decan earlier.

So the Sun's sign closes in degrees that answer to Mars. The Sun gives a planet here its wish to be seen and to create; Mars adds the effort and the nerve to go after it. I start from the Sun's sign and house, then turn to your Mars, which says how readily that courage turns into action.

Mars itself, placed in Leo between 20° and 30°, has a face of its own here ([Mars in Leo](/astrology/mars-in-leo/)): wholehearted action with a small dignity behind it, in a fire sign like its own Aries.

## Its image {#its-image}

Peḥui-tchat, Budge's name for this decan, completes the pair begun by Ḥā-tchat in the decan before ([Leo, second decan](/astrology/leo-second-decan/)). Its other names, list by list: Phu-tet (Greco-Egyptian), Phatiti (Greek Hermetic), Futile or Eisie (Firmicus Maternus), Phuonisie (Joseph Scaliger) and Soubetti (*Testament of Solomon*).

Sarapis appears here in the list of Cosmas of Maiuma, the same god Athanasius Kircher had placed at the start of Taurus as Serapis; for this decan Kircher chose Nephthe. A god who wanders between Taurus and Leo, list to list, is a fair measure of how loosely the figures were attached ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In mid-August, from about the 13th to the 22nd, the Sun moves through these degrees ([Sun in Leo](/astrology/sun-in-leo/)). In 2026 it reached 20° Leo on 12 August at 16:47 UT, and an hour later, at 17:45 UT, the total eclipse of the Sun fell at 20°02′ Leo, just inside the decan ([Eclipse in Leo](/astrology/eclipse-in-leo/)). The Sun left for Virgo on 23 August at 02:18 UT, so births on 22 and 23 August need their time ([Virgo, first decan](/astrology/virgo-first-decan/)).

For births before about 2012, [Regulus](/astrology/regulus/) belongs to this decan, at 28° or 29° Leo; for births since, it is the first star of Virgo.

Seen from the latitude of London, these are among the slowest degrees of the zodiac to rise: about 57 minutes for the whole decan. An Ascendant here stays in the decan for close to an hour, though a time rounded to the hour can still miss it ([Leo rising](/astrology/leo-rising/)).

Pluto's last Leo years belong here, births from July 1952 to October 1956, with visits from 1951 and until 1958; Neptune came through in the late 1920s and Uranus from July 1960 to November 1961. Saturn spent 1947–49, 1977–78 and 2006–07 in the decan. Jupiter is in it now, from 3 October 2026 to 26 February 2027, and comes back from 29 May to 25 July 2027.

A Mars-ruled decan can describe how you go after what you want; what you then do with it stays yours, and a reading is a conversation about that, never an instruction ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).
