The third decan of Leo is 20° to 30° of the sign, the Sun's last ten days in Leo before Virgo. Mars rules it in both lists, and until about 2012 it held Regulus, the star at the Lion's heart.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

Lilly's [faces](/astrology/faces/) reach [Mars](/astrology/mars/) at the end of [Leo](/astrology/leo/), after Saturn and Jupiter. The modern decans arrive at Mars by element: the last ten degrees of a fire sign go to the ruler of the third fire sign, Aries, and Aries is Mars's. The two lists agree here as they did on Jupiter one decan earlier.

So the Sun's sign closes in degrees that answer to Mars. The Sun gives a planet here its wish to be seen and to create; Mars adds the effort and the nerve to go after it. With a planet in this decan, I read the Sun's sign and house first and then your Mars, which says how readily that courage turns into action.

[Mars in Leo](/astrology/mars-in-leo/) between 20° and 30° holds its own face: wholehearted action with a small dignity behind it, in a fire sign like its own Aries.

## Its image {#its-image}

Budge's pair of names closes here: Peḥui-tchat answers the Ḥā-tchat of the decan before ([Leo, second decan](/astrology/leo-second-decan/)). The Greco-Egyptian lists write Phu-tet, the Greek Hermetic list Phatiti, Firmicus Maternus Futile or Eisie, and Joseph Scaliger Phuonisie; the *Testament of Solomon* has Soubetti.

Cosmas of Maiuma set Sarapis over these degrees, the god Athanasius Kircher had placed at the start of Taurus under the name Serapis; Kircher himself chose Nephthe for this decan. The same god wandering between Taurus and Leo, list to list, is a fair measure of how loosely the figures were attached ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun stands here for births from about 13 to 22 August ([Sun in Leo](/astrology/sun-in-leo/)). In 2026 it reached 20° Leo on 12 August at 16:47 UT, and an hour later, at 17:45 UT, the total eclipse of the Sun fell at 20°02′ Leo, just inside the decan ([Eclipse in Leo](/astrology/eclipse-in-leo/)). The Sun left for Virgo on 23 August at 02:18 UT, so births on 22 and 23 August need their time ([Virgo, first decan](/astrology/virgo-first-decan/)).

For births before about 2012, [Regulus](/astrology/regulus/) belongs to this decan, at 28° or 29° Leo; for births since, it is the first star of Virgo.

Seen from the latitude of London, these are among the slowest degrees of the zodiac to rise: about 57 minutes for the whole decan. An Ascendant here stays in the decan for close to an hour, though a time rounded to the hour can still miss it ([Leo rising](/astrology/leo-rising/)).

Pluto held these degrees for births from July 1952 to October 1956, with visits from 1951 and until 1958; Neptune in the late 1920s; Uranus from July 1960 to November 1961. Saturn passed in 1947–49, 1977–78 and 2006–07. Jupiter is crossing the decan now, from 3 October 2026 to 26 February 2027, and again from 29 May to 25 July 2027.

A Mars-ruled decan can describe how you go after what you want; what you then do with it stays yours, and a reading is a conversation about that, never an instruction ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).
