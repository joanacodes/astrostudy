With the ruler of your 5th house in the 2nd, pleasure and money keep each other company. What you love doing tends to come with a price, a value, or a way of paying for itself.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [5th house](/astrology/5th-house/) is where you play, create and fall for people; the [2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) holds what you earn, own and feel you're worth. The planet ruling the sign on your 5th cusp is the go-between ([house rulers](/astrology/house-rulers/)), and from the 2nd it turns the 5th's affairs into questions of value. What does this pleasure cost? What is the thing I made worth? Can I afford to love doing it?

I read it as pleasure you can hold. People with this placement often love what lasts: good bread, a well-made guitar, wool, wood, paint that costs a little more than it should. Many sell what they make, or quietly hope to, and what you create is often how you discover your own worth. In love, affection tends to take a material form, a meal or a present chosen with care, and you value a partner who notices. What the placement asks is that you don't judge every joy by its return. Some pleasures are worth having precisely because they pay nothing.

There's friction built in. In the old count by sign, the 2nd and the 5th are square, and the square is where tradition put strain. Here it runs between spending and keeping: the wish to enjoy something now against the wish to have something put by. Both houses are succedent, the houses where things settle and accumulate, so I read the tension as steady rather than sudden, a lifelong negotiation more than a crisis.

The planet tells you how the negotiation goes. Venus, ruling a Taurus or Libra 5th, stands in the house the [natural zodiac](/astrology/natural-zodiac/) pairs with Taurus, one of its own signs, and spends on beauty with a clear conscience. Saturn, ruling a Capricorn 5th, saves up for its pleasures and may treat a hobby as an investment of years. The Sun, ruling a Leo 5th, wants what you make to be recognised, and payment is one form recognition takes.

With [Pisces on the 5th house cusp](/astrology/pisces-on-5th-house-cusp/), Jupiter and Neptune share the house ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)), and I read both. Jupiter in the 2nd is generous with fun, the friend who pays for the tickets; Neptune, sitting elsewhere, can blur the line between what a pleasure costs and what it's worth.

Horary astrology also gave the 5th gambling and speculation, and with its ruler among your money, the pairing can sound like a gambler's placement. I don't read it that way. It describes your appetite for risk where money is concerned, not whether a stake will pay, and no chart can choose an investment for you ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)). Nor does it count children, the 5th's oldest subject: whether a child comes is beyond a chart ([can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/)), and I won't read a child's chart either ([children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)).

The 2nd sits between the 1st and the 3rd, and its cusps shift with the minutes of your birth. With a slightly later time, a ruler near the top of the 2nd would belong to the 1st, and your pleasures would show in your manner ([Ruler of the 5th in the 1st house](/astrology/5th-house-ruler-in-1st-house/)). With a slightly earlier one, a ruler near the bottom would drop into the 3rd, among words and games ([Ruler of the 5th in the 3rd house](/astrology/5th-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/)). That's one more reason I work in Placidus only from a time that's been recorded ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
