There's a rota on the fridge, two pairs of running shoes by the door, and an argument about the bins that has lasted three years. That appetite for daily work, routine and being useful is composite Mars in the 6th house.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Each planet, in the old scheme, had one house where it rejoiced, and for Mars that was [the 6th](/astrology/6th-house/) ([planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/)): the house of daily work, routines, service and the care of the body, which the old astrologers also called Bad Fortune. In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), a [Mars](/astrology/mars/) placed here puts the relationship's energy into the daily round, where the planet is at ease. The house is cadent, a working house rather than a showy one, and its natural sign is Virgo. Of the twelve, it's the one the old astrologers thought Mars was glad to be in, and the reason is plain enough: there's always work for it here.

A couple like this works well together. It runs a shared business, trains for the same race, cooks in shifts, renovates on weekends with a plan pinned to the wall, volunteers every Saturday. Routine doesn't bore it; a good system feels like an achievement. Competition shows in standards and output: who ran further, whose method is faster. Desire is practical, care shown in useful acts, and the evening of rest is something it has to book. Natal [Mars in the 6th house](/astrology/mars-in-6th-house/) brings energy to spare for work; [their Mars in your 6th house](/astrology/mars-in-6th-house-synastry/) puts a spur, or a goad, to your daily habits; the composite makes work something the two of you share.

Arguments are about the division of labour: who did the bins, who always cooks, who left the job half done. Stress from work outside can come home and land on the rota. The risk is love reduced to a to-do list, where effort is counted and tenderness waits for the weekend. Service isn't servitude: what helps is a fair, visible split of tasks, thanks said out loud, and days when nothing at all gets done.

The old astrologers also read illness in the 6th. This placement predicts no illness, for you or your partner ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)); for a couple it describes shared habits, a training plan or a work rhythm, not a diagnosis.

If the daily round ever runs on fear rather than habit, or one of you is made afraid of getting things wrong, no chart can weigh that: phone Samaritans on 116 123 if you need to talk, and in a UK emergency dial 999.

This house, like the other eleven in a composite, needs both exact birth times and hangs on the method, the averaged cusps of your two charts or houses derived from the composite Midheaven at your home ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Composites never made it into my own practice; the method's users keep your birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) beside this one. [Composite Mars in the 5th house](/astrology/composite-mars-in-5th-house/) plays; [composite Mars in the 7th house](/astrology/composite-mars-in-7th-house/) negotiates with the world; the 6th gets on with the job. Neither of you should find it drawn without having agreed ([Synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
