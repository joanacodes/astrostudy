Sun square Moon is the half moon written into a birth chart: you were born with the Sun and Moon a quarter of the sky apart, close to the first or last quarter. It describes your sense of direction and your need for comfort keeping different hours, and a life spent learning to fit both into the same day.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is the part of a chart that organises who you are and where you're going: your purpose, your vitality, the way you put yourself into the world. The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) regulates what you feel and need: comfort, attachment, the way you respond when things change. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates them through friction: purpose doesn't replace need, or need purpose, but each keeps bumping into the other.

In plain words, what you're aiming for and what you need on the way ask for different things at the same moment. The project that lights you up wants the evening; the part of you that keeps you steady wants the evening too, at home. You can feel restless when you rest and homesick when you push. Neither your drive nor your need for home is wrong, and the square doesn't ask you to pick a winner. It asks you to keep adjusting, and that adjusting is the skill this square builds: knowing which of the two is asking, today.

The Sun and Moon are the two lights of a chart, and with the Ascendant they make its first sketch, so a square between them isn't a side story. It's in your days, every day. That's also why I'm gentle with it: friction you meet daily is friction you get a great deal of practice at, and practice is what a square is for.

Which half moon you were born under changes the direction of travel. At a [first quarter moon](/astrology/first-quarter-moon/) the Moon is 90° ahead of the Sun and the month is growing towards the full moon; at a [last quarter moon](/astrology/last-quarter-moon/) it's 90° behind, and the month is waning towards the next new moon. I read the first as friction that pushes outward, into doing, and the second as friction that turns inward, towards sorting out what to keep. [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) tells you which one is yours.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In a chart, this square tends to show as a back-and-forth between doing and feeling: a clear sense of where you're going, and needs that don't always agree to come along. The signs give it its colour. A square usually joins two signs of the same modality, so your Sun and Moon share a way of moving and disagree about nearly everything else: a Sun in Aries with a Moon in Cancer wants to charge ahead and wants to be home; a Sun in Taurus with a Moon in Aquarius wants to keep things as they are and needs room to change them. The houses show where the two meet in your life. The Sun's house is where you're trying hardest, the Moon's is where you go for comfort, and the square ties those two places together: home and partnership, say, or your work and the way you meet the world.

About 1 person in 11 is born with the Sun and Moon within 8° of a square, and that orb sets the volume. The Moon gains about 12° a day on the Sun, so 8° means a birth less than a day either side of the exact quarter. The closer to exact, the louder the square; near the edge of the orb, it's more of a background hum.

The Moon is the one doing the moving, and that matters. Born before the exact quarter, the Moon was still closing in on the square, which many astrologers feel as a tension that's building; born after, it was already easing away ([applying and separating aspects](/astrology/applying-and-separating-aspects/)). And because the Moon is so quick, the birth time can decide whether you have this square at all: on the day of a quarter moon, a few hours move the Moon in or out of orb, and on some days into another sign. It's one of the reasons I don't read a chart without the time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

The Sun and Moon make every aspect in turn, each month, so each of the pair's other aspects is a phase of the moon:

- **[Sun conjunct Moon](/astrology/sun-conjunct-moon/):** born at a new moon; purpose and need start from the same point, and the work is telling them apart.
- **[Sun sextile Moon](/astrology/sun-sextile-moon/):** born under a crescent moon; the two cooperate whenever you give them an opening.
- **[Sun trine Moon](/astrology/sun-trine-moon/):** born under a gibbous moon, a few days from full; purpose and comfort keep the same pace, so the ease can go unnoticed.
- **[Sun opposite Moon](/astrology/sun-opposite-moon/):** born at a full moon; the two face each other across the chart and ask to be balanced, often through other people.

Between two charts, [Sun square Moon in synastry](/astrology/sun-square-moon-synastry/) is one person's direction rubbing against the other's need for comfort: two rhythms that take some learning, and that I read in both directions.

## In transits {#in-transits}

In [transits](/astrology/transits/), the same square comes back to visit, briefly. Twice a month the Moon in the sky squares your natal Sun, for a few hours each time: tiredness in the middle of a push, or a mood that asks for a quieter day than the one you'd planned. Twice a year the Sun squares your natal Moon. The Sun is the slower of the two, about a degree a day, so it stays close to exact for two or three days: a short stretch when what the world asks of you and what you need to feel steady don't agree.

Born with the square, you'll also see your aspect in the sky at every quarter moon, wherever it falls in your chart. That isn't a transit to your chart, just the sky repeating the angle you were born under, and I take it as a reminder rather than a message. Weather, not a forecast: a Sun–Moon square in transit describes the mood of a day or two, never an event.
