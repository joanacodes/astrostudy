With Aries on the 6th house cusp, the ordinary working day begins in the sign of beginnings. The 6th is the house of tasks, habits and the daily care of your body, and here each of them goes better once it's under way.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The sign on the [6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) cusp is the way you go about an ordinary day ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)), and [Aries](/astrology/aries/) goes about it by starting. You tend to like a task with a clear first step and the freedom to take it, to be trusted to get on with things rather than supervised, and to feel the day has gone well when something has been begun or finished, not merely kept up. Your body's daily care tends to run the same way: a walk, a swim or an hour of exercise does more for you than a rule stuck on the fridge. Virgo, the 6th's natural sign, refines and checks; with Aries in its place, checking is the part of the job to make room for. Among colleagues you're often the one who says let's begin when a meeting goes round in circles, and handing the second half of a job to someone who likes finishing can be a relief on both sides.

Tradition gave each planet a house where it rejoiced, and [Mars](/astrology/mars/) had this one: the planet of effort in the house of toil. With Aries on your 6th cusp, Mars rules it as well. If your Mars also stands in the 6th, it's in its own house and its joy at once, which I read as work that feeds your energy rather than draining it. Wherever it stands, it shows what your daily effort serves: the [1st](/astrology/6th-house-ruler-in-1st-house/), [2nd](/astrology/6th-house-ruler-in-2nd-house/), [3rd](/astrology/6th-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/), [4th](/astrology/6th-house-ruler-in-4th-house/), [5th](/astrology/6th-house-ruler-in-5th-house/), [6th](/astrology/6th-house-ruler-in-6th-house/), [7th](/astrology/6th-house-ruler-in-7th-house/), [8th](/astrology/6th-house-ruler-in-8th-house/), [9th](/astrology/6th-house-ruler-in-9th-house/), [10th](/astrology/6th-house-ruler-in-10th-house/), [11th](/astrology/6th-house-ruler-in-11th-house/) or [12th](/astrology/6th-house-ruler-in-12th-house/) house.

What rises with this cusp ties your work to the rest of you. Usually it's Scorpio, in two London charts out of three and three out of four at New York, and Mars, Scorpio's traditional ruler alongside Pluto, then rules your Ascendant as well as your working day; I read both rulers. Otherwise it's Libra, and Aries then runs on past the 6th to the Descendant: [Aries on the 7th house cusp](/astrology/aries-on-7th-house-cusp/) describes you too, and one planet governs both your work and your partners. Less often, Aries has already opened the 5th ([Aries on the 5th house cusp](/astrology/aries-on-5th-house-cusp/)). A sign on two cusps, as here, is duplicated ([duplicated signs](/astrology/duplicated-signs/)); a sign on none is intercepted ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)), as Aries is in about 1 London birth in 6.

One London birth in 10 has this cusp, and so does one New York birth in 10; only on the Descendant itself is Aries commoner. These are Placidus cusps, the house system I use, and they hang on your birth time, which is why I don't read a chart without it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). As for the illness tradition filed in this house, and its old name, Bad Fortune, I leave them to the history books: a chart can't diagnose or predict a health problem ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)). What I read here is how you work and how you keep yourself going.
