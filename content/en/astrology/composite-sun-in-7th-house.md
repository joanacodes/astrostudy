In a birth chart, the 7th house is the person across the table. A relationship is already two people across a table, so a composite Sun in the 7th points somewhere slightly different: to partnership as the bond's very centre, and to the alliances the pair makes with others.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) of [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) begins at the composite [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), which always sits opposite the composite Ascendant. So it inherits the Ascendant's question: is that point the midpoint of your two Ascendants, or worked out from the composite Midheaven for the place where you live? Whatever settles one settles the other, both depend on both birth times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and a [Sun](/astrology/sun/) near the Descendant can change house between the two methods.

Cooperation, reciprocity and agreements fill the 7th house. A relationship like this tends to see itself as a partnership first, whether it's a marriage, a friendship or a firm with two names on the door, and it's at its best when the terms between you are clear. It can also describe how the pair meets the world as a unit: the other couples you spend New Year with, the clients you take on together, the friends who think of you as a team. Balance revives it, and so does a good negotiation. Two people who each dislike committees can find that, as a pair, they're the ones everyone asks to chair the meeting.

Its natural sign is Libra, where the Sun has its fall; the house is angular, and it was no planet's joy. Someone who comes into focus through partners: that's the [Sun in the 7th house](/astrology/sun-in-7th-house/) in a birth chart. [Their Sun in your 7th house](/astrology/sun-in-7th-house-synastry/), in synastry, lights up what partnership means to the person receiving it. A composite puts partnership at the centre of the bond itself, so the question shifts from *who is my partner?* to *what kind of partner are we, together?*

A bond organised around partnering can lose itself in its alliances, spending more energy on the couples, clients and committees around it than on the two people inside it. Agreements can also stand in for conversation, with the terms kept and the feelings unspoken. Balance isn't people-pleasing, and an evening that belongs to nobody else helps, as does checking now and then that the agreements still suit you both. None of it forecasts a marriage, a signed contract or a break-up: the 7th describes how a pair cooperates, never what happens to it.

On one side, the [composite Sun in the 6th house](/astrology/composite-sun-in-6th-house/) centres a bond on shared work; on the other, the [composite Sun in the 8th house](/astrology/composite-sun-in-8th-house/) on what the two of you share and trust each other with. Composites aren't a technique I use in readings; I describe it as its users do, with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) alongside. A relationship centred on agreements should start with one: both people saying yes to their charts being used ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
