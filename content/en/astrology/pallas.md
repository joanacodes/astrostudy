Pallas turned up in March 1802, little more than a year after Ceres, and for decades the two were counted among the planets. It carries one of Athena's names, and astrologers who use it read her kind of intelligence: the strategist's, the weaver's, the mind that sees how the pieces fit.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Athena was the Greek goddess of wisdom, warfare and handicraft, often called Pallas, and in the myth she was born fully armed from the forehead of Zeus. Her war is the planned kind and her craft the patient kind. Astrologers who use the asteroid read both: recognising patterns, finding a strategy, solving problems, building skill by hand. Where [Ceres](/astrology/ceres/) describes care, Pallas describes the plan.

My readings leave the asteroids out, because the system I built for them doesn't compute any; I describe Pallas here the way its users read it. They look at:

- **Its sign:** how you think a problem through, whether by instinct, by talk, by system or by feel.
- **Its house:** the area of life you tend to organise, defend or redesign.
- **Its aspects:** the planets your strategy has to recruit, or answer to.

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is how you think and speak; Pallas, as these astrologers read it, is the moment the separate facts turn into a shape. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) acts with force, and Pallas would rather win with a plan. A [trine](/astrology/trine/) between Pallas and another planet lets the strategy run smoothly through that function. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) offers an opening the plan has to take, and a square asks the strategy to adapt.

The asteroid has a quirk of its own. Its orbit is tilted 34.8°, unusually steep for the asteroid belt, so Pallas can stand far north or south of the path the planets follow. A chart flattens that and records only its longitude; its [celestial latitude](/astrology/celestial-latitude/) is lost on the wheel.

## Pallas through the signs {#through-the-signs}

Pallas circles the Sun in 4.6 years, much like Ceres, so its sign is shared by people born months apart. I have no ephemeris for it here, so I give no dates, and the house will tell you more than the sign.

- **Pallas in Aries:** quick thinking that solves by doing; the plan is the first move.
- **Pallas in Taurus:** patient, practical problem-solving, and craft with real materials.
- **Pallas in Gemini:** patterns found in words, links and puzzles; strategy worked out in conversation.
- **Pallas in Cancer:** intelligence in the service of protecting people, alert to emotional patterns.
- **Pallas in Leo:** strategy with flair; a craft that carries your signature.
- **Pallas in Virgo:** analysis and method, with an eye for the flaw in the system.
- **Pallas in Libra:** strategy through negotiation; designs that keep two sides in balance.
- **Pallas in Scorpio:** a reader of hidden motives, steady in a crisis, drawn to research.
- **Pallas in Sagittarius:** big-picture plans and patterns that run across ideas and cultures.
- **Pallas in Capricorn:** long-range planning, and a feel for how institutions work.
- **Pallas in Aquarius:** systems thinking, networks and reform.
- **Pallas in Pisces:** intuitive pattern recognition, with imagination as a way of solving things.

## Pallas in the houses {#in-the-houses}

Houses come from the [time of birth](/blog/unknown-birth-time/), and I draw them in Placidus; a few minutes can move a cusp past Pallas. Tradition linked the 6th house with illness, among other things, and a Pallas there says nothing about your health ([Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)).

1. **1st house:** you come across as someone with a plan, quick to size up a room.
2. **2nd house:** strategy with money and resources, and skills that earn their keep.
3. **3rd house:** clever with words and puzzles; you solve things by talking them through.
4. **4th house:** the family strategist, the one who redesigns the home.
5. **5th house:** craft in play: games of strategy, projects made for the pleasure of making.
6. **6th house:** methods and systems at work, and daily problems solved one by one.
7. **7th house:** partnerships as alliances, with a talent for negotiation and fair terms.
8. **8th house:** clear thinking in a crisis or over shared money, and a nose for what is hidden.
9. **9th house:** patterns in ideas, law and belief; teaching what you have worked out.
10. **10th house:** a career built on expertise, planning or design.
11. **11th house:** organising groups and causes so that they actually work.
12. **12th house:** insight that comes in private, worked out away from other people.

## Pallas in synastry {#in-synastry}

The asteroids stay out of my [synastry readings](/synastry/), which count only contacts involving at least one personal body. Astrologers who bring Pallas into a comparison read it from both sides. When one person's Pallas meets the other's Mercury or Sun, the Pallas person tends to see the shape of the other's ideas, and the other tends to feel understood, even improved. The friction comes when one keeps solving what the other only wanted to be heard about. A comparison starts, as every one should, with both people's agreement: [synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/).

## Transits and the Pallas return {#pallas-return}

Roughly every four and a half years, Pallas comes back to the degree it held when you were born. The transits my forecasts use are those of Jupiter to Pluto, Chiron and the true node, and Pallas isn't among them. Those who follow it read Pallas crossing Mercury, or the Midheaven, as a period that favours planning, learning a method or redesigning how something works. Weather, not a forecast, here as everywhere: a season for strategy, never a promise of how it ends. And with four asteroids and more to choose from, [Juno](/astrology/juno/) included, restraint helps a chart stay readable.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Pallas is a dwarf planet, like Ceres.”** Its shape is too far from round for that; only Ceres, of the four, qualifies.
- **“Pallas shows how clever you are.”** It describes a way of solving things, not an amount of intelligence.
- **“The warrior asteroid means conflict.”** Athena's warfare is strategy and defence, and Pallas is read as planning, not fighting.
- **“Pallas travels along the zodiac like the planets.”** Its orbit tilts 34.8°, so it can wander far from the planets' path; a chart keeps only its longitude.
