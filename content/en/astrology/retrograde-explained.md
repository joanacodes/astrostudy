Watch Mars for a few months when it’s at its brightest and you’ll see it do something odd: drift east against the stars, slow down, stop, slide back west for a couple of months, then turn and carry on. Every planet does a version of this. Astrologers call the backward stretch retrograde, and it has a far worse reputation than it deserves.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Retrograde means moving backwards through the zodiac: from Taurus back towards Aries rather than on towards Gemini. No planet does that in space. Seen from the Sun, every one of them goes round its orbit the same way all the time. Retrograde is what that steady motion looks like from Earth, which is going round too, and it comes in two kinds.

- **Mercury and Venus** are closer to the Sun than we are, and faster. They look retrograde when they overtake us on the inside track, passing between Earth and the Sun. Halfway through, the planet is lined up with the Sun and lost in its glare, and it moves from the evening sky, after sunset, into the morning sky, before dawn.
- **Mars and everything beyond it**, Chiron included, are slower than we are. They look retrograde when Earth overtakes them, and halfway through, the planet is opposite the Sun: up all night, and at its closest to us for that year.

It’s the motorway effect. From a fast car, a slower one in the next lane seems to drift backwards as you pass it, although both are going the same way.

Two bodies never look retrograde. The [Sun](/astrology/sun/)’s path through the signs is our own orbit seen from inside, and the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) really does go round us, always the same way. The mean lunar nodes, meanwhile, are always retrograde: they move backwards round the zodiac in about 18.6 years.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A retrograde has a shape. The planet slows until it seems to stand still, backs over a stretch of the zodiac, stands still again, and goes back to [direct motion](/astrology/direct-motion/). The two standstills have their own page, [Stations](/astrology/stations/), and the degrees in between are what astrologers count as [the shadow period](/astrology/shadow-period/) before and after.

How often, and for how long, depends on the planet. In the dates I work from, 2000 to 2040:

- **[Mercury retrograde](/astrology/mercury-retrograde/):** three times in most years, four in a few; 20 to 24 days each time; about one day in five.
- **[Venus retrograde](/astrology/venus-retrograde/):** every 580 to 588 days; 41 to 43 days each time; about one day in fourteen.
- **[Mars retrograde](/astrology/mars-retrograde/):** every 764 to 810 days; 60 to 81 days; about one day in eleven.
- **[Jupiter retrograde](/astrology/jupiter-retrograde/):** once a year, for 118 to 124 days; about three days in ten.
- **[Saturn retrograde](/astrology/saturn-retrograde/):** once a year, for 134 to 141 days; more than a third of the time.
- **Uranus, Neptune and Pluto:** once a year, for 149 to 164 days; about two days in five, and for Pluto close to half.
- **Chiron:** once a year, for 138 to 157 days.

The pattern is simple once you see it. Earth overtakes each of the slow planets once a year, and the slower the planet, the bigger the share of the year it spends going backwards.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

In a birth chart, I read a retrograde planet through the same things as any other, its sign, its house and its aspects, and then through its rhythm. The system I built for my readings describes retrograde as an inward, reflective way of processing: the planet’s function works inside first and shows itself later. I wrote about that rhythm, and why it isn’t delay, in [Natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/).

How personal it is depends on the planet. A retrograde Mercury, Venus or Mars marks a birth inside a stretch of weeks that comes round only every few months or years. A retrograde Jupiter, Saturn or Pluto is shared by everyone born over several months of the same year, so I read it more lightly, through the house it falls in, which needs the birth time.

In the sky, the retrogrades most people hear about are Mercury’s, Venus’s and Mars’s. I read each as a season of review for that planet’s function, in the house of your chart it backs through. That’s weather, not a forecast: a retrograde doesn’t make things happen, it describes a slower, more reflective way of meeting what’s already there.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The planet really goes backwards.”** It never does. Retrograde is an effect of watching from a moving Earth; the planet keeps going round the Sun the same way throughout.
- **“A retrograde planet is broken.”** It keeps its whole meaning. What changes is the rhythm: inward first, outward later.
- **“Mercury’s is the only retrograde that matters.”** The slow planets spend months of every year retrograde. They do it so regularly, and so slowly, that it rarely makes the news.
- **“Retrograde planets in your chart are debts from a past life.”** No chart factor can show a past life. I read retrogrades as a rhythm in this one, the life you’re living now.
