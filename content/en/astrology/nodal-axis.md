The nodal axis is the North and South Nodes taken together: one line across the chart, with an end in each of two opposite signs. A chart may mark only the North Node, but the South Node is always there, exactly opposite, and the line between them is what I read.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The Moon's path around us is tilted about five degrees to the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), the path the Sun seems to follow through the year. Two tilted circles cross at two points, exactly opposite each other. Where the Moon crosses going north is the [North Node](/astrology/north-node/); where it crosses going south, the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/). The line through them is the nodal axis.

Nothing is there to see: the nodes are crossing points, worked out from the Moon's orbit. That's also why there are two ways to calculate them — a smoothed mean node and a wobbling true node — which are a degree or two apart at most ([mean node and true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/)). Near the edge of a sign, it's worth checking which one a chart uses.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The axis doesn't stay put. The Sun's pull slowly swings the Moon's orbit round, so the crossing points slide backwards through the zodiac: about 18 months in each pair of opposite signs, about 18.6 years for the full circle. Everyone born within the same 18 months or so shares the same nodal signs.

The axis is also where eclipses come from. The Sun, the Moon and the Earth line up only when a new or full moon falls near the nodes: a new moon there makes a solar eclipse, a full moon a lunar one. That happens in two seasons a year, about six months apart, and while the nodes cross a pair of signs, the eclipses fall mostly in that pair too ([why eclipses follow the nodes](/astrology/why-eclipses-follow-the-nodes/)).

And it comes back. About every 18.6 years the axis returns to where it was when you were born — the [nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/), at about 18½, 37 and 56 — and halfway between, at about 9, 28 and 46, it lies the other way round, with the North Node over your natal South Node.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read the two nodes as one line, never one without the other. The South Node end shows what came easily or early; the North Node end, the less familiar direction that tends to repay effort. The signs give the style of that pull, and the houses — which need a [birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/) — give the areas of life it runs between: you and your partner across the 1st and 7th, home and public life across the 4th and 10th, everyday talk and the bigger picture across the 3rd and 9th.

Planets on either end take part in the story, and a planet square the axis, halfway between the two ends, sits across the path. Some astrologers read the nodes as karma or past lives; I read them in this life, as a direction rather than a fate, which is [how I read](/how-i-read/) everything in a chart.

Between two people, the axis is where charts can feel familiar or stretching: someone's planets on your South Node feel like an old acquaintance, on your North Node like a nudge forward. I look at those contacts in a [synastry reading](/synastry/), in both directions, and [the nodes in synastry](/astrology/nodes-in-synastry/) goes into the detail. And to be honest about the evidence: my [testing notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) doesn't name the nodes, so I hold them as a question to try on, not a finding.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The nodes are planets.”** They're crossing points between two paths in the sky, with nothing physically there.
- **“The South Node is your past life and the North Node your destiny.”** Some astrologers read them that way. Nothing in a chart can prove it, and a reading doesn't need it.
- **“Eclipses are bad omens.”** An eclipse is the Sun, the Moon and the Earth lining up near the nodes. Astrologers read eclipse seasons as times of change: weather, not a forecast.
- **“The mean and true nodes give different charts.”** They're a degree or two apart at most. The sign is nearly always the same; it only matters near the edge of a sign.
