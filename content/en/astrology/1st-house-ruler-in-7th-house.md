Directly across the chart from your Ascendant, in the 7th house, stands the planet that rules it. The planet that speaks for you lives in the house of the other person: you meet yourself, again and again, in the people you pair with.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) speaks for you and the [7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) for the person facing you: a partner, a close collaborator, the one across the table, anyone you meet as an equal, the open rival included. Your [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/), the ruler of the 1st, takes your story to the house where it sits ([house rulers](/astrology/house-rulers/)), and here it crosses the whole chart to settle on the other side.

You come into focus in company. Partnership is your mirror: what you want becomes clear when you see it, or miss it, in someone else, and the people you choose often carry qualities you're still growing into. Collaboration suits you, and so does a good argument with someone who's your match. What it needs is balance between the two ends of the chart. A life steered from the far side can drift towards other people's plans, and part of the work is keeping a self you'd recognise on your own. Partnership isn't dependence, and a mirror only helps while you're the one looking into it.

There's a structural reason the balance takes effort. A planet is at home in the sign it rules and in [detriment](/astrology/detriment/) in the sign opposite, and the 7th house always begins in the sign opposite your rising sign, at the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/). So if your chart ruler sits in the sign of your Descendant, it's in its detriment, whatever your Ascendant: Mars in Libra for Aries rising, Venus in Aries for Libra rising, the Moon in Capricorn for Cancer rising. Your ruler has to work in the other side's manner, and the people you pair with tend to be the ones who teach it to you. I read detriment as a skill learned the long way, never as a flaw.

Low in the west and close to setting: that's where your ruler stood when you were born, since the 7th, in Placidus, the house system I use, is the stretch of sky just above the western horizon. And in horary astrology, where William Lilly set out how the ruler of a house signifies its matters, the ruler of the 1st stands for the person asking the question; here that planet has gone to stand in the house of the other.

Which side of the Descendant your ruler falls on can come down to a few minutes ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Just below that line, in the 6th, your life would run through daily work and usefulness ([Ruler of the 1st in the 6th house](/astrology/1st-house-ruler-in-6th-house/)); further up, in the 8th, it would move from the partnership itself to what the two of you share and owe ([Ruler of the 1st in the 8th house](/astrology/1st-house-ruler-in-8th-house/)).

With Pisces rising, Jupiter and Neptune share the chart, and I read both ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)): Jupiter in the 7th makes partnership a place of growth and generosity, while Neptune, wherever it falls, adds the ideal you bring to love. When only one of them faces the Ascendant, your life is split between what you build with someone and what you keep for yourself.

None of this says whom you'll meet, or when, or whether you'll marry. The 7th describes what you look for in another person and what you learn by finding it, and your partnerships are yours to live, not something a chart foretells.
