Moon conjunct Regulus is a monthly meeting caught at the right hour. The Moon reaches the royal star of Leo every 27⅓ days and stays within a degree of it for about four hours; a birth inside those hours puts the quickest body of the chart on the Lion's heart.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) as the part of you that regulates: what you need in order to feel safe, how you attach, what you reach for when the day has been long. [Regulus](/astrology/regulus/) belongs to a grander register. Its names are royal from Babylon onwards, and Ptolemy likened it to Mars and Jupiter, the planets of acting and of growing. Laid over the Moon, that comparison gives your needs some of Mars's drive and Jupiter's reach, and the star's names carry a royal tone into the most private corner of the chart, the place where you look after your feelings.

Unlike most star contacts, this one can be a real meeting. Regulus lies less than half a degree from the ecliptic, while the Moon's path swings about five degrees either side of it, so in some years the Moon passes straight in front of the star and hides it for a while. Those occultations come in spells, years apart. Outside them the Moon slides a few degrees north or south of Regulus, and the two share a degree of the zodiac rather than a patch of sky.

Indian astronomers divided the Moon's round into lunar mansions, one for roughly each day of it, and Regulus is the chief star of Maghā, "the bountiful", a name also translated "the mighty". Even in the Moon's own calendar, then, the star keeps its grandeur ([the 28 lunar mansions](/astrology/28-lunar-mansions/)).

The Moon covers a degree in about two hours, so a birth at breakfast and one at lunchtime on the same day can fall either side of the window, and only the birth time settles which. No birth time, no reading: that is my rule for every chart, and this contact shows why ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). The time also places the conjunction in a house, which in Placidus, the system I use, can move with a few minutes when the meeting sits near a cusp.

For births from 1930 to 2009, the Moon on Regulus sits in the last degrees of Leo or the first of Virgo, since the star itself stood at 28°50′ Leo in 1930 and 29°49′ in 2000 ([Moon in Leo](/astrology/moon-in-leo/)). That sign says far more about the manner of your needs than the star does, and with the Moon's house and aspects it's what I read: my readings run on a system with no stars in it, so I describe Regulus here rather than bring it into one ([fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/)).

A new moon on 22 or 23 August puts both lights on the star at once, the Moon beside the [Sun conjunct Regulus](/astrology/sun-conjunct-regulus/). Among the star's other contacts, [Venus conjunct Regulus](/astrology/venus-conjunct-regulus/) is the most frequent, at about 1 in 146; [Saturn conjunct Regulus](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-regulus/) comes to about 1 in 151, [Mars conjunct Regulus](/astrology/mars-conjunct-regulus/) to 1 in 161, and the Moon's own, at 1 in 179, sits almost exactly at the average for a one-degree contact ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)).
