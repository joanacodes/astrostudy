Mars wants to move; Taurus wants to stay. Put them together and drive changes shape: it stops being a burst and becomes a steady pressure, slow to begin and very hard to turn aside. I read Mars in Taurus as stamina before speed, the effort that's still going when the sprinters have sat down.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is how you go after things: initiative, effort, the will to defend what's yours. [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/) is fixed earth, ruled by Venus, the sign of what lasts and what can be touched. Here Mars works the way Taurus does everything: at its own pace, through the body, towards something concrete. Effort is counted in hours rather than bursts, and a decision, once taken, is defended with patience rather than noise.

Tradition puts Mars in [detriment](/astrology/detriment/) in Taurus, because the sign faces Scorpio, one of the two signs Mars rules in the tradition. The planet that likes to act sits in a sign that likes to keep, so it acts by keeping: it holds a position, refuses to be hurried, and wins by still being there. That's a different strength, not a lesser one.

[Venus](/astrology/venus/), the ruler of Taurus, has a say in what moves this Mars, and its sign and house show what you'll work hardest for. With Venus in the picture, desire and effort tend to travel together: you push hardest for what you value or enjoy.

A typical visit lasts about 42 days; a retrograde in Taurus can stretch it to 205. If you were born inside one of those loops, I read the drive as one that turns things over inwardly before it moves ([natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)). In the births of 1930 to 2009, about 1 person in 13 has Mars in Taurus. Everyone born within a few weeks of you shares it; your house says which ground you hold, and that makes it personal. [Mars in Aries](/astrology/mars-in-aries/), just before, is all start; [Mars in Gemini](/astrology/mars-in-gemini/), just after, divides its energy between several tracks. Taurus is where Mars learns to stay on one.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, this Mars is steady and physical. You show desire with time, presence and touch more than with speeches, and once you've chosen someone, you don't make it complicated. Slowness is part of the package: you may take a long while to make a move, and longer still to make a fuss. The difficulty is the other face of that patience. When something hurts, you can hold your position for days, and a partner may find you very hard to shift once you've dug in. Someone who doesn't push, and who leaves you time to come round, usually finds that you do.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, Mars in Taurus is the one who keeps going. Long projects, physical work, building, crafts, the kitchen, the garden, anything that ends in a result you can hold, suit a drive that likes to see what it has made. You're rarely the fastest to start and rarely the first to give up. What brings out the resistance is being rushed: deadlines dropped on you without warning, plans that change every week. Given a clear goal and the freedom to set your own pace, this Mars finishes what others abandon halfway.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Inertia. Starting can feel like pushing a heavy door, so what matters may wait until conditions are just right, which they rarely are. Anger, once it's there, stays a while; a grievance can settle in like a piece of furniture. And the strength of not moving can turn into refusing to move when moving would help you most.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Start small and physical: ten minutes of the job, not the whole job. Walk, lift, dig, knead: this Mars thinks better when the body is busy. Set your own deadlines a little ahead of the real ones, so the slow start has room. When you notice you're holding a position, ask whether you still want what you're defending. And let some of the effort be for pleasure: Venus rules this sign, and a Mars that's allowed to enjoy its work can keep going for years.
