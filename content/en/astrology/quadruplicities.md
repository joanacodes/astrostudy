Where a triplicity is a triangle, a quadruplicity is a cross: four signs of one modality, each a quarter of the circle from the next. It's the shape behind the zodiac's hardest aspects, and an old way of naming how the seasons turn.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Join the four cardinal signs and you draw a cross through the zodiac; the fixed and mutable signs draw two more, each turned one sign further round.

- **The cardinal cross:** Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn.
- **The fixed cross:** Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius.
- **The mutable cross:** Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces.

*Quadruplicity* simply means a group of four. These are the same groups as [the three modalities](/astrology/three-modalities/), which describe how the signs move, starting, holding or adapting; the older word looks at the shape instead, as [triplicities](/astrology/triplicities/) name the triangles of the elements. Each cross holds one sign of every element, and alternates between active and receptive signs.

Ptolemy divided the signs a little differently, into four kinds rather than three. Cancer and Capricorn, where the Sun turns at the solstices, he called solstitial, and Aries and Libra, at the equinoxes, equinoctial: the two halves of the modern cardinal cross. The fixed signs he called solid, because the weather a season brings is felt most firmly while the Sun is in them. And the mutable signs he called bicorporeal, double-bodied, because each shares the end of one season and the beginning of the next.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Signs of one cross sit 90° or 180° apart, so planets in them tend to make [squares](/astrology/square/) and [oppositions](/astrology/opposition/). In the system I built for my readings, a square relates two functions through constructive friction and an opposition through polarity, and neither is failure or enmity. Inside a cross, both come at once, which is what gives these patterns their strength:

- **The T-square:** two planets in opposition, both square a third ([T-square](/astrology/t-square/)).
- **The grand cross:** four planets in two oppositions, each square its neighbours ([Grand cross](/astrology/grand-cross/)).

In the count behind this site, about 3 people in 10 born between 1930 and 2009 have a T-square among the ten planets, and about 1 in 80 a grand cross, with orbs of 8° when the Sun or Moon is involved and 6° otherwise. The count leaves out the angles, Chiron, Lilith and the nodes, and a wider or tighter orb changes it a great deal.

The slow planets travel the crosses too. Uranus, Neptune and Pluto spend years in each sign, so two of them can stand in the same cross, square or opposite, for everyone born over several years. That's a generation's pattern more than a personal one, and it becomes personal through the houses and through contacts with the faster planets ([Personal, social and generational planets](/astrology/personal-social-and-generational-planets/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

When I find a T-square or a grand cross, I look first at which cross it's built on, because the modality says how the tension tends to move. On the cardinal cross, it pulls a person into action on several fronts at once, starting one thing after another to ease the pressure. On the fixed cross, it holds: a standoff that can become great endurance. On the mutable cross, it scatters and adapts, changing direction to relieve the strain. The planets involved say what the tension is about, and the houses, which need [the birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/), say where in life it's felt.

I never read these patterns as doom. Friction is what develops a chart, and the people who carry a cross often become very good at carrying things. The empty arm of a T-square, the sign opposite its focal planet, is often read as the way out: the direction that eases the strain when it's lived on purpose.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A grand cross is a curse.”** It's a great deal of friction held in balance. Effort, not luck, decides what it becomes.
- **“Quadruplicities and modalities are different things.”** They're the same groups of four signs. One word names the pace, the other the shape.
- **“The cardinal signs were always one group.”** Ptolemy split them: Cancer and Capricorn solstitial, Aries and Libra equinoctial.
- **“Squares inside a cross mean a life of fights.”** They describe friction between functions, and friction is how things get made.
