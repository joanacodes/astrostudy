Mercury in the 2nd house keeps a running valuation: what something is worth, what it costs, what it could become with a little work. Sooner or later the same question turns inward, towards what your own skills and ideas are worth.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) holds what you gather and keep once you've arrived: possessions and money, skills, the body's resources, and the sense of worth that goes with them. It's succedent, one of the houses that consolidate what the angles begin ([angular, succedent and cadent houses](/astrology/angular-succedent-and-cadent-houses/)), and its natural sign is Taurus. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) here makes value a subject of thought. You compare, price, budget and plan, often for the pleasure of it, and you tend to learn skills you can actually use.

I don't read this as a forecast about money: a chart can't tell you what you'll earn. I read it as a habit of mind. You think things through in terms of resources, and your knowledge, your skills and your words are among the resources you have. Your sense of self-worth may be tied to what you know, which is a strength when you know a great deal and a strain when you're learning something new.

I divide the houses with Placidus, and there a 2nd-house Mercury was some way below the eastern horizon when you were born, a few hours from rising. The size of each house in Placidus depends on your latitude and the time of day, so the boundary between the 1st and 2nd, or the 2nd and 3rd, moves with the minutes. A Mercury near one of those cusps needs an exact time to be placed at all ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

[Mercury in the 1st house](/astrology/mercury-in-1st-house/) puts the mind in your manner; [Mercury in the 3rd house](/astrology/mercury-in-3rd-house/) sends it out into the neighbourhood. In the 2nd it stays home and takes stock. Add a Taurus Mercury and sign and house speak the same language ([Mercury in Taurus](/astrology/mercury-in-taurus/)).

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You talk about values, in both senses: what matters to you, and what things cost. Shared plans, budgets and the practical side of life together are conversations you're willing to have, which partners often find reassuring. Gifts may be thought out more than wrapped: the useful thing, chosen after research. The risk is reducing a feeling to a calculation. Saying what someone is worth to you, in words that have nothing to do with value for money, is a gift this Mercury can learn to give.

## At work {#at-work}

Trading, buying, selling, pricing, bookkeeping, valuation, negotiation, craft, teaching a practical skill: work where knowing what things are worth is the job. You're often the one who notices that a process wastes time or materials, and who can explain a cost clearly. Your knowledge is a resource, and you're right to treat it as one: name it, keep it current, and don't give it away by reflex.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Measuring yourself by your output of ideas, or by how much you know compared with others. Turning every choice into a cost-benefit sum, including the ones that should be made with the heart. Holding on to information as if it were a possession. And a quiet habit of underpricing your own skills while you carefully price everyone else's.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Write down what you know how to do, and read the list when you doubt your worth. Separate value from price: the walk, the conversation, the afternoon with a friend have no figure and still count. Share what you know with someone who's learning; it rarely costs you anything and often comes back. When a decision is about people, notice if you're doing sums, and let a different question lead: *what would I want, if nothing had to add up?*
