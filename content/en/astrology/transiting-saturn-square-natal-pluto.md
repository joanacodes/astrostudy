Saturn squares the degree your Pluto held at birth about every fifteen years, and the season has a recognisable feel: pressure on whatever you've been holding together by force. Of all the Saturn–Pluto transits, it's the one everyone meets first, and among those met most often.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) keeps structures standing: commitments, routines, the long effort that holds a life together. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) transforms at the root, clearing what has become unsustainable so that strength can return. A [square](/astrology/square/) sets them at right angles, a constructive friction that pushes for adaptation. In this [transit](/astrology/transits/), structures held up by sheer will tend to show the strain: the arrangement you keep running by never resting, the control you keep because letting go feels unsafe, the loyalty you maintain long after it stopped being mutual.

Friction isn't failure, and the square doesn't ask you to tear anything down. It asks you to notice where force has replaced foundations, and to rebuild one of them properly. Some things need more structure, some need less grip, and the hard part is telling which. Many people find the season tiring; the effort usually turns into a stronger, simpler arrangement.

The house of your Pluto shows where the pressure sits: in your 4th, family patterns and what you carry at home; in your 11th, your place in a group and the power you hold there. That house needs your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Within my orbs, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the square lasts about nine months as a rule. Saturn's yearly retrograde gives a little over half of them three exact hits over about nine and a half months; a single hit can bring it down to three weeks.

The two squares of each round come about fifteen years apart, and nobody waits long for the first: everyone born between 1930 and 2009 had it by about 15, most well before. Those first ones fall in childhood, which I don't read ([children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)); adults meet the later ones, five or six by 80 in all. Pluto's slowness keeps a year group together. Born in 1975, the squares came at about 14, 29 and 43, and the next is due at about 58, in 2033; born in 1985, at about 6, 21 and 36, then about 50. Born in 1955, inside the [Saturn square Pluto](/astrology/saturn-square-pluto/) of 1955–56, the first came within a year of birth, and another close to the Saturn return, at about 30.

Between the two squares of a round, Saturn crosses your Pluto ([transiting Saturn conjunct natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-saturn-conjunct-natal-pluto/)) and opposes it ([transiting Saturn opposite natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-saturn-opposite-natal-pluto/)); [transiting Saturn sextile natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-saturn-sextile-natal-pluto/) and [transiting Saturn trine natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-saturn-trine-natal-pluto/) sit two or three years either side of each square.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Find the place where you're holding something up by force alone, and give it a proper support: a routine, an agreement, a load shared with someone else.

Let go of one form of control that no longer protects anything. Pluto tends to return strength once the grip loosens.

Pace yourself. A square is a season of effort, and rest is part of the structure, not a break from it.
