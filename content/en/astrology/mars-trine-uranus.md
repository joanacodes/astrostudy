Mars trine Uranus lets the way you act and the way you change keep the same easy rhythm. The two share an element, so a new method rarely needs a fight before you try it, and independence comes as a matter of course.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) turns wanting into doing: initiative, assertion, effort, the courage to compete and to finish. [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) differentiates and reforms: it experiments, frees what is stuck and finds original solutions. A [trine](/astrology/trine/) relates them through flow, each reinforcing the other: Mars gives a new idea legs, and Uranus keeps effort from going stale.

In plain words, you change course easily and without drama. You may be quick to try a new tool, to work out your own method, or to take a step others hesitate over, and you rarely need permission to do things your way. Effort feels lighter when there's something new in it. People often notice how naturally you adapt; you may not, because it costs you so little.

That's also the trine's quiet catch. Ease is not inevitability: the readiness to start something new doesn't finish it, and a trine left to itself can drift from one fresh idea to the next. Its best years come when you aim it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

With both planets in one element ([the four elements](/astrology/four-elements/)), the element sets the style. In fire the inventiveness is bold and immediate; in air it works through ideas, talk and systems; in earth it shows as practical improvements; in water, as a feel for when to change course. The houses say where it's put to use: Mars in the 2nd trine Uranus in the 10th, say, might earn its living from a new way of working.

Count every birth from 1930 to 2009 and about 1 person in 15 has this trine within 6°. Close to exact, adaptability is likely to be one of your strengths; near the edge of the orb, it's more of a reserve.

Mars moves the trine in and out within a few weeks, about twice every two years, while Uranus, seven years to a sign, sets the sign for a whole span of births. If Mars was retrograde when you were born, the trine may be shared by more people born around you, because Mars can cross the angle more than once; I read that as inventiveness that works inward first ([Natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)). And since Uranus was only found in 1781, by William Herschel, nothing in the old texts covers this trine: what I read in it comes from modern tropical astrology.

The pair's other aspects, beside this one:

- **[Mars conjunct Uranus](/astrology/mars-conjunct-uranus/):** the same two urges fused, more intense and less relaxed than the trine.
- **[Mars sextile Uranus](/astrology/mars-sextile-uranus/):** a lighter, more deliberate cooperation, where you have to reach for the new method.
- **[Mars square Uranus](/astrology/mars-square-uranus/):** where the trine flows, the square jolts, and inventiveness grows out of friction.
- **[Mars opposite Uranus](/astrology/mars-opposite-uranus/):** action and freedom face each other, a balance the trine mostly doesn't have to find.

Between two charts, [Mars trine Uranus in synastry](/astrology/mars-trine-uranus-synastry/) often feels like freedom that works: one person's drive and the other's need for room get along. I read it in both directions.

## In transits {#in-transits}

With 84 years to go round, Uranus trines your natal Mars twice at most in a lifetime, each time staying near the angle for about a year, often in three passes: [Transiting Uranus trine natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-uranus-trine-natal-mars/). It's often a good season for a change you choose rather than one that's forced on you. Anyone born with the trine sees it change shape around 42: at the [Uranus opposition](/astrology/uranus-opposition/), Uranus in the sky makes a sextile to Mars instead.

Mars in the sky trines your natal Uranus twice every two years, for a few days at a time: a light, quick window for trying something new.

Weather, not a forecast, as always with transits: they offer an easier moment for change, never a promise of one.
