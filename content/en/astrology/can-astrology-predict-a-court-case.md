A case can take over a life: the letters, the waiting, the dates moved and moved again. It's no wonder people turn to the sky and ask how it will end. Here is what a chart can and can't do while you wait.

## The short answer {#the-short-answer}

No. No chart, transit or horary question can tell you who will win, what a court will decide, how long it will take, or whether to settle, appeal or drop it. Ongoing legal proceedings are on my list of [what I won't read](/how-i-read/), whichever side you're on.

Your case belongs with a lawyer, who can read what a chart never can: the evidence, the law and the procedure.

## Why it matters {#why-it-matters}

Astrology has judged lawsuits before. In horary astrology, where a chart is cast for the moment a question is asked, William Lilly gave the [7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) to "the defendant in a law-suit", along with public enemies and the opposing party in a war (*Christian Astrology*, 1647), and horary astrologers ruled on who would prevail ([horary astrology](/astrology/horary-astrology/)). Some still do. I read the 7th house as the people across the table from you, partners and rivals alike, and I don't judge trials.

The reasons are practical.

- **A chart can't read a case.** Outcomes turn on evidence, law, procedure and people. None of that is in the sky.
- **Legal time doesn't follow the sky.** A case has its own steps and deadlines, and only your lawyer can tell you what they are. Waiting for a "better" transit is never a reason to miss one.
- **False confidence is expensive, and so is false fear.** A reading that promises a win can lead someone to refuse a fair offer; one that predicts a loss can push them to give up a good case.
- **The other side has a chart too.** Reading an opponent's or a judge's chart would mean reading someone's chart behind their back, which I never do ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

What a chart can describe is you, under pressure: how you handle conflict (Mars), how you endure a long process (Saturn), how you take in complicated information (Mercury), what steadies you while you wait (the Moon). That's weather, and it can help you look after yourself through a hard season.

The sky gets blamed for legal trouble in other ways too. [Mercury retrograde](/astrology/mercury-retrograde/) is often held responsible for delays and lost paperwork, and some websites call Jupiter the planet of the law. A settlement is as good as its terms, whatever Mercury is doing, and Jupiter describes growth and meaning, never a verdict in anyone's favour. Questions about having a child get the same answer from me ([can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/)), and so do questions about money ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)).

## What to do instead {#what-to-do-instead}

- **For your case:** a lawyer: a solicitor in England and Wales, an *avocat* in France.
- **If you can't afford one:** ask about legal aid. In England and Wales, legal aid can help with the costs of legal advice, depending on the kind of case and your means, and it works differently in Scotland and Northern Ireland (GOV.UK). In France, the *aide juridictionnelle* can cover all or part of a lawyer's and court costs for people with modest means (Service-public.fr).
- **For the meetings themselves:** write your questions down before each one, and ask your lawyer to explain anything you don't follow. That does more for your case than any chart.
- **For the strain of it:** the people around you, and a doctor or a therapist if the stress is taking over your sleep or your days ([when to see a professional instead](/astrology/when-to-see-a-professional-instead/)).
- **For understanding yourself through it:** a reading of your own chart can sit beside legal advice, as a way to think about how you meet pressure. It never replaces that advice.
