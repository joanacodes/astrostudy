Every 18.6 years or so, the North Node, moving backwards, comes back to the top of your birth chart: the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/), where the zodiac crossed the meridian above the horizon at your birth. That degree needs your birth time to the minute, because ten minutes of clock time move it by about two and a half degrees wherever you were born, the whole width of the window I give this transit ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What changes {#what-changes}

The Midheaven is the part of the chart that faces outwards: the role you take, the responsibility you carry, the contribution other people can see and rely on. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) marks a capacity that only grows with practice. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) runs the two together, so under this [transit](/astrology/transits/) the practice happens in public: taking on a role you've watched others hold, speaking for your own work, being answerable for something that carries your name.

It can feel exposed, and exposure isn't failure. Two confusions are worth avoiding: visibility with value, which is the Midheaven's, and unfamiliar with right, which is the node's. The month gives a newer kind of contribution somewhere to start. It doesn't guarantee recognition, a title or success, and it passes no verdict on the work you already do.

Across the chart, the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) crosses your [IC](/astrology/imum-coeli/): your foundations, the support and belonging you've relied on for years. That ground stays yours, and the step up is easier with it under you.

In the houses, which I draw in Placidus, the crossing is the moment the North Node leaves your 10th for your 9th ([North Node transiting the 10th house](/astrology/north-node-transit-10th-house/)) and the South Node your 4th for your 3rd: the end of its months in the house of public life, the start of its months in the house of study and belief. Born with the North Node in your 10th, you meet this transit within a year or two after each [nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/), once the returning node has worked its way back to your Midheaven.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

My orbs run from 1½° before exact to 1° after, and the true node is usually through them in about a month, with a single exact hit nineteen times in twenty. A quick pass, one in ten, is done within 25 days; one in twenty-five touches the Midheaven three times over about 100 days; and one in ten stays more than 100 days, caught by an eclipse season.

An eclipse season can also put an eclipse on the axis itself. With a Midheaven at 8°30′ Scorpio, the node comes within orb on 26 March 2032 and leaves on 5 July, exact on 22 June; a month after it arrives, the total lunar eclipse of 25 April 2032 takes the Moon into the Earth's shadow at 6°00′ Scorpio, two and a half degrees from that Midheaven. That's close enough for the eclipse to count in my reading ([eclipses on your natal planets](/astrology/eclipses-on-your-natal-planets/)).

There is one in each round of the nodes, so four or five by 80, for everyone. The sextile, square and trine last as long ([Transiting North Node sextile natal Midheaven](/astrology/transiting-north-node-sextile-natal-midheaven/), [Transiting North Node square natal Midheaven](/astrology/transiting-north-node-square-natal-midheaven/), [Transiting North Node trine natal Midheaven](/astrology/transiting-north-node-trine-natal-midheaven/)), but my forecast leaves them out: on your Midheaven it follows the node only by this conjunction and by the opposition, when the North Node reaches your IC ([Transiting North Node opposite natal Midheaven](/astrology/transiting-north-node-opposite-natal-midheaven/)).

The dates here are the true node's. The mean node, never more than a degree or two away, would shift the window by weeks, and I look at both ([mean node and true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/)).

## What helps {#what-helps}

Put into one sentence what you'd like your work to stand for, and say it to someone who will remember it.

Take on one responsibility in view of others that you'd usually leave to someone with more experience.

Ask for feedback early, while the new role is still easy to adjust.

Keep the foundations fed: sleep, home, the people who knew you before your work did.
