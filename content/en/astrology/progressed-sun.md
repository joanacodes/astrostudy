Your Sun sign never changes. Your progressed Sun does, slowly: it walks forward about a degree for each year you live, and over a long life it crosses into two or three new signs. It's the hour hand of the progressed chart.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

[Secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/) take the sky one day after birth for each year of life, and the progressed Sun is where the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) stood on that day. The real Sun covers about 57′ of arc a day in early July and 61′ in early January, because Earth moves fastest when it's closest to the Sun, at the start of the year. The progressed Sun keeps the same pace per year, so it starts a little quicker for someone born in January than for someone born in July: by 30 it has moved between 28½° and 30½°, by 90 between 86° and 91½°.

That pace makes its changes of sign rare and easy to date. Count the degrees between your natal Sun and the end of its sign, and you have roughly the age at which the progressed Sun moved on. Born with the Sun at 22° of a sign, you were about 8; born at 3°, about 27. After that a new sign comes about every 30 years.

Beside it, the [progressed Moon](/astrology/progressed-moon/) runs about thirteen times faster, and the angle between the two marks the phases of a cycle of about 29½ years ([Progressed lunar phases](/astrology/progressed-lunar-phases/)).

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

A program draws the progressed chart around your natal one. By hand, find the day as many days after your birth as you are years old, and read the Sun's position for your birth time in an ephemeris. For a rough idea, no ephemeris is needed: add your age, in degrees, to your natal Sun.

Then I read three things:

- **Its sign.** A new sign changes the manner in which you go after what matters to you. It adds to your Sun sign and never replaces it.
- **Its house.** The area of life where that purpose gathers. In Placidus, the house system I use, houses differ in size, so the progressed Sun can cross one in fifteen years and need forty for another.
- **Its aspects.** Progressed aspects are usually counted within a degree of exact, and at a degree a year that means about two years in all: one to come in, one to leave.

It also turns to face its own birth position. The progressed Sun is 30° from the natal Sun between 29½ and 31½, 60° between 59 and 63, and 90° between 88½ and 94, earlier for a January birthday, later for a July one.

The birth time matters less for the Sun than for anything else in a progressed chart, and still decides a great deal. An hour's error moves the timing of its aspects to the planets by only about two weeks. Its house, though, and its aspects to the Ascendant and Midheaven, hang on the angles, which an hour moves by about 15°. That's why I don't read progressions without a birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

The Sun organises identity, purpose and direction. Progressed, it describes how those ripen: from the inside, over decades rather than months. When it enters a new sign, I read a change in style more than in substance: someone born with the Sun in Virgo whose progressed Sun reaches Libra may find that what they set out to do now needs other people in it.

Its aspects are among the longest chapters progressions offer. The progressed Sun reaching natal Saturn can mark two years in which what you aim for and what you're responsible for have to be worked out together. Saturn, Chiron and Lilith held best in my notebook, so the years when the progressed Sun meets one of them get my closest reading ([The three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)).

Its arc has a second life. The distance the progressed Sun has travelled since birth is the arc that [solar arc directions](/astrology/solar-arc-directions/) add to every other point of the chart.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

The progressed Sun is symbolic time, not a position in today's sky, and what it describes moves at the speed of a life. It tells you which theme is maturing, never what will happen in a given month.

Its slowness also makes it easy to over-read. A two-year aspect can absorb almost anything that happens in those two years, and memory is generous with patterns after the fact. I hold it to the same test as the rest of the timing: it counts when [transits](/astrology/transits/) or other progressions point to the same part of the chart at the same time.

Some stretches of life it hardly speaks. The progressed Sun can go several years without an exact aspect to any planet, and those quiet years are part of the picture, not a fault in the method.
