Mars square Uranus is drive with a restless streak. The part of you that pushes ahead and the part that wants things done differently meet at a right angle, so effort and change keep interrupting each other, and inventing as they do.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) pushes ahead: initiative, assertion, effort, the energy to compete and to hold a line. [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) innovates and liberates: it differentiates, experiments and reforms whatever has become too rigid. A [square](/astrology/square/) joins them through constructive friction. Neither gives way: your effort keeps meeting the urge to change course, and that urge keeps needing effort to become real.

In plain words, you may start fast and stop abruptly, or feel a sudden need for room just when things settle. Routines others find comfortable can feel tight to you; instructions you can't see a reason for are hard to follow. None of that is a flaw. The friction tends to make you very good at spotting what doesn't work, and the square's lesson is to aim that: to reform a system rather than only break off from it, and to finish the change you start.

Over time, many people with this square become the ones who fix what others had stopped noticing.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The square's two signs usually share a modality, and in the fixed signs it can be striking: Mars in Scorpio, its traditional sign, square Uranus in Aquarius, Uranus's modern one, has both at home by different reckonings, and the friction is between two kinds of determination. In cardinal signs, the square tends to show in how you start; in mutable ones, in how often you change direction. The houses show where it's felt: Mars in the 4th square Uranus in the 7th, say, could set the energy you put into home against a partner's need for freedom, or your own.

About 1 person in 15 carries this square, within 6°. Tight, within a degree or two, the start-and-stop rhythm runs through much of what you do; near 6°, it's mainly felt when you're hemmed in.

Mars sets the timing: it makes the square twice in each two-year cycle with Uranus and holds it for a few weeks of births, while Uranus, seven years to a sign, hardly moves. In my notebook I checked Mars and Uranus by sign and house like everything else; neither stood out the way Saturn, Chiron and Lilith did, while overall about a third held. So this square is a starting point I check with you, not a conclusion ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

The pair's other aspects, for comparison:

- **[Mars conjunct Uranus](/astrology/mars-conjunct-uranus/):** no corner to turn; acting and changing are one move, which the square splits in two.
- **[Mars sextile Uranus](/astrology/mars-sextile-uranus/):** invention is invited rather than forced, an opening you choose to take.
- **[Mars trine Uranus](/astrology/mars-trine-uranus/):** the same originality with the friction gone, flowing in one element.
- **[Mars opposite Uranus](/astrology/mars-opposite-uranus/):** the urge for change faces you across the chart, often arriving through someone else.

In a couple or a partnership, [Mars square Uranus in synastry](/astrology/mars-square-uranus-synastry/) can be exciting and unsettling at once: one person's push keeps meeting the other's need for room. Saying so helps, and I read it from each side.

## In transits {#in-transits}

Most people meet [Transiting Uranus square natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-uranus-square-natal-mars/) twice in a long life, once on each side of Uranus's 84-year round. Each time it holds for a year or so, often with three exact passes: a season of impatience with what's fixed, and of energy for changing it. Born with the square, you also meet it at the [Uranus opposition](/astrology/uranus-opposition/), around 42, when Uranus squares your Mars from the other side.

Mars in the sky squares your natal Uranus twice every two years, a few days each time: brief fits of impatience, or of energy to change one small thing.

What these transits describe is restlessness that can be put to work, never an event: weather, in other words, not a forecast.
