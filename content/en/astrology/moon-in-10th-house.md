With the Moon in the 10th house, your feelings go public. What you need, and the way you care, tends to shape the work you're known for, and people often glimpse your inner life in the role you play.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 10th house](/astrology/10th-house/) is the most public part of a chart: your standing, your vocation, the responsibilities you shoulder and the mark you hope to leave. Angular and topped by the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/), it has Capricorn as its natural sign. With the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) here, the chart's need for safety and belonging is worked out in view of others. Many people with this Moon end up in work that looks after people, or find that the public role they take on has the shape of care.

When you were born, the Moon was near the top of the sky, about to cross the meridian. In Placidus, the house system I use, the Midheaven opens the 10th, so a Moon a little past it is a 9th-house Moon, and whole sign houses can put the Midheaven in another house altogether ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)).

The 10th faces the 4th across the chart, the house that echoes the Moon's own sign. A Moon here stands as far from that private house as a chart allows, in its most public place, which is why I read it as feelings that look for a role in the world: care given to many, not only to a household.

Before it in the round comes the [Moon in the 9th house](/astrology/moon-in-9th-house/), which finds home in the far and the meaningful; after it, the [Moon in the 11th house](/astrology/moon-in-11th-house/), which finds it among friends.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Your public life and your private feelings are closely tied, so someone who takes your work seriously, and understands what it costs you emotionally, matters a great deal. You may bring home the responsibility you carry outside, or look to a partner as the one place where you don't have to perform. Being appreciated for what you do matters to you; being loved only for what you achieve doesn't feel like enough. A partner who knows both the public you and the tired one at the end of the day gives this Moon room to breathe.

## At work {#at-work}

You're often drawn to work with a caring or public face: social care, teaching, hospitality, food, housing, public service, anything that tends to people's needs. Your mood can show in your professional life, and the reverse is true too: a good day at work lifts you, a hard one follows you home. Recognition matters, though less as status than as proof that your care has landed. You can be a reassuring figure at the head of a team, the one people bring their worries to.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Your feelings can become public property. When your role is part of how you feel safe, a setback at work can feel like a personal failure, and a reputation can feel like something you must constantly guard. You may give so much care in your public role that little is left for yourself or for home. And it can be hard to let anyone see you without the role on, even the people closest to you.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Keep a part of your life that has nothing to do with your work or your reputation: a hobby, a friendship, a place where nobody knows your title. Separate the job from your worth, especially on bad days. Let some of your care flow inward. And choose roles where looking after people doesn't mean disappearing behind them, because this Moon is at its best when the public work feeds you as well.

On my list, the Moon's house was one line among many, ticked or crossed like the rest ([my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)); the placements that pulled far ahead were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, and of everything I checked roughly a third held. I read a 10th-house Moon accordingly, as one public thread of your story, and a gentle one.
