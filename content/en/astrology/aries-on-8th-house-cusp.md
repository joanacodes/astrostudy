Aries on the 8th house cusp means the most private kind of sharing starts with a decision to act. Trust, intimacy, joint money, debts and the moments that change you all live in this house, and here someone has to move first.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [8th house](/astrology/8th-house/) is what happens once you let someone in, and the sign on its cusp is how you go about it ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)). [Aries](/astrology/aries/) goes about it by committing: trust shown by doing something rather than saying it, a joint account opened because it made sense that week, a hard conversation started before it had time to fester. In a crisis your instinct is to get moving, and the waiting is the part that costs you. Libra sits opposite, on the 2nd, so the pattern can surprise people: easygoing and fair about your own money, brisk and clear about what's shared. You tend to want the terms of any joint arrangement written plainly, who owes what and who decides, because vagueness in this house feels worse to you than a frank disagreement.

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) rules your 8th by its cusp, and it's also the traditional ruler of Scorpio, the 8th's natural sign. Modern astrologers hand Scorpio to Pluto; I read both. Whichever way you count, the planet of effort speaks for this house, so its condition matters more than usual: Mars at home in Aries or Scorpio, exalted in Capricorn, or less at ease, by tradition's account, in Libra, Taurus or Cancer. Scorpio itself nearly always opens your 3rd in these charts, which gives Mars a share of your everyday words too: the conversations most people avoid, about money, trust or what's owed, tend to be the ones you start. Mars's house shows where intimacy and shared stakes are worked out: the [1st](/astrology/8th-house-ruler-in-1st-house/), [2nd](/astrology/8th-house-ruler-in-2nd-house/), [3rd](/astrology/8th-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/), [4th](/astrology/8th-house-ruler-in-4th-house/), [5th](/astrology/8th-house-ruler-in-5th-house/), [6th](/astrology/8th-house-ruler-in-6th-house/), [7th](/astrology/8th-house-ruler-in-7th-house/), [8th](/astrology/8th-house-ruler-in-8th-house/), [9th](/astrology/8th-house-ruler-in-9th-house/), [10th](/astrology/8th-house-ruler-in-10th-house/), [11th](/astrology/8th-house-ruler-in-11th-house/) or [12th](/astrology/8th-house-ruler-in-12th-house/).

Aries opens the 8th in about 1 birth in 10 in both cities, just as it opens the 6th, the 8th's mirror across the Descendant. These are Placidus cusps, the system I use. Virgo usually rises with it, so the careful, exact manner people meet first often hides how directly you deal with what's shared. When Libra rises instead, in about 1 London chart in 5, Aries opens the 7th as well, and one planet rules both partnership and what partnership holds ([Aries on the 7th house cusp](/astrology/aries-on-7th-house-cusp/)); now and then it runs on to the 9th instead ([Aries on the 9th house cusp](/astrology/aries-on-9th-house-cusp/)). Astrologers call a sign on two cusps duplicated ([duplicated signs](/astrology/duplicated-signs/)). The reverse, a sign on no cusp, never leaves Aries in the 8th: when it happens, in about 1 London birth in 6, Aries lies in another house, most often the 12th, 11th or 1st ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)).

The old books filled the 8th with death and inheritance. Inheritance still belongs with shared resources; death I leave out of every reading, because no chart can tell when or how anyone will die ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)). And since the cusp comes from the birth time, its degree shifts with every few minutes of it: I don't read a chart without the time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
