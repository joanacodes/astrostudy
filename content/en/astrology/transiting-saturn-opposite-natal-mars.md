At the far point of their round, some fifteen years after the conjunction, Saturn stands across the zodiac from your Mars. Your drive meets resistance from outside, from a person, an institution or a set of terms, and the season is about learning where your push ends and someone else's limit begins.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) asserts, competes and defends; [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) holds the rules, the timetable and the long-term consequences. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) relates them through polarity, so a tension you might have carried inside shows up across the table, in another person.

It often looks like competition, negotiation or authority: a boss who slows a project, a rival who plays by stricter rules, an institution with procedures, a partner whose pace isn't yours. Competition isn't hostility, and this transit is rarely a fight to be won. It's a measure to be taken: of your strength, of the other side's legitimate limits, of what's worth pushing for.

Boundaries are part of Mars's job, and the opposition tests them both ways: where you've let others push past yours, and where you've leaned on theirs. Saying no clearly, and hearing no without taking it as an insult, are the skills it builds. Disputes sometimes come up, and I give no forecast of how they end ([can astrology predict a court case?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-a-court-case/)).

With your Mars in the 10th and Saturn crossing your 4th ([Saturn transiting the 4th house](/astrology/saturn-transit-4th-house/)), ambition meets the needs of home and family, and the push for standing has to make room for them. Without your birth time, the line from your 10th house to your 4th can't be drawn, and the transit stays unread ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Born with [Mars opposite Saturn](/astrology/mars-opposite-saturn/), you've had a lifetime's practice at effort that meets a counterweight, which tends to make this season more familiar than frightening.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Saturn opposes your Mars three times over about nine and a half months whenever its retrograde loop lands on the point, as happens in a little over half of its [transits](/astrology/transits/). I read the three passes as one season, not three events. Measured with my orbs, 1½° before exact and 1° after, the whole season usually runs to about nine months; the quickest version, a single crossing, takes three weeks.

Everyone meets this opposition two or three times by 80, once in each round. It sits halfway between two passes of [transiting Saturn conjunct natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-saturn-conjunct-natal-mars/): [transiting Saturn sextile natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-saturn-sextile-natal-mars/), [transiting Saturn square natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-saturn-square-natal-mars/) and [transiting Saturn trine natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-saturn-trine-natal-mars/) lead up to it, and come again in reverse on the way back.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Negotiate before you push. Find out what the other side needs; the agreement is often quicker than the fight.

Pick the battles worth having. Energy spent on every point runs out under this opposition; energy spent on the one that matters lasts.

Keep your own aim in sight. Meeting someone else's limits doesn't mean dropping what you want: it means finding the route both sides can live with.
