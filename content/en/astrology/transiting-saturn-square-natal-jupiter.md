Twice in each round of about 29½ years, Saturn squares the degree your Jupiter held at birth. Expansion meets a limit: the plan too big for the hours, the belief too wide for the facts, the promise that now has to be kept.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) grows things: knowledge, plans, faith in what's possible. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) asks what can be sustained. A [square](/astrology/square/), 90°, relates them through constructive friction, growth and limit rubbing until each adjusts to the other. Friction isn't failure, and here it is mostly a question of scale.

It often shows when something has outgrown its structure. A project taken on with enthusiasm now needs administration; a belief meets an inconvenient fact; generosity meets the limits of your time. Expansion isn't excess, but under this square the difference becomes visible. Tradition puts Jupiter in its fall in Capricorn, a sign Saturn rules, as if the two had always argued about size, and the argument is usually settled by a better plan rather than a smaller dream. Beliefs aren't dogmatism either: the square can loosen a conviction you had stopped examining. In study, it's often the point where enthusiasm for a subject has to become the discipline of practising it.

No loss, missed chance or failure is foretold here, and financial decisions are on the list of things I won't read ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

With your Jupiter in the 10th, ambitions meet the limits of your own time and energy, because Saturn is then usually crossing your 1st ([Saturn transiting the 1st house](/astrology/saturn-transit-1st-house/)) or your 7th. Those houses depend on your birth time, without which I don't read the transit ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). If your chart has [Jupiter square Saturn](/astrology/jupiter-square-saturn/), the argument about scale is an old one for you, and the transit gives it a deadline.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

With my orbs, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the square usually lasts about nine months. In a little over half of Saturn's [transits](/astrology/transits/) it brings three exact passes over about nine and a half months; a single quick crossing can take three weeks.

The first square depends on your exact birth date. For everyone born on 1 January of a year ending in 5, from 1935 to 2005, it came between about 12 and 14; across all birth dates from 1930 to 2009, anywhere from the first weeks of life to about 15½, with 7 in the middle. Two a round then make five or six squares by 80, for everyone.

The first square of a round falls between [transiting Saturn sextile natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-saturn-sextile-natal-jupiter/) and [transiting Saturn trine natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-saturn-trine-natal-jupiter/), about seven years after [transiting Saturn conjunct natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-saturn-conjunct-natal-jupiter/); the second follows [transiting Saturn opposite natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-saturn-opposite-natal-jupiter/), about seven years before the conjunction comes round again.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Scale the plan, not the hope. Keep what you're aiming for and cut what you're promising this year.

Finish one thing before starting the next. Under this square, completion is what earns the right to expand.

Check one belief against the facts, and let it be tested rather than defended.
