Fire, earth, air and water are the oldest grouping in astrology, and the one most people half-know already. They sort the twelve signs into four families of three, and they give you the tone of a chart before you've read a single placement.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Each element gathers three signs, spaced evenly round the zodiac:

- **Fire:** [Aries](/astrology/aries/), [Leo](/astrology/leo/) and [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/). They act on enthusiasm: warmth, initiative, the wish to go and do ([Fire signs](/astrology/fire-signs/)).
- **Earth:** [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/) and [Capricorn](/astrology/capricorn/). They trust what they can touch and check: the practical, the measurable, what lasts ([Earth signs](/astrology/earth-signs/)).
- **Air:** [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/), [Libra](/astrology/libra/) and [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/). They live through ideas and exchange: talk, comparison, the view from a step back ([Air signs](/astrology/air-signs/)).
- **Water:** [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/) and [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/). They read the world through feeling: closeness, intuition, what goes unsaid ([Water signs](/astrology/water-signs/)).

The idea is older than astrology's use of it. In the fifth century BCE, the philosopher Empedocles taught that everything is made of four roots, fire, air, water and earth, and Aristotle later described each one by a pair of qualities: fire hot and dry, air hot and moist, water cold and moist, earth cold and dry. Astrology took up the same four to group its signs.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Go round the zodiac in order and the elements take turns: fire, earth, air, water, then again, three times over. Aries is fire, Taurus earth, Gemini air, Cancer water, Leo fire, and so on to Pisces. That rhythm builds the geometry of the aspects.

- **Same element, 120° apart.** Signs of one element sit four signs apart, so planets in them tend to make a [trine](/astrology/trine/), the aspect of ease. Three planets in the three signs of one element make a [grand trine](/astrology/grand-trine/).
- **Fire with air, earth with water.** These pairs sit two or six signs apart, in sextile or in opposition, and they share a polarity: fire and air are active, earth and water receptive ([Polarity](/astrology/polarity/)).
- **Neighbours.** Signs side by side never share an element, which is part of why the [semi-sextile](/astrology/semi-sextile/) and the quincunx are read as aspects of adjustment.

Each sign also has one of [the three modalities](/astrology/three-modalities/), cardinal, fixed or mutable, and no two signs share both an element and a modality. That's what makes each of the twelve particular: Aries is cardinal fire, Leo fixed fire, Sagittarius mutable fire.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Many astrologers begin by counting: how many planets fall in each element, sometimes with the Ascendant and Midheaven added. The count gives a first impression, a chart heavy in water and light in air, say, before any placement is read ([Element balance and missing elements](/astrology/element-balance-and-missing-elements/)).

I treat that impression as a sketch, never a verdict. In the system I built for my readings, a sign styles a planet and never replaces it, and an element is a style three signs share. A chart full of fire isn't reckless, and a chart with no planets in earth isn't impractical: earth's signs still sit on some of its house cusps, its ruling planets are still somewhere in the chart, and a style that comes less automatically can still be learned. What I look at more closely is where each element falls. An earth Moon and an air Mercury describe someone who needs things solid and thinks in ideas, and both are true at once.

The elements also account for much of the ease and friction in a chart, through the trines between signs of one element and the squares between signs of one modality. And since the houses decide where each element shows up in a life, the full picture needs [the birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Fire and water signs can't get on.”** Elements describe styles, not whom you can love. Two whole charts say far more than two Sun signs ([Sun-sign compatibility, and why it isn't enough](/astrology/sun-sign-compatibility-and-why-it-isnt-enough/)).
- **“A missing element means you lack it.”** It means no planet sits in its signs. Those signs and their rulers are still in your chart.
- **“Water signs are too emotional, earth signs are dull.”** Those are stereotypes, not descriptions. Water reads through feeling and earth through what's real: each is a strength, and neither excuses or condemns anyone.
- **“The four elements are chemistry.”** They're an ancient way of sorting qualities, and in a chart they describe temperaments, not substances.
