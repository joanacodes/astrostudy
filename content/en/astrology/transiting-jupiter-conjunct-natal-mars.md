Every twelve years or so, Jupiter crosses the degree of your natal Mars, and the part of you that acts meets the part that asks what for. It's one of the most energetic seasons Jupiter brings, and the energy arrives with a question attached: what is all this effort serving?

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) initiates: it starts things, makes the effort, asserts what you want and holds your limits. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) widens and looks for meaning. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) runs them as one engine, so for the length of this [transit](/astrology/transits/) the push to act comes with a reason built in, and effort tends to fasten onto something bigger than the task in front of you.

People often take something on: a cause, a training, a venture, a stand at work, a start postponed for years. Courage comes more easily, and so does asking for what you want. The cautions are the usual ones for this pair: courage isn't recklessness, and more energy isn't more time. Taking on as much as the reason deserves, and no more, is the skill of this crossing.

Tradition called Jupiter the greater benefic and Mars the lesser malefic ([benefics and malefics](/astrology/benefics-and-malefics/)), and the old astrologers liked to see the first temper the second. I don't read Mars as harmful; I read it as the part of you that can act. The picture still holds, though: with Jupiter on it, your drive goes looking for a reason worth the push.

With Mars in your 1st house, the crossing comes during [Jupiter transiting the 1st house](/astrology/jupiter-transit-1st-house/), and the push is personal, in how you start things and meet the world. Your Mars's house matters this much because Jupiter shares it while the conjunction lasts. Born with [Mars conjunct Jupiter](/astrology/mars-conjunct-jupiter/), you'll have this season at each [Jupiter return](/astrology/jupiter-return/).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Counted from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the crossing usually lasts about eleven days. Roughly three crossings in ten are slower: Jupiter turns back over your Mars, then forward again, and the season runs to about eight months with three exact hits.

The date comes from your birth date alone, because Mars shifts so little in a day. The meaning comes from the house, and the house from your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)): no birth time, no reading.

Six or seven of these crossings come by 80 for anyone born between 1930 and 2009, spaced about twelve years apart: often enough to compare them, rarely enough for each to open a chapter. Six years after each one, [transiting Jupiter opposite natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-opposite-natal-mars/) holds up what the effort has grown into; on the way there and back come [transiting Jupiter sextile natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-sextile-natal-mars/), [transiting Jupiter square natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-square-natal-mars/) and [transiting Jupiter trine natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-trine-natal-mars/).

## What helps {#what-helps}

Pick the cause before a cause picks you. With Jupiter on Mars, almost any project can look like a calling; choose the one you'd still push for in a dull month.

Start, then set a pace you could keep for a year. The energy of these weeks fades, and the habit is what stays.

Back somebody else's effort too. Mars grows by doing and Jupiter by sharing, and this crossing is often best spent on an aim you hold with others.
