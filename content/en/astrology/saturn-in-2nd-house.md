Ask the 2nd house what you can count on and, with Saturn there, the answer comes slowly and carefully itemised: what you've earned, saved, learned and kept, and underneath it all, what you believe you're worth.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Money, belongings, the skills you could live from and the self-worth beneath them all belong to [the 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/). It's a succedent house, one where things settle and build up, and its natural sign is Taurus. With [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) here, security is something you build rather than assume. You tend to be careful with resources, slow to spend and quick to plan, and your sense of your own value may take longer to settle than your bank balance does. Taurus, the house's natural sign, likes things solid and lasting; Saturn agrees, and together they give a patience with money and possessions that shows its worth over years rather than months.

None of this says how much money you'll have, when, or where to put it. No chart can, and I don't read one for that ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)). What it describes is your relationship with having: patience, caution, and sometimes fear. Across the chart sits the 8th house, what's shared and owed, so with Saturn in the 2nd you'd usually rather rely on your own than on anyone else's.

Placidus, the system I use, draws the 2nd house from the exact time and place of birth; get the hour wrong and Saturn can slide back into the 1st or on into the 3rd ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). The sign Saturn is in, shared with everyone born in the same couple of years, colours the style. The house is what makes this lesson about your own security.

It sits a step on from [Saturn in the 1st house](/astrology/saturn-in-1st-house/), where the work is on the self that shows, and a step before [Saturn in the 3rd house](/astrology/saturn-in-3rd-house/), where it turns to words. In the 2nd, it's about what you keep.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Money talk can be the hardest conversation in a couple, and with Saturn here it's also the most revealing. You may show love through reliability, paying your share, planning ahead, never being a burden, and feel uneasy about receiving gifts or depending on a partner's income. The lesson is that being looked after isn't the same as losing your footing: security can be shared without being surrendered.

## At work {#at-work}

You want work that pays steadily and builds something over time: a trade, a craft, a skill that grows more valuable each year. Undercharging can be the trap here, because naming your worth out loud feels presumptuous. The opposite trap is staying in work you've outgrown simply because it's safe, long after it has stopped teaching you anything.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Scarcity, real or remembered. A sense that there will never quite be enough, however much there is, and guilt about spending on yourself. Worth measured in income, productivity or things you can show. And a habit of saving everything for later, joy included. Sometimes it's time and energy that get hoarded, not only money: saying no to things you'd love because they might cost too much.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Decide what *enough* is, in figures if that helps, and notice when you reach it. Keep your worth and your earnings in separate columns in your own head, even when the world mixes them up. List the skills you've built over the years; they're assets too, and they don't depreciate the way you fear. Spend a little, on purpose, on something whose only use is pleasure. And when security does come, let yourself feel it: this Saturn is good at building and slower at believing in what it has built.
