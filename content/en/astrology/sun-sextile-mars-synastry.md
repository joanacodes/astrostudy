With Sun sextile Mars between two charts, one person's drive is close enough to the other's purpose to help, and far enough to wait for the asking. It's one of the most practical contacts two people can have: things get done between them, once someone says what needs doing.

## What flows {#what-flows}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is a person's sense of where they're going, and the vitality to get there. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) supplies the effort: drive, motivation, the willingness to get on with it. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) relates two functions through opportunity, help that answers when it's asked for; read as one of the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/), it gives the purpose to one person and the effort to the other.

The two signs usually belong to elements that suit each other. A Gemini Mars with a Leo Sun: quick, adaptable energy for someone who wants their plans carried through with flair. A Taurus Mars with a Cancer Sun: patient, steady effort for someone whose aims centre on looking after people.

If it's your Sun, you have an ally for the doing. Mention a goal and the other person is already thinking about how to get there, without taking it from you; their energy makes your plans more real. If yours is the Mars, your drive finds somewhere good to go. You like being useful to their aims, and their appreciation of the effort keeps it coming.

It's a project lead and the colleague who gets things done, friends where one plans the trip and the other gets everyone to the station on time, siblings clearing out the family home together.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

An alliance this convenient can harden into a division of labour: one person decides, the other does, and after a while neither remembers agreeing to it. The Mars person's effort can go unthanked because it's so reliable; the Sun person's aims can start running on someone else's energy. And like every sextile, it needs the asking: help that's never requested sits idle, and so does a drive with no aim offered to it. As I read these two functions, agency is not domination, in either direction.

At the pair's other angles, the effort behaves differently:

- **[Sun conjunct Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mars-synastry/):** the drive sits right on the self; initiative shared at close range, and harder to untangle.
- **[Sun square Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-square-mars-synastry/):** a sign further round; the push arrives unasked, and pace becomes a contest.
- **[Sun trine Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-trine-mars-synastry/):** at 120°, usually in one element; effort and purpose already moving together.
- **[Sun opposite Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-opposite-mars-synastry/):** across the zodiac; the effort faces the purpose, a counterweight more than a helper.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Ask clearly and thank specifically: "can you take this on?" and later "that made the difference" keep the alliance from becoming an assumption. Swap roles now and then, letting the Mars person set an aim and the Sun person do some of the lifting. At work, put the doing on the same list as the deciding, so the effort is seen; at home, the same list works for chores.

About 1 pair of people in 10 is linked by this sextile, one way round or the other, within 4.8°.

Whether a birth fell in the morning or the evening barely changes where either body stood, so the time seldom decides the sextile; it fixes the houses, the areas of each life where the help is given, and without both times I don't read ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). In one person's chart, [Sun sextile Mars](/astrology/sun-sextile-mars/) is an inner alliance between aim and effort; between two people, the alliance has to be negotiated, and thanked.
