Sun opposite Chiron places your tender spot across the chart from your Sun, facing you rather than sitting inside your sense of self. It describes a self that often meets its sore spot in someone else first, and that learns over time that care can flow both ways.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is your identity and your sense of direction: the self you live from. [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) is where something didn't come easily, and where adapting taught you a skill you can share. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) relates them as a polarity: each makes you more aware of the other, and the balance between them is something you keep finding again.

In plain words, the people around you may carry the subject of your tender place. You might find yourself helping someone through exactly what you once found hardest; or someone close might, without meaning to, press on it. Both are ways of meeting it from across the room. The opposition doesn't ask you to choose between being the one who helps and the one who needs help. It asks for both: care that you give, and care that you let in. Saying your own limits out loud, and asking for support in proportion to them, is part of the same skill.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In a chart, it tends to show in close relationships, in caring work, or wherever you meet people at a vulnerable moment. Each sign holds what the other lacks. A Sun in Libra opposite Chiron in Aries wants harmony and keeps meeting the subject of confidence in other people; a Sun in Virgo opposite Chiron in Pisces wants things done properly, and meets in others a tender place that's hard to name. With the Sun in the 1st and Chiron in the 7th, partnership is where the tender place appears, and where a rare way of listening grows.

About 1 person in 24 has the Sun within 8° of the point opposite Chiron, and one quiet detail comes with it: Chiron was retrograde at your birth, since the Sun only ever faces Chiron during its backward months. I read that as a tender subject you think through alone before you share it ([natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)). The closer to exact, the more the seesaw between helping and being helped is felt.

The pair's other aspects bring the tender place closer to home:

- **[Sun conjunct Chiron](/astrology/sun-conjunct-chiron/):** inside your sense of self, not across from it.
- **[Sun sextile Chiron](/astrology/sun-sextile-chiron/):** what you learned is on offer without anyone else having to bring it out.
- **[Sun square Chiron](/astrology/sun-square-chiron/):** the friction comes from the side, in your own plans, rather than through others.
- **[Sun trine Chiron](/astrology/sun-trine-chiron/):** self and tender place in step, with no seesaw at all.

Between two charts, [Sun opposite Chiron in synastry](/astrology/sun-opposite-chiron-synastry/) sets one person's centre across from the other's tender place: recognition, tenderness, and a real chance of pressing on a sore spot. That's why I read it only when both people have said yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Chiron opposite natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-chiron-opposite-natal-sun/) comes at an age that depends on your generation, and lasts as long as Chiron's speed in that stretch allows. It's often read as a time when other people bring an old tender subject back into view, and when asking for help gets easier. The Sun in the sky repeats the angle for a day or two each year, opposite your natal Chiron. Weather, not a forecast: it describes what asks for care, never an illness or an event.
