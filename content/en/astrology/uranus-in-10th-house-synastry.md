Career advice from someone whose Uranus sits in your 10th house rarely sounds like anyone else's. Leave, start your own thing, change field, do it differently: some of it may be reckless, and some of it may be the push you'd been waiting for.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) innovates and reforms: it questions the established route and builds a new one. [The 10th house](/astrology/10th-house/) is the life people see you lead: your work and role, the responsibilities you carry, the reputation that follows you. This [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/) sets freedom and invention against your public direction.

For you, it can open doors you hadn't seen. Around them, an unconventional career can look possible, a sideways move sensible, independence at work worth asking for. You may become known for something original rather than for climbing the expected ladder.

For them, your public life can look like a system ripe for reform, and your ambitions like material for their ideas. They may feel inventive and free when they think about your work, and impatient with its slowness. [Capricorn](/astrology/capricorn/), the 10th's natural sign, belongs to Saturn, so in this angular house change walks into the territory of structure and long climbs, and old and new have to negotiate. On your [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) itself, the hinge of your public direction, their Uranus makes a contact that outweighs the house.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Reinvention can cost stability. Their encouragement to change course may land at the wrong moment, or ignore what your reputation took years to build; and if they're your colleague or your boss, unpredictable decisions can shake your standing. Your public life may never feel settled.

From their side, what can go missing is respect for continuity. A career is a long structure, and yours may need fewer revolutions than they'd like.

Job changes, dismissals and successes are beyond what this overlay can tell; its subject is the meeting of your ambition with their appetite for change.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Test before you leap. Try a side project before a resignation, a pilot before a restructure, and keep the parts of your reputation you'd want to keep anyway. In a couple, discuss any big career change together, with a timeline and a safety net; between colleagues, innovation works when it comes with a plan and a review; between friends, this is the one who believes you could do something entirely different, and is sometimes right; between adult relatives, their encouragement may free you from a family idea of what a proper job is.

Change places and you have your Uranus in their 10th house: you shake up their career, and you respect what they've built. [Their Uranus in your 9th house](/astrology/uranus-in-9th-house-synastry/) changes your beliefs; [their Uranus in your 11th house](/astrology/uranus-in-11th-house-synastry/), your friendships and hopes.

Your Midheaven needs your birth time to the minute, and in Placidus, the house system I use, so does every cusp after it, which makes your time the deciding one for this overlay; their Uranus, fixed well enough by their date, needs their time only because I start no synastry without it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). A boss or mentor twenty or thirty years older usually has Uranus in a different sign from yours, so their Uranus in your 10th is an earlier generation's idea of a career meeting yours, two eras rather than two people; a colleague your age may share your sign, and if your own Uranus is in your 10th too ([Uranus in the 10th house](/astrology/uranus-in-10th-house/)), the urge to work your own way may belong to your whole cohort. A workplace is shared; a reading isn't, and it waits for both people's explicit yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
