The quadnovile is the fourth of the ninths: two planets 160° apart, a trine plus a novile, 20° short of the opposition. It's the most distant member of a family I read as ripening, and the one I hold most lightly.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

Four times 40° is 160°: five signs and ten degrees. From the first twenty degrees of a sign, a quadnovile reaches the sign five on; from the last ten, the sign six on, the opposite one. That second case is worth knowing: anyone who reads [whole-sign aspects](/astrology/whole-sign-aspects/), where planets in opposite signs count as opposed, will see an opposition where I see a quadnovile.

The name has a gap in it. The ninths run 40°, 80°, 120° and 160°, but three ninths are a third of the circle, the [trine](/astrology/trine/), one of Ptolemy's four, so the count jumps from binovile to quadnovile.

I don't give the quadnovile a meaning of its own. I read it with the rest of the ninths, as maturation: two planets whose connection ripens slowly, through something like gestation, and becomes ready in its own time. That reading keeps the novile's limits: no destiny, no perfection, no automatic enlightenment, nothing guaranteed to turn out well. Nothing I've tested speaks for it either: [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) counted placements, a planet in a sign and a house, so it has no figure for any aspect, let alone this one.

Its orb is about a degree (see [orbs](/astrology/orbs/)), which matters in a crowded part of the circle, with the triseptile less than six degrees back. Twenty degrees on lies the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/), and beyond it the next ninth, at 200°, which is simply a quadnovile measured the other way round.

Of the five major aspects, only the trine is a ninth. The [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) is where the ninths start, and the [sextile](/astrology/sextile/), the [square](/astrology/square/) and the opposition fall between them. The ninth-harmonic chart, one of the [harmonic charts](/astrology/harmonic-charts/), multiplies 160° by nine, which comes to four full turns: there, the quadnovile becomes a conjunction.

## Between which planets {#between-which-planets}

In a chart I come to the quadnovile only after everything else, and the site gives it no pages pair by pair. Speed still shapes it.

Linking two personal planets, the Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus or Mars, it's a slow ripening between two parts of you that face each other almost across the chart. When one of the pair is slow, from Jupiter out to Pluto, the maturing stretches over years. Two slow planets in quadnovile are a generation's affair, which I read through the houses, if at all.

The minor aspects on either side of it are the [binovile](/astrology/binovile/), at 80°, half of it, read as two ripenings side by side, and the [decile](/astrology/decile/), at 36°, which opens the family of tenths, read as creative potential being refined.

## The quadnovile in synastry {#in-synastry}

In synastry, a quadnovile sets one person's planet almost across from the other's, 20° shy of an opposition. I'd read it only when it's tight and touches a personal planet, and then as a slow ripening between two people who stand in very different places: something that takes time to become mutual. I read it from both sides, as in all my [synastry readings](/synastry/), and I never let it outweigh the major aspects.

## The quadnovile in transits {#in-transits}

In transit, the quadnovile is brief, as every minor aspect kept to a degree is, and I rarely mention it. If a slow planet holds it for weeks, it may describe something maturing quietly, at a distance from your other concerns. Weather, not a forecast: a quadnovile transit describes ripening at the edge of view, not an outcome.
