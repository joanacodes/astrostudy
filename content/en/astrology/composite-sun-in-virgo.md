Some relationships show love by declaring it. One whose composite Sun is in Virgo tends to show it by fixing the hinge, checking the train times and getting the small things right for each other, and it knows itself by how well its everyday works.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Halfway between your two natal Suns, by the shorter way round, lies the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) of [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), read as what the relationship organises itself around; here it lies in [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/), mutable earth. That gives the bond a discerning centre: it notices what works and what doesn't, and it improves things a little at a time. It's no rarer than any other sign: one pair in twelve, or near enough.

Such a relationship tends to grow competent together. You may find you've become very good at something as a pair, running a house, a shop, a garden, a long journey, in a way neither of you was before. It's revived by a job done well, and it often finds its purpose in being useful: to each other first, and then to the people around you. Its affection is mostly practical, and the people who know you best learn to read it in the details. Two colleagues can have it as easily as two lovers, and the shared spreadsheet may be where this bond feels most like itself.

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) rules Virgo and, by tradition, finds its [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/) there too, so composite Mercury is the next place to look. It can be a surprising one. No birth chart puts Mercury more than about 28° from the Sun, yet composite Mercury lands opposite the composite Sun in about 1 pair in 26: for a Virgo composite Sun, that sets its ruler in Pisces, on the far side of the chart. That's an artefact of midpoints, not a message about the two of you. In a birth chart, precision of this kind would be one person's gift ([Sun in Virgo](/astrology/sun-in-virgo/)); here it belongs to the bond, and two rather untidy people can find themselves, together, unexpectedly careful.

The precision can turn inward. A bond organised around improvement can keep reviewing itself, finding the next thing to fix when nothing needs fixing, and analysis can start to sound like criticism even when it isn't meant that way. What helps is keeping a list of what already works, and reading it as carefully as the list of what doesn't. Seen from either side, the difference is where the attention goes: the [composite Sun in Leo](/astrology/composite-sun-in-leo/) points it at what the bond makes, the [composite Sun in Libra](/astrology/composite-sun-in-libra/) at the balance between you, and a Virgo bond at how well the everyday runs.

I'd rather be exact about what this page is: a description of a technique I don't use in my own readings. Astrologers who do use it check a composite against both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and none of it says how long a relationship will run or how it will turn out. Doing it properly starts with asking: a composite is built from two people's birth data, and the first step is checking that both are happy to share it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
