The tradition lets a fixed star into a birth chart by one door only: a planet sitting on it. The rule is strict, a conjunction within about a degree, and the arithmetic behind it explains both how rare each contact is and why a chart often has one somewhere.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A conjunction to a fixed star is a planet, or an angle, at the same degree of the zodiac as a bright star. Take [Regulus](/astrology/regulus/). For a birth in 1930 it stood at 28°50′ Leo, so the window ran from 27°50′ to 29°50′ Leo; for a birth in 2026 it stands at 0°11′ Virgo, and the window runs from 29°11′ Leo to 1°11′ Virgo. The star creeps forward about a degree every 72 years, so the window moves with the year of birth ([fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/)).

The match is made along the zodiac only. A star close to the Sun's path, as Regulus is, can really stand beside the planet in the sky. [Fomalhaut](/astrology/fomalhaut/) lies 21° south of that path, so a planet "on" Fomalhaut shares its zodiac degree and nothing more. Some astrologers prefer a method that follows the actual sky, a star and a planet on the horizon or meridian at the same moment at the place of birth ([parans](/astrology/parans/)).

And the conjunction is the only contact. A planet square or trine a star isn't part of the tradition: Nicholas DeVore's *Encyclopedia of Astrology* (1947) gives stars no effect by aspect. As in any [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/), the two are read together: the planet keeps its own function, and the star colours it.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A window of 1° either side of a star covers 2° of the zodiac's 360°, a 180th of the circle, so any one planet sits in it for about 1 person in 180. The counts for people born from 1930 to 2009 bear that out, with differences that come from each planet's rhythm:

- **[The Sun on Regulus](/astrology/sun-conjunct-regulus/):** about 1 in 176, for births around 22–23 August. The Sun reaches each star on the same two days every year, give or take a day: Aldebaran about 30–31 May, Algol about 16–17 May, [Spica](/astrology/sun-conjunct-spica/) about 16–17 October, Antares about 1–2 December, Fomalhaut about 21–22 February.
- **The Moon on Regulus:** about 1 in 179. The Moon crosses the whole 2° window in about four hours, so only the birth time can settle it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
- **Venus on Regulus:** about 1 in 146; on Spica, about 1 in 218. The faster planets loop back over some stars and not others, depending on where they turn retrograde.
- **[Saturn on Regulus](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-regulus/):** about 1 in 151, in three stretches: September 1948 to May 1949, October 1977 to July 1978, and August to September 2007. Saturn on Spica is about 1 in 332, because in those eighty years it passed Spica only twice, the second time briefly.

The angles move fastest of all, a degree in about four minutes, so a star on the Ascendant or Midheaven needs a birth time you trust to the minute.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I don't compute stars in a reading; the system I built for my readings leaves them out, and this page describes how others use them. If someone shows me a star contact from another chart, I start where the system I use always starts: with the planet. Its function, sign and house come first. The star's tradition, such as Ptolemy's description of Regulus as having the nature of Mars and Jupiter, is old lore laid on top, and the old books' promises of royalty, fame or ruin stay what they are: promises in old books.

Stars weren't among the placements I tested, so I have no figure for how often a star contact holds, and I won't borrow one ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A star conjunction is rare, so it marks you out.”** Each one is rare, about 1 person in 180. Across a dozen planets and points and a list of bright stars, a chart with at least one is common.
- **“The planet takes on the star's meaning.”** The planet stays itself. In a conjunction two things are read together; neither replaces the other.
- **“Stars make squares and trines too.”** In the tradition, only the conjunction counts.
- **“If the degrees match, the two meet in the sky.”** Only when the star lies near the Sun's path. Fomalhaut is 21° from it, so a planet on its degree is nowhere near it.
