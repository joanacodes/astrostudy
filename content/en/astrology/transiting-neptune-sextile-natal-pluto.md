For most people this transit came before they could remember anything. Neptune reached 60° from their natal Pluto in the first year or two of life, finishing a sextile they were born close to.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) marks where you hold on hardest and where, in time, you renew. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) softens and widens, towards imagination and compassion. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/), 60°, relates two functions through an opening that needs a response, and under this [transit](/astrology/transits/) the opening runs between imagining and transforming: the capacity to let an ideal change what no longer works.

Because it comes so early, I don't read it as a season lived. Many people born in these years have the natal [Neptune sextile Pluto](/astrology/neptune-sextile-pluto/), and for them the transit is the same angle in the sky becoming exact a few months or a year or two after birth, sometimes already inside the orb on the day itself. What an adult can use is the natal pair, read through the houses of the two planets, which come from the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

I don't read children's charts ([Children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)), and here the rule costs nothing: nothing in this transit asks a child or a parent to act. Neptune's blind spot is confusing symbolic meaning with literal prediction, and a first year of life is the last place to make that mistake.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

A pass lasts about two and a half years in my orbs, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the shortest about twenty-two months, with three exact hits as a rule and five in about one pass in four ([Neptune retrograde](/astrology/neptune-retrograde/)). For people born inside the orb, part of it ran before they were born.

In the site's count for births from 1930 to 2009, it came within orb at a median age of 0.7, and never later than 7: at 4.7 for someone born at the start of 1935, at 0.9 for 1945, within a few months for 1955 to 1985, at about 2 for 1995 and 2005. About 1 person in 6 never meets it. Most of them were born in the 1960s, some in the late 1950s and the 1970s: Neptune already stood a degree or more past the sextile to their Pluto, and it was moving away. No one meets it twice.

The adult steps come later, at ages shared by everyone born within a year or two of you: [transiting Neptune square natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-neptune-square-natal-pluto/) in adolescence, [transiting Neptune trine natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-neptune-trine-natal-pluto/) between about 24 and 34, [transiting Neptune opposite natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-neptune-opposite-natal-pluto/) between 51 and 62. [Transiting Neptune conjunct natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-neptune-conjunct-natal-pluto/) doesn't come at all in these lives.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Start from your natal chart rather than from the transit: is the Neptune–Pluto sextile there, and in which houses do the two planets sit? That is where the opening lives in an adult life.

If you are a parent, let this one go. What a young child needs is not written in a transit, and I don't read the chart that would claim otherwise.

Look ahead to the step your age is near: the trine of the late twenties and early thirties, or the opposition of the fifties.
