With the North Node in the 1st house, the growth happens in the most personal place a chart has: you, as you walk into a room. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) sat in [the 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) when you were born, and it points towards self-definition: your own wishes, your own presence, your own first step.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The 1st is an angular house, the one that begins at the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), and its natural sign is Aries. What sits here tends to show: in your manner, your body, the way you start things. The [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) lies exactly opposite, in [the 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/), the house of partners. That's the familiar ground: you know how to read the person across the table, adjust to them, and define yourself in relation to them. The 1st-house end asks a question that can feel oddly difficult from there: *and what about you?*

No house is more sensitive to the clock. In Placidus, the house system I use, the 1st begins exactly at the Ascendant, and the horizon moves about a degree every four minutes, so with a node near that line a few minutes of [birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/) decide whether it sits here or in the 12th. Without the time, there's no house to read. If the node is near either edge of the 1st, [North Node in the 12th house](/astrology/north-node-in-12th-house/) or [North Node in the 2nd house](/astrology/north-node-in-2nd-house/) is worth a look too. I look at both the mean and the true node, a degree or two apart at most, and near a cusp they can land in different houses; I'll tell you when they do.

The node's sign is shared by everyone born within a year and a half of you; the house is the part that's yours. And the angle my [testing notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) reports, the rising sign, is the one on this house's cusp: it held about a third of the time, and the three that stood out were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In a couple, the 7th-house end is fluent: you notice what your partner needs, smooth the way, become who the relationship seems to want. The 1st-house end asks to be met as yourself. That means preferences in plain view, letting your partner see you on a bad day, and staying the same person whether or not they approve.

It can feel selfish at first. Usually it's the opposite: a partner finally gets to know who they're with. It helps, too, to keep a life of your own inside the relationship: friends, plans and pleasures that don't wait for anyone's approval.

## At work {#at-work}

You may be an excellent second: the adviser, the deputy, the one who makes someone else's plan run. The North Node asks you to put your own name and face to something. Starting a project, working for yourself, leading a team, presenting your own idea rather than polishing another's: anywhere your initiative is visible, it counts.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Whenever a decision comes up, the old reflex is to check with someone first. Under strain you may vanish into a partnership, take on its moods and plans, and lose track of your own.

Then there's the overcorrection: proving your independence by refusing all help, or announcing decisions nobody was consulted on. That's the 1st-house end learnt in a hurry. Self-definition doesn't need an audience to push against.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Start with the body, because the 1st house does: movement, rest, clothes that feel like you. Make small decisions alone and notice how that feels. Begin one thing this month on your own initiative, and take the first step before asking anyone's opinion. Notice how you introduce yourself, too: the first sentence people hear from you is 1st-house territory, and it can be about you rather than about the people you belong to.

Keep the 7th-house gifts. You're good with people because you see them; now let them see you.
