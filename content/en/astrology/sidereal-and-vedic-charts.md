The sky at your birth can be written down in more than one alphabet. A sidereal chart writes it with the signs counted from the stars, and Jyotish, the astrology of India, draws that chart in a square of its own.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A sidereal chart is the same moment, seen from the same place, with every position moved back by the ayanāṃśa, the gap between the March equinox and the sidereal starting point, about 24° today ([Sidereal zodiac](/astrology/sidereal-zodiac/)). The gap exists because of the [precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/). Two families of chart are built on it.

The first is Western. The Irish astrologer Cyril Fagan (1896–1970) and the American Donald Bradley are generally considered the founders of Western sidereal astrology, and together they set the Fagan–Bradley ayanāṃśa; Fagan published a *Fixed Zodiac Ephemeris for 1948* and *Zodiacs Old and New* (1950). Their charts keep Western methods and change only where the signs begin.

The second is the *kuṇḍali*, or *janmapatra*, of [Vedic astrology (Jyotish)](/astrology/vedic-astrology-jyotish/). It is drawn as a square of twelve cells, one for each sign, and holds the nine *grahas*: the Sun, the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, and the two lunar nodes, Rahu and Ketu, which I read as the [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) and the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/). Uranus, Neptune and Pluto are not among the nine. Each sign is one house, from its 0th degree to its 30th, counted as a rule from the sign of the *lagna*, the rising sign ([Whole Sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/)). Beside it come divisional charts: the *Bṛhat Parāśara Horāśāstra* defines sixteen, and among those considered most significant are the *navāṃśa*, which cuts each sign into nine, and the *daśāṃśa*, into ten.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Software does it in one setting, but the steps are simple. Take the tropical positions, the Ascendant included, and subtract the ayanāṃśa from every one. Which ayanāṃśa is a choice: Lahiri's, named after the astronomer Nirmal Chandra Lahiri (1906–1980), is by far the most used in India; the Raman and Krishnamurthy systems are used too; Western sidereal astrology has the Fagan–Bradley one.

For a Vedic chart, the houses then follow from the lagna's sign alone. Take a tropical Ascendant at 5° Leo: about 24° back, the lagna falls at 11° Cancer, so Cancer is the 1st house, Leo the 2nd, Virgo the 3rd. A Moon at 20° Leo tropical becomes about 26° Cancer, in the 1st house; a Mars at 28° Virgo stays in Virgo at about 4°, in the 3rd.

The square comes in three regional layouts:

- **North Indian:** set by house. The 1st house is always the top middle diamond, the others follow anticlockwise, and a number in each cell, 1 for Aries to 12 for Pisces, names its sign.
- **South Indian:** set by sign. Aries is always the second cell from the left in the top row, the signs run clockwise, and the lagna is marked with a diagonal line or the word itself.
- **East Indian:** also set by sign, with Aries in the middle of the top row and the signs running anticlockwise.

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

The same planets, at the same distances from one another, sorted into other signs, and for a Vedic chart into other houses. Each layout puts something first. The North Indian square keeps the houses in fixed places and makes you read the signs from the numbers; the South Indian one keeps the signs fixed, an arrangement said to resemble the sky more closely, with the same room for every sign.

I don't draw either. I read the [tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/), with Placidus houses, and I keep my meanings with the frame they were built for ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). A Vedic chart is read with tools I don't use, and deserves someone trained in them.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

The ayanāṃśas don't agree to the degree. In 2020, the Sri Yukteswar ayanāṃśa put sun signs about 23° behind the tropical ones, a degree short of the usual figure, so a planet near the edge of a sign can change sidereal sign with the setting.

The lagna carries every house. The [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) changes sign about every two hours on average, so near a boundary a few minutes of birth time, or a different ayanāṃśa, can move all twelve houses at once ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

And a kuṇḍali is an accurate diagram of the sky; what is read into it is interpretation, in Jyotish as in my own astrology. Other frames on this site keep the zodiac and change the view instead: the [Uranian 90° dial](/astrology/uranian-90-dial/) folds it into quarters.
