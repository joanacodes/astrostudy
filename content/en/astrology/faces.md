Cut every sign into three equal slices of ten degrees and you have the 36 faces, the smallest equal pieces of the old zodiac. Each slice has a planet of its own, and a planet standing in its own face gains a sliver of dignity: the slightest the tradition gives.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The 36 slices are older than the signs. In ancient Egypt, the decans were 36 groups of stars that rose one after another through the night and told the hours; a new one reappeared at dawn about every ten days, so the Greeks called them *dekanoi*, tenths. When the zodiac of twelve signs took over, the decans became its 10° divisions, three to a sign.

As a dignity they are called faces, and each has a planet. The planets follow the Chaldean order, the seven visible planets ranked from slowest to fastest: Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, the Sun, Venus, Mercury, the Moon ([Planetary speed](/astrology/planetary-speed/)). The sequence starts with Mars in the first face of Aries, then runs on without a break: the Sun and Venus for the rest of Aries, Mercury, the Moon and Saturn for Taurus, Jupiter, Mars and the Sun for Gemini, all the way to Saturn, Jupiter and Mars in Pisces ([William Lilly](/astrology/william-lilly/), *Christian Astrology*, 1647). Thirty-six faces don't divide evenly between seven planets, so Mars, which opens and closes the round, gets six and every other planet five.

The same order gave the [planetary hours](/astrology/planetary-hours/), and through them the names of the days of the week. A modern name for it, the Chaldean order, is misleading: the ordering seems to be a Hellenistic innovation of the second century BCE (Eviatar Zerubavel, *The Seven Day Circle*, 1985).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Of the five essential dignities, the face is the last and the lightest. Lilly scored it a single point, against domicile's five, and he said its one use was to keep a planet from being [peregrine](/astrology/peregrine-planets/), entirely without dignity. For post-classical astrologers such as [Guido Bonatti](/astrology/guido-bonatti/) and Lilly, faces had almost no other part to play.

Faces are not the [terms](/astrology/terms-bounds/). Terms cut a sign into five strips of unequal width and give them to five planets only; faces cut it into three equal parts and give them to all seven, Sun and Moon included, in a fixed rotation.

There is also a second set of rulers for the same slices. In the modern decans, the first ten degrees of a sign belong to its own ruler, the second to the ruler of the next sign of the same element, the third to the ruler of the last: Aries runs Mars, the Sun (Leo), Jupiter (Sagittarius) (Margaret Hone, *The Modern Text-Book of Astrology*, 1978). Each decan has a page of its own, with both sets of rulers and the old images that went with it, starting with [Aries, first decan](/astrology/aries-first-decan/).

After the face, a table of dignities turns from strengths to weaknesses: [detriment](/astrology/detriment/), opposite a planet's own sign, and fall, opposite its exaltation.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I treat a face as a small, fine detail: a further shading inside the sign, never the main colour. Its value is in precision. Venus at 25° Aries stands in its detriment, but the last ten degrees of Aries are Venus's own face, so even in a sign built for Mars it isn't altogether a stranger. That's about as much as a face can say, and I don't stretch it further.

It is also the dignity that moves fastest with the clock. The Ascendant changes sign about every two hours on average, so it crosses a face in roughly forty minutes, faster or slower depending on the sign and your latitude. The face of your Ascendant depends on the birth time, and a time rounded to the hour can miss it altogether ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Faces and decans are different things.”** They are the same ten-degree slices. What differs is the set of rulers: the Chaldean faces or the modern decans by element.
- **“The Chaldean order is arbitrary.”** It ranks the planets by how fast they move against the stars, slowest first.
- **“A planet in its own face is strongly placed.”** It has the faintest of the dignities: one point in Lilly's scoring.
- **“Decans are about Sun signs.”** Every planet and point falls in a decan. The Sun is only one of them.
