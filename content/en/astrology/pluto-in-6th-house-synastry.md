Routines rarely survive unchanged when someone's Pluto falls in your 6th house. The way you work, the habits that hold your week together and the people at the next desk can all be reorganised, sometimes thoroughly, by another person's depth.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) clears out what has stopped working and rebuilds it on sounder lines. [The 6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) is the machinery of ordinary days: tasks, methods, habits, the people who share your workplace, and the upkeep of body and time. Through this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their depth gets to work on your routine.

With them, you may overhaul how you work: new systems, dropped habits, a job you finally leave, a discipline you finally keep. Their presence makes it hard to go on doing something you know doesn't work.

Your daily methods intrigue them. They may want to understand how you organise yourself, improve it, or find their own habits transformed by your discipline. Cadent houses keep things in repair, the 6th most of all, and [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/) is its natural sign; tradition gave Mars its joy here, the same Mars that ruled Scorpio before Pluto did, which ties this house to Pluto's sign by an old thread. Near the 6th's far edge, your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), colleagues can start to feel like partners.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Questions of control can surface at work: who sets the rules, whose method wins, how closely one of you watches the other. Change can arrive faster than your week can absorb, and small habits turn into battlegrounds.

From their side, your systems may look like resistance, and they can feel shut out when you protect a routine they'd like to improve.

Illness was once read in this house; I leave that to the old texts. A chart can say nothing about your health ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)), and what this overlay describes is power and change in your working day. Your body's care belongs with a doctor.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Change one habit at a time, and agree who decides what. In a couple, divide the household systems so that each of you owns some; between friends, a shared routine, whether training, cooking or a long project, thrives on respect for each other's method; between colleagues, clear authority and agreed processes stop intensity from turning into a power struggle; between adult relatives sharing care or errands, write down who does what.

From their chart, this reads as your Pluto in their 6th house, your depth reorganising their days. [Their Pluto in your 5th house](/astrology/pluto-in-5th-house-synastry/) deepens pleasure and creative work; [their Pluto in your 7th house](/astrology/pluto-in-7th-house-synastry/) meets you as an equal, face to face.

Every generation reinvents work in its own way, and Pluto's sign carries some of that. Close in age, you probably share this overlay's backdrop with your own Pluto ([Pluto in the 6th house](/astrology/pluto-in-6th-house/)); with years between you, two eras' ideas of how work should be done meet over one task list. The degree of their Pluto comes from the day they were born, while the overlays running the other way, into their houses, need the hour too. Your birth time places your 6th, whose far edge in Placidus, the house system I use, is your Descendant ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#why-i-wait-for-the-time)). Working side by side gives no one a right to the other's chart: the reading waits for consent ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#what-i-ask)).
