Her colleague's partner, his old friend's flatmate, the couple who came with the neighbours: some pairs are only ever introduced through someone else. Composite North Node in the 1st house asks a relationship like that to walk into the room under its own name.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), the relationship's own chart, the [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) shows the direction the bond grows in and the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/), always opposite, the ground it already knows. Here the North Node sits in [the 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/), the angular house of identity and first impressions, whose natural sign is Aries. The South Node sits across the horizon in [the 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/), the house of the other party, and the familiar ground is accommodation: fitting in with another couple's plans, keeping agreements other people drafted, being described by the company you keep. The direction is a face of the relationship's own: what the two of you stand for, how you arrive, what you start together.

Two people with strong personalities can still form a bond that vanishes in company, as if it had no outline until someone else drew one. The 1st-house direction gives it an outline: a shared style, a way of introducing yourselves, a willingness to be the pair that proposes the plan rather than the pair that fits around it. Tradition gave this house to Mercury as its joy, and a relationship growing here often finds its identity by naming it, in how it talks about itself.

[North Node in the 1st house](/astrology/north-node-in-1st-house/) describes one person learning to put themselves forward, and [their North Node in your 1st house](/astrology/north-node-in-1st-house-synastry/) someone whose growth is tangled up with how you present yourself. The composite version belongs to neither of you; it's the relationship learning to present itself.

The house is also where method weighs most. It begins at the composite Ascendant, and the two ways of drawing composite houses can put that degree far apart: one halves the distance between your two natal Ascendants, the other works the Ascendant out afresh from the composite Midheaven for the latitude you live at. Both rest on two birth times, so with either time missing there's no Ascendant and no house to speak of ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). For birth charts I use [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/); with a composite, the house system is only one of the choices, and I take no side between the methods.

Near that line the node can sit in the 12th by one method and the 1st by the other, and the mean and true node, a degree or two apart, can split it as well. I look at both, and [composite North Node in the 12th house](/astrology/composite-north-node-in-12th-house/) or [composite North Node in the 2nd house](/astrology/composite-north-node-in-2nd-house/) may hold part of the reading.

The danger is self-absorption: a relationship so busy defining itself that it stops listening, or treats every outside opinion as an attack. The 7th-house end keeps it fair, since the habit of considering others is what makes a strong identity good company.

I describe composites without using them in my own work; those who do read this node beside both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). It doesn't promise that the relationship will be admired or that it will last. It points a direction, and a pair learning to stand under its own name can start by standing behind the chart: both of you know about it and agree to it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
