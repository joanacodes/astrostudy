For 38 years after Vesta was found in 1807, no new asteroid turned up. Then a postmaster with a telescope found a fifth, named after the goddess of justice who, in myth, left the earth for the stars, and the long pause was over.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Astraea measures about 115 km, and its stony surface is bright and reflective. It goes round the Sun in about 4.1 years, between 2.1 and 3.1 times the Earth's distance, inside the belt that lies between Mars and Jupiter, and turns on its axis in a little under 17 hours. A star it passed in front of on 6 June 2008 gave its width.

Karl Ludwig Hencke, a German amateur astronomer who ran the post office at Driesen, found it on 8 December 1845 while looking for [Vesta](/astrology/vesta/). The King of Prussia rewarded him with a yearly pension of 1,200 marks. The find broke the long pause: eight more asteroids followed in the next five years and twenty-four in the five after that, and by the time [Kassandra](/astrology/kassandra/) was named, the list had passed a hundred. It also began the demotion of the first four, which had been counted as planets, once it was clear they were only the largest of a crowd.

Hencke gave Astraea an inverted anchor for a symbol, perhaps a stylised pair of scales; it is no longer used, and astronomers now write a 5 inside a circle. Astrologers use a percent sign, because % sits on the 5 key.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Astraea means starry, star-like. She was the virgin goddess of justice, shown holding a pair of scales, and often treated as the same figure as Dike, Justice, daughter of Zeus and Themis. In the story as Aratus and later poets told it, she was the last of the immortals to live among people during the Golden Age. As the ages darkened, through silver and bronze to iron, she withdrew from the towns to the hills, then left altogether and took her place in the sky as the constellation Virgo, the Maiden, with the bright star Spica as the ear of grain in her hand. In about 40 BC Virgil announced her return: *Iam redit et virgo*, now the maiden comes back.

Justice had a harder face as well. Hesiod, four centuries before Aratus, had already pictured retribution and humility deserting a world gone bad, and the first of the pair, [Nemesis](/astrology/nemesis/), was counted as Justice at her fiercest.

The sky kept her scales next to her. Milton set God's golden scales "betwixt Astrea and the Scorpion signe", which is where the constellation of Libra lies, between Virgo and Scorpio. For a chart this needs one caution. The signs of the [tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/), the one I use, are named after those constellations but counted from the equinox, so [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/) and [Libra](/astrology/libra/) in a chart are stretches of 30° tied to the seasons, not the stars of the Maiden and her scales.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Astraea stays out of my readings. About a third of the placements I [weighed in my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign, each planet, Chiron and Lilith, held overall. Astraea was never weighed there, and the system I built computes no asteroid, so she can't borrow a figure from the placements that were.

It's also the asteroid most likely to tempt a neat story. Astraea in Virgo or in Libra can look like a goddess come home, and a homecoming is a pleasing thing to read into a chart. But the asteroid's sign comes from where it was when you were born, at about 4.1 years a circuit, so it's shared by many people born around the same time, and the constellations behind the signs have drifted away from them. A pleasing coincidence isn't a reason ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

If you want Astraea in your chart, weigh her last, after the planets: count only a tight contact, and give her a house only with a birth time ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Astraea was the first asteroid found after Ceres.”** She was the fifth, after Ceres, Pallas, Juno and Vesta, and the first in 38 years.
- **“The percent sign is an ancient symbol.”** It's a modern astrologer's shortcut from the keyboard; Hencke's own symbol was an anchor turned upside down.
- **“Astraea rules Virgo.”** In myth she became the constellation Virgo. Rulership is something else: the tropical sign Virgo is ruled by Mercury.
