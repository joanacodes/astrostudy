Most aspects tell the same story twice: the degrees say trine, and so do the signs. An out-of-sign aspect is where the two counts part company, and it helps to know which one a chart is using.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

An aspect can be measured in two ways. By degree, two planets are in aspect when the distance between them is within orb of the aspect's angle ([orbs](/astrology/orbs/)). By sign, they're in aspect when their signs are: Aries and Leo, four signs apart, are trine; Aries and Cancer, three apart, are square. Most of the time the two agree.

Near the edge of a sign they can disagree. Mars at 28° Cancer and Saturn at 2° Aries are 116° apart, a [trine](/astrology/trine/) by degree, four degrees short of exact; yet Cancer and Aries are three signs apart, square by sign. That's an out-of-sign aspect, also called a dissociate aspect. Every aspect can do it: a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) can straddle two neighbouring signs, a [square](/astrology/square/) can join signs two or four apart, an opposition signs five apart.

One kind of aspect never can. At the exact angle, both planets stand at the same degree of their signs, and the signs themselves are in aspect ([exact aspects](/astrology/exact-aspects/)). Only the orb lets the two counts drift apart.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The tradition defined each aspect twice over. Ptolemy gives the opposition as six signs and 180°, the trine as four signs and 120°, the square as three signs and 90°, the sextile as two signs and 60°. Western astrology now counts from degree to degree, which is where out-of-sign aspects come from. Indian astrology counts from sign to sign, with each planet aspecting whole signs, so the question never arises there; an aspect can even run one way only, and when two planets aspect each other it's called mutual ([mutual aspects](/astrology/mutual-aspects/)).

How likely an aspect is to fall out of sign depends on its orb. Within a degree of exact, it happens about one time in thirty; at 6° from exact, about one time in five. In births from 1930 to 2009, by The Astro Study's count, nearly one aspect in eight involving the Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus or Mars was out of sign, and more than half of all charts had at least one.

What changes is how the signs relate. A square in sign joins two signs of one modality, both cardinal, say, and that shared pace is part of the friction ([the three modalities](/astrology/three-modalities/)). Out of sign, it joins signs of one element or of elements that get along, so the angle grinds while the signs agree. A trine out of sign does the reverse: easy by degree, between signs with no element in common ([the four elements](/astrology/four-elements/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I count an out-of-sign aspect as the aspect its distance makes, within the same orbs as any other: in my system, 116° is a trine, whatever the signs say. Then I read the signs as its temper. The trine between Mars in Cancer and Saturn in Aries still lets effort and patience work together, as [Mars trine Saturn](/astrology/mars-trine-saturn/) does, but between two cardinal signs that each want to lead, so the ease comes with some jostling. An out-of-sign conjunction is still a meeting, between two signs that speak differently.

When the two counts disagree, I say which one I'm using, because an astrologer who counts by sign would call that Mars–Saturn pair a square, and neither of us would be misreading the chart. It's the same openness I keep about orbs ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

The Moon makes these cases most often, since it changes sign every two and a half days, so the birth time can decide whether a Moon aspect stays in sign: one more reason I want the time before I read ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“An out-of-sign aspect doesn't count.”** Counted by degree, it does, within its orb. Only a count by sign leaves it out.
- **“Out of sign means weaker.”** The orb sets the volume; the signs set the temper.
- **“A program that shows one has made a mistake.”** A program counting by degree will show it, one counting by sign won't. Both are following a rule.
