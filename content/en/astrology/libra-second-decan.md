The middle of Libra is Saturn's ground. Both ways of sharing out the decans give these ten degrees to the same planet, and the sign that already honours Saturn with its exaltation adds a second, smaller honour.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

After the Moon's face ([Libra, first decan](/astrology/libra-first-decan/)), the [Chaldean order](/astrology/chaldean-order/) starts again from its slowest planet, so the second face of [Libra](/astrology/libra/) falls to Saturn ([faces](/astrology/faces/)). The modern decans reach the same planet by another road. They give the middle third of a sign to the next sign of its element, which for Libra is Aquarius, and Aquarius has two rulers: Saturn in the tradition, Uranus in modern tropical astrology, and I read both, for reasons I give under [traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/).

Counting Aquarius's older ruler, this is one of nine decans in the zodiac where the two systems name the same planet. It also stacks Saturn's dignities. Saturn is [exalted](/astrology/exaltation/) in Libra, so [Saturn in Libra](/astrology/saturn-in-libra/) between 10° and 20° holds exaltation and face together; its exaltation degree, the 21st, lies just ahead ([Libra, third decan](/astrology/libra-third-decan/)).

Uranus, the modern co-ruler, puts its own question beside Saturn's: not only whether the rules hold, but whether they could be fairer. A planet here can feel both pulls, keeping its word and wanting to rewrite the terms, and I would rather name both than choose.

## Its image {#its-image}

In Wallis Budge's list of the Egyptian decans, the one set against these degrees is Ṭepā-khentet, the first of four neighbours whose names share the element khentet, a run that carries on into Scorpio. [Firmicus Maternus](/astrology/firmicus-maternus/) has Sepisent or Atebenus. The most telling name comes from the list of Cosmas of Maiuma: Kairos, the Greek word for the right or critical moment, as opposed to chronos, time that simply passes.

A decan of Saturn, the planet astrologers have long read as time itself, named after the other kind of time: it's a neat fit. Whether Cosmas meant anything by it, I can't say. The old lists rarely explain themselves.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun reaches these degrees about 3 October and leaves them about 12 October: a [Sun in Libra](/astrology/sun-in-libra/) in the middle of its sign. I read Saturn's shade on it as fairness that wants a frame. Agreements are meant to be kept here, and promises tend to be made slowly, because they're meant to last.

Saturn is one of the three placements that held 75 to 90% of the time in my notebook ([the three that held: Saturn, Chiron and Lilith](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)), so when planets fill this decan I look at where Saturn itself sits before I say anything about the decan. With these degrees rising, Venus is the [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/), and Saturn earns a second look.

Two stars share the decan. [Vindemiatrix](/astrology/vindemiatrix/), the grape-gatherer of Virgo, crossed into it about 2005 and stands at 10°18′ in 2026; [Algorab](/astrology/algorab/), the Crow's star, is at 13°48′, and the Sun passes it about 6 October.

Of the slow planets, [Uranus in Libra](/astrology/uranus-in-libra/) crossed these degrees from late 1970 to 1973, and Pluto from late 1975 to 1980. Everyone born in those years shares them; only their house makes them personal.
