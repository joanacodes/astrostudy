Draw a line across your sky from due south to due north, passing straight over your head: that's your meridian. The meridian axis is where the zodiac crossed it when you were born — the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) above the horizon, the [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/) below.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The Sun crosses the meridian every day around midday, at the highest point of its arc. In the same way, at every moment one degree of the zodiac is crossing the meridian above you, and the opposite degree is crossing it below, on the far side of the Earth. At your birth, the upper one became your Midheaven, the MC, from the Latin *Medium Coeli*, the middle of the sky; the lower one your Imum Coeli, the IC, the bottom of the sky. In Placidus and the other quadrant systems they are the cusps of the [10th house](/astrology/10th-house/) and the [4th house](/astrology/4th-house/).

The Midheaven is not the point straight overhead. That point is the zenith, and outside the tropics the zodiac never passes through it: seen from Britain or France, the Midheaven always lies to the south, somewhere between the zenith and the southern horizon.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Like the horizon, the meridian turns with the sky: the whole zodiac crosses it in about a day, so the Midheaven changes sign about every two hours on average, and finding it takes a birth time and place ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

The meridian axis and the [horizon axis](/astrology/horizon-axis/) cross to make the four angles. People often assume the two form a neat cross, with the Midheaven always 90° from the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/). It isn't so. The zodiac is tilted, and it meets the horizon and the meridian at angles that change through the day and with latitude, so the gap between the Midheaven and the Ascendant is usually a little more or less than 90°, and far from the equator it can be much more or much less. That's why, in many charts, the houses on one side of the Midheaven look squeezed and those on the other side stretched.

Where the horizon splits the chart into above and below, the meridian splits it into east and west: an eastern half around the Ascendant, a western half around the Descendant. Many astrologers read planets gathered in the east as a life steered from within, and in the west as one shaped with and through other people ([hemisphere emphasis](/astrology/hemisphere-emphasis/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read the meridian as the line between where you're seen and where you come from. The Midheaven end is public life: the work, reputation and role you grow into, what people know you for — a direction, not a job title. The IC end is the private root of the chart: home, family background, the place you go back to when the door closes. Tradition gave the 10th house action and reputation, and the 4th parents, home and land, and the axis still reads that way: the higher you climb, the more it matters where you stand.

So I read the two ends together. An Aries Midheaven with a Libra IC might describe someone who takes the lead at work and needs peace and fairness at home, and the question becomes how the two feed each other. A planet close to either end speaks loudly: near the Midheaven, it's often visible in your public life; near the IC, in your private one.

My [notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) doesn't name the Midheaven or the IC. The angle it reports is the rising sign, which held about a third of the time, and the three that stood out were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith. So I read the meridian with care, and say so when a conclusion leans on it: that's [how I read](/how-i-read/).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Midheaven is the point straight overhead.”** That's the zenith. The Midheaven is where the zodiac crosses the meridian, and from Britain or France it always lies to the south, lower in the sky.
- **“The Midheaven is always square the Ascendant.”** Only in particular cases; the gap changes with the time of day and the latitude.
- **“Your Midheaven tells you which job to do.”** It describes a direction and a way of being seen, not a job title, and I never predict careers, money or success.
- **“The IC is your mother and the Midheaven your father.”** Astrologers don't agree on which parent goes with which end. I read the IC as the family you come from, without the labels.
