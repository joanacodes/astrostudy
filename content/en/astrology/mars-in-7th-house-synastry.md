Their Mars in your 7th house sets another person's drive directly across the table from you. Whatever the bond, it takes on the feel of a meeting between equals who push back: a partnership that challenges you, an alliance that argues, a rivalry you respect.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) asserts: it says what it wants and goes after it. [The 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) is the place of the one-to-one: the person you share a life or a business with, close collaborators, the people you negotiate with and the ones who oppose you in the open. As a [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their Mars here makes them the counterpart who pushes.

They tend to meet you head-on. With them you learn what you want by having to say it, defend it and sometimes trade for it, and a partnership can feel alive precisely because it isn't always easy. There's often a spark in it, of attraction or of competition, and some pairs find the two hard to tell apart.

For them, you are the match their drive was looking for. They find an answer when they push and you push back, and they may respect you more for standing your ground. In an angular house, what arrives is plain to see. Its natural sign is [Libra](/astrology/libra/), the sign of balance, where tradition puts Mars in detriment; I read that as assertion that has to learn to take turns. Right on your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), the degree sinking below the western horizon at your birth, their Mars touches the very frame of your partnerships, and that conjunction is part of the reading too.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Two people who both want to lead can turn a partnership into a contest. Decisions become negotiations, negotiations become matches, and every plan may seem to need winning rather than agreeing. If their temper rises easily, you may find yourself either fighting more than you'd like or giving way to keep the peace.

From their side, your search for balance can feel like delay. They want a decision; you weigh it. Your diplomacy may look to them like dodging, and their directness to you like pressure, though each is simply the other's way of playing fair.

Tradition put open enemies in the 7th, the adversary you can see coming. In synastry I read that as honest opposition, not hostility: this overlay describes how two people take each other on, never whether a bond will hold or end.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Settle the rules of engagement early. Take turns leading, let a "no" be heard without punishing it, and decide in advance who has the last word on what. In a couple, a disagreement is easier when you both know it won't end the relationship; between business partners, write down who decides which matters; between friends, a sporting rivalry can carry the competition kindly; between rivals, respect for the person opposite keeps the contest clean.

In their chart, the mirror image is your Mars in their 7th house, and it's your push they meet across the table. [Their Mars in your 6th house](/astrology/mars-in-6th-house-synastry/) is the colleague beside you; [their Mars in your 8th house](/astrology/mars-in-8th-house-synastry/) takes that force into trust, intimacy and what you share.

Your Descendant is your Ascendant seen from the other side of the sky, so it moves with your birth time, about a degree every four minutes; in Placidus, the house system I use, it is always the cusp of the 7th. Their Mars stands almost still for a few days whenever it turns retrograde or direct, and rarely moves enough in one day to change houses, so their time seldom decides this overlay, though the reading as a whole waits for both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). For a couple, I'd wait until you've shared a few months before reading this at all ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#what-i-ask)).
