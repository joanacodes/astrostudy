Tradition calls Scorpio the Moon's fall, and the old word sounds harsher than what it describes. I read a Scorpio Moon as feelings that run deep and stay private, given whole to the very few people who have earned them.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/) is fixed water. With the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) here, feelings don't pass through; they settle in and keep their shape for a long time. You register what happens beneath the surface of a room, the thing nobody is saying, and you remember it. Safety, for this Moon, comes from depth and from trust that has been tested. Once given, it's given completely, and a betrayal is felt to the bone.

Ptolemy gives Scorpio as the Moon's fall, the sign opposite its exaltation in Taurus. Where Taurus lets the Moon rest on what's solid and visible, I read Scorpio as asking it to feel what's hidden before it can relax: a harder condition, not a lesser heart. A [fall](/astrology/fall/), to me, describes how much effort comfort takes here. It says nothing about how deeply you can love, and a Scorpio Moon often loves more deeply than most.

Scorpio has two rulers, and I read both. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/), the modern one, shows where this Moon goes deep and how it renews itself after a loss. [Mars](/astrology/mars/), the traditional one, shows how it protects what it loves and where it draws its boundaries. When the two tell different stories, the reading lives in the space between them.

Scorpio keeps the Moon two and a half days or so each time round, which puts about 1 person in 12 under this Moon. Before it comes the [Moon in Libra](/astrology/moon-in-libra/), which settles through harmony; after it, the [Moon in Sagittarius](/astrology/moon-in-sagittarius/), which settles through space. If your birth date falls on one of those crossings, only the time of birth can place the Moon ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You love with everything, and you need to know it's safe to. Trust is slow: you watch, you test a little, you see what someone does with a small confidence before you give them a large one. Once you're sure, your loyalty is total, and you can hold a partner's darkest moods without flinching. What you need back is honesty, especially the uncomfortable kind. A pleasant surface with something hidden beneath it unsettles you far more than a hard truth does. Privacy matters too: being close doesn't mean showing everything all the time, and a partner who understands that gives this Moon room to open.

## At work {#at-work}

Depth suits you. Research, investigation, crisis work, therapy, anything that asks you to stay with what's difficult and keep a confidence can draw on this Moon's steadiness under pressure. You tend to notice what's really going on in an organisation long before it's announced. Shallow office politics bore you, and you may keep your own feelings well out of sight at work, which protects you but can leave colleagues unsure where they stand.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Old hurts. A hurt can be kept, examined and carried for years, long after the person who caused it has moved on. Testing people before trusting them can become a test nobody passes. And because you show so little on the surface, others may not realise how much you feel, which can leave you lonely inside a relationship. All or nothing is the pattern to watch: the door wide open, or bolted shut.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Let trust grow in stages instead of waiting for certainty: a small confidence, then a bigger one. Give the feelings you keep somewhere to go outside you, a journal, therapy, one person who can hear anything. When an old hurt surfaces, ask what it would take for it to change shape, since this Moon's strength is renewal, not just endurance. And let people see a little more of what you feel, a little earlier. They can only meet the depth they know is there.

Fall or no fall, the Moon got the same tick-or-cross in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) as everything else, and it wasn't among the three that came out far ahead: Saturn, Chiron and Lilith. Overall, about a third of the list held. So I read a Scorpio Moon gently and in depth, and never as a verdict on how anyone feels.
