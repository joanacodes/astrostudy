Some lots carry the name of a planet; others carry the name of a subject of life. The Part of Marriage is one of the second kind, a point the old astrologers built to look at marriage.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A lot takes the distance between two points of a chart and carries it over to a third, usually the Ascendant ([doctrine of the lots](/astrology/doctrine-of-the-lots/)). The best-known lots belong to planets: the seven [Hermetic lots](/astrology/hermetic-lots/), one for each traditional planet, from Fortune and Spirit to Nemesis. Lots named after subjects came alongside them, and their number kept growing: Abu Ma'shar's 9th-century list ran to 55 he thought significant, and the medieval lists mixed more than one tradition of formulas.

The Part of Marriage isn't among the seven. The nearest of them is Venus's lot, the Lot of Eros, counted from Spirit, but that one belongs to a planet, not to a subject.

Ptolemy, who avoided the lots apart from Fortune, gave marriage a chapter of its own in the fourth book of the *Tetrabiblos* and judged it without any such point. He read a wife from the Moon in a man's chart and a husband from the Sun in a woman's, and compared the lights of the two charts, an early form of synastry. Those were rules written for the marriages of his own world.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

For the Part of Marriage I give no formula. I haven't found one I could check against a source, and the old lists of subject lots draw on more than one tradition, so a formula without its author's name can send you looking for meaning in a degree nobody intended. If a book or a program gives you one, find out whose it is before you use it.

Whatever the formula, three things hold for this lot as for any other:

- **It needs the birth time.** It moves with the Ascendant, about a degree every four minutes, so a time out by an hour can shift it by some 15° on average, and without the time there is no lot at all ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
- **It may change by night.** Many lots reverse their count for a night birth, so the chart's [sect](/astrology/sect-day-and-night-charts/) has to be settled first.
- **It is placed like the others.** Astrologers who use it give it a sign and a house, and look at the planet ruling that sign and at any planet beside it.

The lots named for other subjects of family life, such as the [Part of Children](/astrology/part-of-children/), are built on the same principle.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The system I built for my readings draws two lots only, the Part of Fortune and the Part of Spirit; the Part of Marriage isn't one of them. Nor would I read it as the old books did. No point in a chart can tell whether you'll marry, when or whom, and a chart that seemed to promise a wedding could weigh on someone for years.

When a question is about partnership, what I read is how you meet it: the [7th house](/astrology/7th-house/), in Placidus, [Venus](/astrology/venus/) and the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/). In an adult's life they describe what you look for and how you relate, never an outcome. Between two people, [synastry](/synastry/) shows how two charts meet, and I read it only with both people's consent ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Part of Marriage shows whether you'll marry.”** It describes a theme in a chart. Whether and when you marry isn't written in any point.
- **“It's one of the seven Hermetic lots.”** It isn't; Venus's lot among the seven is Eros.
- **“It describes your future spouse.”** It's a point in your own chart, about a theme of your own life, not a portrait of anyone else.
- **“Ptolemy read marriage from this lot.”** He judged marriage from the Sun and the Moon, and avoided the lots apart from Fortune.
