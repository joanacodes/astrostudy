The sidereal zodiac answers the question many people ask the first time they hear about precession: why not keep the signs where the stars are? Some astrologers do exactly that, and knowing what they measure explains why the same birth can carry two different Sun signs.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

*Sidereal* comes from the Latin *sidus*, a star. A sidereal zodiac has the same twelve signs as any other, each 30° wide, in the same order from Aries to Pisces, but it fixes the start of Aries to a point among the fixed stars rather than to the March equinox. The signs then stay where they are against the stars, century after century.

It's the zodiac of [Vedic astrology (Jyotish)](/astrology/vedic-astrology-jyotish/), the tradition of India, which builds its own techniques on it. Some Western astrologers use it too, keeping Western methods but measuring the signs from the stars ([Sidereal and Vedic charts](/astrology/sidereal-and-vedic-charts/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The two zodiacs once began in much the same place. They drift apart because of the [precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/): the Earth's axis wobbles slowly, so the March equinox, where the [tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/) begins, slides back along the ecliptic by about 50 seconds of arc a year, roughly a degree every 72 years. The sidereal zodiac stays with the stars; the tropical one moves with the equinox.

The gap between them is called the ayanamsa, a Sanskrit word, and today it is about 24°. Sidereal astrologers don't all fix their starting point in the same place: there are several systems, Lahiri's among them, and their figures differ slightly. For most purposes, *about 24°* is the number to remember.

In practice, take a tropical position and move it back about 24°:

- **The Sun at 10° Gemini** in the tropical zodiac is at about 16° Taurus in the sidereal one: a different sign.
- **The Sun at 27° Gemini** is at about 3° Gemini: the same sign.
- **A rising sign** moves back in the same way, and so does every house cusp. With the same house system, the houses fall where they did; only the signs written on them change.

So anyone with a planet in the first 24° or so of a tropical sign will find it one sign earlier in a sidereal chart. That's most planets for most people, which is why a first Vedic reading can feel as if someone has swapped your chart.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read in the tropical zodiac, the frame of modern tropical astrology, and I don't mix the two: the meaning I give a planet comes with its tropical sign. But I don't think the sidereal zodiac is a mistake, and I wouldn't tell anyone who uses it that they're doing it wrong. The two traditions measure different things. The tropical zodiac follows the Earth's year and its turning points; the sidereal zodiac follows the stars behind the Sun. Each has a long history, and each has built its meanings to fit its own frame.

What I'd ask for is consistency. If you've had a Vedic reading and a Western one, compare them as two languages rather than two answers to the same sum: the Sun sign in each is right in its own system. And check which zodiac an app or a website uses before you read anything into the signs it gives you. The setting is often buried, and it moves most of the signs in a chart. ## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The sidereal zodiac uses the real constellations.”** It keeps twelve equal signs of 30°, like the tropical zodiac. It ties them to the stars, but it doesn't follow the constellations' uneven borders.
- **“Sidereal astrology proves Western astrology wrong.”** It shows that the two zodiacs start from different zeros. That was a choice on each side, and Ptolemy made the tropical one knowing about precession.
- **“The two zodiacs disagree about everything.”** They agree on the order of the signs and on the planets, and with the same house system, on the houses. They differ by about 24° in where each sign begins.
- **“My real sign is my sidereal sign.”** Your tropical and sidereal signs are both real, each in its own system. Which one speaks for you depends on the tradition you read in ([Did my sign change?](/astrology/did-my-sign-change/)).
