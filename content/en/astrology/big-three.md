Ask someone for their big three and you'll get three signs in one breath: Sun, Moon, rising. It's a handy shorthand, and a better one than a Sun sign alone, because each of the three answers a different question about a person.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Your [Sun](/astrology/sun/) sign is the sign the Sun was in when you were born: what you're aiming for, the way you want to grow. Your [Moon](/astrology/moon/) sign shows what you need to feel safe: comfort, moods, the way you look after yourself. Your rising sign, the sign on your [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), is how you meet the world: the first impression, the first reaction, the way you start things.

Three placements, three clocks. The Sun changes sign about every 30 days, so everyone born in the same month or so shares it; the Moon about every two and a half days; the rising sign about every two hours on average. That's why the big three are more personal than a Sun sign. Twelve Suns, twelve Moons and twelve rising signs make 1,728 combinations, and the rising sign alone can tell apart two people born in the same town on the same day, a few hours apart.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Read together, the three tend to explain each other. A Sun in a fire sign with a Moon in a water sign can feel like enthusiasm that keeps stopping to check how it feels; an earth rising sign over an air Sun often looks steadier from outside than it feels within. Where the three agree, things are simple; where they don't, you get the friction that makes people interesting. Their elements are a quick first comparison ([the four elements](/astrology/four-elements/)).

Each one needs different information:

- **The Sun sign** comes from the date, unless you were born on the day the Sun changed sign; then the time and place decide ([born on the cusp](/astrology/born-on-the-cusp/)).
- **The Moon sign** usually comes from the date as well, but on the day the Moon changes sign, only the time can say ([the Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).
- **The rising sign** needs the exact time and place. If you don't have yours, [how to find your rising sign](/astrology/how-to-find-your-rising-sign/) starts with tracking the time down.

The rising sign brings a fourth planet with it: its ruler, the [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/), which shows where your way of meeting life goes once it's through the door.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The big three is where I start, and I say so plainly: it's a first sketch, not the portrait. In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the Sun sign and the rising sign each held about a third of the time, close to what a flattering horoscope scores; the Moon, checked like every other placement, wasn't one of the three that held far more often. Those were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, and they're where the closest reading goes ([the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)).

So I read the big three for orientation: the tone of a chart, how a person is likely to come across, what they're likely to need. Then I spend the most time where the notebook says to, and I tell you which parts of a reading rest on placements that held less often. That's [how I read](/how-i-read/).

I also need all three, which means I need the time. Without it, the rising sign is missing and the houses go with it, so every planet loses the part of life it speaks to; that's why there's no reading until the time is found.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Your big three are your whole chart.”** They're three placements out of dozens: eight more planets, the houses, the aspects, the nodes. Two people can share a big three and live very different charts.
- **“You can work out your big three from your birthday.”** The date gives the Sun and usually the Moon. The rising sign takes the time and the place.
- **“Matching big threes make a good couple.”** Comparing two charts takes far more than six signs, and [Sun-sign compatibility](/astrology/sun-sign-compatibility-and-why-it-isnt-enough/) is the least reliable kind there is.
- **“Your Moon sign is the real you, and the Sun is for show.”** Each describes something real: the Sun what you're working towards, the Moon what you need on the way. Neither is a disguise.
