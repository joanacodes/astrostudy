Liz Greene's book on Saturn, in 1976, took the planet tradition called a malefic and read it through Jung's psychology. The books, the school and the research that followed made her one of the writers who gave psychological astrology its standing.

## Who {#who}

She is an astrologer and author whose books apply Jungian and other depth psychology to the birth chart; a newspaper interview of 1992 described her as a qualified Jungian analyst. She and Howard Sasportas, a psychosynthesis psychotherapist, former tutors at the Faculty of Astrological Studies in London, founded the Centre for Psychological Astrology there in 1983. After Sasportas died in 1992, the astrologer Charles Harvey became her co-director. The Faculty now lists her among its patrons.

From 1985 she worked with Alois Treindl, founder of the Swiss company Astrodienst, on computer-written interpretations designed to follow her own method of reading a chart; the first, the *Psychological Horoscope Analysis*, came out in 1987.

Her books run from *Saturn: A New Look at an Old Devil* (1976), *Relating* (1977), *The Outer Planets & Their Cycles* (1983) and *The Astrology of Fate* (1984) to *The Astrological Neptune and the Quest for Redemption* (1996), *The Dark of the Soul: Psychopathology in the Horoscope* (2003), *Chiron in Love* (2023) and *By Jove: The Meaning of the Astrological Jupiter* (2024). With Sasportas she wrote four more, from *The Development of the Personality* (1987) to *The Inner Planets* (1993). She has also written novels, and created *The Mythic Tarot* (1986) with Juliet Sharman-Burke.

She is a historian as well. In 2010 the University of Bristol awarded her a doctorate for a thesis on the Kabbalah in British occultism from 1860 to 1940; she has co-edited *Sky and Symbol* with Nicholas Campion for the Sophia Centre Press; and in 2018 Routledge published *Jung's Studies in Astrology*, her study of Jung's own astrology. The United Astrology Congress has given her its Regulus Award for Theory and Understanding.

## What they changed {#what-they-changed}

She read the planets one at a time, and in depth. Her titles show where she looked: Saturn, the outer planets, Neptune, Chiron, Jupiter, the luminaries, fate. *Saturn* is the clearest case, recasting the old image of a planet of misfortune in Jungian terms ([Psychological astrology](/astrology/psychological-astrology/)). The model behind her books is [Carl Jung](/astrology/carl-jung/)'s, the frame is depth psychology, and in 1984 she and Stephen Arroyo published *The Jupiter/Saturn Conference Lectures* together.

She took the same psychology into relationships. *Relating* (1977) is subtitled *An Astrological Guide to Living with Others on a Small Planet*. Reading two people's charts together is something I do only with both people's knowledge ([Synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

Two of her subjects stood out in my own notebook. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) and [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/), with Lilith, were the three placements that held 75 to 90% of the time, for me and for the people I tested ([The three that held: Saturn, Chiron and Lilith](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)). That says nothing about whether her readings of them are right; my notebook only recorded that those placements described the people I tested far more often than the rest did.

She put a method into software. The interpretations she wrote with Astrodienst made a reading modelled on her way of working something that could come from a computer rather than from a consultation.

She also wrote about psychopathology in the horoscope, and there I keep to my own line. A chart isn't a diagnosis, and when what someone carries is psychological or medical, I say so and send them to the right door ([Astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/)).

Her *Saturn* came eight years after [Linda Goodman](/astrology/linda-goodman/)'s *Sun Signs* and went the other way, from the twelve Sun signs to a single planet read in depth. The 2011 edition carries a foreword by [Robert Hand](/astrology/robert-hand/).
