Fomalhaut is the bright star in the mouth of the Southern Fish, best seen low over the southern horizon in autumn from Europe. Of the four royal stars in astrology books it comes last along the zodiac, at 4°13′ Pisces in 2026, and its crown rests on the shakiest ground.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Ptolemy counted it as part of Aquarius; Johann Bayer gave it to the Southern Fish in the seventeenth century, as α Piscis Austrini, and there it stays, magnitude 1.16 and some 25 light-years away. From 40° north it climbs only 20° above the horizon and is up for eight hours, so from Britain or France it never rises far.

Astronomers know it for its wide rings of dust. Fomalhaut b, the first candidate planet ever photographed in visible light, now looks like an expanding cloud of debris from a collision rather than a planet.

In the tropical zodiac it opens [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/), at 2°52′ in 1930, 3°51′ in 2000 and 4°13′ in 2026. Its latitude, 21.1° south of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), matters more than the degree, for it turns every contact with Fomalhaut into a line drawn along the zodiac rather than a meeting in the sky.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

Its name is Arabic, fam al-ḥūt, the mouth of the fish, a translation of the way Ptolemy described its place in the figure; the Latin names say the same, *os piscis meridiani*, the mouth of the southern fish. Early Arab sky-watchers had a folk name for it too, the first frog.

Fomalhaut's royal title comes from R. H. Allen's *Star Names* (1899), which places it among the Persians' four royal stars with [Antares](/astrology/antares/), Aldebaran and Regulus. The Persian sources are less obliging. The Zoroastrian texts do set a commander over the southern quarter of the sky, Sadwēsh, and older scholars identified him with Fomalhaut, or else with Canopus. Both identifications have been challenged, and in 1942 W. B. Henning argued that the southern commander was most likely Antares. Of the four royal stars, the Southern Fish's is the crown most in doubt.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The [Sun conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/sun-conjunct-fomalhaut/) belongs to late-February birthdays, about the 21st and 22nd: roughly 1 person in 183 from 1930 to 2009, a Sun in the first degrees of Pisces. The [Moon conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/moon-conjunct-fomalhaut/), at 1 in 184, is as frequent, and so is [Saturn conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-fomalhaut/), 1 in 183, for births from March to November 1935, from April 1964 to January 1965, or in February and March 1994. Mars and Venus trail behind, [Mars conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/mars-conjunct-fomalhaut/) turning up once in 210 births and [Venus conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/venus-conjunct-fomalhaut/) once in 216.

A crown in doubt, a contact made by degree alone, and a system for my readings that places no stars: three reasons this page describes Fomalhaut rather than reads it. The rule the tradition uses is set out in [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/), and if Fomalhaut sits on one of your planets, that planet's own sign and house will say far more about you than the mouth of a fish ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).
