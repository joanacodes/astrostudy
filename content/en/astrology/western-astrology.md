Western astrology is the one most people picture when they hear the word at all: the Sun signs, the twelve houses, a wheel with the planets drawn around it. It has a long family tree, and knowing it explains a good deal about why a chart looks the way it does.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

It's the branch of [astrology](/astrology/astrology/) that grew up around the Mediterranean and in Europe, and spread worldwide from there. Its oldest layer is Babylonian: centuries of watching the sky for omens, and then the first birth charts ([Babylonian astrology](/astrology/babylonian-astrology/)). Its shape, though, comes from the Greek-speaking Mediterranean, where from about the first century BCE astrologers read individual birth charts with tools still in use today: signs, places counted from the rising sign, the planets' rulerships, the aspects ([Hellenistic astrology](/astrology/hellenistic-astrology/)).

From there it passed into Persian and Arabic ([Persian and Arabic astrology](/astrology/persian-and-arabic-astrology/)), returned to Latin in medieval Europe ([Medieval European astrology](/astrology/medieval-european-astrology/)), lost its standing after the seventeenth century ([The decline after the 17th century](/astrology/decline-after-the-17th-century/)) and came back in the twentieth, first in a more psychological form ([Psychological astrology](/astrology/psychological-astrology/)), then with a return to the old texts ([The traditional revival](/astrology/traditional-revival/)). Each stage left something in the charts you see today.

Three things set it apart. It uses the [tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/), in which the signs are counted from the March equinox and stay tied to the seasons. It reads a chart through twelve houses as well as twelve signs. And since 1781 it has added planets nobody in antiquity could see: Uranus that year, Neptune in 1846, Pluto in 1930.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A Western chart is read in layers. The planets are the functions. The twelve signs of the [zodiac](/astrology/zodiac/) give them their styles, each sign with an element, a modality and a ruling planet. The houses set each planet in an area of life, and there are several ways of drawing them, Placidus, Whole Sign and Equal among the best known ([Which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)). The aspects, the angles between planets, relate one function to another. Around them sit calculated points: the lunar nodes, Lilith, the Part of Fortune.

The outer planets brought a change of their own. Before 1781, Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces were ruled by Mars, Saturn and Jupiter; once Uranus, Neptune and Pluto were found, many astrologers gave those signs to the newcomers. Western astrology now carries both sets ([Traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)).

Its nearest neighbour is [Vedic astrology (Jyotish)](/astrology/vedic-astrology-jyotish/), which shares the signs and the planets visible to the eye but counts the zodiac from the stars rather than the equinox, so the same sky lands about 24° earlier in its signs, and builds its own techniques on that. Some Western astrologers use a star-based zodiac too ([sidereal zodiac](/astrology/sidereal-zodiac/)). [Chinese astrology](/astrology/chinese-astrology/) is a different system altogether, built on the calendar and its twelve animal years.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

What I practise is modern tropical astrology with traditional techniques. In practice that means a few fixed choices. In Placidus, the house system I use, the angles sit on the cusps of the 1st, 4th, 7th and 10th houses, and the houses come out unequal. For Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces, I read both rulers, the old and the new. I look at both lunar nodes, mean and true, and say so when they fall in different signs. And I don't read a chart without its birth time, because without it there are no houses and no rising sign ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

None of that makes the other choices wrong. A Whole Sign astrologer or a Vedic one reading the same birth will tell you some things I wouldn't, and the honest thing is to say which tradition a reading comes from. Mine is set out in [How I read](/how-i-read/), along with what held when I tested it.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Western astrology got the signs wrong when the stars moved.”** The signs were never the constellations. Ptolemy counted them from the equinoxes and solstices, and the tropical zodiac is a choice, not a slip.
- **“Vedic astrology is the real thing and the Western kind a copy.”** They're two traditions with different zodiacs and different techniques. Neither is a corrupted version of the other.
- **“Western astrology is just Sun signs.”** The Sun-sign column is a newspaper form of the twentieth century ([horoscope](/astrology/horoscope/)). The tradition itself reads the whole chart.
- **“Traditional techniques and modern tropical astrology don't mix.”** I combine them in every reading: Mars and Pluto both have a say in Scorpio, and a planet found in 1930 sits in the same chart as rulerships set down in antiquity.
