With the Moon in Aquarius, you often need to stand a little way back from a feeling to see it clearly. Close up it's noise; at arm's length it makes sense, and then you can let it in.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/) is fixed air. With the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) here, feelings are processed through understanding: you work out what you feel by thinking it through, often by talking it over with a friend who won't make a fuss. From outside this can look like distance; from inside, it's how you stay steady. Fixed air also makes this Moon consistent. Your loyalties, to people and to principles, rarely change once you've settled them, and a friendship of twenty years can matter to you as much as any romance.

Tradition attaches no dignity to the Moon in Aquarius; its condition here comes from the rest of the chart, not from the sign.

Two rulers share Aquarius, and I read both. [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), the ruler modern astrologers give it, marks where this Moon needs room to be itself, to differ, to change the rules. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), the one the older books give, marks the commitments and boundaries that let it stay close without feeling caged. Autonomy isn't isolation, and an Aquarius Moon that is well understood usually has both: its freedom, and a few people it would cross the country for.

Each pass the Moon makes through Aquarius lasts about two and a half days, which is why it belongs to about 1 person in 12. On one side sits the [Moon in Capricorn](/astrology/moon-in-capricorn/), which copes by taking responsibility; on the other, the [Moon in Pisces](/astrology/moon-in-pisces/), which feels its way into other people's moods. For a birth on the day of the change, I need the time before I can tell you which ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Friendship is the ground your love grows from. The partner who suits you best is also your friend: someone who respects your time alone, your opinions and your odd projects, and who doesn't need constant reassurance to feel secure. Equality matters, and so does honesty about what each of you wants. Intense emotional demands can make you step back, not because you don't care but because you need a moment to understand what's happening. Telling a partner so, *I need a minute to think, I'm not leaving*, can save a great deal of hurt.

## At work {#at-work}

You do well in teams with a shared purpose: a cause, an idea, a project that might make things better for many people. You often see how a system could work more fairly, and you're willing to say so. Being micromanaged, or working somewhere that runs on unspoken rules and office feelings, wears you down. You bring calm in a crisis, a clear head when others are flooded, and real loyalty to colleagues you respect.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Thinking about a feeling can take the place of having it. You can describe your sadness accurately and still not have felt it. Your own needs may come last, behind the group's, the cause's or a friend's, and it can be hard to admit you need someone in particular. People who feel through closeness may read your distance as coldness, and you may read their closeness as pressure. Neither reading is quite right.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Say a feeling plainly, in one short sentence, before you analyse it: *I'm lonely*, *I'm hurt*. Keep at least one friend you can be awkward and uncertain with. Build freedom into your commitments rather than keeping commitment at bay: separate evenings, shared adventures. And let yourself need people one by one, not only as a group. Wanting one person's company isn't a loss of independence.

My notebook was a small experiment, the kind an Aquarius Moon might enjoy: every placement ticked or crossed, my own chart first, then other people's with their consent ([the invoice method](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). The Moon didn't finish among the leaders, which were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, and overall roughly a third of my ticks and crosses were ticks. I read this Moon with that in mind, and I say so when it matters.
