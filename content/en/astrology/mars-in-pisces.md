Water finds its way round a rock without ever fighting it. Mars in Pisces works like that: it rarely meets an obstacle head on, and it's often already past it before anyone noticed it moving.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is effort and assertion: the push towards what you want, and the defence of what matters. [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/), mutable water, belongs to imagination, compassion and the softening of edges. In Pisces, Mars acts on instinct and feeling, often for someone else, and its strength is adaptability: you change course without fuss, sense what a situation needs, and can work very hard for a cause, a person or a dream that moves you.

Two planets rule Pisces, and I read both. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/), which modern tropical astrology gives to Pisces, adds imagination and compassion: this Mars is moved by what it can picture and by what others feel. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/), its traditional ruler, adds meaning: the effort has to be *for* something larger than itself. Their signs and houses show which pull is stronger in you. Mars has no dignity here, in either direction.

Mars usually crosses Pisces in about 39 days, as fast as it crosses any sign, and even its longest stay here, 186 days with a retrograde, is shorter than its longest in any other sign. For births between 1930 and 2009 that comes to about 1 person in 14; the people born in the same few weeks share it, and the house shows where you act on others' behalf. From Pisces, Mars moves on into Aries, its own sign. Born on the day it crossed, only the time of birth can say which side of the line it was on ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). [Mars in Aquarius](/astrology/mars-in-aquarius/), before it, fights for the group's ideas; [Mars in Aries](/astrology/mars-in-aries/), after it, for its own first step.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, Mars in Pisces is tender and romantic. You pursue through imagination, gentleness and attention to what the other person feels, and you may act for a partner more readily than for yourself. Direct conflict is uncomfortable: you'd rather let a disagreement dissolve than face it, which can leave it unresolved underneath. Compassion isn't self-erasure, though. The relationships that work let you say *I want this too*, and treat your sensitivity as a strength rather than something to manage.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, this Mars does its best when it believes in what it's doing: care work, the arts, music, film, charities, teaching, any role with a human or creative purpose. You tend to work in waves, long bursts when inspired and slack water when not, and you're often better at sensing what a team needs than at fighting for your own position. Rigid targets with no meaning attached drain you fast. A clear structure round a meaningful task helps most.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Boundaries. Saying no can feel unkind, so you may take on other people's battles and leave your own unfought. Anger tends to turn into tiredness or sadness rather than action, and you may not realise you were angry until much later. Effort can drift when it has no clear shape, and the urge to rescue can draw you towards people who never asked to be rescued.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Put edges round the effort: a start time, an end time, a finished piece. Before you help, ask whether help was wanted. Give the drive a channel with rhythm in it, dance, swimming, music, so that feeling can turn into movement. Write down one thing a week that you want for yourself, and do it. And remember that a gentle approach is still an approach: this Mars gets there by flowing, but it does get there.
