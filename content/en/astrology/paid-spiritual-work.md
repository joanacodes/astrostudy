It's sold under many names: spiritual work, rituals, bringing back a lost love, opening the way, protection. What the offers share is the shape of the deal: someone performs something on your behalf, somewhere you can't see, and you pay for a result before you can know whether it came.

## The short answer {#the-short-answer}

No one can be paid to change another person's feelings, a judge's decision, an employer's choice or your luck by working on it out of sight. I don't sell any ‘work’ of any kind: no rituals, no protections, no returns of lost love. A reading from me is something you read and think over, [a conversation with yourself rather than an instruction](/how-i-read/), and never a service performed on anyone else.

Faith, prayer and ritual belong to the people who practise them, and this page isn't about anyone's beliefs. It's about a transaction: money for an outcome that no one can show you and no one can check.

## Why it matters {#why-it-matters}

Three things set paid spiritual work apart from anything you could test:

- **You see the bill, never the work.** You can't watch a ritual done for you in another city. You're told it has begun, that it's going well, that it needs one more step. When something good happens, the work takes the credit; when nothing does, there's a reason that costs more: a stronger opponent, a curse underneath, a second ritual. That's how one fee becomes a series, by the same arithmetic as [curse removal scams](/astrology/curse-removal-scams/).
- **It makes promises about other people.** Bringing back a partner means bending another adult's choice, and even if it could be done, it shouldn't be: someone who has left a relationship had the right to leave it. It's the principle that stops me reading [a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/): another person's chart, and another person's heart, aren't ours to work on in secret.
- **It costs more than money.** While you wait for the work to act, you're not grieving, applying for the next job or finding a lawyer. The waiting is part of the price.

There's nothing foolish in being tempted. When someone has left, or a decision hangs over you, doing something feels better than waiting, and a ritual sells exactly that feeling. The feeling is real. What comes with it is a promise nobody can keep, and the only part you can count on is the invoice.

The ritual can be sold by whoever first ‘saw’ the problem, in a reading you never asked for. [Fake psychics](/astrology/fake-psychics/) is about that other half of the business: the vision that comes before the invoice.

## What to do instead {#what-to-do-instead}

Before paying anyone for anything in this field, astrologers included, ask three questions and listen closely to the answers:

1. **What exactly will you do?** A vague answer, ‘I'll work on your energy’, is an answer.
2. **How will I know it worked?** If nothing you could see or check would show it, the result can never be disputed, and neither can the bill.
3. **What happens if it doesn't?** If the reply is more work at more cost, you've learnt what you needed to know.

Then go back to what you hoped the work would change, and to who can really help with it. A break-up needs time, sleep and people who love you. A job search needs applications, and someone to read them over with you. A court case needs a lawyer. None of that is magic, and all of it moves something.

If you've already paid and you're being asked for more, stop there. Getting out takes the same steps as with a curse: keep every record, talk to someone close to you, call your bank, and report what happened ([How to report a scam](/astrology/how-to-report-a-scam/)). And before you book anyone, me included, [How to tell a careful astrologer from a scam](/astrology/how-to-tell-a-careful-astrologer-from-a-scam/) gathers the signs worth checking.
