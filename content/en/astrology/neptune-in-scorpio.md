Some placements reach for the sky; Neptune in Scorpio goes underneath. For the generation born from December 1955 to November 1970, the dream is depth itself: to know someone, and be known, all the way down.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/), fixed water, goes deep and stays: it wants the truth under the surface and doesn't let go easily. That changes the way [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) dissolves boundaries. Here its compassion is the kind that can sit with someone in their worst hour, and its imagination is drawn to what other people would rather not look at: secrets, longing, loss, the life of dreams and symbols. The sign is fixed, so this longing doesn't drift from one ideal to the next. It settles on one and holds.

Scorpio has two rulers, and I read both. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/), its modern ruler, shows where this dream of depth wants to renew something at the root; [Mars](/astrology/mars/), the traditional one, shows how it goes after what it desires. Neptune itself has no dignity here, no exaltation and no fall, so it is those two planets that tell me how a Scorpio Neptune moves through your chart.

From December 1955 to November 1970 Neptune stayed in Scorpio, which gives the placement to about 1 person in 6 born between 1930 and 2009, a generation now in its mid-fifties to about seventy. Until August 1957 Neptune was still crossing back into Libra, and from January 1970 it was stepping forward into Sagittarius, so for births in those months the chart has to decide between this sign and [Neptune in Libra](/astrology/neptune-in-libra/) or [Neptune in Sagittarius](/astrology/neptune-in-sagittarius/). Everyone you went to school with probably shares your Scorpio Neptune. What they don't share is its house, and the house is where this longing for depth becomes yours: the part of life you most want to understand from the inside.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Intimacy is the heart of this placement. You may want a closeness with no masks at all, where nothing is hidden and everything can be said, and you offer the same: loyalty, and a willingness to see a partner's shadows and love them anyway. That is a real gift. It can also blur where you end and the other person begins. When merging feels like the point, privacy can feel like a betrayal, and an intense bond can pass for a true one. The relationships that hold tend to be deep and slow, with room for each of you to keep a few rooms of your own.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, Neptune in Scorpio looks beneath. Research, psychology, investigation, crisis work, the care of people going through the worst of something, writing or film that goes where others won't: these suit an imagination that isn't afraid of the dark. You may sense what isn't being said in a meeting long before anyone says it. The risk is carrying other people's secrets, or their pain, as if they were yours to keep.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

A gift for seeing what's hidden can turn into seeing it everywhere. This Neptune can idealise intensity, as if only what hurts were real, or hold on to a dream long after it stopped holding you. Compassion for someone's darkness can keep you close to a person who doesn't return it. And because Scorpio is fixed, letting go, of a grudge, a hope or an old loyalty, may be the hardest thing this placement is asked to do.

## What helps {#what-helps}

My [notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) went beneath the surface too, one tick or cross at a time. Neptune's sign and house had their turn like every placement. Neptune isn't among the three that held far more often, which were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, and about a third of the whole notebook held.

What helps most is letting some things be simple: not every silence hides something, and not every bond needs to be total. Give the depth a form you choose, a journal, a craft, a conversation with someone trained to listen. And practise letting go in small ways, a plan, an opinion, an evening, so that the large letting-go isn't the first one you ever try.
