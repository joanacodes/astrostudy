Mercury in Aquarius likes to look at a problem from somewhere slightly outside it. From there the pattern is easier to see, and so are the rules everyone follows without asking why, which this mind will ask, politely and more than once.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/), fixed air, [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) thinks in systems: how the parts connect, what the whole is for, what would change if one rule were dropped. Fixed air is a paradox worth knowing about. It makes a mind open to any idea and very hard to move once it has reasoned its way to one. You think for yourself, you're drawn to the future more than the past, and you're often a step ahead of the conversation, which can be lonely as well as useful.

Aquarius is where tradition puts the Sun in its detriment, opposite Leo; Mercury, here, has no dignity either way.

Like Scorpio and Pisces, Aquarius has two rulers: [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), which held it for the whole of the tradition, and [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), which modern tropical astrology gave it after the planet was discovered in 1781, and I read the pair together ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)). Uranus gives this Mercury its leaps, the sudden connection, the idea that arrives whole. Saturn gives it structure, the patience to turn a leap into a working system. Where each one sits in your chart shows which tends to lead.

Roughly 1 person in 12 thinks with this Mercury, going by everyone born 1930–2009. A stay usually lasts about 19 days; a retrograde turn in Aquarius can stretch it to 67, a long rethink in a sign that likes rethinking.

[Mercury in Capricorn](/astrology/mercury-in-capricorn/) trusts what has lasted and [Mercury in Pisces](/astrology/mercury-in-pisces/) trusts what it senses; Mercury in Aquarius trusts what it can reason out for itself. The Sun is close by in any case. With a Capricorn Sun, a builder by temperament has an unconventional mind; with a Pisces Sun, a porous, feeling self has a clear, cool way of thinking; with the Sun in Aquarius as well, independence runs all the way through.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You talk to a partner the way you'd talk to a close friend: honestly, about ideas, with room to disagree. You want to be understood, and you want someone who thinks for themselves. Feelings may be approached through analysis first, *why do I feel this?*, which can sound detached when you're actually being careful. A partner who enjoys a debate, and doesn't mistake your calm for distance, gets this Mercury at its warmest. You may need more space than most, including the space to come round to a new view in your own time.

## At work {#at-work}

Technology, science, research, systems design, policy, social causes, community work: anything that asks what the future should look like. You're the colleague who spots the flaw in the process and proposes a new one, and you work best with freedom to experiment. Hierarchies that can't explain themselves frustrate you. A team with a shared purpose brings out your best ideas, and your generosity with them.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Holding on to an idea after the facts have moved, because you reasoned your way to it and reasoning feels like proof. Being contrary for the sake of it, or seeming to be. Turning a feeling into a theory before you've let yourself feel it. And impatience with *we've always done it this way*, which is sometimes a good reason after all.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Test your best idea against someone who disagrees, and listen for the part they're right about. Turn a concept into an example a friend could follow. Name the feeling before you explain it. Keep a list of the ideas you've changed your mind about: it's good evidence that an independent mind can also be an open one. In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), about a third of the Mercury placements I checked held, which seems to suit an Aquarian mind: a result to think with, not a rule to obey.
