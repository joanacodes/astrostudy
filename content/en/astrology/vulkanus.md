Two hypothetical planets carry the smith god's name, at opposite ends of the Solar System. Vulcan was sought inside Mercury's orbit in the nineteenth century; Vulkanus is a point of Uranian astrology, out beyond Neptune, proposed in 1927.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Friedrich Sieggrün proposed Vulkanus as the third of his four points, after [Admetos](/astrology/admetos/) and before [Poseidon](/astrology/poseidon/), extending the four that Alfred Witte had already set beyond Neptune ([The Hamburg School (Uranian astrology)](/astrology/hamburg-school-uranian-astrology/)). It is spelled two ways: Vulkanus, as on the school's list, and Vulcanus, the god's Latin name, which is also the name Unicode gives its symbol. Software still draws it: the free program Astrolog names Vulkanus among the Uranian points it computes.

Vulcan was the Roman god of fire, of volcanoes and of the forge, shown with a blacksmith's hammer and honoured at the Vulcanalia every 23 August; the Greeks called him Hephaestus. In Greek myth Hephaestus was thrown out of Olympus, by his mother, Hera, because he was born lame, or in another account by Zeus, for taking Hera's side. In that version he fell for a whole day and landed on Lemnos, where the islanders cared for him and taught him his craft. He became the gods' smith, maker of all their weapons, and in time he went back up to Olympus. One story has him settle the score first: he sent Hera a golden chair with invisible fetters, which held her fast the moment she sat down, and he listened to no god but Dionysus, who made him drunk and brought him home.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Uranian astrologers read Vulkanus for overcoming the difficulties a person carries through life, and they take the reading straight from the smith's story: cast out of Olympus, he came back to make what the other gods needed. They also read it for raw force, in their words a brute force of masculine energy that wants freedom.

*Masculine* is astrology's old word for active, outgoing force, the word it uses for Mars. Every chart has a Vulkanus, whoever was born.

The god himself was a figure of fire under the ground: Greek settlers in southern Italy linked Hephaestus with the volcano gods of the region, Adranus of Etna and Vulcanus of the Lipari islands. The Uranian reading draws on the smith and his return rather than on the volcanoes.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Vulkanus isn't in my readings. Force belongs there to [Mars](/astrology/mars/), how you go after what you want: its sign tells me how someone pushes, its house where the push goes, and its aspects whether it runs freely or snags. The wish to be free belongs to [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) and the house it falls in.

As for the difficulties someone has carried through life: a chart can't see a body's limits, and I don't read one for health ([Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)). What a person has had to overcome, I'd rather hear from them than read off a point no one has seen ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Vulkanus is the planet Vulcan.”** Vulcan was a planet sought between Mercury and the Sun, announced in 1860 and given up after 1915; Vulkanus is a Uranian point beyond Neptune, proposed in 1927 ([Vulcan](/astrology/vulcan/)).
- **“Vulkanus only matters in men's charts.”** *Masculine* is the tradition's word for active force; the point stands in every chart.
- **“Vulkanus means anger.”** Uranian astrologers read it for force and the will to be free; anger, in my readings, is one of the things Mars describes.
