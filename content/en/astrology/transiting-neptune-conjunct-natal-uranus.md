This conjunction belongs to childhood or to no one. Neptune can only catch up with a Uranus that stood a little ahead of it at birth, and that became possible only as the two planets met in the sky in 1993; among people born between 1930 and 2009, the transit came to those born from the early 1990s on, always while they were children, and to nobody else.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) is the part of a chart that wants to change things: to invent, to break a pattern, to try another way. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) dissolves boundaries and imagines beyond them. In a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) two functions work as one process, so under this [transit](/astrology/transits/) invention and imagination merge: a sense that the world could be quite different, and an ease with what can't yet be named.

A transit that early isn't a season anyone chose how to live, so I don't read it as one. I don't read children's charts at all ([children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)), and when an adult asks me about it, I treat it as part of their background rather than their story: something in the air of their first years, not an explanation of who they became.

It also sits on top of something larger. Uranus and Neptune met three times in 1993, in Capricorn, and everyone born from 1990 to 1996 carries [Uranus conjunct Neptune](/astrology/uranus-conjunct-neptune/) in the birth chart; for many of them the transit is the same meeting coming back to their own chart within a few years.

Where it fell depends on the house of your Uranus, which needs your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)); for an adult, that house is still where the urge to reinvent things with imagination tends to live.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Like every Neptune transit with my orbs, 1½° before exact to 1° after, it lasted about two years and seven months, rarely under twenty-two, with three exact hits in most passes and five in about one in four.

Nearly 4 people in 5 born between 1930 and 2009 never meet it, and those who did met it once, between their first months and about 13: in the first months for someone born at the start of 1995, at about 7 for 2005. Born before the early 1990s, with Uranus still behind Neptune, you won't meet it in a lifetime.

The next generation to meet it is the one born with Uranus in Aries, from 2010 to 2019: Neptune's years in Aries bring it to them between about 13 and 20, most of them still younger than anyone I read for.

For someone born at the start of 1995, the conjunction came in the first months; [transiting Neptune sextile natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-neptune-sextile-natal-uranus/) follows at about 27, [transiting Neptune square natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-neptune-square-natal-uranus/) at about 40½ and [transiting Neptune trine natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-neptune-trine-natal-uranus/) at about 54. [Transiting Neptune opposite natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-neptune-opposite-natal-uranus/) lies near 80 or beyond.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Read it as your generation's story before your own. The conjunction of 1993 colours everyone born around it; the transit was a private echo of it.

Look at the house your Uranus occupies in your adult chart. That's where the theme of inventing with imagination still asks for room.

If you're the parent of a child born in the 2010s, let the chart wait: it's theirs to open when they're grown.
