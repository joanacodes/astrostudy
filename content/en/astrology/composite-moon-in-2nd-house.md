Some relationships feel safest when they know what they have: savings put aside together, a few good things looked after, a clear idea of what the two of you value and what you can do without. A composite Moon in the 2nd house ties the bond's sense of security to what it holds in common.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

The [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) sets its [Moon](/astrology/moon/) halfway between your two natal Moons, and the house it lands in shows where the relationship looks for comfort. [The 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/), succedent, with Taurus as its natural sign, covers resources, possessions, values and self-worth. With the Moon here, the pair's feelings are bound up with what it shares and keeps: the furniture chosen together, the habits of spending and saving, the things you would both rescue first.

These aren't either person's resources. Your own [Moon in the 2nd house](/astrology/moon-in-2nd-house/) would describe how safe you feel with what you have; [their Moon in your 2nd house](/astrology/moon-in-2nd-house-synastry/), how they touch your sense of worth. The composite's 2nd is what the two of you hold as a pair, from a joint account to a shared taste, and the security this Moon looks for is the feeling of standing on something you built together. Values count as much as objects: the relationship settles when you agree on what matters. Taurus, the 2nd's natural sign, is where tradition exalts the Moon, and the house shares some of that feeling: comfort here is concrete, something you can touch, count on and come back to. Exaltation belongs to a sign rather than a house, though, so the composite Moon's own sign still sets its manner.

Its difficulty is that worry about having enough can become the bond's main mood. A pair whose comfort depends on what it holds may cling to things or routines, read every expense as a threat, or measure its worth by what it owns. Resources are not wealth, and security is not fear. Better to keep naming the values underneath the possessions, which no setback can take away.

Money is where I'm careful. No chart, composite or natal, can say whether a pair will gain or lose money, and I don't read one for that ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)). The house itself needs care too. Even once a house system is chosen, Placidus is mine, there are two ways to build composite houses, from the midpoints of the two charts' cusps or from the composite Midheaven at the place where you live, and they can put this Moon in different houses. Both need both birth times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)).

Before it comes the [composite Moon in the 1st house](/astrology/composite-moon-in-1st-house/), whose feelings show on its surface; after it, the [composite Moon in the 3rd house](/astrology/composite-moon-in-3rd-house/), which settles through talk and daily contact. The composite is described here as a technique rather than a reading I offer, and those who use it set this Moon near the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) and the two natal charts. It describes what makes a relationship feel secure, not what it will earn or own.
