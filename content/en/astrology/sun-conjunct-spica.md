Sun conjunct Spica belongs to the birthdays of 16 and 17 October: a Sun in late Libra on the degree of the ear of wheat in the Virgin's hand. Unlike most star contacts, this one is also a meeting in the sky, since Spica lies only two degrees from the Sun's path.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) as your sense of direction and the vitality that keeps you moving along it. [Spica](/astrology/spica/) has gentle names, and almost all of them are about grain. R. H. Allen recorded one more that suits an October birthday: Alaraph, the grape-gatherer or gleaner. Laid on the Sun, those names suggest a purpose that grows and ripens rather than one that conquers, and I'm happy to pass that picture on, as a picture.

The Persians went further. When the Zoroastrian Bundahishn lists the twelve zodiac constellations, as W. B. Henning translated them, the sixth is not the Virgin but Spica itself: the star once named the whole sign. [Precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/) has since moved it on: in the tropical zodiac it now stands in Libra, at 22°51′ in 1930 and 24°12′ in 2026.

So the Sun on Spica is always a [Sun in Libra](/astrology/sun-in-libra/), which the tradition counts as the Sun's [fall](/astrology/fall/). Fall is an old measure of fit, not of worth, and it belongs to the sign rather than the star: every Sun from about 23 September to 22 October is in Libra, and Spica marks only two of those days. A Libra Sun finds its purpose through proportion, judgement and other people rather than by striking out alone, and that is the part of this contact I would actually read, together with the Sun's house and aspects.

In the sky the meeting is real. Spica lies 2.1° south of the ecliptic, so each year around 16 October the Sun passes a little more than two degrees north of it. The star is invisible while that happens, lost in daylight, and it reappears in the dawn about two weeks later, at its [heliacal rising](/astrology/heliacal-rising/), the first morning it can be seen again. Your birthday falls in the weeks when Spica is hidden by the Sun.

About 1 person in 181 born between 1930 and 2009 has the Sun there, on 16 or 17 October whatever the year. The date settles the contact; the hour of birth adds the house it falls in.

Because Spica sits so near the Sun's path, its other contacts can be close in the sky as well as in the zodiac. The [Moon conjunct Spica](/astrology/moon-conjunct-spica/) can cover the star outright; [Venus conjunct Spica](/astrology/venus-conjunct-spica/) has passed within half a degree of it; [Mars conjunct Spica](/astrology/mars-conjunct-spica/) has come within a degree and a quarter; [Saturn conjunct Spica](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-spica/) goes by four or five degrees to the north. The Sun's two degrees sit in the middle of that range.

None of the charts I read include Spica or any other star ([How I read](/how-i-read/)), so this page describes the contact rather than interprets it. How the tradition counts a star on a planet, and why the conjunction is the only contact that counts, is set out in [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/).
