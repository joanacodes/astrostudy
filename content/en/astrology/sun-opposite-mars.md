Sun opposite Mars is the rarest of the five major aspects the two can make. You were born on one of the few days, every couple of years, when Mars stood across the sky from the Sun: retrograde, near its closest to the Earth, rising as the Sun set. In your chart your purpose and your drive face each other across the circle.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is identity and purpose, the self you keep becoming. [Mars](/astrology/mars/), seen from the Sun's side here, is the function that acts on things: initiative, assertion, effort, competing, defending a boundary. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) relates them through polarity: each keeps its identity, and each becomes clearer for being seen from the other end.

In plain words, your drive doesn't sit inside your purpose; it stands across from it. Often it shows up first in other people: the rival who seems to have all the push, the partner or colleague who acts while you're still deciding, the person you keep ending up in contests with. Competition is not hostility, though, and an opposition isn't a feud. Those encounters are where you find out how much force is yours, and how you want to use it.

Over time the work is to take both ends back: to let your sense of who you are include your own assertion, and to let your effort answer to your purpose rather than to whoever happens to be pushing. When that happens, I find people with this aspect unusually fair in a contest. They know what it's like on both sides of the line.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The two fall in opposite signs that share a modality: a Sun in Gemini with Mars in Sagittarius, quick wits across from a drive for the far horizon; a Sun in Cancer with Mars in Capricorn, care across from ambition. Opposite houses often hold them too. With the 1st and 7th, your own initiative faces a partner's; with the 4th and 10th, home faces an ambition you fight for.

Only about 1 person in 66 is born with Mars opposite the Sun, within 8°; none of the pair's other aspects comes close to being so rare. Mars faces the Sun only once in a little over two years, in the middle of its retrograde, and at that point the Sun moves forward while Mars appears to move back: the two pull through the orb in a matter of days. So everyone with this aspect has Mars retrograde in their chart ([Mars retrograde](/astrology/mars-retrograde/)), which I read as drive that works inward first, then acts ([natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)).

The other aspects of the pair sit elsewhere in the same cycle:

- **[Sun conjunct Mars](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mars/):** the other end of the cycle, about a year away, with Mars beyond the Sun; the drive lives inside your purpose rather than across from it.
- **[Sun sextile Mars](/astrology/sun-sextile-mars/):** drive that helps when purpose asks, with no tug of war, and the most frequent of the five.
- **[Sun square Mars](/astrology/sun-square-mars/):** the friction moves inside, between two parts of your own life, instead of across a table.
- **[Sun trine Mars](/astrology/sun-trine-mars/):** a few weeks before or after your phase, near Mars's turning points; effort and purpose in one element, side by side.

Between two charts, [Sun opposite Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-opposite-mars-synastry/) puts one person's drive across from the other's sense of self: lively, sometimes competitive, and in my reading always two-sided.

## In transits {#in-transits}

Mars opposes your natal Sun once on each trip round the zodiac, about every two years, for a few days ([transits](/astrology/transits/)). Born with the opposition, you have a neat overlap: Mars coming back to its own place in your chart also stands opposite your Sun, so each return of Mars echoes your birth.

A few days, then, when the push seems to come from outside, and you choose how to meet it: weather, not a forecast.
