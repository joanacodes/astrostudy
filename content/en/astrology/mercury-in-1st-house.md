With Mercury in the 1st house, your mind shows on the outside: in the way you look round a room, the question you ask within a minute of arriving, the expression that changes as you think. People tend to know you're curious before they know much else about you.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) is the self you lead with: your body, your manner, your first reactions, the way you start things. It's angular, the kind of house where whatever sits there shows, and its natural sign is Aries. Tradition gave [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) its joy here ([planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/)): of the twelve houses, this is the one where the planet of thought and speech was said to rejoice.

I read the joy as ease rather than rank. Mercury here works in plain view. Your curiosity, your way of speaking and the speed of your thinking are among the first things people learn about you, and you tend to meet a new situation by asking about it. The sign Mercury is in sets the style: a 1st-house Mercury in Taurus asks slow, practical questions; in Sagittarius, large ones. I enjoy seeing the joy in a chart, but in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) Mercury held about a third of the time, so I read a 1st-house Mercury the way you'd read anyone's first impression: as a true start, and never the whole story.

In Placidus, the house system I use, the 1st house begins at the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), so a Mercury here was just under the eastern horizon when you were born, still to rise. Since Mercury stays close to the Sun, that usually means a birth in the hours around sunrise. The horizon moves a degree about every four minutes, so a Mercury near the Ascendant can land in the 12th house or the 1st on a few minutes' difference: there's no reading of it without the time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). In whole sign houses, the 1st is simply your rising sign, and a Mercury in that sign counts here even if it had already risen.

Just above the horizon, [Mercury in the 12th house](/astrology/mercury-in-12th-house/) keeps its thinking private; further below it, [Mercury in the 2nd house](/astrology/mercury-in-2nd-house/) turns its thinking to what things are worth.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You talk your way into closeness. A first meeting is a conversation, and you show interest by asking questions, often more than the other person expects. Partners usually know what you think, because it crosses your face before you say it, which makes you easy to read and hard to fool. The adjustment, in a long relationship, is letting the other person lead the conversation sometimes, and noticing the parts of you that aren't talk: your care, your company, your silence when it's kind.

## At work {#at-work}

Any role where first impressions and quick thinking count: interviews, client-facing work, teaching, sales, presenting, reception, broadcasting. You're often the one sent to explain the project, ask the awkward question in the meeting, or welcome the new colleague. A reputation for curiosity tends to follow you from job to job. Work that keeps you behind a closed door, away from people and questions, can feel like being asked to hide part of yourself.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Being taken for your mind alone, all questions and quick answers, when there's much more to you. People may expect you always to have something to say, and a quiet day can feel like failing a part you never auditioned for. Restlessness shows: the foot tapping, the attention moving round the room. And because your thinking is visible, so are your doubts, sometimes before you've decided what you think.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Let silence be part of your presence; you don't have to fill every pause. Give people time to meet the rest of you, the parts that aren't questions. When you're unsure, say *I'm still thinking*, which is honest and buys you time.
