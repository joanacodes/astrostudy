The Harvest moon is the one full moon of the year that goes by the equinox rather than the calendar: it is the full moon nearest the September equinox, whichever month it lands in. The name is an old English one, and it isn't astrology, but the rule happens to fix its sign, because in the tropical zodiac the equinox is a point in the sky.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The rule is simple. The September equinox falls on 22 or 23 September, and the [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/) closest to it, before or after, is the Harvest moon. Full moons come about 29½ days apart, so the nearest one is never more than about fifteen days away: the Harvest moon always falls between about 8 September and 8 October. The [Sturgeon moon](/astrology/sturgeon-moon/) of August comes before it; the [Hunter's moon](/astrology/hunters-moon/), the next full moon, comes after.

Its dates, in Universal Time:

- **2026:** 26 September, 16:49 UT, at 3°37′ Aries, nearly four days after the equinox
- **2027:** 15 September, 23:04 UT, at 22°53′ Pisces, about a week before it
- **2028:** 3 October, 16:25 UT, at 10°59′ Aries, eleven days after it
- **2029:** 22 September, 16:29 UT, at 29°57′ Pisces, about an hour before it
- **2030:** 11 September, 21:18 UT, at 19°11′ Pisces, eleven days before it

You can check the rule against the signs. In the tropical zodiac, the September equinox is the moment the Sun enters Libra, and a full moon puts the Moon in the opposite sign, so the full moon nearest the equinox is the one whose Moon is nearest 0° Aries. In 2029 the Moon stands at 29°57′ Pisces and the Sun at 29°57′ Virgo, three minutes of arc short of Libra, which the Sun covers in a little over an hour. In 2028 the September full moon, on the 3rd at 11°59′ Pisces, is eighteen days before the equinox, while the one of 3 October is eleven days after: October's wins.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Unlike the almanacs' month names, this one is old in English. NASA's full moon guide, citing the Oxford English Dictionary, gives 1706 as its first known written use, calls it an old European name, and states the rule: the full moon closest to the autumnal equinox. When the Harvest moon falls in October, as in 2020 and again in 2028, the September full moon keeps the almanac's September name, the Corn Moon, and NASA's September guide adds two European ones, the Fruit Moon and the Barley Moon.

The name comes from the light. On average the Moon rises about fifty minutes later each day. Around the September equinox, though, the Moon's path meets the eastern horizon at a shallow angle in the evening, so at northern latitudes the full moon rises not long after sunset for several evenings in a row. Before electric light, NASA's guide notes, farmers depended on that bright moonlight to work past sunset and bring the harvest in.

It's a northern name: south of the equator, the September equinox opens the spring.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The Harvest moon is a folk name, not astrology, and I don't read a harvest into anyone's chart because of it. But the rule that defines it gives it a fixed place in the zodiac: always the Moon in late Pisces or early Aries, with the Sun in late Virgo or early Libra. Astrologically, that's either the [full moon in Pisces](/astrology/full-moon-in-pisces/), across the Virgo–Pisces axis, or the [full moon in Aries](/astrology/full-moon-in-aries/), across the Aries–Libra axis, the line on which the equinoxes themselves sit ([tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/)).

I treat it like any other full moon: it counts in a birth chart when it lands within a degree of a planet or angle, at either end of the axis, and as a marker inside something a transit or progression has already opened, never as a forecast. The 2029 Harvest moon, at the very end of Pisces, would reach a planet near 29° Pisces or 29° Virgo, or near 0° Aries or Libra, just across the line.

Which house it falls in depends on your birth time, and that's [how I read](/how-i-read/) any full moon: through your chart, never as a message for everyone.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Harvest moon is September's full moon.”** It's the full moon nearest the equinox, and in some years that's in October, as in 2028.
- **“The Harvest moon is bigger and more orange than other full moons.”** It rises in the evening twilight, low and warm-coloured as any full moon near the horizon looks, but it isn't larger.
- **“It's a Native American name.”** It's an English name, recorded since 1706, older in print than most of the almanacs' moon names.
- **“The Harvest moon carries its own astrological meaning.”** It's read like any full moon, by its sign and its house.
