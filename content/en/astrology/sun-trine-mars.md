Sun trine Mars puts the Sun and Mars a third of the circle apart, usually in the same element. It's the aspect of drive and direction moving together, and, for a reason that lies in the way Mars moves, it's a good deal rarer than the sextile.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is your sense of self and of where you're going; [Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the part of you that gets up and does something about it: begins, asserts, makes the effort, holds a line. A [trine](/astrology/trine/) relates the two through flow and mutual reinforcement: purpose gives effort a reason, effort gives purpose a body, and neither has to argue for its turn.

In plain words, when you know what you want, the energy to go after it is usually there, and it goes in the right direction. You can work hard without feeling you're fighting yourself, and competing, defending a boundary or starting from scratch tends to feel natural rather than costly. Courage, with this trine, looks less like daring and more like simply getting on with it.

Ease is not inevitability, and flow is not passivity. The trap of this trine is never having had to push where nothing flows: the task that needs effort you don't feel, the moment when your energy and your aim disagree. I read the trine at its best in people who have learned to use it on purpose, and to keep going on the days it doesn't carry them.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Four signs apart, your Sun and Mars nearly always share an element, and with it a temperament: a Sun in Leo with Mars in Sagittarius acts with warmth and appetite; a Sun in Pisces with Mars in Scorpio acts by feel, quietly and thoroughly. The houses show where effort comes most easily in the service of who you're becoming. Without a birth time those houses go missing, and with them the most personal half of this trine ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

About 1 person in 19 has the Sun and Mars within 8° of a trine, against 1 in 8 for the sextile. Both trines fall close to Mars's retrograde turn: one as Mars slows down to change direction, the other just after it has set off forward again. Mars barely moves then, so the Sun runs through the trine at nearly its full pace, and the window is short. Within it, the Sun makes and breaks the aspect, and before exact the trine was still forming ([applying and separating aspects](/astrology/applying-and-separating-aspects/)).

The rest of the family, seen from here:

- **[Sun conjunct Mars](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mars/):** purpose and drive in one place; the flow becomes a fusion, and rest is the harder lesson.
- **[Sun sextile Mars](/astrology/sun-sextile-mars/):** the commonest of the five; the same cooperation, but it waits for you to start it.
- **[Sun square Mars](/astrology/sun-square-mars/):** a sign closer, and the two signs no longer share an element; drive runs across purpose and has to be aimed.
- **[Sun opposite Mars](/astrology/sun-opposite-mars/):** Mars in the middle of its retrograde, facing the Sun; what flows in you stands opposite you there, often embodied by others.

Between two charts, [Sun trine Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-trine-mars-synastry/) lets one person's energy run with the other's aims: shared projects that move without much pushing.

## In transits {#in-transits}

Every year or so, Mars in the sky trines your natal Sun for a few days ([transits](/astrology/transits/)), and in a year when Mars turns retrograde near that degree, the trine stretches over weeks: a long run of stamina, if you have something worth spending it on.

I'd keep those days for work that needs staying power. Weather, not a forecast: a following wind for effort, never a decision made for you.
