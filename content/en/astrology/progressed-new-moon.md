Once every 29½ years or so, the progressed Moon catches up with the progressed Sun. Astrologers call the meeting a progressed new moon, and read it as the quiet start of a chapter that takes about three decades to run its course.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

In secondary progressions, the days after your birth stand for the years of your life, so the new moons of the weeks after your birth become the progressed new moons of your life. The first falls at an age equal to the number of days between your birth and the next real new moon: born a day before a new moon, at about one; born a day after, at about 28½. After that, one comes every 29¼ to 29¾ years: as many years as the following lunar months had days. By 80, everyone has had two or three; by 90, three, and a few people four.

Each one falls where the [progressed Sun](/astrology/progressed-sun/) stands at that moment, and the progressed Sun advances by roughly a degree each year. So each progressed new moon lands about 29° further on than the one before, near the same degree of the next sign: your progressed new moons step through the zodiac one sign at a time.

About fifteen years after each comes the progressed full moon, the halfway point. The stages in between are the [progressed lunar phases](/astrology/progressed-lunar-phases/).

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

You can date your first one with nothing but an ephemeris: count the days from your birth to the next [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/), and read that number as years. A program that offers secondary progressions gives the exact date and position, and shows what it falls on.

Then I look at three things. Its sign, the manner of the chapter. Its house, the area of life where it's planted, which depends on your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). And any natal planet it falls on, which joins its function to the new start.

One technical point ties it to another method. The progressed Sun is also the Sun as [solar arc directions](/astrology/solar-arc-directions/) move it, since the arc is nothing but the progressed Sun's movement since birth. At a progressed new moon, the Moon of the progressed chart sits on that directed Sun, and both point at the same degree.

It isn't the [prenatal lunation](/astrology/prenatal-lunation/), the new moon that came just before your birth. That is a fixed point in the birth chart; the progressed new moon is a moment that recurs through life.

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

I read a progressed new moon as a seed: something begins, often before it has a name, that the next three decades will develop. In the 4th house it can start a chapter about where, and with whom, you feel at home; in the 10th, about the work you want your name on; in the 7th, about the people you choose to build with.

Astrologers read the years just before it, the progressed balsamic phase, as a clearing out. The start itself can pass almost unnoticed, and is easier to see looking back, once what was planted has grown enough to recognise.

Its rhythm is almost exactly Saturn's. The two don't keep step, though: the [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/) comes between about 28 and 30 for everyone, while a progressed new moon can come at any age. For some people they arrive together, a double reckoning with structure and with fresh starts; for others they're years apart.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

The exact conjunction is a short moment even in progressed time: the Moon gains about a degree a month on the Sun, so it is within a degree of exact for about two months. What it opens unfolds over years, and I don't read the month as an event.

The [progressed Moon](/astrology/progressed-moon/) describes needs and feeling, and the Sun purpose: their meeting describes an inner beginning. It can't tell you what will start, whether it will succeed, or what anything will cost. It's read with the natal chart and with the slow transits of the same years, and it carries more weight when they point the same way.
