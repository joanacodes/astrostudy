Neptune opposite Pluto is the widest angle these two planets can make, and the one furthest from anything people born between 1930 and 2009 carry: their charts show the pair about 60° apart, a third of the way round to an opposition. You can still meet it as a transit, once Neptune has travelled a long way from your natal Pluto, and between two charts.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) is the side of a chart that longs to merge: with an ideal, with other people's feelings, with something larger than a single life. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) is the side that insists on depth: getting to the root, letting the dead part go, taking back strength that was handed over. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) relates them through polarity, so the wish to dissolve into the whole and the need to stand on your own deep ground would face each other, each making the other easier to see.

In plain words, the part of you that wants to belong to everything and the part that wants to get to the bottom of one thing would sit at either end of a line. An opposition is often lived through other people first: you might meet the dreamer in someone else while you dig, or the digger while you dream. The work is to hold both ends yourself. Polarity isn't contradiction, and compassion with depth is the same skill as depth with compassion, seen from the other side.

Were it in a chart, it would join two opposite houses, the 4th and the 10th, say, or the 1st and the 7th, and like any angle between these planets, a whole generation would share it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Nobody born between 1930 and 2009 has Neptune within 6° of an opposition to Pluto. Through those eighty years the two stayed close to a sextile ([Neptune sextile Pluto](/astrology/neptune-sextile-pluto/)), and an opposition needs three times that distance. Of all the pair's major angles, it was the furthest out of reach.

It can form between two charts, though only between people born several decades apart: [Neptune opposite Pluto in synastry](/astrology/neptune-opposite-pluto-synastry/) puts one person's ideals across from the other's depth, so each sees in the other what they don't do themselves. I read it from both sides.

In my testing notes, Neptune and Pluto were checked like every other placement, and neither was among the three that held far more often, so when a reading leans on them, I say so ([the invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

The same two planets at their other angles:

- **[Neptune conjunct Pluto](/astrology/neptune-conjunct-pluto/):** the reverse of this page, both ends gathered in one place.
- **[Neptune sextile Pluto](/astrology/neptune-sextile-pluto/):** what most people born 1943–1993 have, an easy opening between dream and depth.
- **[Neptune square Pluto](/astrology/neptune-square-pluto/):** halfway to the opposition, a friction rather than a polarity, and absent too.
- **[Neptune trine Pluto](/astrology/neptune-trine-pluto/):** flow between the two, with no births in these years either.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Neptune opposite natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-neptune-opposite-natal-pluto/) is the form this opposition can take in the lives of people born in these years. Neptune starts out ahead of natal Pluto and keeps drawing away, passing the square, then the trine, and only after both, the opposition; your birth year sets when. Once there, Neptune stays close for a year or more, often in three passes.

[Transiting Pluto opposite natal Neptune](/astrology/transiting-pluto-opposite-natal-neptune/) is out of reach: Pluto, behind Neptune in these charts, would need to cover well over half the zodiac, far more than a lifetime at its pace.

Weather, not a forecast: transiting Neptune opposite natal Pluto describes a long season in which what you long for and what you need to face come into view together. It doesn't tell you which will give way.
