No one can show you their gift. What anyone can show you is how they work, and that's good news, because the way someone works is something you can judge, whatever you believe about the rest.

## The short answer {#the-short-answer}

I can't tell you whether anyone sees the future. I can tell you what I don't claim: [a gift, or the future itself](/how-i-read/). A birth chart describes tendencies and seasons, and there's nothing in my readings you couldn't learn yourself, with time. So I'd be wary of anyone whose whole offer rests on powers you can't check, and most of all of anyone who uses those powers to frighten you.

A fake psychic is easier to recognise by method than by message: they contact you first, find something wrong, sell the fix, keep a meter running, and ask you to tell no one. Any one of those is a reason to slow down. Together, they're a business model.

## Why it matters {#why-it-matters}

These methods are worth knowing, because none of them depends on the person being gullible:

- **They seem to know you.** Much of it comes from you: what you said in the first minutes, how you reacted, what is public about you online. The rest is [cold reading](/astrology/cold-reading/) and lines broad enough to fit almost anyone, which feel personal because they're said to you ([the Barnum effect](/astrology/barnum-forer-effect/)). Being moved by it isn't foolish; it's what being listened to feels like.
- **The meter keeps running.** On a per-minute line or chat, the important part can always be one more minute away. Some services sign you up to a weekly or monthly payment that's easy to start and hard to stop: [subscription traps](/astrology/subscription-traps/).
- **Fear comes before the fee.** ‘I see a dark cloud around you.’ ‘Someone is working against you.’ A frightening reading followed by a price is a sales technique, and the price can be for [paid ‘spiritual work’](/astrology/paid-spiritual-work/) to clear what was ‘seen’.
- **You stop deciding without them.** Daily check-ins, a reading before every choice. That dependence pays the seller and costs you. It's true of charts too: consult them all the time and you lose practice at the one thing no reading can do for you, which is deciding.

Approaches like these find people at their most open: after a death, a break-up, a diagnosis, in a lonely stretch. Anyone can be reached at such a moment, and no one should be mocked for it.

## What to do instead {#what-to-do-instead}

- **Ask first, pay after.** A careful reader tells you up front what they charge, what they won't read and what you'll receive. If they can't or won't, don't start. [How to tell a careful astrologer from a scam](/astrology/how-to-tell-a-careful-astrologer-from-a-scam/) goes further.
- **Set your limit before you call.** Decide the total you're willing to spend, and end the call when you reach it, whatever is still left ‘to tell’.
- **Never pay to remove something they found.** A curse, a block, a dark cloud: when the finder sells the removal, the problem was the product.
- **Test what you were told.** Write it down, date it, and come back in six months with a pen: a tick for anything that came true in a way that couldn't have fitted anyone else, a cross for anything vague, wrong, or true of anyone. It's [the invoice method](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) I used on my own chart, and it's just as useful on predictions.
- **If you've lost money,** keep the records and report it: [How to report a scam](/astrology/how-to-report-a-scam/).

And if you keep going back because a reading is the one place you feel heard, that need deserves better than a meter running: people who know you and, if it would help, someone trained to listen ([When to see a professional instead](/astrology/when-to-see-a-professional-instead/)).
