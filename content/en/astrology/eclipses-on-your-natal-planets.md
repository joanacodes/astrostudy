Several times a year there is an eclipse somewhere on Earth, and many of them pass over your chart without touching anything in it. Now and then one lands on the degree of a planet you were born with. That contact, not the eclipse itself, is what I read.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

An eclipse is a new or full moon close enough to the Moon's nodes for the Sun, Moon and Earth to line up ([why eclipses follow the nodes](/astrology/why-eclipses-follow-the-nodes/)). A solar eclipse has the Sun and Moon together at one degree ([solar eclipses](/astrology/solar-eclipses/)); a lunar eclipse has the Moon at one degree and the Sun at the degree opposite ([lunar eclipses](/astrology/lunar-eclipses/)). Either way, the eclipse marks an axis across the zodiac, and your chart has something at one end of it or it doesn't.

The rule I follow gives it about three degrees, at either end. The total solar eclipse of 2 August 2027, at 9°55′ Leo, reaches a natal planet between about 7° and 13° Leo, and one between about 7° and 13° Aquarius. A penumbral lunar eclipse, when the Moon only grazes the Earth's outer shadow, weighs less than a partial or total one.

The idea is very old. Ptolemy wrote about eclipses for whole countries, in the second book of the *Tetrabiblos*, and added that the people who feel one most are those born with the Sun or the Moon at the eclipse's degree, or at the degree opposite. The rule I use extends that to every planet and angle, and to nothing beyond three degrees.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Checking your own chart takes your planets' degrees and a list of eclipses. These are the twenty from February 2027 to the end of 2030, grouped by the pair of signs they fall on; a lunar eclipse is listed at the Moon's degree, with the Sun opposite.

- **Leo and Aquarius:** 17°37′ Aquarius, annular solar, 6 February 2027 · 9°55′ Leo, total solar, 2 August 2027 · 24°04′ Aquarius, penumbral lunar, 17 August 2027 · 6°10′ Aquarius, annular solar, 26 January 2028.
- **Virgo and Pisces:** the last of that axis, 1°58′ Virgo, penumbral lunar, 20 February 2027.
- **Cancer and Capricorn:** 25°57′ Capricorn, penumbral lunar, 18 July 2027 · 21°33′ Cancer, partial lunar, 12 January 2028 · 15°15′ Capricorn, partial lunar, 6 July 2028 · 29°50′ Cancer, total solar, 22 July 2028 · 10°34′ Cancer, total lunar, 31 December 2028 · 24°49′ Capricorn, partial solar, 14 January 2029 · 4°49′ Capricorn, total lunar, 26 June 2029 · 19°37′ Cancer, partial solar, 11 July 2029.
- **Gemini and Sagittarius:** 21°29′ Gemini, partial solar, 12 June 2029 · 13°45′ Sagittarius, partial solar, 5 December 2029 · 29°18′ Gemini, total lunar, 20 December 2029 · 10°50′ Gemini, annular solar, 1 June 2030 · 24°38′ Sagittarius, partial lunar, 15 June 2030 · 3°02′ Sagittarius, total solar, 25 November 2030 · 17°47′ Gemini, penumbral lunar, 9 December 2030.

Eclipses also come back. One that touched a planet of yours often has a twin about nineteen years later, on nearly the same date and within half a degree: the total solar eclipse of 1 August 2008, at 9°32′ Leo, returns as the one of 2 August 2027, at 9°55′. The saros, 18 years and 11 days, brings back a similar eclipse too, but about ten degrees further on, so rarely onto the same planet ([saros cycles](/astrology/saros-cycles/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

An eclipse adds nothing a chart doesn't hold. It points at a natal point for a while, and the point says what is being pointed at:

- **The Sun:** identity, purpose and direction.
- **The Moon:** needs, habits and what makes you feel safe. The Moon moves about 13° a day, so without the birth time its degree is unknown by more than the three-degree orb ([the Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).
- **Mercury, Venus, Mars:** how you think and exchange, what you value and are drawn to, how you act.
- **Jupiter and Saturn:** what you grow into, and what you build and carry.
- **Uranus, Neptune, Pluto:** placements shared with everyone born within years of you, so the house they occupy is what makes the contact yours.
- **The angles:** the Ascendant–Descendant line, you and the people you meet; the Midheaven–IC line, your public direction and your roots. Both need the exact birth time.

Your natal nodes have their own rhythm: about every 18.6 years, at your nodal return, the eclipses come back to their axis ([nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/)). And some astrologers read the last eclipse before a birth as part of the chart ([prenatal eclipse](/astrology/prenatal-eclipse/)).

An eclipse on a natal point is weather, not a forecast. It never foretells illness, death, money or a break-up ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)), and I read it beside the [transits](/astrology/transits/) and [secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/) of the same months, never alone.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Every eclipse affects everyone.”** Only an eclipse within about three degrees of something in your chart reaches it.
- **“An eclipse on your Sun brings a crisis.”** It brings your Sun's function into focus for a while. What you do with that focus is yours.
- **“Only solar eclipses count.”** Lunar eclipses count too, at the Moon's end or the Sun's; only the penumbral ones weigh less.
- **“A missed eclipse returns the next year.”** The same degrees usually come back about nineteen years later, not one.
