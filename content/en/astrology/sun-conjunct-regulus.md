Sun conjunct Regulus is the birthday contact of the Lion's heart: born on about 22 or 23 August, you have the Sun within a degree of the brightest star in Leo. Of all the bright stars, Regulus lies closest to the Sun's yearly path, so on those days the Sun passes almost over it.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) as the part of a chart that organises who you are becoming: purpose, vitality, the way you hold yourself together and put yourself forward. [Regulus](/astrology/regulus/) arrives with a crown. Its names make it a king in Babylon and a little king in Latin, and Chinese astronomers counted it among the stars of Xuanyuan, the Yellow Emperor. Ptolemy compared it to Mars and Jupiter together. Put the two side by side and the tradition's reading is easy to guess: a sense of purpose that wants to lead, and to be seen leading.

That is the old reading, and I keep it at arm's length. Recognition can matter to the Sun, but rank and fame aren't part of its job, so I'd say the tradition lays a royal colour over your sense of direction, and leave the crown in the old books. The Sun still speaks first through its sign, its house and its aspects; a star is a tone laid over all that ([fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/)).

The sign deserves a second look. Regulus stood at 28°50′ Leo in 1930 and 29°49′ Leo in 2000, so for births across those eighty years the Sun on Regulus is nearly always a [Sun in Leo](/astrology/sun-in-leo/) in its last degrees: the Sun in its own sign, on the heart of the Lion itself. Only the far edge of the window reaches into Virgo. Precession has since carried the star over the cusp, to 0°11′ Virgo in 2026, so a birth on 23 August now puts the same Sun on the same star in the next sign, a [Sun in Virgo](/astrology/sun-in-virgo/).

In the sky, this conjunction is close to literal. Regulus lies less than half a degree from the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), so on your birthday the Sun passed within about its own width of the star. Nobody on Earth sees it happen: Regulus is lost in the glare for about a month either side of 22–24 August. Only SOHO, a spacecraft that watches the Sun, catches the star sliding through one of its cameras every August.

About 1 person in 176 born between 1930 and 2009 has the Sun there, on the same two days of August in 1930, 1970 and 2009 alike. The Sun moves about a degree a day, so a birth on 21 or 24 August can sit just inside or just outside the window: the year and the hour decide, and the hour also decides which house of the chart the meeting falls in ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

The other bodies reach the star on their own rhythms: the [Moon conjunct Regulus](/astrology/moon-conjunct-regulus/) for a few hours each month, [Venus conjunct Regulus](/astrology/venus-conjunct-regulus/) more often than any of them, [Mars conjunct Regulus](/astrology/mars-conjunct-regulus/) roughly every two years, and [Saturn conjunct Regulus](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-regulus/) once a generation. The charts I read are drawn without stars, so this page describes the contact rather than reads it; how astrologers who do use stars count it is set out under [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/).
