Some placements you discover in yourself. Lilith in the 7th house you tend to discover in other people first. [Black Moon Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/), the point of the Moon's orbit farthest from Earth, was in [the 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) when you were born, the house of the person facing you, and that's very often where the part you learned to hide turns up.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The 7th house is angular, one of the four that begin at the angles, and its natural sign is Libra. It's the house of the person facing you: partners in love and in work, close collaborators, contracts, and, in tradition, the open rival too. With Lilith here, the hidden part often meets you in someone else. You may be drawn, again and again, to people who live what you were taught not to — the outspoken one, the free one, the one who won't be managed — or find them maddening, which is often the same pull from the other side.

This Lilith refuses to be the smaller half. It won't sign up, in love or in business, to an arrangement where one person's needs are the weather and the other's an inconvenience. Its boundary is equality: a bond between two whole people, or nothing it will stay in for long.

In Placidus, the system I use, the 7th begins at the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), the degree that was setting in the west when you were born, so it rests on an accurate birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

This is home ground for the placement. Partners may sense the hidden part before you do; some are drawn to it and others try to tame it, and the second kind rarely lasts. The work is to notice what fascinates or frightens you in the people you choose, and to ask how much of it is yours. Once you can carry your own Lilith, you stop needing someone else to carry it for you.

If you compare your chart with a partner's, do it together ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)). Not every close bond lives in this house: the colleague at the next desk belongs to [Lilith in the 6th house](/astrology/lilith-in-6th-house/), and the trust and shared resources of a deep bond to [Lilith in the 8th house](/astrology/lilith-in-8th-house/). The 7th is the one who stands opposite you, as an equal.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, the 7th covers business partners, clients, negotiations and the people you sign agreements with. You may be a formidable negotiator once you stop apologising for having a position, and you'll walk away from a deal that asks you to disappear inside it. The hidden part also shows in rivals: the person you can't stand at work may be carrying something you were never allowed to be.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Projection is the trap. If your own refusal stayed hidden, you may keep finding it in partners — admiring it, resenting it, or provoking it — without ever quite owning it. The opposite happens too: you shrink to keep a relationship steady, and the hidden part waits, then ends things abruptly when it can't wait any longer.

Neither means something is wrong with you. Both show a self that needs room in company.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Make a private list of what you most admire and most resent in the people closest to you, and look for the overlap with what you were told not to be. Then practise the plain sentence that keeps two people equal: *this is what I need, too.* Say it early, while it's small.

Choose people who can stand opposite you without wanting you smaller. A partner who enjoys your refusal, rather than managing it, isn't a fantasy. It's simply the person this Lilith has been waiting for permission to want.
