Capricorn turns up a little less often than any other composite Sun sign. Commitment is what the relationship turns around here: the bond knows itself by what the two of you have planned, built and kept going.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) of [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) is the midpoint of your two natal Suns, and when it falls in [Capricorn](/astrology/capricorn/) it falls in the sign the fewest pairs share: about 1 pair in 13, the lowest share of the twelve when two million random pairs born 1930–2009 are counted. The reason is the Earth's orbit. We're nearest the Sun in early January, when it seems to move fastest, so it crosses Capricorn in about 29 days, quicker than any other sign, and a smaller share of the calendar, and of the midpoints, belongs to it.

The composite also ignores the years. Two people born on 10 January, five years apart, have their composite Sun at about 20° Capricorn, right between their natal Suns. Their [Davison chart](/astrology/davison-chart/), cast for the moment halfway between the two births, falls in mid-July of the year in between, with its Sun in Cancer, opposite. With an even number of years between them, the Davison Sun would be back in Capricorn. Two relationship charts can give the same pair opposite centres, which is one more reason neither is read on its own.

Read as the bond's own Sun, Capricorn puts its identity in commitment. The house renovated over a decade, the business with both names on the door, the promise to look after an ageing parent together: a relationship like this knows itself by what it has kept up. It's steadied by progress it can see, and it shows its care through reliability, turning up, doing what it said. In a birth chart, the [Sun in Capricorn](/astrology/sun-in-capricorn/) describes one person's sense of responsibility; in a composite the responsibility is shared, and two people who each prefer to improvise can find that, together, they plan.

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) rules Capricorn, and composite Saturn's sign and house show where this bond carries its weight. In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Saturn was one of the three placements that held best, 75 to 90% of the time, with [Chiron and Lilith](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/), but my notebook checked birth charts, and I wouldn't lend its figure to a composite.

Its difficulty is that the plan can crowd out the people. A bond that measures itself by what it has achieved can forget to enjoy what it has, and responsibility can harden into duty. Structure isn't coldness, though, and an evening with no agenda, kept as faithfully as any meeting, is part of the work. The [composite Sun in Sagittarius](/astrology/composite-sun-in-sagittarius/) next door looks to the horizon, the [composite Sun in Aquarius](/astrology/composite-sun-in-aquarius/) to what a group might become; a Capricorn bond looks to the next stage of what it has already begun.

Composites aren't charts I draw; I describe them as the astrologers who use them do, alongside both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). A Capricorn composite Sun says how a bond builds, and guarantees nothing about how long it stands or what it achieves. Like any long project, it starts with agreement, from both of the people whose birth data it uses ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
