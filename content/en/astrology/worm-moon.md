The Worm moon is the full moon of March, named in the almanacs for the end of winter, when the ground softens and worms come back. It's a folk name, and astrology doesn't use it: what an astrologer reads that night is a full moon in Virgo or in Libra.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

It's the third full moon of the almanacs' year, after the [Snow moon](/astrology/snow-moon/) and before the [Pink moon](/astrology/pink-moon/). Like every [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/), it is the Moon exactly opposite the Sun. Its dates, in Universal Time, with the Moon's sign and degree:

- **2026:** 3 March, 11:38 UT, at 12°54′ Virgo, a total lunar eclipse
- **2027:** 22 March, 10:44 UT, at 1°35′ Libra
- **2028:** 11 March, 01:06 UT, at 20°59′ Virgo
- **2029:** 30 March, 02:26 UT, at 9°40′ Libra
- **2030:** 19 March, 17:57 UT, at 29°10′ Virgo

What decides the sign is the equinox. In the tropical zodiac, the spring equinox is the moment the Sun enters Aries, around 20 March. Before it, the Sun is still in Pisces and the full moon opposite falls in Virgo; after it, the Sun is in Aries and the full moon lands in Libra. So the Worm moon closes the winter in 2026, 2028 and 2030, and opens the spring in 2027 and 2029. The 2030 one cuts it finest: with the Moon at 29°10′ Virgo, the Sun stands at 29°10′ Pisces, less than a day before it enters Aries.

The 2026 Worm moon, now behind us, was also a total lunar eclipse. The full moon fell near one of the Moon's nodes, and the whole Moon passed into the Earth's shadow.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The name comes with two stories, and two different worms.

The older is Jonathan Carver's. In his *Travels Through the Interior Parts of North-America* (1778), he listed the months as he understood “the Indians” to name them, without saying which people, and began the year in March with the Worm Month. His reason: at that season the worms leave the bark of the trees and the wood where they have sheltered all winter. Worms that live in bark sound more like insect grubs than earthworms.

The second story belongs to the Maine Farmer's Almanac, which began printing its “Indian” names for each month's full moon in the 1930s, as NASA's full moon guide recounts. Its Worm Moon is named for the earthworm casts that appear on the soil as the ground thaws. The same almanac gives March several other names, each a sign of the turning season: the Crow Moon, when the cawing of crows signalled the end of winter; the Crust Moon, when snow thawing by day and freezing by night forms a crust; and the Sap or Sugar Moon, the time for tapping maple trees.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The Worm moon is a folk and almanac name, not astrology, and I don't read worms, crows or maple sap into a chart. I read the full moon, and in March that means one of two quite different axes. The [full moon in Virgo](/astrology/full-moon-in-virgo/) has the Sun in Pisces, across the Virgo–Pisces line; the [full moon in Libra](/astrology/full-moon-in-libra/) has the Sun in Aries, across the line that starts the zodiac. Two Worm moons a year apart can have almost nothing in common astrologically.

A full moon gets a light touch from me. It's a monthly marker, and it only counts on a point of your chart it reaches within a degree, on the Moon's side or the Sun's. When it does, I look for what else is working on that point, a transit, a progression or an eclipse, because a lunation brings into focus what is already active and starts nothing on its own. An eclipse such as the 2026 Worm moon has its own rule: it counts within about three degrees, and never as an omen.

The house it falls in, where Virgo or Libra sits in your chart, needs the time you were born. That is [how I read](/how-i-read/) every lunation: through your chart, never as a forecast for everyone at once.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Worm moon is named after earthworms.”** That's the almanac's story. Carver's worms, a century and a half earlier, came out of tree bark.
- **“The Worm moon is the first full moon of spring.”** Only when it falls after the equinox, as in 2027 and 2029. In the other years it's the last full moon of winter.
- **“The 2026 Worm moon eclipse was a warning.”** An eclipse is the Sun, the Earth and the Moon in line near a node. I read it through the chart it touches, as emphasis, never as an omen.
- **“The name carries a meaning in astrology.”** Its meaning comes from its sign and house, which change from year to year.
