A harmonic chart takes your birth chart and multiplies it. Every position is multiplied by one number, and the aspects that divide the circle by that number collapse into conjunctions, where they can be seen at a glance.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Every aspect is a fraction of the circle. The opposition is a half, the trine a third, the square a quarter, the sextile a sixth. Johannes Kepler, who sought to explain the aspects in terms of music, added the [quintile](/astrology/quintile/), a fifth, in *Harmonices Mundi* (Linz, 1619) ([Johannes Kepler](/astrology/johannes-kepler/)). Seen that way, each aspect is a small whole-number division of 360°, a harmonic of the circle, and each number has its own family: the fives, the sevens, the nines.

A harmonic chart gives one family a chart of its own. In the chart of the fifth harmonic, quintiles and biquintiles become [conjunctions](/astrology/conjunction/); in the seventh, the [septile](/astrology/septile/) and its multiples; in the ninth, the [novile](/astrology/novile/) family, and the trines with it, since a trine is three ninths; in the eleventh, the [undecile](/astrology/undecile/) family. The eighth harmonic, where semi-squares, squares, sesquiquadrates and oppositions all become conjunctions, holds the aspects the Hamburg School and cosmobiology count ([The Uranian 90° dial](/astrology/uranian-90-dial/)).

In the 20th century, the English astrologer John Addey (1920–1982) became a major proponent. He helped found the Astrological Association of Great Britain in 1958, and his starting point was a complaint about the twelve signs and houses: a twelvefold scheme shows patterns of 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12 easily, but not cycles of 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 or more than 12. His fundamental law was that "all astrological effects can be understood in terms of the harmonics of cosmic periods", and computers made the arithmetic practical.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Count each position in degrees from 0° Aries, so that 22° Gemini is 82°. Multiply by the harmonic number, then take away 360° as many times as you can. What's left is the position in the harmonic chart.

Take a Sun at 10° Aries and a Moon at 22° Gemini, 72° apart: a quintile. Multiplied by five, the Sun's 10° becomes 50°, which is 20° Taurus; the Moon's 82° becomes 410°, and one circle less, 50° again. In the fifth-harmonic chart the two stand together at 20° Taurus. James Holden, in his *History of Horoscopic Astrology*, noted that most horoscope programs can calculate harmonics.

Two things multiply along with the positions:

- **The orb.** A quintile 2° from exact in the birth chart is 10° from exact in the fifth-harmonic chart, so only close aspects come out as close conjunctions ([Orbs](/astrology/orbs/)).
- **Any error in the birth time.** The Ascendant moves about a degree every four minutes on average, so a birth time four minutes out moves the ninth-harmonic Ascendant by about 9°. The Moon moves about half a degree an hour, and so about 5° an hour in the ninth harmonic ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

By its supporters' account, one thread of the birth chart at a time. The fifth harmonic gathers the quintiles, which I read as creative organisation; the seventh gathers the sevens, patterns that emerge by repetition; the ninth, the noviles, things that ripen. A planet with no major aspect can turn out to be closely tied to others in one harmonic ([Unaspected planets](/astrology/unaspected-planets/)).

Addey also used harmonics for research. He applied harmonic analysis to Michel Gauquelin's statistics ([Gauquelin sectors](/astrology/gauquelin-sectors/)), and, according to a philosophy of science textbook, found that personality traits taken from biographies varied smoothly with a planet's position at birth, like a spectrum repeating itself every quadrant. As Addey himself pointed out, planets whose meanings vary like that are quite contrary to what astrology predicts. His survey of people born at almost the same moment is told in [Time twins: Dean and Kelly](/astrology/time-twins-dean-and-kelly/).

I don't draw harmonic charts. The fives, sevens, nines and elevens I read in the birth chart itself, as aspects with tight orbs ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Its limits {#its-limits}

The reception has been cool. Holden wrote that Addey's theory "excited some enthusiasm when it was first announced, but it has not found favor with most astrologers".

The arithmetic magnifies whatever is uncertain. Orbs and birth-time errors grow with the harmonic number, so the higher harmonics need a birth time known to the minute.

The possible charts are endless. Every number from two upwards makes a new one, and the more charts you draw, the more likely one of them shows a striking pattern by chance.

The signs lose their meaning. 20° Taurus in a fifth-harmonic chart is five times a position, not a point in the seasons, so I'd give the signs of a harmonic chart no weight.

And a harmonic chart adds no new sky. It is the birth chart rearranged: anything it shows was already there as an aspect. Other alternative frames change more. A [heliocentric chart](/astrology/heliocentric-chart/) moves the observer to the Sun, and [declination charts](/astrology/declination-charts/) measure north and south of the celestial equator instead of along the zodiac.
