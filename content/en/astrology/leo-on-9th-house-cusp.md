Long journeys, higher study, belief, the questions you'd travel to answer: that's the 9th house. Tradition called it the God and said the Sun rejoiced there. With Leo on the 9th house cusp, it opens in the Sun's own sign.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The sign on a cusp sets the tone of its house ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)), and [Leo](/astrology/leo/) brings warmth and conviction to the [9th house](/astrology/9th-house/). I read it as beliefs held wholeheartedly and shared generously: you don't keep a good idea to yourself, and the subject that lights you up tends to become one you teach, write about or bring to the dinner table. You learn best from teachers you admire, and you're often moved to become one. Travel is taken in a big way too: the journey that turns into a story told for years, the city you've adopted as yours after a week. Leo, a fixed sign, holds on to what it believes, while the 9th is also the house of what you're willing to question; with this cusp, the hard part is revising a view you've already defended in public. Pride, more than stubbornness, is usually what makes it hard.

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) rules your 9th house, and here it rules the very house where tradition placed its joy ([planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/)). I don't read that as a promise, rather as a fit: the Sun's business, purpose and recognition, sits easily among the 9th's questions. Wherever the Sun is in your chart is where your beliefs get put to use: as ruler of the 9th, in the [1st](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-1st-house/), [2nd](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-2nd-house/), [3rd](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/), [4th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-4th-house/), [5th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-5th-house/), [6th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-6th-house/), [7th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-7th-house/), [8th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-8th-house/), [9th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-9th-house/), [10th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-10th-house/), [11th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-11th-house/) or [12th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-12th-house/). In nearly half the London charts with this cusp, and 7 in 10 at New York, [Sagittarius rising](/astrology/sagittarius-rising/) comes with it, so Jupiter, the 9th's natural ruler, rules the whole chart while the Sun rules the house itself: a chart that leans towards the far horizon. Above, Virgo holds the Midheaven in about 3 London charts in 4, giving your public role to Mercury, and what you believe tends to show in work done carefully.

Of a day's births at London, about one in fourteen has Leo on the 9th cusp in Placidus, my house system; at New York, about one in thirteen. The 9th cusp lies above the horizon on the western side, between the Midheaven and the Descendant, where Leo, slow to rise in the north, passes quickly: a little under two hours a day on this cusp at London. Leo never opens a second house from here. The 9th runs about 38° to 40° wide at London, and in a little over a quarter of those charts it holds the whole of Virgo too, bringing Mercury into your studies and travels ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)). Roughly 1 London birth in 7 has no Leo cusp anywhere.

Births a little earlier than yours put Leo on the Midheaven ([Leo on the 10th house cusp](/astrology/leo-on-10th-house-cusp/)); births a little later, on the 8th ([Leo on the 8th house cusp](/astrology/leo-on-8th-house-cusp/)). The minute places the cusp, and the cusps place every planet in its house, so I don't read a chart without it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
