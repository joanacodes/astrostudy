About every fifteen years, Saturn squares the degree Neptune held when you were born, and the season tends to begin with a small mismatch: a plan and a hope that no longer quite line up. The square doesn't take the hope away. It asks which of the two needs adjusting.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) asks what will survive time and reality; [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) imagines, idealises and feels its way towards something larger. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates them through friction, and in this [transit](/astrology/transits/) the friction shows as a gap: between the job you believed in and the job as it is, the person you pictured and the person in front of you, the practice you meant to keep and the weeks that went by without it. Saturn wants the gap measured. Neptune would rather not look.

Looking is the work. Friction isn't failure, and the adjustment can go either way: sometimes the plan becomes more honest, sometimes the hope more precise, and sometimes a boundary that had quietly dissolved gets drawn again. Doubt and tiredness can come with it, a season when believing in something and turning up for it don't come together easily. If the heaviness doesn't lift, a doctor or a therapist will help more than any chart ([astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/)).

Your Neptune's house shows where the gap opens: in the 7th, the picture you hold of a partner; in the 10th, the vocation you hoped your work would be. The house Saturn is crossing shows where the pressure comes from, and both depend on your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Read against a natal [Saturn square Neptune](/astrology/saturn-square-neptune/), the transit tends to feel familiar: the same friction, now with dates on it.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

I count the square from 1½° before exact to 1° after, and within those orbs it typically runs about nine months. Saturn's yearly retrograde gives a little over half of them three exact hits, over about nine and a half months; the rest touch exact once, and the briefest are done in three weeks.

The squares cut Saturn's round with your Neptune into quarters: one on the way out from Saturn's crossing of it ([transiting Saturn conjunct natal Neptune](/astrology/transiting-saturn-conjunct-natal-neptune/)) to the opposition ([transiting Saturn opposite natal Neptune](/astrology/transiting-saturn-opposite-natal-neptune/)), one on the way back. Everyone has had the first by about 15, at an age the whole year group shares: about 11 for people born in 1985, about 4 for 1975, about 13 for 1965. It then returns every fourteen or fifteen years, five times by 80 for most people and six for some. Saturn in Aries, until April 2028, squares every degree of Capricorn: with Neptune in Capricorn, as for most people born between 1984 and 1998, this is yours now, at about 40 if you were born in 1985 and about 32 if you were born in 1995.

The sextile and the trine sit two or three years either side of each square ([transiting Saturn sextile natal Neptune](/astrology/transiting-saturn-sextile-natal-neptune/), [transiting Saturn trine natal Neptune](/astrology/transiting-saturn-trine-natal-neptune/)).

## What helps {#what-helps}

Measure the gap on paper: what you hoped for in one column, what's actually there in the other. Seen plainly, most gaps are smaller than they feel.

Change one term rather than the whole picture: the hours, the role, the promise, the timetable. A square is satisfied by an honest adjustment.

Keep one practice that feeds you, even in a reduced form. Neptune's sources don't have to be abandoned to be made realistic.
