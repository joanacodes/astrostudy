Most Western house systems treat a cusp as a door: the house begins there. Sripati, from Indian astrology, treats it as the centre of a room, with the walls halfway to the next cusp on either side.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Sripati starts like [Porphyry](/astrology/porphyry/). The four angles are found, the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), or lagna, the Descendant, the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) and the [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), and each quarter of the zodiac between two neighbouring angles is cut into three equal parts. That gives twelve points, the Sripati cusps. Then comes the step that sets it apart: the meeting point of two houses, the bhava sandhi, lies halfway between two neighbouring cusps. Each house runs from one sandhi to the next, with its cusp in the middle.

So the rising degree is the centre of the 1st house rather than its edge. Half of the 1st house lies above the horizon, in the stretch of sky a Western quadrant system gives to the 12th, and a planet a little above the Ascendant belongs to the 1st house in Sripati.

In Indian astrology, Jyotish, the most common way of counting houses is by whole sign, the method Western astrologers call [Whole Sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/). Sripati is one of the main alternatives, and the modern Krishnamurti Paddhati uses Placidus ([Vedic astrology (Jyotish)](/astrology/vedic-astrology-jyotish/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Śrīpati (c. 1019–1066), who gave the system its name, was an Indian astronomer, astrologer and mathematician born in what is now Maharashtra. He followed the teachings of Lalla and worked above all on astrology: his astronomy served his astrology, and his mathematics his astronomy. His *Siddhānta-śekhara* is a major treatise on astronomy in 19 chapters. His *Jātaka-paddhati*, eight chapters on horoscopy, became, in the historian David Pingree's words, one of the fundamental textbooks of later Indian genethlialogy, the casting of birth charts, and was copied, commented on and imitated widely. The house method credited to him is one of the main ones in Jyotiṣa.

Since its only inputs are the four angles, Sripati has no polar limit of its own: wherever the horizon and the meridian cross the zodiac, the quarters can be divided, even where Placidus and Koch give out ([houses at high latitudes](/astrology/houses-at-high-latitudes/)). Western astrology has its own long list of quadrant systems, from Porphyry and Placidus to Koch and [Krusinski](/astrology/krusinski/), which has gained ground in Central Europe. Sripati is the one that moves the cusp from the door to the middle of the room.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read in the tropical zodiac with [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/) houses, so Sripati is a tradition I describe, not one I use. The difference from Placidus is larger than the cusps alone suggest. Placidus puts the Ascendant at the door of the 1st house; Sripati puts it at the centre. So planets in the half-house just above the horizon count as 12th-house planets in my reading and 1st-house planets in a Sripati chart, and the same shift happens at every angle. When you compare readings from the two traditions, look first at planets near the angles ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)).

An Indian chart usually differs from mine in the zodiac too: Jyotish measures the signs against the stars, so a planet often falls one sign earlier ([sidereal zodiac](/astrology/sidereal-zodiac/)). Keep the two frames apart rather than mixing a Sripati house with a tropical sign ([which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)). Whatever the system, the angles come from the birth time, and Sripati's cusps and sandhis move with them ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Sripati is just Porphyry.”** It finds the same points, but uses them as the middles of houses, not their starts.
- **“Indian astrology only counts whole signs.”** Whole signs are the most common method, but Sripati is a main alternative, and Krishnamurti Paddhati uses Placidus.
- **“The Ascendant always begins the 1st house.”** In Sripati it sits in the middle of it.
