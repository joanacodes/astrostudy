Ask where a 4th-house Mercury thinks best and the answer is usually the kitchen table. The mind here is private and rooted, and it often turns towards the past: family stories, the language you grew up with, the things a home taught you without anyone saying them aloud.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 4th house](/astrology/4th-house/) is home, family, roots and private life: where you come from, and what you build to come back to. Tradition also gave it land and houses, the things that stay put. One of the four angular houses, it begins at the [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), the lowest point of the chart, and its natural sign is Cancer. With [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) here, the mind's most important conversations happen out of public view: at home, with family, or alone with a book.

You may think a great deal about where you come from, about family history, old places, the words and phrases your family used. Memory is one of this Mercury's resources. You often need a quiet, familiar place to think well, and you may do your best work from home. A Cancer Mercury here, in the 4th's own sign, makes memory the main subject ([Mercury in Cancer](/astrology/mercury-in-cancer/)).

The IC is where the zodiac crosses the meridian below you, so a 4th-house Mercury was near the lowest point of its daily circle when you were born, under your feet. With the Sun never far from Mercury, that usually points to a birth in the hours around midnight. In Placidus, my house system, the IC is the cusp of the 4th, and a Mercury close to it can fall in the 3rd or the 4th on a few minutes of birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Just before it, [Mercury in the 3rd house](/astrology/mercury-in-3rd-house/) is out in the street, talking; just after, [Mercury in the 5th house](/astrology/mercury-in-5th-house/) wants to play.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You open up at home. In public you may say less than you think; at the kitchen table, late, with someone you trust, the real conversation starts. The ways your family talked tend to come back in your own relationships: the subjects nobody raised, the way arguments were settled or never were. A partner who makes home a safe place to speak, and who's curious about your history, wins this Mercury's trust. Talking about the family you came from can be part of how you shape the one you make.

## At work {#at-work}

Working from home, writing, history, genealogy, archives, family businesses, property and housing, running a household: work that builds on memory and roots. You may think most clearly away from an open-plan office, in a room of your own. Colleagues sometimes discover how much you think only when they read what you've written, so let them read it.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Thoughts that circle back to the past instead of moving on. Family scripts, *we don't talk about that*, that still decide what you say as an adult. Keeping your best ideas at home, where nobody outside can use them or credit you for them. And a private mind can seem closed to people who would love to hear from it.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Write the family stories down, the ones you know and the ones you'd like to ask about. Notice which family sentences you still hear in your own voice, and keep the ones that help. Take one private idea a month out into the world: a post, a talk, a letter. Make home a place for good conversations, with the phone away and the kettle on. This is where your mind keeps its roots, and roots are for growing from.
