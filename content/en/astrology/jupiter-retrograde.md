Once a year, give or take a month, Jupiter stops against the stars, drifts back about ten degrees over four months, and moves on. It keeps such regular time that its loops step round the zodiac like the hand of a clock, one sign further every year.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

[Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) takes about twelve years to go round the zodiac, so Earth, on its faster inside track, laps it every 395 to 403 days. As we pass, Jupiter seems from here to back up for 118 to 124 days, and halfway through it sits opposite the Sun, rising as the Sun sets and visible all night.

The loops of the next few years, in the dates I work from:

- **11 November 2025 to 11 March 2026**, from 25°09′ back to 15°05′ Cancer, the sign of Jupiter's [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/);
- **13 December 2026 to 13 April 2027**, from 27°01′ to 16°59′ Leo ([Jupiter in Leo](/astrology/jupiter-in-leo/));
- **12 January to 13 May 2028**, from 27°30′ to 17°32′ Virgo, one of Jupiter's two detriments;
- **10 February to 13 June 2029**, from 27°25′ to 17°30′ Libra.

Every loop from 2023 to 2031 stays inside a single sign. The next to straddle two comes in 2032, when Jupiter turns back at 2°08′ Aquarius and stations direct at 22°18′ Capricorn.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Jupiter's orbit is a little oval, so it travels faster through one side of the zodiac than the other, and the length of each loop follows. In the dates from 2000 to 2040, the shortest, 118 days, fall in Aries, Taurus and Gemini, as in 2022 to 2024; the longest, 123 or 124 days, between Libra and Sagittarius, as in 2017 to 2019 and again in 2029 to 2031. Each one backs over about ten degrees.

Those ten degrees are crossed three times: forward before the [station](/astrology/stations/), backwards during the loop, forward again after it. For a point in your chart that sits inside the loop, the passes add up. With the orbs my forecast uses, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, about 7 in 10 of Jupiter's transits to a natal point are a single pass of about eleven days. The other 3 in 10 touch the point three times and keep Jupiter near it for about eight months. Astrologers count the stretches crossed before and after the loop as [the shadow period](/astrology/shadow-period/).

Its rhythm sits between its neighbours'. [Mars retrograde](/astrology/mars-retrograde/) comes only every two years or so and lasts two to two and a half months; [Saturn retrograde](/astrology/saturn-retrograde/), the next planet out, comes every year and lasts about four and a half.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

In my readings Jupiter is the part of you that grows by learning, the function that widens understanding and makes meaning; luck, wealth and success are not part of it. About 3 people in 10 are born with it retrograde, and there that widening turns inward first: convictions tested in private before they are voiced, a sense of direction pieced together from reflection rather than encouragement. The growth is real, and it shows later than it starts, as I explain in [Natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/).

Everyone born across the same four months of a year shares it, so on its own it says little about one person. What makes it yours is the house it falls in and the aspects it makes, and the house needs the birth time.

Over your own chart, the [transit](/astrology/transits/) of a retrograde Jupiter spends about four months reversing through a single house, the one your birth time puts it in. I read those months as a review of where you have been growing: a course reconsidered, a belief examined, a plan widened or trimmed before the next stretch forward. It's weather, not a forecast: it doesn't bring opportunities or take them away, and it never decides money or outcomes ([Can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Jupiter retrograde takes your luck away.”** Jupiter isn't luck to begin with. It describes how you widen your understanding, and retrograde turns that inward for a while.
- **“It's a rare event.”** It comes round every thirteen months or so and fills about three days in ten.
- **“Growth stops while Jupiter is retrograde.”** Reflection is growth too. The months of review are often when a direction becomes clear.
- **“Born with Jupiter retrograde, you'll miss your chances.”** It describes a way of growing that takes its time, not a door that closes.
