Trade is one of the oldest things people have asked astrologers about, and the Part of Commerce was meant to answer it from one degree of your chart. It's also the lot most likely to be asked something I won't answer, so this page is about its limits as much as about the lot.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

It's built the way every lot is, from two points of the chart and the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) ([doctrine of the lots](/astrology/doctrine-of-the-lots/)). In the run of lots made for the parts of a life, it comes after the [Part of Travel](/astrology/part-of-travel/), the road, and before the lots the old lists kept for darker questions, which this site treats as history only ([lots of death and illness, in history](/astrology/lots-of-death-and-illness-in-history/)).

Trade had a god before it had a lot. The Romans gave it to [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), god of commerce and financial gain, and also of boundaries, luck and thieves; his name may come from the Latin *merx*, merchandise, the word behind *merchant* and *commerce*. Yet when the seven [Hermetic lots](/astrology/hermetic-lots/) gave each planet a lot, Mercury's wasn't called Commerce. It was called Necessity, and it's counted from the [Part of Fortune](/astrology/part-of-fortune/). The Part of Commerce is a different lot, made for the trade itself.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Ptolemy is a useful contrast. He distrusted lots, yet for material wealth he used one, the "so-called Lot of Fortune", and counted it from the Sun to the Moon by day and by night alike, where most astrologers of his time reversed it at night (*Tetrabiblos*, book IV, chapter 2). What a person did for a living he handled separately, two chapters later, under the quality of action ([Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos](/astrology/ptolemys-tetrabiblos/)). Wealth and work were two questions for him, and a lot of commerce sits awkwardly between them.

The Part of Commerce itself works like the other lots: two points, the Ascendant, often a reversal for a night birth. Which two points, this page doesn't say: I found no source I could check, and with a lot about money, guessing is the last thing I'd do. If your program offers it, its documentation should name the points; your birth time, to the minute, does the rest, since the lot moves about a degree every four minutes.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

My system draws no lot but Fortune and Spirit, and the questions people bring to a lot of commerce, whether a business will work, what a deal will earn, when to sell, are ones I don't read. Investments and financial decisions are on my list of refusals ([how I read](/how-i-read/)), and [Can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/) explains why.

What a chart can describe is your side of a transaction. Mercury in your chart shows how you think and talk, and so how you bargain, explain, persuade and keep track. The [2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) describes your relationship with resources, never an amount in an account. The [10th house](/astrology/10th-house/) describes the work you're seen to do. A conversation about why you undersell yourself, or why every negotiation feels like a fight, is one I'm glad to have.

Nor do I choose dates. Picking a moment to open a shop or sign a contract is [electional astrology](/astrology/electional-astrology/), and it isn't part of my practice. Astrologers have sold market timing before ([financial astrology, in history](/astrology/financial-astrology-in-history/)); that it has been sold doesn't mean it has worked.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Part of Commerce shows how rich you'll be.”** No point measures a fortune, and none predicts one.
- **“It picks the right day to sign.”** A day can't make a contract fair. Its terms and the people behind it do.
- **“Mercury's lot is the lot of commerce.”** In the Hermetic set, Mercury's lot is Necessity. Commerce got a lot of its own.
- **“A strong Part of Commerce means you should go into business.”** No degree chooses a career. What you're good at, and what you want, decide that.
