Pluto conjunct North Node puts the planet of deep renewal right on the point a chart grows towards. What you're learning and what you're letting go of turn out to be one process, and it's one you get better at with practice.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) is the function that transforms: it eliminates what is obsolete, regenerates, and makes your agency more truly your own. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) is a calculated point, where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's heading north, and I read it as a direction of development: a less familiar capacity built by practice, always paired with the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) opposite, the established strengths you start from. In a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) the two act as one process, each keeping its identity.

In plain words, renewal is the skill your chart asks you to practise. Pluto is often read through a few deep turns in a life; on the North Node, those turns become a craft: noticing what has had its day, letting it go cleanly, building something truer, and doing it again with more ease each time. The North Node's own question, what must be practised rather than merely understood, gets a Plutonian answer here.

Development isn't destiny: this conjunction neither promises upheaval nor makes it your mission. The South Node opposite still matters: the familiar ground you come from holds strengths the renewal should build on, not discard.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The two usually share a sign. The nodes move backwards through a sign in about 18 months while Pluto takes 12 to 31 years, so it's the node that does the moving: the conjunction belongs to a few months of births, a little more often than every 18.6 years, since the two travel towards each other. In Libra, the renewal you practise runs through relationships, with the South Node in Aries as the old habit of going it alone; in Scorpio, it runs through trust and sharing, with the South Node in Taurus holding on to what feels secure.

The house shows where the practice happens, and it needs a birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)): in the 10th, for example, a working life that keeps renewing its aims, with the pull of home in the 4th.

About 1 person in 36 born between 1930 and 2009 has Pluto within 6° of the North Node, counted with the true node. I check it with the mean node as well: the two differ by a degree or two at most, so only a birth at the very edge of the orb can have the conjunction by one reckoning and not the other ([mean node and true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/)).

The pair's other aspects:

- **[Pluto sextile North Node](/astrology/pluto-sextile-north-node/):** renewal offering openings to growth, with an easy trine to the familiar end too.
- **[Pluto square North Node](/astrology/pluto-square-north-node/):** Pluto halfway between the nodes, a deep change that cuts across the path.
- **[Pluto trine North Node](/astrology/pluto-trine-north-node/):** renewal flowing into growth, and a sextile to the South Node as well.
- **[Pluto opposite North Node](/astrology/pluto-opposite-north-node/):** with Pluto beside the South Node, deep change is familiar ground and growth lies elsewhere.

Between two charts, [Pluto conjunct North Node in synastry](/astrology/pluto-conjunct-north-node-synastry/) sets one person's Pluto on the other's direction of growth, a meeting that can feel like a strong push forward, welcome or not. The push is rarely felt equally, so I read it from each side.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting North Node conjunct natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-north-node-conjunct-natal-pluto/) arrives once every 18.6 years, as the nodes complete their backward round, and stays for a month or two. It brings the eclipses with it: in that season, the new and full moons that fall near the nodes also fall near your Pluto.

Born with the conjunction, the timing is built in. Each [nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/), at about 18½, 37 and 56, brings the North Node back over your Pluto, and each [nodal reversal](/astrology/nodal-reversal/) in between, at about 9, 28 and 46, sets the South Node on it instead.

Weather, not a forecast: these are seasons when the question of what to let go of, and what to grow into, comes round again. They don't choose for you.
