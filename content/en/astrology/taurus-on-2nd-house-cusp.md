Taurus on the 2nd house cusp is the one place where the sign of keeping opens the house of keeping. Your resources, your belongings and your sense of what you're worth begin in the sign that belongs to them by nature.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

In the natural order of the zodiac, the second sign goes with the second house, so here the sign on the cusp and the house agree ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)). [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/) brings to the [2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) its way with anything worth having: keep it, look after it, add to it. Security grows by habit: a little put aside each month, a coat mended rather than replaced, a skill kept up long after you learned it. You tend to judge value by how long a thing will last, and the purchase you take your time over is usually the one you never regret. Self-worth works the same way. It accumulates with every promise you keep to yourself, and once it's there, it isn't easily shaken. When something new comes along, the question you tend to ask is how it will hold up in five years, and it's a good question.

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) rules Taurus and so rules your 2nd: the natural ruler of every 2nd house is, in your chart, the actual one too. I read Venus as the planet of valuing, of preference and of what you're drawn to, so its house shows where your sense of worth comes from and where your resources go. Venus's other sign, Libra, always lands in your 7th with this cusp, either on the Descendant itself or wholly inside the house: your resources and your partnerships answer to one planet, and a partner's tastes often shape your idea of what's worth keeping. Venus's house is what your security leans on, and there's a page for each: the [1st](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-1st-house/), [2nd](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-2nd-house/), [3rd](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/), [4th](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-4th-house/), [5th](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-5th-house/), [6th](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-6th-house/), [7th](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-7th-house/), [8th](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-8th-house/), [9th](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-9th-house/), [10th](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-10th-house/), [11th](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-11th-house/) or [12th](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-12th-house/).

Of every 17 births at London's latitude, about one has Taurus on the 2nd cusp; at New York's, one in 15, in the Placidus houses I use. It rises fairly quickly north of the equator, in about 69 minutes at London, so the cusps beside the Ascendant see it less often than the western ones do. Pisces or Aries rises with it, Pisces a little more often at London and Aries more often at New York; [Taurus rising](/astrology/taurus-rising/) itself takes a birth an hour or two later. Opposite, Scorpio opens the 8th, so both signs of this axis sit in their natural houses, the 2nd for what's yours and the 8th for what's shared. Only rarely does Taurus run on to open the 3rd as well ([Taurus on the 3rd house cusp](/astrology/taurus-on-3rd-house-cusp/)). When it has no cusp at all, about 1 London birth in 7, it never lies in the 2nd; it's found higher in the chart, most often in the 12th or the 11th ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)).

And the 2nd, even in its own sign, never says how much you'll have. No chart can promise an income or pick an investment ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)); what it shows is how you look after whatever comes. The cusp itself is set by the minute you were born, which is why the time comes before any reading ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
