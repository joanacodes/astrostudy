Inside a single chart, Venus opposite Mars is the rarest way these two planets meet. Between two charts it is ordinary, and it carries one of the oldest stories about attraction: the pull of opposites. One person's Venus sits about 180° from the other's Mars, so a way of choosing faces a way of pursuing, and each recognises in the other what they don't do themselves.

## What flows {#what-flows}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) is a person's sense of value and their way of relating: what draws them, what they'd keep, what feels fair and graceful. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) is their way of acting: going first, making the effort, holding their ground. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) stretches two functions across one axis, so each throws the other into relief; between two charts, each end has its own owner ([synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/)).

If you hold the Venus, the other's directness can be refreshing, or a lot. They go after things in a way you don't, and around them you may find your own preferences sharpened, because something is always being proposed for you to weigh.

If you hold the Mars, the other's sense of what's worth having gives your drive a direction. Their taste, their poise, their care for fairness can be exactly what your effort lacked, and it can also feel like a standard you are being measured against.

Who holds which end follows the two charts, never anyone's gender: either person can be the one who initiates or the one who chooses. With one person's Venus in Libra facing the other's Mars in Aries, each planet in a sign it rules, the contrast is at its clearest: a strong sense of balance and grace meets a strong urge to go first. In a business partnership, one may design and the other sell; between siblings or friends, one keeps the peace while the other makes the plans.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Seesaws settle into roles. One person always pursues and the other always responds; one always decides and the other always accommodates. Both can end up calling the other "too much" or "too passive", when each holds half of something shared. Harmony that only one person maintains turns into appeasement, and assertion that never waits turns into pressure. It's a seesaw, though, not a contest, and other angles arrange the pair differently:

- **[Venus conjunct Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-conjunct-mars-synastry/):** the two ends meet in one place, and interest becomes action at once.
- **[Venus sextile Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-sextile-mars-synastry/):** a friendly angle where invitations pass easily both ways.
- **[Venus square Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-square-mars-synastry/):** halfway round, where timing rather than role is the question.
- **[Venus trine Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-trine-mars-synastry/):** a sign closer, in harmony, taste and drive in step.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Swap ends on purpose. Let the Venus person start something, and the Mars person do the choosing; notice which end you each drift to under stress. Say what you admire in the other's end, out loud, because an opposition works through recognition. In a couple or a friendship, alternate who plans; in a team, let each person sometimes do the other's job.

About 1 pair of people in 15 has this opposition, one way round or the other, within 6°, the same share as the conjunction. That surprises people who know it as a rare natal aspect: in one chart only about 1 person in 87 has it, because Venus never strays more than about 47° from the Sun, and Mars has to be far round the sky to face it. Between two charts, nothing ties the Venus of one to the Mars of the other. With Venus moving a degree or so in a day and Mars more slowly still, the hour hardly matters for the aspect, and entirely for the houses ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

In a single person's chart, [Venus opposite Mars](/astrology/venus-opposite-mars/) asks them to hold both ends. Between two, each holds one, and both have to agree to look at it together ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
