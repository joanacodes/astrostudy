Unukalhai is the Serpent's neck, the brightest star of the one constellation that comes in two pieces, and its degree falls in Scorpio: 22°26′ in 2026. Its Latin name moves it down the snake and calls it the heart.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Unukalhai is α Serpentis, number 5854 in the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, and at magnitude 2.65 the brightest star of Serpens. Serpens is the only constellation split into two separate parts: when Eugène Delporte drew the modern borders in the 1920s, he cut the snake into a head, Serpens Caput, to the west and a tail, Serpens Cauda, to the east, with the Serpent-bearer, Ophiuchus, standing between them. Unukalhai is in the head. It is an orange giant about 74 light-years away, some thirteen times as wide as the Sun.

In the tropical zodiac it stood at 22°04′ [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/) in 2000 and stands at 22°26′ in 2026. So a star of the Serpent sits in the sign of the Scorpion, while the Scorpion's own sting, [Shaula](/astrology/shaula/), lies more than a month further along the Sun's year, in Sagittarius ([constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/)).

Its latitude is 25.5° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/). The Moon and planets all pass well to the south of it, and in a birth chart they can share its degree, never its place in the sky.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

Its two names disagree about which part of the snake it is. The Arabic ʽunuq al-ḥayya means the serpent's neck; the Latin *Cor Serpentis* makes it the serpent's heart.

Older Arabic star lore also strung it into al-Nasaq al-Yamānī, the Southern Line, one of a pair of star lines, together with stars of the Serpent and the Serpent-bearer. In Chinese astronomy most of the Serpent's stars belong to the wall of the Heavenly Market, and Unukalhai is the seventh star of its right-hand wall, standing for Shu, one of eleven old states whose names the wall carries. The same enclosure holds the Emperor's Seat, [Ras Algethi](/astrology/ras-algethi/).

A neck, a heart, a line, a state on a wall: Unukalhai's lore is a set of places in other people's maps of the sky, and on this page it stays that.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun reaches Unukalhai on about 13 and 14 November, a week or so before it leaves Scorpio ([Sun in Scorpio](/astrology/sun-in-scorpio/)). A planet counts as on the star from 21°26′ to 23°26′ Scorpio in a 2026 chart, the single degree either side that this site uses ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)).

The slow planets stay longer, and come back. Neptune was within a degree of it, on and off, from December 1965 to October 1967 ([Neptune in Scorpio](/astrology/neptune-in-scorpio/)); Uranus in parts of 1979 and 1980; Pluto from late 1991 to the autumn of 1993 ([Pluto in Scorpio](/astrology/pluto-in-scorpio/)). A planet that slow crosses the star's degree, turns back over it while retrograde, then crosses it a third time, so people born many months apart can share the same contact ([natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)).
