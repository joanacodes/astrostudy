Mars square North Node places your drive crosswise to the axis of your growth, a quarter of the circle from each node. Your effort stands at the crossing between old ground and new, and keeps being asked which way to go.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is your will in action: starting, pushing, competing, holding ground. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) describes a capacity that develops through practice and feedback; the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/), opposite, holds what is already consolidated, the responses you reach for without thinking. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates Mars to that axis through constructive friction: neither the drive nor the direction gives way, and each keeps pressing on the other.

In plain words, your effort doesn't fit neatly at either end. When you push in the familiar way, the North Node's direction tugs at you; when you push towards the new, you feel the pull of what you already know works. The square doesn't ask you to pick one end for good. It asks you to choose, case by case, which kind of effort a situation needs, the practised one or the one you're still learning, rather than deciding by habit or by principle alone.

None of this is a fault. The friction is a kind of training, and over the years it tends to produce someone who can switch between old strengths and new ones deliberately: an unusual skill, and one many people never have to learn.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The nodes fall in opposite signs of one modality, and a square usually puts Mars in that modality too, so all three move in the same manner. With the North Node in Capricorn and the South Node in Cancer, Mars would usually be in Aries or Libra, all cardinal: effort that starts things, set between home and a public role. The houses make it personal: if the nodal axis runs from the 4th house to the 10th, Mars in the 1st or the 7th turns your own initiative, or a partnership, into the hinge between home and work. Houses need a birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

About 1 person in 15 has this square within 6°, counted with the true node. The mean node sits within a couple of degrees of it and can shift the orb a little, so I check both ([mean node and true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/)). Tight, the question of which end to serve comes up constantly; wide, mostly at turning points.

Against the square, the pair's other aspects:

- **[Mars conjunct North Node](/astrology/mars-conjunct-north-node/):** Mars on the North Node; effort is the direction itself.
- **[Mars sextile North Node](/astrology/mars-sextile-north-node/):** an opening towards growth, with an easier current back to the familiar.
- **[Mars trine North Node](/astrology/mars-trine-north-node/):** effort flows towards growth and keeps a friendly link to old skills.
- **[Mars opposite North Node](/astrology/mars-opposite-north-node/):** Mars on the South Node, where effort is most at home and growth lies across the chart.

[Mars square North Node in synastry](/astrology/mars-square-north-node-synastry/) sets one person's drive across the other's path of growth: bracing, sometimes unsettling, and different depending on whose chart you start from.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting North Node square natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-north-node-square-natal-mars/) comes round about every nine years, because the node, travelling backwards, squares any point from both sides of its round. With this square in your chart, the timing is set by your own nodes: the two points the node must reach to square your Mars are the very places your own nodes occupy. That makes every [nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/) and every [nodal reversal](/astrology/nodal-reversal/) a square to your Mars as well, each lingering for weeks.

Weather, not a forecast: for a few weeks, choosing between old ground and new simply asks for more of your attention than usual.
