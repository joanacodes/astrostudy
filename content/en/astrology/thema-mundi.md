Hellenistic astrologers drew a chart for a moment nobody saw: the birth of the world. They called it the Thema Mundi, and it works less like a horoscope than like a diagram, the one that shows why each planet rules the signs it rules.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The chart rises in [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), with the Moon in it. The Sun stands in [Leo](/astrology/leo/), and the five planets follow in the signs beyond: Mercury in Virgo, Venus in Libra, Mars in Scorpio, Jupiter in Sagittarius and Saturn in [Capricorn](/astrology/capricorn/). Every body sits in a sign it rules ([Domicile](/astrology/domicile/)), and the planets run outward from the lights from the quickest to the slowest, the ladder that also gives the [Chaldean order](/astrology/chaldean-order/). Its best-known telling is in Macrobius's *Commentary on the Dream of Scipio*, and it belongs to [Hellenistic astrology](/astrology/hellenistic-astrology/).

It was never a sky anyone saw. It puts Venus two signs from the Sun and Mercury one, about 60° and 30°, while in fact Venus never strays more than about 47° from the Sun and Mercury about 28°. Nor does it follow the modern habit of reading Aries as the natural first house: the first house of the world's chart is Cancer.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The rulerships come out of the chart in two moves. First, the two lights take the signs of the brightest, warmest weeks of the northern year: the Moon takes Cancer, the Sun Leo. Then the zodiac is split along the line between them, a solar half running from Leo to Capricorn and a lunar half from Cancer back round to Aquarius, and each planet takes one sign on each side, in order of its distance from the lights:

- **Mercury** takes the signs next to the lights, Gemini and Virgo, because it never strays more than a sign from the Sun.
- **Venus** takes the next pair, Taurus and Libra: it never strays more than two.
- **Mars** takes Aries and Scorpio, and **Jupiter** Pisces and Sagittarius, in the same way.
- **Saturn** takes Capricorn and Aquarius, the signs facing the lights, for the cold and dark of that time of year.

The aspects come from the same picture. In the Thema Mundi, Saturn opposes the Moon, Mars squares the Sun, Jupiter is trine to the Sun and Venus sextile to it. Tradition gave each aspect the nature of the planet that makes it there: the opposition Saturn's, the square Mars's, the trine Jupiter's, the sextile Venus's. The two hard aspects went to the two malefics and the two easy ones to the two benefics, which are the tradition's words, not mine ([benefics and malefics](/astrology/benefics-and-malefics/)). Mercury sits 30° from the Sun and from Venus, and a sextile from the Moon and from Mars.

The same habit of starting from the two lights shapes the lots. The seven Hermetic lots begin with Fortune and Spirit, the lots of the Moon and the Sun, and count the five planets' lots from them ([Doctrine of the lots](/astrology/doctrine-of-the-lots/)). And the ladder that runs outward from Leo, Mercury to Saturn, is the one the [planetary hours](/astrology/planetary-hours/) climb down from the other end, Saturn first. Neither the chart nor the clock is something you can watch happen overhead; both are a tradition's way of putting the seven in order.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Nobody is born with the Thema Mundi, so I never read it as a chart. I read it as the reasoning behind rules I use in every reading. When I look at the ruler of a sign, it's this scheme's ruler, and for Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces I read both: the traditional ruler the Thema Mundi hands down, Mars, Saturn or Jupiter, and the modern one, Pluto, Uranus or Neptune ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)).

The aspects are where I part company with it. The chart made the square Martial and the opposition Saturnine, and the old books turned that into a ranking, with the opposition the harder of the two. I keep the geometry and leave the verdict. A square, to me, is friction that pushes two functions to adjust, an opposition a polarity that asks for balance, and neither is a misfortune or a sentence on anyone's life: a chart shows how you tend to move, not where you must end up ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Thema Mundi is the real birth chart of the world.”** It was symbolic from the start: Venus and Mercury sit at distances from the Sun they never reach.
- **“The rulerships are arbitrary.”** The Thema Mundi sets out a scheme: light and warmth for the lights, distance from the Sun for the planets, cold and dark for Saturn.
- **“Aries rising is the natural chart of the world.”** The ancient chart rises in Cancer. Reading Aries as the natural first house is a modern habit.
- **“It makes squares and oppositions unlucky.”** It gave them the nature of Mars and Saturn, the tradition's malefics. That's the old books' verdict; the geometry doesn't need it.
