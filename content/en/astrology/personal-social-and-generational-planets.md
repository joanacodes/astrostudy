Astrologers sort the ten planets of a chart into three groups, and the sorting rule is speed. The fast ones describe you; the slow ones describe the times you were born into; the two in the middle connect the one to the other.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The groups go by how long each body takes to cross a sign:

- **Personal planets:** the [Sun](/astrology/sun/), about a month in each sign; the [Moon](/astrology/moon/), about two and a half days; [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), usually two to three weeks; [Venus](/astrology/venus/), usually three to four weeks; and [Mars](/astrology/mars/), about six to seven weeks.
- **Social planets:** [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/), about a year in each sign, and [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), about two and a half years.
- **Generational planets:** [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), about seven years in each sign, [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/), about fourteen, and [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/), anywhere from twelve to thirty-one.

The Sun and the Moon aren't planets to an astronomer, but the word once meant a wanderer, anything that moved against the fixed stars, and astrology has kept it. They're also the [luminaries](/astrology/luminaries/), the two lights, and many astrologers set them apart at the head of the personal group. The generational planets are the ones nobody could see without a telescope: Uranus was discovered in 1781, Neptune in 1846 and Pluto in 1930, which is why the tradition worked with seven ([The seven traditional planets](/astrology/seven-traditional-planets/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Speed decides who shares a placement. Two people born a month apart usually have different Sun signs, often different Mercury and Venus signs, and almost certainly different Moon signs. Two people born in the same school year often share a Jupiter sign; two born within a couple of years, a Saturn sign. Everyone born over the seven years of a Uranus sign shares it, and the same goes for Neptune and Pluto over much longer stretches ([Generational placements by birth year](/astrology/generational-placements-by-birth-year/)).

The groups also describe what the planets do. In the system I built for my readings, the personal planets are the functions of an individual life: the Sun organises identity and purpose, the Moon regulates needs and feelings, Mercury processes and exchanges information, Venus weighs value and attraction, Mars starts action and effort. The social planets connect that life to a wider world. Jupiter expands understanding and meaning, Saturn builds structure and responsibility, and their cycles mark stages of life that people share, the [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/) at about 29½ among them. The generational planets work at the scale of collective change: Uranus innovation and liberation, Neptune imagination and compassion, Pluto transformation and renewal. None of the three is chaos, deception or destruction.

When two slow planets meet in a hard aspect, they can hold it for years, often from signs of the same cross ([Quadruplicities](/astrology/quadruplicities/)), and a whole cohort is born with it.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The groups set an order of attention, not of importance. I read the personal planets closely, through sign, house and aspects, because they're where a chart is most individual. I read a generational planet mostly through its house and through the aspects it makes to the fast ones: Pluto in a sign you share with millions of people becomes yours when it sits on your Ascendant or squares your Moon. That's also why the houses matter so much for the slow planets, and why I need [the birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/).

And here my notebook turned the usual order upside down. The placements everyone reads first, the Sun sign, Venus and Mercury, held about a third of the time when I checked them. The three that held 75 to 90% of the time were Saturn, a social planet, with Chiron and Lilith ([my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). The Moon, Mars, Jupiter and the three outer planets were checked like every placement, and weren't among those three. So speed tells you who shares a placement, not which placements will describe you best, and that's why Saturn gets so much of my time in a reading.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Generational planets don't matter in a personal chart.”** Their signs are shared, but their houses and their aspects to your personal planets are yours alone.
- **“The personal planets are the important ones.”** They're the most individual. In my notebook, Saturn held far more often than the Sun sign, Venus or Mercury.
- **“Pluto isn't a planet any more, so it doesn't count.”** Astronomers classed it as a dwarf planet in 2006. Astrology reads it for what it describes, not for its size.
- **“Your generation's placements are your personality.”** They describe the times you were born into, shared with everyone born over the same years. Your personal planets, houses and aspects decide how you meet them.
