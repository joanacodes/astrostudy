A relationship with its composite Sun in Scorpio does its real living below the surface. What the bond is about tends to live in what the two of you have been through and trusted each other with, more than in anything you show.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/) is fixed water, and when the midpoint of your two natal Suns lands there, [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) gives the relationship a [Sun](/astrology/sun/) that organises itself around depth. It's as common as most composite Suns, about 1 pair in 12. Its rulers are what set it apart. Scorpio has two, a modern one, [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/), and a traditional one, [Mars](/astrology/mars/); in a birth chart I read both, and in a composite they behave very differently.

Pluto moves so slowly that two people of the same age usually share its sign, and their composite Pluto sits there too. An age gap moves it to the midpoint between two generations: someone born in 1950, with Pluto in Leo, and someone born in 1978, with Pluto in Libra, have their composite Pluto in Virgo, a sign neither was born with. Mars, much faster, varies from pair to pair, so it's the more personal of the two rulers, and its sign and house show where this bond puts its effort.

Read as the relationship's own Sun, Scorpio puts its identity in what the two of you have lived through together. Such a bond often comes into its own in the hard conversation, the long night after bad news, the year when everything had to be rebuilt, and it tends to grow back stronger after a loss. It wants the truth from itself, and it tends to pool what it has, time, effort, old loyalties, into a shared reserve the two of you can draw on when it matters. Where a natal [Sun in Scorpio](/astrology/sun-in-scorpio/) keeps one person's core private, a composite gives that private core to the bond, so two quite open people can find that, together, they keep certain things just between them.

Depth has its weather. Intensity can turn into pressure, and a relationship that wants the truth can start testing itself when nothing needs testing. Depth isn't secrecy, though, and trust needs air as well as roots. When the pressure builds, saying the smaller true things helps, before they gather weight. A bond this serious also needs things it does lightly, a film, a walk, a joke that goes nowhere, and borrowing from its neighbours helps: the even-handedness of the [composite Sun in Libra](/astrology/composite-sun-in-libra/), the open horizon of the [composite Sun in Sagittarius](/astrology/composite-sun-in-sagittarius/).

A composite describes; it doesn't predict, and however deep its Sun, it can't measure how long a bond will last or what will become of it. I describe the technique rather than use it; those who do use it keep both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) open beside it. A bond built on trust would be the last to want its chart read in secret: a composite takes both people's birth data, and it's drawn only when both of them know ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
