Some feelings need to be talked through, others slept on. With the Moon in Aries, a feeling usually needs something to do: it arrives at full strength, asks for an answer, and once it has had one, it tends to be over.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In a chart, the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) handles what you feel and need: how you take in what happens, how you bond, what makes you feel safe again. [Aries](/astrology/aries/), the first sign, cardinal fire, gives all of that a quick and direct manner. You find out what a feeling is by answering it: you speak up, get up, put it into motion. The distance between feeling and doing is short, and that's both the gift of this Moon and the thing to watch.

Tradition gives the Moon no dignity in Aries: not its domicile or its exaltation, not its detriment or its fall. Nothing in the sign helps or hinders it by rule; the question is pace. Fire suits the Moon's responsiveness, and a cardinal sign starts things, so an Aries Moon meets a change by moving first rather than waiting to see how it feels.

Then I turn to [Mars](/astrology/mars/), the ruler of Aries, because this Moon borrows its outlet from it. The house Mars occupies is where these feelings tend to go once they're up: into a sport, a cause, a job with a deadline. Give that Mars real work and an Aries Moon settles faster.

About 1 person in 12 has the Moon in Aries, since it stays in each sign about two and a half days. Just before, it was in Pisces, where the [Moon in Pisces](/astrology/moon-in-pisces/) takes in every feeling in the room; just after, the [Moon in Taurus](/astrology/moon-in-taurus/) holds a feeling slowly and for a long time. Born on the day the Moon crossed either border, you need your birth time to know which sign is yours ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, you tend to need honesty more than tact: you'd rather hear the problem today, plainly, than sense it for a week. Your care is quick and practical. You turn up, sort it out, stand in front of anyone treating your people badly. What can catch you out is someone else's slower clock. A partner who goes quiet to think may still be entirely on your side, and after a flare, asking whether it's over for them too saves a lot of misunderstanding, because for you it often already is. Let yourself be comforted now and then: the need to be looked after doesn't vanish because you'd rather act.

## At work {#at-work}

Urgency often steadies you. A problem that needs an answer now gives the first surge somewhere to go, and you can be at your best in the very moment others freeze. Waiting is harder: a decision held up for weeks, a meeting about a meeting, a manager who never says what they think. Work with clear beginnings suits this Moon, a launch, a rescue, a first draft, in a team where people say what they mean. A flash of frustration may cross your face and be gone in minutes; tell colleagues it has gone, since for them it may not have.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Speed. When feeling and action arrive almost together, you can answer before you know what you need, and answer the wrong question: a sharp word where the feeling was hurt, a quick exit where it was fear. The softer needs are often the hardest to admit, because asking for comfort can feel like standing still. And since your own feelings burn off quickly, you may forget how a heated moment landed on someone else.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Give the first surge a body to move through: a walk round the block, a run, cold water on your face, anything that lets the heat out before the words. Then ask one question, *what do I actually need?*, and answer it before you reply to anyone. Name the softer feelings out loud when you can: *I was hurt*, *I was scared*. With the people you love, agree on a short pause either of you can call, so speed never has to win the argument.

When I tested my chart and other people's ([my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)), the Moon got its tick or its cross like everything else. Saturn, Chiron and Lilith were the ones that kept their ticks far more often; the Moon wasn't among them, and about a third of the whole list held. So I hear an Aries Moon as one quick, honest voice in a chart, and let the rest speak before deciding what it means.
