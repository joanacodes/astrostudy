When Jupiter in the sky reaches 90° from your birth Saturn, as it does on the way out from each crossing and again on the way back, the urge to grow and the part of you that sets limits get in each other's way. For a few days, or for months when Jupiter loops, the plan has to be redrawn so that both can live with it.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) holds the line in a chart: it sets limits, keeps commitments and asks what will still be standing in ten years. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) wants more room, more meaning, a bigger frame. The [square](/astrology/square/) between them is friction of the constructive kind: each has to adjust to the other rather than win.

The friction tends to take one of two shapes. Either growth pulls against structure, with impatience at your own caution, rules that suddenly feel too tight, a plan that outruns the hours; or structure pushes back against growth, with a doubt that makes a good idea look reckless, or a duty that leaves no room for it. Saturn tends to believe that control keeps things safe, and Jupiter that more is better: the square puts both beliefs under a little pressure, which is useful for each.

Where it bites depends on Saturn's house and on the house Jupiter is crossing, both of which need your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Born with [Jupiter square Saturn](/astrology/jupiter-square-saturn/)? Then you know this friction from the inside, and the sky stages it again close to each [Jupiter return](/astrology/jupiter-return/).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

From 1½° before exact to 1° after, the window I watch for every [transit](/astrology/transits/), a single crossing takes Jupiter about eleven days. A looping one, three crossings in about eight months, turns up in roughly three cases out of ten ([Jupiter retrograde](/astrology/jupiter-retrograde/)). With a waxing and a waning square in each round, people born between 1930 and 2009 meet thirteen or fourteen by 80.

One detail of the count stands out. Everyone born on 1 January of a year ending in 5, from 1935 to 2005, met the first square within about a year: at about four months for a birth on 1 January 1965, at about a year for 1 January 1985. Those dates fall a few years either side of a great conjunction, when Jupiter and Saturn stand far enough apart that a square to Saturn lies just ahead of Jupiter.

Of the five contacts Jupiter makes with your Saturn, the square is the one that asks for work. [Transiting Jupiter conjunct natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-conjunct-natal-saturn/) starts each round, three years before the first square; [transiting Jupiter opposite natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-opposite-natal-saturn/) sits between the two squares; [transiting Jupiter sextile natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-sextile-natal-saturn/) and [transiting Jupiter trine natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-trine-natal-saturn/) are the easier stops along the way. [Transiting Saturn square natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-saturn-square-natal-jupiter/) is the slow form of the same friction, with Saturn pressing on your growth for months.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Find the real constraint and name it: time, energy, a rule, a fear. It's easier to negotiate with a limit once you know which one it is.

Change one thing rather than everything. Shrink the plan, move the deadline, or let another commitment go: a square usually asks for a single adjustment, not a new life.

And keep the promise you can keep. Saturn trusts what is done more than what is announced, and a smaller plan carried through will teach you more than a large one left half-built.
