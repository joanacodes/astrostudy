The Ram, the Bull and the Twins were in the sky long before anyone told the stories now attached to them. The myths came in layers, and seeing the layers shows how loosely each story fits its sign.

## What it was {#what-it-was}

The first layer is Babylonian. In the star list known as MUL.APIN, probably compiled in the twelfth or eleventh century BCE, the figure we call [Aries](/astrology/aries/) is the Hired Man, or the Agrarian Worker. A ram stands apart on boundary stones from 1350 to 1000 BCE, and it probably won through its link with Dumuzi the shepherd. [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/) was already the Bull of Heaven, the bull the goddess Ishtar sends against Gilgamesh in the epic. Gemini held the Great Twins and Leo the Great Lion; [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/) was the Furrow, the goddess Shala with her ear of grain, which survives in the name of the star Spica, Latin for an ear of grain. Libra was either the Scales, sacred to the sun god Shamash, or the Claws of the Scorpion, and Scorpius was a scorpion already.

The Greek stories came later and fit more loosely. In Hellenistic astrology the Ram became the golden ram sent to save Phrixus from sacrifice; his sister Helle fell from its back into the strait named after her, the Hellespont. The Bull became Zeus in disguise, carrying Europa out to sea, which also explained why only its front half was drawn; or else Io, whom Zeus turned into a heifer. The Crab bit Heracles' foot while he fought the Hydra, and Hera, his enemy, set it among the stars; the Lion was the Nemean lion of his first labour. The [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/) fishes were Aphrodite and Eros escaping the monster Typhon by turning into fish in the Euphrates, in the version Hyginus preserves. Several signs carry more than one story: Virgo is Demeter, or Persephone, or Astraea, the goddess of justice who left the earth and became the constellation; Aquarius is Ganymede, carried off to pour for the gods, or Deucalion, who survived the flood.

Some figures were rearranged on the way. The Greeks, Aratus among them, called [Libra](/astrology/libra/)'s stars the Claws of the Scorpion, and Libra became a constellation of its own only in Rome, as the scales held by Astraea.

The planets' names came in the same layers. The Babylonians gave Venus to Ishtar, goddess of love, Mars to Nergal, their god of war, Mercury to Nabu, god of wisdom, Jupiter to Marduk, their chief god, and Saturn to Ninurta, a god of farming. The Greeks borrowed the practice, and there are too many matches between the two systems for them to have arisen separately, but the fit was loose: Nergal was a god of war, so the Greeks paired him with Ares, yet Nergal also brought pestilence and ruled the underworld. The Greeks first named the planets for their light, Phainon the shiner for Saturn, Phaethon the bright one for Jupiter, Pyroeis the fiery for Mars, Phosphoros the light-bringer for Venus and Stilbon the gleamer for Mercury, and gave each to a god: Kronos, Zeus, Ares, Aphrodite and Hermes. The Romans put their own gods' names on them, the ones we use, without full agreement: Pliny records that Mars's planet was also given to Hercules, Mercury's to Apollo, and Venus's to Juno and to Isis.

## What it gave astrology {#what-it-gave-astrology}

Much of the vocabulary. The pictures of the signs come from the old figures, and the characters of the planets keep a likeness to their gods: [Venus](/astrology/venus/) goes with love, pleasure and what you value, [Mars](/astrology/mars/) with drive and anger, [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) with the quick mind and the spoken word. Because each layer left a trace, a sign or a planet often carries more than one story.

That is exactly why I hold the myths at a distance. They changed as they travelled: Nergal's plague stayed in Babylon, Libra's claws became scales, Virgo is three goddesses at once. So I use a myth to illustrate a placement, never to prove what it means. In a reading, the meaning comes from the planet's function, the manner of its sign and the house it falls in, and I tell you when something rests on a placement that held less often when I tested my own and other people's charts ([my testing notes](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

The figures also belong to constellations, and those no longer match the signs. The signs I read are twelve equal parts of the zodiac counted from the March equinox, and the stars that gave them their names have drifted away from them ([Constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/)). Babylon's part in all this goes beyond names: [Babylonian astrology](/astrology/babylonian-astrology/) gave the twelve signs themselves.

The newer planets were given gods on purpose, to keep the old pattern going ([How the outer planets got their names](/astrology/how-the-outer-planets-got-their-names/)). And the symbols that stand for all of them on a chart have a history of their own, partly made of the same gods' attributes ([The origin of the glyphs](/astrology/origin-of-the-glyphs/)).
