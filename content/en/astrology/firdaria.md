Firdaria give each planet a turn in charge of a stretch of your life, on a clock set at birth: ten years for one, eight for the next, thirteen after that. What makes the sequence yours is the hour of your birth, which decides where it starts, and the state of each planet in your chart when its turn comes.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Firdaria (singular *firdar*, from the Persian *fardār*) belong to the family of time-lord techniques, in which one planet takes charge of a stretch of years. Here the seven classical planets each govern a fixed number of years, then the Moon's two nodes follow, and the whole cycle comes to 75 years. Medieval Latin texts wrote the word *alfridaria*.

Where it comes from is still argued. The scholar Martin Gansten describes the system as fixed planetary periods, probably of medieval Persian origin, while noting that neither its origin nor the etymology of its name has been settled. The best-documented early form is in [Abu Ma'shar](/astrology/abu-mashar/), in the ninth century. [Al-Biruni](/astrology/al-biruni/) described it again in his *Book of Instruction in the Elements of the Art of Astrology*, and so did the eleventh-century Persian author Shāhmardān; Latin translations then carried it to [Guido Bonatti](/astrology/guido-bonatti/) in the thirteenth century ([Persian and Arabic astrology](/astrology/persian-and-arabic-astrology/)).

What sets firdaria apart is that nothing in them has to be calculated. [Primary directions](/astrology/primary-directions/) time a life by the calculated movement of points through the chart; here the lengths and the order are fixed, and only the starting point changes. The same texts also mention much longer firdaria, used in [mundane astrology](/astrology/mundane-astrology/) for dynasties and eras; the 75-year cycle is the one used for a person.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

First, day or night. A birth with the Sun above the horizon starts with the Sun; one with the Sun below it starts with the Moon. That is the old distinction of [sect](/astrology/sect-day-and-night-charts/), and near sunrise or sunset a few minutes decide it, which is one reason the birth time comes before anything else ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Then the sequence, in the version Abu Ma'shar gives:

- **Day birth:** Sun from birth to 10, Venus 10 to 18, Mercury 18 to 31, Moon 31 to 40, Saturn 40 to 51, Jupiter 51 to 63, Mars 63 to 70.
- **Night birth:** Moon from birth to 9, Saturn 9 to 20, Jupiter 20 to 32, Mars 32 to 39, Sun 39 to 49, Venus 49 to 57, Mercury 57 to 70.
- **Both:** the North Node from 70 to 73, the South Node from 73 to 75, then the cycle starts again.

Shāhmardān explained the numbers as a series built around the Sun: Mars seven years, Venus eight, the Moon nine, the Sun ten, Saturn eleven, Jupiter twelve, Mercury thirteen.

Each planet's period then divides into seven equal sub-periods. The first belongs to the period's own lord; the other planets follow in the same order, each as a partner. A day birth at 35, for instance, is in the Moon's period, whose nine years give sub-periods of about fifteen months: Moon, Saturn, Jupiter, then Mars, which covers the 35th birthday. The nodes' years aren't divided. Abu Ma'shar's reason was that the nodes rule no sign, so they can't share out time the way the planets do.

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

Which planet's function is in front for a stretch of years, and which one colours it for a few months. The texts never read the lord by its nature alone: its sign, house, aspects and dignities in the birth chart describe how that period is likely to be lived. A Saturn period read through a natal Saturn at ease in its own sign is a different season from one read through a Saturn under pressure, and the sub-period's lord adjusts the tone again.

Shāhmardān himself warned that judging a year from the table alone gives an excessively general result: people born by day don't all live the same events because they share the same lords. That warning is the best way to read firdaria today, as emphasis set against the whole chart.

Firdaria aren't part of my forecasts. The time-lords I use are the lord of the year in [annual profections](/astrology/annual-profections/) and the chapters of [zodiacal releasing](/astrology/zodiacal-releasing/), read with transits and progressions ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Its limits {#its-limits}

Everyone born by day passes through the same lords at the same ages, and everyone born by night through theirs. Whatever is personal comes from the natal condition of each lord, never from the table.

The versions don't agree about night births. Abu Ma'shar and Shāhmardān place the nodes after Mercury, at 70 to 75, for everyone; a reading of Bonatti puts them straight after Mars in a night chart, so from 39 the two versions name different lords. A program may use either, so check which one before reading anything into a period.

And a period is a frame, not an event. It says which planet is in charge, never what will happen while it is.
