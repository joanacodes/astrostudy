Most house systems start from the Ascendant and the Midheaven. Morinus uses neither. It divides the sky's equator into twelve and hands each piece straight to the zodiac, so its houses come out nearly equal and the horizon plays no part at all.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

In Morinus, the [celestial equator](/astrology/celestial-equator/) is cut into twelve equal arcs of 30°, two sidereal hours each, counted from the point where the meridian crosses it. Each dividing point is then carried onto the zodiac along a great circle through the poles of the ecliptic, the two points of the sky 90° from the Sun's path. Where that circle meets the zodiac is the cusp. In effect, each twelfth of the equator is translated into the zodiac's own coordinates.

Because the zodiac is tilted against the equator by about 23½°, equal arcs of the one don't become exactly equal arcs of the other, but nearly: Morinus houses run from about 28° to about 32° of the zodiac, at any birthplace.

Nothing in that construction involves the horizon. The [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) doesn't open the 1st house and the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) doesn't open the 10th. The 10th cusp falls up to about 5° from the Midheaven, and the 1st cusp lies exactly 90° on from it, wherever the Ascendant happens to be. The latitude of the birthplace changes nothing, so two people born at the same moment on the same line of longitude, one far north of the other, share the same Morinus houses.

The name is the Latin form of Jean-Baptiste Morin (1583–1656), a physician by training, professor of mathematics at the Collège Royal in Paris from 1630 until his death, an opponent of Galileo and a would-be solver of the longitude problem. As an astrologer he is remembered for his *Astrologia Gallica*: 26 books in a folio of some 850 pages, published at The Hague in 1661, five years after his death.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Morinus belongs to a family that divides the equator into equal steps of two sidereal hours from the meridian. Its members differ in one thing: the circles that carry the steps onto the zodiac. [Regiomontanus](/astrology/regiomontanus/) uses circles through the north and south points of the horizon, which bring in the latitude and put both angles on cusps. [Meridian (axial) houses](/astrology/meridian-axial-houses/) use circles through the celestial poles, which keep the Midheaven as the 10th cusp and give the 1st to the [East Point](/astrology/east-point/). Morinus uses circles through the ecliptic's poles, the one choice that keeps neither angle.

Set beside the time-based systems, [Alcabitius](/astrology/alcabitius/) or Placidus, Morinus is their opposite: they are built on the horizon and on the pace at which degrees rise, and it ignores both. The [Topocentric](/astrology/topocentric/) system, a recent one from Argentina, goes the other way and stays within a degree of Placidus.

Morin's own astrology, as the *Astrologia Gallica* sets it out, was about prediction from the birth chart: directions above all, solar and lunar returns, and transits as a subsidiary tool for timing. He championed directions "in mundo", a technique largely based on the work of Regiomontanus.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Morinus is the system furthest from mine on the point I care about most. In [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/), the Ascendant is the door of the 1st house and the Midheaven the door of the 10th, so the most personal points of a chart are also where its houses begin ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)). Morinus gives that up on purpose, and gains in return houses of almost equal size that behave the same way at every latitude, including beyond the polar circles, where Placidus can't be drawn ([houses at high latitudes](/astrology/houses-at-high-latitudes/)).

A Morinus chart still has an Ascendant and a Midheaven, with their signs and aspects; they just don't open houses. So when you compare it with a Placidus chart, expect a planet near the Ascendant to change house more often than between two systems that both start from it. And though Morinus ignores the latitude, it doesn't ignore the moment: its divisions start from the meridian, which turns with the sky, so the birth time matters as much as in any system ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“In Morinus, the Ascendant opens the 1st house.”** Neither angle opens a house: the 1st cusp is 90° from the Midheaven, and the 10th cusp is not the Midheaven.
- **“Morinus fails near the poles, like Placidus.”** Latitude plays no part in it, so it can be drawn beyond the polar circles too.
- **“Morinus houses are 30° each.”** Nearly, but not quite: between about 28° and 32°.
