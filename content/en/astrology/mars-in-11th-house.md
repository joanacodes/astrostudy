Mars in the 11th house is strongest in company. It's at its strongest with a team behind it and a future in front of it: the friend it stands up for, the group it organises, the hope it's working towards.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is effort, initiative and courage. [The 11th house](/astrology/11th-house/) is friends, groups and hopes: the people you build a future with, and the future you'd like to build. With Mars here, networks and shared goals get your energy. You're often the one who gets a group moving, organises the event, defends a friend, or puts real work into a cause. Friendships tend to be active, built on doing things together, and you'll go into battle for the people you count as yours.

Tradition named this succedent house the Good Spirit and placed Jupiter's [joy](/astrology/planetary-joys/) in it; its natural sign is Aquarius. So this Mars works in the house where hope rejoices. Opposite sits the 5th, your own pleasures; the 11th is about what you want with and for other people.

It's also the house of hopes, and with Mars here they tend to come with a plan: you'd rather work towards a future than wait for it. Your ambitions are often collective, a project, a community, a better way of doing something together, and you can carry a group's hope a long way on your own energy.

When you were born, Mars had risen a few hours earlier and was climbing the eastern sky. I read the 11th, like every house, in Placidus, which is why I ask for your birth time before I say anything about it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). [Mars in the 10th house](/astrology/mars-in-10th-house/) makes its own name; [Mars in the 12th house](/astrology/mars-in-12th-house/) works where no one sees.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Friendship is often the root of love for this Mars: a partner who is also a teammate, someone to plan the future with. You may meet partners through groups and causes, and a circle of friends who also know your partner often makes the relationship stronger. Loyalty matters, and so does freedom: you want a relationship that leaves room for your friends and projects. Friction can come when a partner feels they're competing with your group for your time, and a fair share of both is the skill.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, Mars in the 11th thrives in teams with a shared aim: campaigns, start-ups, unions, community work, technology projects, any network where people work for something bigger than a pay slip. You're good at rallying people and at turning hopes into plans with deadlines. Volunteering can matter to you as much as paid work, if the cause is right. Office politics and cliques test you; you'd rather fight for the group than inside it.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Friction in teams, especially when you think the group is moving too slowly. Taking over a shared project because it's quicker to do it yourself. Fighting for every cause at once until you're worn out, or carrying a group's hopes so far on your own that you forget to ask for help. And friendships that end over a single quarrel, when a little patience might have kept them.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Share the lead: let others take a turn, and back them when they do. Choose one or two causes and give them real, sustained effort. Join a team sport or a group with a clear goal, so that the drive has somewhere structured to go. When a friendship hits a quarrel, wait a day before deciding what it means. And keep one hope that's yours alone, alongside the ones you hold with others.
