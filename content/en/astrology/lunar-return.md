Once a month the Moon comes back to the exact degree it held when you were born. A chart cast for that minute is a lunar return: the month's counterpart to the solar return, and the shortest of the returns astrologers use.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) goes once round the zodiac in about 27⅓ days, 27 days 7 hours and 43 minutes on average. Each time it reaches its birth degree, it makes a lunar return, thirteen or fourteen times a year. The chart drawn for that moment has your Moon exactly where it was at your birth; everything else has moved. The Sun stands about 27° further on than at the previous return, so each month's chart has the Sun roughly a sign along, and the Ascendant, the houses and the planets are new.

The idea mirrors the [solar return](/astrology/solar-return/), the chart for the Sun's yearly comeback, on a faster clock. Because the Moon's speed varies along its orbit, the gap between two returns shifts by hours, and each return falls at a different hour of the day.

It isn't the new moon. A new moon is the Moon meeting the Sun, every 29½ days, the same moment for everybody. A lunar return is the Moon meeting your own birth degree, a moment that belongs to you.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Three things decide what a lunar return chart shows.

- **The birth time.** The Moon moves about half a degree an hour, so an hour's error in your birth time moves your natal Moon by half a degree, and the return by about an hour. An hour moves the return's Ascendant by about 15° on average, often into another sign. A lunar return is as sensitive to the birth time as the birth chart itself ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
- **The place.** As with the solar return, astrologers disagree: some cast it for the birthplace, others for where you are at the moment of the return. The Moon's degree is the same either way; the Ascendant and the houses are not.
- **The rest of the sky.** Each return finds the slow planets a little further on, and the Sun about a sign along, so the same Moon sits among different company every month.

Seen from the birth chart, every return happens in the same place: the transiting Moon is back on its natal degree, in the natal house your Moon occupies. The month in between, it crosses all twelve, about two and a half days in each ([the Moon through the houses](/astrology/moon-through-the-houses/)).

What doesn't always come back is the Moon's distance from the celestial equator. In some years the returning Moon is out of bounds, beyond the Sun's furthest reach, as it is twice a month until January 2030; in others it can't be ([the Moon out of bounds](/astrology/moon-out-of-bounds/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The system I built for my readings defines the lunar return as a monthly chart of emphasis: where feelings, needs and habits tend to gather for the coming month, read against the birth chart and alongside slower techniques, never as a set of monthly predictions.

It isn't one of the charts I use for timing. When I look ahead, I work with [transits](/astrology/transits/) from Jupiter to Pluto, Chiron and the true node, [secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/), solar arcs, the solar return, [profections](/astrology/annual-profections/) and zodiacal releasing, because those describe seasons of months and years. A lunar return describes a month in the life of your Moon, and the Moon was checked like every placement in my notebook without being one of the three that held far more often ([my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

If you follow your own lunar returns, read them the same way: which house of the return the Moon falls in, which angle it is near, what it meets, set against your birth chart. Keep the birth time in mind, and keep the scale in mind too. It's weather for a month, not a forecast.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A lunar return predicts what will happen this month.”** It describes where the month's emphasis tends to fall, never events.
- **“It falls on the same date every month.”** It comes about every 27⅓ days, so it moves through the calendar, earlier each month.
- **“The lunar return is your personal new moon.”** The new moon is the Moon meeting the Sun; the return is the Moon meeting its own birth degree.
- **“It needs only your birth date.”** The Moon's degree, the return's moment and its Ascendant all hang on the birth time.
