The second decan of Cancer runs from 10° to 20° of the sign, the first ten days of July for the Sun. It holds the degree of Jupiter's exaltation and, in today's zodiac, Sirius, the brightest star of the night sky.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

Lilly's [faces](/astrology/faces/) give it to [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), next after Venus in the Chaldean order. The modern decans give the middle of a water sign to the ruler of the next water sign, Scorpio, and Scorpio has two: [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) in modern tropical astrology, Mars in the tradition. I read both.

So a planet in these degrees answers first to the Moon, which rules [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), then to Mercury, or to Pluto and Mars, depending on the list. Through Mercury, it puts feelings into words and names what it needs; through Pluto, it goes beneath the surface and reorganises; through Mars, it acts to protect what matters. I look at all three in your chart before letting the decan say anything, and I say which list a remark comes from.

Jupiter has a stake here too. The old tables put the exact degree of its [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/) at the 15th of Cancer, between 14° and 15°, so a [Jupiter in Cancer](/astrology/jupiter-in-cancer/) on that degree stands where the tradition honoured it most, in a decan ruled by other planets.

## Its image {#its-image}

Budge's Egyptian name is Ṭepā-Kenmut, the first of three decans named for Kenmut, a run that carries on into the first decan of Leo. The Greco-Egyptian lists write Seta, Firmicus Maternus Sith, Joseph Scaliger Syth; the *Testament of Solomon* has Katanikotael.

Two later figures face each other across the boundary with the next decan. Cosmas of Maiuma gave this one Herakles and the following one Hekate, while Athanasius Kircher, in 1652, put Hecate here, one decan earlier ([Cancer, third decan](/astrology/cancer-third-decan/)). The lists disagree freely, which is one more reason I treat them as history ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun stands here for births from about 2 to 11 July ([Sun in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-cancer/)). In 2026 it reached 10° Cancer on 1 July at 20:04 UT and 20° on 12 July at 07:46 UT. It passes Sirius on about 5 and 6 July, and the star sits inside the 15th degree of Cancer, Jupiter's exaltation degree.

[Sirius](/astrology/sirius/) stands at 14°26′ Cancer in 2026 and [Canopus](/astrology/canopus/) at 15°19′, though both lie far south of the Sun's path, Canopus by almost 76°. A planet here shares their zodiac degree without passing near either. The decan before this one carries Sirius's old Egyptian name ([Cancer, first decan](/astrology/cancer-first-decan/)).

Pluto was crossing these degrees when it was discovered in 1930; it left them that August and kept coming back on its retrograde loops until the spring of 1932 ([Pluto in Cancer](/astrology/pluto-in-cancer/)). Uranus held them for births from July 1951 to July 1953, Saturn in 1944–46, 1974–75 and 2003–04. The total eclipse of the Moon on 31 December 2028 falls at 10°34′ Cancer, and the partial eclipse of the Sun on 11 July 2029 at 19°37′ ([Eclipse in Cancer](/astrology/eclipse-in-cancer/)).

In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Mercury, the planet of this face, was among the placements that held about a third of the time; Pluto and Mars, checked like everything else, weren't among the three that held far more often. So I keep the decan's voice light and let the planets in it speak through their own sign and house.
