Sirius, the brightest star of the night sky, now has its zodiac degree in the middle of Cancer, and so does Jupiter's exaltation. Both fall inside the second decan of Cancer, 10° to 20° of the sign, which the Sun crosses in the first ten days of July.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

Lilly's table of [faces](/astrology/faces/) moves on from Venus to [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) for the face. By element the decan passes to Scorpio, the next water sign, which modern tropical astrology gives to [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) and the tradition to Mars; I read both.

The Moon, ruler of [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), still comes first for a planet here; the decan then points to Mercury, or to Pluto and Mars, depending on the list. Through Mercury, such a planet puts feelings into words and names what it needs; through Pluto, it goes beneath the surface and reorganises; through Mars, it acts to protect what matters. I look at all three in your chart before letting the decan say anything, and I say which list a remark comes from.

Jupiter's mark here is a single degree. The 15th of Cancer, from 14° to 15°, is where the old tables set the peak of its [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/), so a [Jupiter in Cancer](/astrology/jupiter-in-cancer/) there sits on the most honoured degree the tradition gave it, though neither list of decan rulers names Jupiter.

## Its image {#its-image}

Kenmut gives its name to three decans in a row in Budge's list, and this is the first of them, Ṭepā-Kenmut; the run ends at the start of Leo. The other lists follow a different thread: Seta in the Greco-Egyptian ones, Sith for Firmicus Maternus and Syth for Joseph Scaliger, while the *Testament of Solomon* has a longer name, Katanikotael.

Two later figures face each other across the boundary with the next decan. Cosmas of Maiuma gave this one Herakles and the following one Hekate, while Athanasius Kircher, in 1652, put Hecate here, one decan earlier ([Cancer, third decan](/astrology/cancer-third-decan/)). The lists disagree freely, which is one more reason I treat them as history ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The decan covers birthdays from about 2 to 11 July ([Sun in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-cancer/)); the 2026 Sun was here from 20:04 UT on 1 July until 07:46 UT on 12 July. Around 5 and 6 July it passes Sirius, whose degree, the 15th of Cancer, is also Jupiter's exaltation degree.

[Sirius](/astrology/sirius/) stands at 14°26′ Cancer in 2026 and [Canopus](/astrology/canopus/) at 15°19′, though both lie far south of the Sun's path, Canopus by almost 76°. A planet here shares their zodiac degree without passing near either. The decan before this one carries Sirius's old Egyptian name ([Cancer, first decan](/astrology/cancer-first-decan/)).

Pluto was crossing these degrees when it was discovered in 1930; it left them that August and kept coming back on its retrograde loops until the spring of 1932 ([Pluto in Cancer](/astrology/pluto-in-cancer/)). Uranus's turn came for births from July 1951 to July 1953; Saturn passed through three times, in 1944–46, 1974–75 and 2003–04. The Moon is totally eclipsed at 10°34′ Cancer on 31 December 2028, and the Sun partially eclipsed at 19°37′ on 11 July 2029 ([Eclipse in Cancer](/astrology/eclipse-in-cancer/)).

In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Mercury, the planet of this face, was among the placements that held about a third of the time; Pluto and Mars, checked like everything else, weren't among the three that held far more often. So I keep the decan's voice light and let the planets in it speak through their own sign and house.
