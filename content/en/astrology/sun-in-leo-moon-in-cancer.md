Tradition gave the Sun and the Moon one sign each, Leo and Cancer, next door to each other, and in your chart each light is in its own. You were born in the last days of the lunar month, the Moon a sliver at dawn or too close to the Sun to be seen, and I read the pair as two households under one roof, a public one and a private one, both well kept.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Leo is fixed fire and Cancer cardinal water, and the Moon sits one sign behind the Sun: neighbours with nothing in common but the border. The [Sun in Leo](/astrology/sun-in-leo/) wants to be seen accurately and to stand behind what it makes, in the open; the [Moon in Cancer](/astrology/moon-in-cancer/) needs a safe place to come back to, and people to look after inside it. One faces outward, the other inward, and the step between them is the step across your own front door.

This is the only pair in which both lights are in their [domicile](/astrology/domicile/). The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) rules Leo and the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) rules Cancer, so neither hands its business to another planet and the trail of rulers stops at the [luminaries](/astrology/luminaries/) themselves. Their two houses therefore carry unusual weight, describing where your purpose and your comfort live with no go-between. It also means no ruler elsewhere in the chart arbitrates when they disagree; the aspects each light makes become the mediators.

The Moon trailed the Sun by 1° to 59°, counted as 301° to 359° ahead, twenty-four and a half to twenty-nine and a half days after the previous new moon: the month was nearly spent. About seven births in eight are [balsamic moon](/astrology/balsamic-moon/) births, the earliest belong to the [last quarter moon](/astrology/last-quarter-moon/), and the next [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/) was at most five days off. I read the balsamic phase as a feel for endings, for finishing things and clearing the ground, and [your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) describes all eight.

About 1 person in 141 has the pair. The Moon spends only some two and a half days in Cancer, so if it arrived from Gemini or left for Leo on your birthday, the time is what tells this page from the [Sun in Leo, Moon in Gemini](/astrology/sun-in-leo-moon-in-gemini/) and from the double Leo of the [Sun in Leo, Moon in Leo](/astrology/sun-in-leo-moon-in-leo/) ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). Born around 23 July, when the Sun itself changes sign, you need the time for your Sun as well. And with both lights ruling themselves, their houses carry the reading, one more reason I wait for the hour.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Love, for you, is a home with the lights on. The Cancer Moon makes the nest, remembers what everyone needs and closes ranks around its people; the Leo Sun throws the doors open on special days and wants the household proud of itself. You give a great deal of care and warmth, and you can be hurt by a partner who takes the shelter for granted or never says it's appreciated. The Moon's needs are often quieter than the Sun's gestures, so a partner may miss them: name them in words, not only in sighs.

## At work {#at-work}

You tend to lead the way a good host leads: protectively, generously, and with real pride in the team. Workplaces that feel like a household suit the pair, a small company, a restaurant kitchen, a school, a theatre troupe, anywhere loyalty runs both ways. A coldly transactional place starves the Moon, and an invisible role starves the Sun. Watch for treating every colleague's trouble as yours to carry home.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Pride and vulnerability live next door. The Leo Sun doesn't like to be seen struggling, and the Cancer Moon, when hurt, may withdraw into its shell, so a wound can go into hiding behind a cheerful public face. Old hurts keep well in Cancer, and pride keeps them company. What helps is a short list of people with whom you never have to be magnificent, and the habit of saying *I'm not all right* to one of them before the shell closes.
