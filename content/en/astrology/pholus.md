Pholus turned up in January 1992, the first centaur found after [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/), fifteen years later. The two carry the names of the two civilised centaurs of Greek myth. Some astrologers set Pholus beside Chiron in the chart. I don't, and this page says what it is, how it's read, and why.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Astronomers call a centaur any small body that orbits the Sun between Jupiter and Neptune and crosses the path of at least one giant planet. Such orbits don't last: the giants pull them out of shape within a few million years. Centaurs also behave partly like asteroids and partly like comets, and Pholus leans to the asteroid side. Unlike Chiron, it has never shown the haze of gas and dust that marks a comet.

It is about 100 km across, very red, and its orbit is a long ellipse. At its closest it comes 8.8 times as far from the Sun as the Earth, inside the orbit of [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/); at its farthest, 31.9 times, beyond [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/), crossing [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/)'s path on the way. One circuit takes about 92 years. David Rabinowitz of the Spacewatch survey picked it up at Kitt Peak, Arizona, on 9 January 1992, and it was named that July.

The name belongs to a centaur who, like Chiron, was gentle where the rest of his kind were wild. Heracles stopped at his cave and drank from a jar of wine Pholus kept there. The smell brought the other centaurs charging in, and Heracles drove them off with arrows dipped in the Hydra's poison; in the chase, one of them wounded Chiron. Back in the cave, Pholus picked up an arrow, wondering, in one version, how so small a thing could kill a centaur, and wounded himself with it by accident. He died of the poison.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A body found in 1992 has no ancient texts behind it, so what astrologers say about Pholus comes from its myth and from watching charts since. The myth turns on small things with large effects: one jar opened, a whole herd roused, a death from one small arrow. Those who use Pholus place it like any other body:

- **Its sign.** At about 92 years a circuit, it stays in a sign for years, longer in some than in others because its orbit is so stretched. Everyone born in those years shares it, so the sign is generational rather than personal ([personal, social and generational planets](/astrology/personal-social-and-generational-planets/)).
- **Its house.** That comes from the birth time, as every house does ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
- **Its contacts.** A close conjunction or opposition to the Sun, the Moon, a personal planet or an angle is what would make it personal.

Seen from Earth, it also turns [retrograde](/astrology/retrograde-explained/) for part of every year, as every body beyond our own orbit does. Its symbol was drawn in the late 1990s by the German astrologer Robert von Heeren: Chiron's key glyph with a P where the K was, or a Greek φ in a common variant. Unicode carries it, alongside one other centaur symbol, for [Nessus](/astrology/nessus/), found the following year by the same astronomer.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I don't read Pholus. The system I built for my readings works with the planets, Chiron, Lilith and the lunar nodes, and Pholus isn't among them, so this page is a description for anyone who meets it elsewhere.

There's a second reason, and it's the one I care about most. When I went through charts [like an invoice](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), placement by placement, I covered the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign, each planet, Chiron and Lilith. Pholus wasn't on the list. I can't tell you how often a Pholus placement holds, and a reading that leaned on it would rest on something I've never checked. The placements that held far more often than the rest were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, 75 to 90% of the time, and they're where a reading of mine spends its time ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

If you work with Pholus, three habits keep it in proportion: count only tight contacts, a degree or two, to the Sun, the Moon, the personal planets and the angles; read its sign as something you share with everyone born around the same years; and never let it outvote the planets ([How to use asteroids without drowning the chart](/astrology/how-to-use-asteroids-without-drowning-the-chart/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Pholus is an asteroid, like Ceres.”** It's a centaur, out among the giant planets, far beyond the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.
- **“Pholus was one of the wild centaurs.”** In the myth he was a host, as civilised as Chiron. The mythographer Apollodorus even gave him different parents from the herd: Silenus and an ash-tree nymph.
- **“Your Pholus sign describes you.”** It's shared by everyone born during the years Pholus spends in that sign. Only its house and close contacts could be yours alone, and the house needs your birth time.
- **“Astrologers have always used it.”** It was found in 1992 and given a symbol later that decade. What is said about it is a few decades old, not centuries.
