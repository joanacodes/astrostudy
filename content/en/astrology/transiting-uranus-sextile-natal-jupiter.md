Jupiter is where your chart keeps its sense of meaning: what you believe, what you're learning, how big you let your world be. Most people see Uranus reach 60° from it twice, and each time, for about twenty months, a different way of seeing things comes within reach.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) is the function of innovation and difference: the part of a chart that questions what it inherited and tries other arrangements. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) expands understanding and gathers experience into meaning; in your chart it shows how you grow and what you put your faith in. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/), 60°, relates two functions through opportunity and cooperation, so this [transit](/astrology/transits/) opens a door rather than pushing you through it.

What tends to open is the frame. A subject you had filed under *not for me* starts to look interesting, a book or a teacher rearranges ideas you've held for years, a trip or a change of desk shows how differently other people live. The new thing rarely contradicts what you believe; it makes it larger, or more precise. It can be the season you begin a course, a practice or a line of work whose importance only shows later.

Two traps sit on either side. One is waiting: a sextile is the quietest of the angles Uranus makes, and twenty months can pass with the window open and nobody climbing through. The other is mistaking novelty for growth, collecting new interests without letting any of them change how you think. Uranus brings the better question here; Jupiter, the patience to follow it somewhere.

The houses say where the door is. With Jupiter in your 9th and Uranus crossing your 11th ([Uranus transiting the 11th house](/astrology/uranus-transit-11th-house/)), a group, a network or a friend's project tends to be the way into a subject you've circled for years. Both houses hang on the minute you were born; without it I wouldn't know which door this is, and I don't do the reading ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

With the orbs I use for timing, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the sextile holds for about twenty months, and the shortest passes take about eleven. That outlasts any of Jupiter's own transits: in twenty months, Jupiter itself moves on through nearly two signs. In more than nine passes out of ten, the yearly retrograde loop carries Uranus across the exact sextile three times.

Your birth chart sets the age, because Uranus starts from its own natal degree and has to travel to one of the two points 60° from Jupiter. People born a few years apart can meet it decades apart: in the site's count, someone born at the start of 1995 met the first sextile at about 1, someone born at the start of 1965 at about 41. The two sextiles come roughly 28 or roughly 56 years apart, depending on which point Uranus reaches first, and by 80 everyone born between 1930 and 2009 has met at least one; most have met both.

Born with [Jupiter sextile Uranus](/astrology/jupiter-sextile-uranus/), you had the first sextile running at birth, and the second arrives with [transiting Uranus trine natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-uranus-trine-natal-uranus/), in your late twenties or your mid-fifties. Around the rest of the round, the sextiles sit about fourteen years either side of [transiting Uranus conjunct natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-uranus-conjunct-natal-jupiter/); [transiting Uranus square natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-uranus-square-natal-jupiter/) and [transiting Uranus trine natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-uranus-trine-natal-jupiter/) lie further out, and [transiting Uranus opposite natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-uranus-opposite-natal-jupiter/) halfway round.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Follow one lead properly. A sextile rewards the person who books the class, writes to the author or goes to see the place, and twenty months is long enough to move from curiosity to real skill in one thing.

Test the new idea against the old ones. Jupiter grows by synthesis, so the useful question is what the new way of seeing explains that the old one couldn't, not which of the two wins.

Keep a note of what opened. Sextiles are easy to forget, and a written record often shows, years later, that this was where a larger change began.
