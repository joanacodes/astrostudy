Does my chart show a weak heart? Will this transit make me ill? Is my 6th house warning me about something? People ask about health in many ways, and my answer is the same each time. I'd rather explain it properly than just say no.

## The short answer {#the-short-answer}

No. A birth chart can't diagnose an illness, foresee one or say how healthy you'll be, and no transit can make you ill or tell you whether a symptom is serious. I don't read health in a chart, for anyone: health diagnoses and medical questions are on my list of [what I won't read](/how-i-read/).

If you're worried about your health, a doctor is the person to see, and if it's urgent, the emergency services. Not a chart, not a transit, and not me.

## Why it matters {#why-it-matters}

Astrology hasn't always said so. Ptolemy gave a whole chapter to "bodily injuries and diseases" (*Tetrabiblos*, book III), and astrologers went on reading illness from charts long after him ([medical astrology, in history](/astrology/medical-astrology-in-history/)). The [6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) was given illness, and its old name was Bad Fortune. I describe that history honestly, and I don't practise it.

The reasons are practical as much as principled.

- **A chart can't see a body.** It describes tendencies of temperament and attention, in symbols. A blood test, a scan or an examination sees what's actually there.
- **A false alarm does harm.** Telling someone a transit "threatens their health" can leave them anxious for months, watching their body for a sign that was never coming.
- **False reassurance does more.** "Your transits look calm" is the worst reason to put off a check-up, and a chart that seems to promise good health can quietly delay the visit that matters.
- **Health is where trust is easiest to abuse.** Readings that diagnose from a chart, or sell remedies on the strength of one, prey on fear. Be wary of anyone who offers them ([fake psychics](/astrology/fake-psychics/)).

What a chart can do is smaller and still useful. It can describe how you tend to look after yourself: the rhythm of work and rest that suits you, what steadies you when things are hard (the Moon), how you handle limits (Saturn), where you tend to push past them (Mars). I read the 6th house as daily work and daily care, never as a forecast. Some books call a slow planet crossing it a health warning; I read [Saturn transiting the 6th house](/astrology/saturn-transit-6th-house/) as a season for reworking routines and the load you carry, and Neptune there as a time when routines blur and need a gentler structure. Weather, not symptoms. And Chiron, often called the wounded healer, describes how you adapt to something that persists, not an illness.

The same line holds for the hardest question of all: no chart can tell anyone when or how a life ends ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)).

## What to do instead {#what-to-do-instead}

- **For a symptom, or a worry about one:** see a doctor. If something feels urgent, call the emergency services where you are.
- **For a diagnosis you've already been given:** your doctor and the specialists caring for you are the people to ask about what it means and what comes next. Second opinions come from medicine, not from a chart.
- **For worry that won't settle, low mood, or fear about your body that takes over your days:** talk to a doctor or a therapist. Asking for that help is the right step, not a failure of anything ([when to see a professional instead](/astrology/when-to-see-a-professional-instead/), [astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/)).
- **For understanding how you look after yourself:** this is where a chart can sit beside care, never instead of it. A reading can help you think about rest, routine and what restores you; your doctor stays in charge of your health.

If a reading of mine ever touches something that sounds medical, I'll tell you so plainly and suggest who to see.
