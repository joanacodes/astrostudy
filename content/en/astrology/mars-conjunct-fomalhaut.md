Mars conjunct Fomalhaut puts Mars in the opening degrees of Pisces, on the degree of the star in the mouth of the Southern Fish. In most years that bring it, Mars is across in two or three days; in 2003, when Mars came about as close to the Earth as it ever does, it crossed three times.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read [Mars](/astrology/mars/) as the part of a chart that starts things and keeps at them: effort, initiative, the nerve to begin, and the line you hold when something presses against it. [Fomalhaut](/astrology/fomalhaut/) has a Chinese name that suits Mars better than any royal title. Chinese astronomers called it Běiluòshīmén, the Gate of the Northern Military Camp: a star standing alone at the entrance of the camp, among the stars of the Encampment, one of the twenty-eight lunar mansions.

So the planet named after the Roman god of war arrives at the gate of an army camp, and a star reading could make much of that. I don't take either name as a forecast of conflict. One of Mars's jobs, as I read it, is keeping a boundary: deciding what comes in and what stays out, and saying so plainly. A gate does that work too, and that is as far as I'd take the image.

The sign tells you more. The star's degree, 2°52′ Pisces in 1930 and 4°13′ in 2026, makes this a [Mars in Pisces](/astrology/mars-in-pisces/), where effort tends to follow a feeling or an image before it follows a plan, and where acting on someone else's behalf can come easier than acting on your own.

Usually the contact is brief. Between 1930 and 2009 Mars passed Fomalhaut's degree 45 times, roughly once every two years, and most passes lasted two or three days. 2003 broke the pattern. Mars crossed the degree going forward on 28 June, came back over it on 2 September while retrograde, and crossed forward again on 22 October. On 28 August, between the first two, it stood opposite the Sun a degree further on, close to its perihelion, about 56 million km from the Earth, near the 54 million that is as close as an opposition of Mars gets. Births in late June, in the first days of September and in late October 2003 all have the contact, the September ones with Mars retrograde on the star ([Mars retrograde in Pisces](/astrology/mars-retrograde-in-pisces/), [natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)).

That retrograde pass was also the nearest Mars came to Fomalhaut in the sky in those eighty years. Close to the Earth, its path dipped about six and a half degrees south of the ecliptic, which left some 14½° between planet and star; on every other pass the gap was wider, up to 20°. A planet on this degree shares the star's longitude and nothing like its place.

About 1 person in 210 born between 1930 and 2009 has Mars there, a little below the average for a one-degree window, since only 2003 brought a loop. Of the star's other contacts, the [Sun conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/sun-conjunct-fomalhaut/) is the most regular, the [Moon conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/moon-conjunct-fomalhaut/) the most frequent visitor, [Venus conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/venus-conjunct-fomalhaut/) the rarest and [Saturn conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-fomalhaut/) the slowest.

The star itself never enters my readings: the system I use draws no stars at all. What I'd read is Mars, through its sign, house and aspects, and the rule that brings stars into other astrologers' readings is set out under [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/).
