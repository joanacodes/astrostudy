A square from the nodes catches your Moon halfway between them: the North Node a quarter of the zodiac to one side, the South Node a quarter to the other. For a month or so, neither the familiar nor the new has your needs on its side, and both ask something of them.

## What changes {#what-changes}

The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) holds your needs: comfort, attachment, the rhythm of rest that keeps you steady. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) is where practice builds a less familiar capacity, and the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/), opposite, is where capacity is already built. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates two functions through friction that asks for adjustment, and under this [transit](/astrology/transits/) your needs sit across the path between the two ends rather than on it.

In plain words, a need that doesn't fit the plan asks to be fitted in. You may notice it as tiredness at the wrong moment, as a pull towards home just when the next step wants you out, or as an old routine that no longer settles you while the new one doesn't yet. Friction here isn't failure: it's the moment a plan learns what your Moon requires, and a direction that leaves out what you need doesn't hold for long.

The sky shows the geometry plainly. A quarter of a circle from its nodes, the Moon's path is as far from the Sun's as it ever gets, a little over 5°, so in these weeks the Moon reaches your Moon's degree well north or well south of the Sun's path, never on it. With no crossing near your Moon, my forecast has nothing to list: it counts the node only when a crossing sits on your Moon or opposite it.

With your [Moon in the 10th house](/astrology/moon-in-10th-house/), the friction often runs between what keeps you feeling safe and what your work or public role expects. The Moon's house, and its exact degree, come from your birth time, and this square can't be read without it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). And if the friction turns into a low mood or an anxiety that doesn't lift, that is for a trained professional to help with, not a chart ([astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

I give every transit the same window, opening 1½° before exact and closing 1° after, and the node usually crosses it in about a month. The quickest tenth are done within three and a half weeks; the slowest tenth take three and a half months or more, because an eclipse season has all but stopped the node near the square, and about one pass in twenty-five brings three exact contacts. The eclipses of such a season land about 90° from your Moon, a square, which I don't count as an eclipse on your chart.

The square returns every nine years or so, from each side in turn: some four and a half years on from the node's crossing of your Moon ([Transiting North Node conjunct natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-north-node-conjunct-natal-moon/)), and again about four and a half years after it stands opposite ([Transiting North Node opposite natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-north-node-opposite-natal-moon/)). Each time it falls between [Transiting North Node sextile natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-north-node-sextile-natal-moon/) and [Transiting North Node trine natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-north-node-trine-natal-moon/). By 80, most people have met it nine times, nobody fewer than eight. With [Moon square North Node](/astrology/moon-square-north-node/) in your chart, the nodes recreate the square whenever they line up with your natal axis, at the [nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/) and at the [nodal reversal](/astrology/nodal-reversal/) alike.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Put a need in the diary as you would an appointment: an evening at home, a call to a friend. Needs kept out of the plan tend to come back as tiredness.

When you feel torn, write the two sides down, what the old way wants and what the new one wants, then add a third line: what you need from both.

Let one plan bend around you this month, instead of bending around it.
