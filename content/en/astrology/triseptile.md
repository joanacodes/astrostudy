The triseptile is the widest of the sevens: two planets three-sevenths of the circle apart, about 154°17′, a little past the quincunx. Of the three sevenths, it's the one I read as convergence: strands that went separate ways, coming back together.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The angle is 154°17′, three sevenths of 360°: five signs and just over four degrees. So a triseptile nearly always joins signs five apart, which share no element, modality or polarity. The exception comes from the last four degrees or so of a sign, where it reaches across to the opposite sign.

Convergence is the word I'd use for it. Two planets develop along separate lines, and over repeated rounds the lines come back together into one system that works on several levels at once, a coordination you only see once it's assembled. Convergence isn't destiny, and there's nothing magic or certain about it; nor can it be hurried: forced, it hardens into a rigid way of making things fit, and the healthier version takes its time.

This stretch of the circle is crowded. Between the biquintile at 144°, which Kepler's *Harmonices Mundi* (Linz, 1619) brought in beside Ptolemy's aspects, and the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) at 180°, sit the [quincunx](/astrology/quincunx/) at 150°, the triseptile and the quadnovile at 160°. Hence a tight orb, about a degree (see [orbs](/astrology/orbs/)), so that each keeps its own meaning.

A [square](/astrology/square/) and a [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) together make the quincunx; the triseptile lies just beyond it, 34°17′ past the [trine](/astrology/trine/) and 25°43′ short of the opposition. Three-sevenths times seven is three full turns, so in [harmonic charts](/astrology/harmonic-charts/) of the seventh harmonic it falls on a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) with the rest of its family.

## Between which planets {#between-which-planets}

I haven't written triseptile pages for pairs of planets, and I wouldn't: it's read in context, by the speed of the bodies involved.

Between two personal planets, the convergence happens in ordinary life: two habits developed separately, say how you work and how you rest, that eventually fall into one rhythm. Between a personal planet and Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune or Pluto, it can take years for the personal side to meet the larger one, and the meeting is the point. Between two slow planets, a generation shares it, and the houses show where it converges in your life.

The [biseptile](/astrology/biseptile/), at 102°51′, is the branching before the convergence. The [novile](/astrology/novile/), at 40°, starts another family, the ninths, read as ripening rather than recurrence.

## The triseptile in synastry {#in-synastry}

Between two charts, a triseptile tends to describe two people who arrive at the same place from different directions: different histories, different methods, and a shared result neither would have reached alone. It takes time to show, and it's easy to overlook beside the major aspects. I read it from both sides in [synastry readings](/synastry/), and only when it's tight; it never means two lives were destined to converge.

## The triseptile in transits {#in-transits}

A transiting triseptile is a short window: a few hours from the Moon, a couple of days from the Sun, and only the outer planets keep it in orb for months. If you notice anything, it may be things that had been developing apart starting to come together, a choice that suddenly suits several parts of your life at once. Weather, not a forecast: a triseptile transit describes things converging, not what they'll become.
