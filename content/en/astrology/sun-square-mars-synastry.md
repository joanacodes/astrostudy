Some pairs move at different speeds, and keep noticing. In Sun square Mars between two charts, one person's Mars sits about 90° from the other's Sun: the drive of one cuts across the direction of the other, and the two of you learn a great deal about pace, timing and who gets to decide.

## What flows {#what-flows}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is someone's direction, and the coherence that keeps a life pointed one way. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the engine: initiative, assertion, the urge to compete, the line someone defends. A [square](/astrology/square/) makes two functions rub, and the rubbing is what develops them; among [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/), few make two people as aware of each other's pace.

The signs usually share a modality, which shapes the contest. A Libra Mars against a Cancer Sun: one person's push for fairness and partnership, pressing on another's instinct to protect. A Sagittarius Mars against a Virgo Sun: an urge to go far and fast, meeting a purpose built on care and detail.

The friction gives stamina here, and better aim. Each person keeps the other from coasting: plans get tested by someone who wants to move now, and drive gets tested by someone who wants to know why. Sparring partners, rival colleagues who end up respecting each other, siblings who still race each other up the stairs: competition is not hostility, and here it can be how two people make each other better.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

If it's your Sun, the push can seem to come at the wrong moment: you're still deciding and they've started; you've chosen a road and they want a faster one. It can feel as though your direction is being questioned by someone's impatience.

On the Mars side, the other person can seem to hold you back. Your energy meets plans you didn't make and a pace that isn't yours, and the urge to just get on with it can come out sharper than you meant. Courage is not recklessness, as I read Mars, though a square can blur the line on a bad day.

Ease the angle, or widen it, and the push changes character:

- **[Sun conjunct Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mars-synastry/):** no angle at all; the drive lands on the self, and the question becomes whose initiative.
- **[Sun sextile Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-sextile-mars-synastry/):** a sign closer; effort that waits to be asked instead of arriving uninvited.
- **[Sun trine Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-trine-mars-synastry/):** 120° and a shared element; drive and direction pulling the same way.
- **[Sun opposite Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-opposite-mars-synastry/):** the far side; the contest faces you head-on instead of crossing your path.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Agree on the aim before arguing about the method; most clashes in this square are about how and when, and they shrink once the what is shared. Give contests rules and a place, a game, a race, a deadline, so the push has somewhere to go. Say when you need a pause, and let a cooling-off hour be normal rather than a sulk. At work, split the sprint from the planning; in a couple or a family, decide together who sets the pace for which job.

About 1 pair of people in 7 has this square between them, in one direction or the other, within 6.8°.

Mars moves slowly enough, and the Sun steadily enough, that the hour of birth rarely decides the square. The houses are another matter: they show which areas of each life the contest is about, and they need both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). No synastry starts without them. In one chart, [Sun square Mars](/astrology/sun-square-mars/) describes a single person aiming their own force; between two charts, two people learn to aim theirs together, which goes better when both have chosen to look ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
