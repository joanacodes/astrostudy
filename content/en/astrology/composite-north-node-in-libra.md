Some relationships are two soloists sharing a stage: separate plans, separate friends, decisions announced over breakfast rather than made together. Composite North Node in Libra asks a bond like that to learn the duet.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

There are two ways to build a chart for a relationship, and for the nodes they can disagree completely. [The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) takes the midpoint of each pair of natal positions; [the Davison chart](/astrology/davison-chart/) is cast for the real moment halfway between two births. Born in April 1960 and October 1975, with natal North Nodes near 24° Virgo and 22° Scorpio, two people have a composite North Node near 23° Libra, while their Davison chart, cast for January 1968, has its North Node near 23° Aries: the same axis, held the other way round. For births between about nine and about twenty-eight years apart, the two methods always split like this. I use neither chart in my readings, and neither is more accurate in itself; both are read beside the two birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). On this page it's the composite's Libra end that is being read.

In the composite, the [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) marks the direction the relationship grows in and the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/), opposite, its home ground. Here the home ground is [Aries](/astrology/aries/): independence, quick decisions, two people who can each manage perfectly well alone. The North Node in [Libra](/astrology/libra/), cardinal air ruled by [Venus](/astrology/venus/), points towards partnership itself: consulting before deciding, give-and-take, a fairness both people can feel, and the pleasure of making something lovely together.

That independence belongs to the bond. Each of you may be a generous collaborator at work and still, together, slip into parallel lives that meet at dinner. The Libra direction isn't about dissolving into each other; it's about the habit of the joint decision, the one neither of you would have reached alone. Composite Venus, ruler of the sign, shows where that harmony comes most naturally.

A single [North Node in Libra](/astrology/north-node-in-libra/) is one person learning partnership after years of going it alone. In a composite both are learning it at once, with each other, and it can feel clumsy at first: who gets consulted about what, how long a decision may take, when a compromise is fair and when it's just one person giving way.

Libra and Virgo share the lowest figure in the site's count, about 1 pair in 14 among two million random pairs born 1930–2009. Same-age pairs find it in their shared birth stretch: September 1939 to May 1941, June 1958 to December 1959, January 1977 to July 1978, or August 1995 to January 1997.

At the start of 1977, the two ways of computing the node didn't agree on when it reached Libra: the mean node arrived on 30 December 1976, the true node on 8 January 1977. Someone born in those nine days has a Libra node by one reckoning and a Scorpio node by the other, and a composite drawn with that chart near a sign's edge inherits the doubt, with [composite North Node in Virgo](/astrology/composite-north-node-in-virgo/) and [composite North Node in Scorpio](/astrology/composite-north-node-in-scorpio/) on either side. I look at both.

Libra's trap is keeping score, or its quieter twin, a peace kept by one person always yielding. The Aries end is the remedy as much as the starting point: two people who know what they each want make better partners than two who have stopped asking. None of this forecasts a marriage, its timing or its future; the node shows how a bond grows, never a verdict on it. And since a relationship chart reads two people, both say yes before anyone draws it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
