Two fire signs, and two kinds of fire: the Aries Sun is the spark that starts things, the Sagittarius Moon a blaze that needs room to travel. You were born a few days after a full moon, as it began to wane, and I read the pair as enthusiasm with a long view.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Fire to fire, the Moon four signs behind the Sun: a trine by sign, where the element is shared and little needs translating. Aries is cardinal, all beginnings; Sagittarius is mutable, ready to change course whenever a better road appears. The [Sun in Aries](/astrology/sun-in-aries/) wants a target to run at; the [Moon in Sagittarius](/astrology/moon-in-sagittarius/) needs open space and something to look forward to. Purpose and need rarely quarrel here. Mostly they egg each other on.

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) rules Aries and [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) rules Sagittarius: the planet of effort and the planet of meaning and growth. Where Jupiter sits shows where this Moon goes looking for a bigger picture, in study, travel, belief or long conversations, and where Mars sits shows where the Sun spends its push. When Mars and Jupiter aim at the same thing, effort and meaning line up, and that alignment is the pair's real strength. The Sun keeps its exaltation in Aries; Sagittarius gives the Moon no dignity of any kind.

The Moon was 210° to 269° ahead of the Sun, which is 91° to 150° behind it, seventeen to twenty-two days after a new moon: a Moon past full, shrinking, rising later each night. The [waning gibbous (disseminating) moon](/astrology/waning-gibbous-disseminating-moon/) claims seven of these births in eight, the tail of the [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/) phase the eighth, and a Moon about 120° behind the Sun turns the trine of signs into a waning [Sun trine Moon](/astrology/sun-trine-moon/). The disseminating phase is the one I associate with passing on what you've understood ([your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/)), and a Sagittarius Moon tends to do that without being asked.

About 1 person in 142 shares the pair. Two and a half days is about all the Moon spends in Sagittarius. If it arrived from Scorpio, or left for Capricorn, on your birthday, the time decides whether you belong here, with the [Sun in Aries, Moon in Scorpio](/astrology/sun-in-aries-moon-in-scorpio/) or with the [Sun in Aries, Moon in Capricorn](/astrology/sun-in-aries-moon-in-capricorn/) ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). From that time comes the rising sign as well, and with the two lights it gives a first sketch: a starting point, which suits a pair that loves setting out, and no more than that.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You bring momentum and good humour to love, and you need room inside it. The Aries Sun likes to propose; the Sagittarius Moon likes the plan to lead somewhere new, a trip, a project, a different way of living. Honesty comes easily, sometimes too easily: two fire signs can say the true thing before the kind one. A partner who shares your appetite for horizons, and who will also sit with you through an ordinary Tuesday, gives this pair what it wants and what it forgets it needs.

## At work {#at-work}

You're often the one who sees where a project could go and gets people moving towards it. The Sun supplies the push, the Moon the sense of purpose and the long view, so you tend to do well in work that grows: a venture, a course of study you build, a cause, anything with travel or learning in it. Routine maintenance and tight supervision chafe both lights at once. Build variety in on purpose, and keep one long goal that makes the dull weeks worth it.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

There's little ballast between the lights. The Aries Sun wants the new thing and the Sagittarius Moon wants the open road, so when life narrows, a hard conversation, a long wait, a commitment in its dull middle, both may look for the door at once. Promises can outrun what one week can hold. What helps is choosing one thing to see through each season and saying so out loud, and noticing that a few fixed points, people and places you return to, make the roaming better rather than smaller.
