Two fixed signs meet here, and neither is in a hurry to give way. The Sun in Taurus holds on to what lasts; the Moon in Leo holds on to the people it loves, and wants that love returned out loud. The birth came within a couple of days of the first quarter, when the evening Moon is half lit.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Three signs separate fixed earth from fixed fire, so the pair forms a square by sign, and because both signs share the same one of the [three modalities](/astrology/three-modalities/), both lights have staying power and an equal reluctance to bend. The [Sun in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-taurus/) measures worth by what endures; the [Moon in Leo](/astrology/moon-in-leo/) needs warmth, play and to be seen by the people who matter. They agree on loyalty. They disagree about display: the Sun's love is in what it keeps doing, year after year, the Moon's in saying so, generously and in front of others.

The rulers tie the pair together in an unusual way. Leo belongs to the [Sun](/astrology/sun/), so your Moon answers to your own Sun, which here stands in Taurus, ruled by [Venus](/astrology/venus/). Your need for warmth reports to a self that values what is solid and earned, and with this pair I read the Sun's sign and house twice: once as the Sun, once as the Moon's ruler. Neither light has a dignity in its sign.

This is a birth from the first half of the lunar month, five to ten days after the new moon, with the Moon 61° to 120° ahead of the Sun and the first quarter somewhere inside that span. Two of the eight phases share these births equally, the [waxing crescent](/astrology/waxing-crescent/) on the near side of the quarter and the [first quarter moon](/astrology/first-quarter-moon/) on the far side. Right at the quarter, the lights form a waxing [Sun square Moon](/astrology/sun-square-moon/), which between two fixed signs asks for patience on both sides. [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) takes the two phases in turn.

About 1 person in 140 is born with this pull between two fixed signs. Leo has the Moon for about two and a half days before handing it on to Virgo, just as Cancer handed it to Leo; a birth on either handover day needs the time to tell this page from the [Sun in Taurus, Moon in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-taurus-moon-in-cancer/) and the [Sun in Taurus, Moon in Virgo](/astrology/sun-in-taurus-moon-in-virgo/) ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). The rising sign needs that same time, and the three together are only the chart's opening lines; the houses, the other planets and their aspects say the rest.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You're steadfast and warm at once, which partners tend to treasure. The Taurus Sun stays and provides; the Leo Moon celebrates, praises and defends. What you need back is a little of both: reliability you can feel, and appreciation you can hear. Two fixed signs also mean a quarrel can turn into a standoff, each light waiting for the other to move first. A small gesture from you, made early, usually ends it, and costs your pride far less than a week of silence.

## At work {#at-work}

Your gift at work is warmth with staying power. You can lead a team through a long project and keep its spirits up, and people trust both your loyalty and your generosity. The Sun wants something solid to show at the end; the Moon wants the effort recognised by name along the way. Roles in which creativity is part of the job, and in which you're allowed to see things through, tend to suit; places that ignore good work, or change direction every quarter, frustrate both lights.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Two fixed signs at right angles can dig in. When the Leo Moon's need is overlooked, the hurt can harden into pride; when the Taurus Sun feels its security threatened, it plants its feet. A disagreement may then become a contest over who gives way first, and neither of your lights likes to. What helps is separating the issue from the pride: say what you need (*I need you on my side in this*), then decide what you're actually willing to change, and change it on your own terms.
