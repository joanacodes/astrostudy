One of you keeps describing the other as gentler than they are; the other keeps correcting the description. Neptune square Lilith in synastry puts one person's Neptune at right angles to the other's Black Moon Lilith, and the friction is about a picture that won't quite fit.

## What flows {#what-flows}

[Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) sees the best in people, sometimes before it's there: compassion, idealism, a talent for meaning and mood. [Black Moon Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/) is a point astrologers place at the Moon's farthest reach and read, each school in its own way, as the instinct that won't be domesticated and the boundary someone keeps no matter what; I treat it as a symbol. A [square](/astrology/square/) is constructive friction: the two functions keep catching on each other, and both develop for it. In [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) like this one, each person carries one side of the catch.

What the friction makes possible is focus. The Neptune person, rebuffed often enough, learns to look at the person in the room rather than the one they imagined. The Lilith person learns that being met gently isn't the same as being tamed. A pair that works through this square ends up with something rare: affection that has seen the edges and stayed.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

If the Lilith is yours, this person keeps seeing a softer version of you than the one standing there. When you refuse something, they explain it kindly, a long week, a bad mood, and you'd rather be taken at your word. Their tenderness can feel like being sanded down.

If the Neptune is yours, the other person's edge keeps puncturing the picture you hold of them, or of the two of you. You offer understanding and get a flat no; you forgive what they never asked to be forgiven for. It can feel as though your kindness itself is the problem. It isn't. A square is friction between two ways of seeing, never a failure of either person, and neither of you is wrong about what you feel.

Set at the other angles:

- **[Neptune conjunct Lilith in synastry](/astrology/neptune-conjunct-lilith-synastry/):** imagination fused with the hidden part, warmer and far less clear.
- **[Neptune sextile Lilith in synastry](/astrology/neptune-sextile-lilith-synastry/):** a playful offer of room instead of a correction.
- **[Neptune trine Lilith in synastry](/astrology/neptune-trine-lilith-synastry/):** the refusal is welcomed rather than explained away.
- **[Neptune opposite Lilith in synastry](/astrology/neptune-opposite-lilith-synastry/):** a seesaw between closeness and autonomy instead of a crossing.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Take each other literally for a while. If the Neptune is yours, treat a no as information, not a mood to be soothed, and ask before you interpret. If the Lilith is yours, when sympathy arrives that you didn't ask for, say what you'd like instead; the kindness is real even when its aim is off. At work this square can look like a manager who keeps excusing a colleague's objections instead of answering them; in a couple, like one partner forgiving what the other meant.

Who has it is a matter of dates. Someone born in mid-1975 has their Neptune, in Sagittarius, square the Lilith of people born from April to June 1975, only weeks or months older, whose Lilith was in Pisces. Their own Lilith, in Pisces, squares the Neptune of anyone born between November 1976 and January 1979. Because so many births share each side, I don't count this square among a synastry's contacts, which must involve the Sun, the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, the Ascendant or the Midheaven. In the houses it falls in, it shows two eras catching on each other more than two characters.

The hour makes no difference to whether two people have it, since both points crawl; the houses need both birth times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). The dates follow mean Lilith ([Which Lilith is mine?](/astrology/which-lilith-is-mine/)).

[Neptune square Lilith](/astrology/neptune-square-lilith/), in one chart, is a person caught between keeping the peace and keeping their line. Shared between two, the catch becomes a conversation, and I only open it when both people ask ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
