Sun conjunct North Node means you were born in the weeks of the year when the Sun passes the North Node, a stretch astronomers call an eclipse season. In a chart it lines up who you are with the direction you're growing in: a real help, and still a path you walk rather than one that walks you.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) organises your identity and purpose: who you are, and what you're for. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) is a direction: the less familiar capacity your life grows towards through practice, with the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) across from it as what you already know. In a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/), the two become one process, so your purpose and your direction of growth turn out to be the same project.

In plain words, the things that feel slightly new and exposing are also the things that feel most like you. Growth doesn't pull you away from yourself; it brings you closer. That can make development feel natural, even inevitable, and it's worth remembering that it isn't: the North Node grows through practice and feedback, and the first attempts may be clumsy. Because the South Node sits opposite your Sun, what came easily, the familiar habits and skills, sits across from you. It isn't wrong; it's the ground you push off from, and growth adds to it rather than replacing it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In a chart, it tends to show as a strong sense that your life is heading somewhere particular, and as some impatience with old roles that no longer fit. The sign the two share describes the kind of growth: with both in Libra, learning partnership after going it alone; with both in Capricorn, taking up responsibility and a place in the world. The house shows where in life the growth is asked for, and it needs a birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

About 1 person in 21 is born with the Sun within 8° of the North Node. The nodes move backwards, so the Sun meets the North Node a little under once a year, about every 346 days, some 19 days earlier in the calendar each time. Born then, you were born in an [eclipse season](/astrology/eclipse-seasons/): the weeks, twice a year, when a new or full moon falls near the nodes and eclipses happen. My count uses the true node. I look at both the true and the mean node ([mean node and true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/)), and since they're a degree or two apart at most, they only disagree when your Sun sits near the edge of the orb.

The Sun meets the nodes at other angles in the pair's other aspects:

- **[Sun sextile North Node](/astrology/sun-sextile-north-node/):** the direction is offered rather than fused, an opening you take when you're ready.
- **[Sun square North Node](/astrology/sun-square-north-node/):** the Sun sits across the path, between the two nodes, and asks to be included in the growth.
- **[Sun trine North Node](/astrology/sun-trine-north-node/):** growth flows with who you are, and the familiar helps too.
- **[Sun opposite North Node](/astrology/sun-opposite-north-node/):** the Sun on the South Node, resting on the familiar, with the new direction facing you.

With [Sun conjunct North Node in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-north-node-synastry/), the person whose North Node is touched often feels encouraged, sometimes pushed, towards what they find hardest, so I read it from both sides.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting North Node conjunct natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-north-node-conjunct-natal-sun/) comes about every 18.6 years. The node takes about 18 months to cross a sign, so it stays near your Sun for a month or two, and the eclipses of that stretch fall near your Sun too.

Born with the conjunction, you meet it at each [nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/), at about 18½, 37 and 56; halfway between, at the [nodal reversal](/astrology/nodal-reversal/), it's the South Node that crosses your Sun. Weather, not a forecast: checkpoints when the question of direction comes back, never events to wait for.
