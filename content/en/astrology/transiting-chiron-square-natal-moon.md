Chiron square your Moon is friction at the very point where you need comfort. About three times in a life, the moments you most need care are the ones that brush an old sensitivity, and the ways you learned to cope with that begin to chafe.

## What changes {#what-changes}

Your [Moon](/astrology/moon/) is how you meet your needs and steady your feelings: comfort, attachment, the sense of being safe enough. [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) is an old sensitivity about being looked after, and the compensations you built around it. A [square](/astrology/square/), 90°, sets your needs and those compensations at odds, and the friction pushes for a new arrangement. Under this [transit](/astrology/transits/), those compensations start to present their bill. Going quiet when you're hurt, managing alone, looking after everyone else first: each worked once, and each can start to leave you more tired than it should. A demanding month at work presses on a need for quiet; a friend's crisis lands just when you were hoping someone would notice yours.

Needing support isn't the same as not coping, and this square is where many people learn the difference. It doesn't ask the sensitivity to go away. It asks for an arrangement that takes it into account: a boundary around your rest, a person you can call, a steadying habit kept even when the week is full.

The houses say where the pressure comes from, which takes your birth time. With the Moon in the 7th, one of these squares comes while Chiron crosses your 10th ([Chiron transiting the 10th house](/astrology/chiron-transit-10th-house/)): what your work or public role asks of you rubs against what you need from the person closest to you. Born with [Moon square Chiron](/astrology/moon-square-chiron/), you know how easily your needs and the old sensitivity collide; the transit makes the collision louder for a while, and a chance to rearrange things comes with it.

If what comes up feels heavier than a hard season, please talk to a doctor or a therapist; no chart should stand in for them ([astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/), and the help lines in my [disclaimers](/disclaimers/#help)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

With my orbs, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, nearly two squares in three bring Chiron over the exact degree three times, spread over about ten and a half months; the quieter weeks between the crossings are part of the transit too. One square in three is quick, a single crossing of three weeks or so, and one in twenty slow, five crossings over some twenty-one months.

A birth time known only as "in the morning" leaves the Moon's place uncertain by a few degrees, and Chiron in Taurus needs most of a year to cover that much, so the square's dates stay vague until the time is found ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). Expect to meet this square about three times by 80, sometimes two or four: your Moon has a square point on each side, and Chiron crosses both in every round. The same sensitivity meets your Moon more gently under the sextile ([Transiting Chiron sextile natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-chiron-sextile-natal-moon/)) and the trine ([Transiting Chiron trine natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-chiron-trine-natal-moon/)), each as frequent as this square, and more directly under the conjunction ([Transiting Chiron conjunct natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-chiron-conjunct-natal-moon/)) and the opposition ([Transiting Chiron opposite natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-chiron-opposite-natal-moon/)), which come once or twice.

Right now, at the end of 2026, Moons at the end of Cancer and of Capricorn are under this square, with Chiron in the last degrees of Aries. Through Taurus, from 2027 to 2033, it moves on to Moons in Leo and Aquarius.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Name the need underneath the irritation: rest, company, quiet, reassurance. A named need is easier to meet.

Ask for help in the size you actually need, not the smallest size you can bear to ask for.

Keep one daily habit that steadies you, however full the season gets.
