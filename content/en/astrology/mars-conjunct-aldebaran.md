Mars conjunct Aldebaran sets the red planet on the red star that Ptolemy compared to Mars: like meeting like, in the tradition's terms. About 1 person in 181 born between 1930 and 2009 has Mars within a degree of it, at around 9° or 10° Gemini.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read [Mars](/astrology/mars/) as the part of you that moves: initiative, effort, the courage to act when it would be easier not to, and the readiness to defend a boundary. Ptolemy gave [Aldebaran](/astrology/aldebaran/) a temperature like that of Mars, and in his Greek it is Lampadias, the torch-bearer. Put the planet on the star and the tradition doubles the colour: Mars's own temperature, laid on Mars. Later astrology called the Torch a fortunate star as well, one of riches and honour, which sits oddly beside that Mars-like heat; I pass both labels on as the old books' words, not as anything a chart promises.

In the zodiac this is a [Mars in Gemini](/astrology/mars-in-gemini/), at around 9° or 10° of the sign for births from 1930 to 2009: drive that works through words, argument and several projects at once, as Gemini colours it. Gemini is Mercury's sign, so this Mars answers to the planet of words and exchange as well as to the star. That sign, with Mars's house and aspects, is what I read. The star I only describe, because the system behind my readings doesn't place stars.

Mars reaches Aldebaran about every two years and is usually past it within a few days. Twice in these eighty years it lingered. In September 1943 and again in September 1990 it crossed the star, turned retrograde further on in Gemini, came back over it in December 1943 and November 1990, and crossed it a third time the following February. Births in those months can have Mars retrograde on the Torch ([Mars retrograde in Gemini](/astrology/mars-retrograde-in-gemini/)).

In the sky the two never quite meet. Aldebaran lies five and a half degrees south of the ecliptic, and Mars, on all its passes from 1930 to 2009, went by between four and eight degrees north of it: two red lights sharing a degree of the zodiac, well apart overhead.

Mars was on my list like every placement, sign and house, a tick or a cross; the star wasn't, since no star ever went into my notebook, so I have no count of my own for how often this contact holds ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). When I meet it in a chart drawn by someone else, I start with Mars: where you put your effort, how you go after what you want, what you are ready to stand up for.

Each of the star's other contacts draws on a different thread of its lore: the [Sun conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/sun-conjunct-aldebaran/) its old tie to the spring equinox, the [Moon conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/moon-conjunct-aldebaran/) the Indian story of Rohini, [Venus conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/venus-conjunct-aldebaran/) the medieval list that pairs the star with Venus, and [Saturn conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-aldebaran/) the Saturn-like Hyades around it. [Conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/) explains the one-degree rule, and [fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/) where the lore comes from.
