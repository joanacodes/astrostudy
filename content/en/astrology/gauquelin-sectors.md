Most of astrology measures the sky by the zodiac. Michel Gauquelin measured it by the clock of each planet's own day: when it rises, when it reaches its highest point, when it sets. His sectors are that clock cut into twelve, and they are where all his statistics were counted.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

As the Earth turns, every planet crosses the sky once a day, like the Sun. It rises in the east, climbs to its culmination on the meridian, sets in the west and passes its lowest point under the earth before rising again. Gauquelin divided that daily round into twelve sectors, six above the horizon and six below, and numbered them from the rising, in the direction the planet moves: sectors 1 to 3 carry it from the eastern horizon up to the meridian, sectors 4 to 6 from the meridian down to the west.

Two of them carried his findings. Sector 1, just after a planet has risen, and sector 4, just after it has culminated, were his key sectors. In *L'influence des astres*, in 1955, he reported that sports champions were born more often than chance would predict with Mars in them ([The Mars effect (Gauquelin)](/astrology/mars-effect-gauquelin/)). He went on to make the same kind of claim for five planets, each linked with eminence in a profession that astrology traditionally gives it ([Michel Gauquelin](/astrology/michel-gauquelin/)).

The sectors were what survived his own sifting. His calculations led him to set aside most of the conventions of natal astrology as practised in the West, the signs and the aspects among them. What he kept was where a planet stood in its daily round at the moment of birth.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A sector belongs to a planet, not to the chart. Each planet rises and culminates at its own time of day, so in the same birth chart the Moon can be in sector 1 while Venus is in sector 9. The houses work differently: they divide the zodiac once, from the angles, and every planet falls into that one division.

Near the angles, the two measures agree. A planet that has just risen stands above the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), in the [12th house](/astrology/12th-house/) in Placidus; one that has just culminated has passed the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) into the [9th house](/astrology/9th-house/). So Gauquelin's key sectors lie just past the angles rather than on them, in houses the tradition ranked as cadent, which is why they sit at the edge of what astrologers call [angular planets](/astrology/angular-planets/).

Twelve sectors in a day make two hours each on average, so a birth time rounded to the hour can move a planet into the next sector. Like the houses, the sectors exist only for a known time and place ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Then there is the arithmetic of searching. With ten bodies and twelve sectors, a planet and a pair of sectors can be chosen in 1,320 ways. Alexander Panchin's illustration of the problem puts the chance that a random set of births, as large as Gauquelin's, throws up at least one combination as striking as his at about one in four. That alone settles nothing, but it is why a pattern found by searching has to be found again, in new data, by someone else.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I don't draw Gauquelin sectors. I read the houses, in [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/), and a planet just past the Ascendant or the Midheaven gets the attention any planet near an angle gets: its function first, then its sign, its aspects and the house it has just entered. Mars a few degrees above your Ascendant is, to me, Mars in the 12th close to the horizon, not a statistical category.

My own notebook counted differently again. It went through each placement by sign and house, with a tick or a cross, and never by sector, so it has nothing to say about Gauquelin's key sectors ([my testing notes](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). It's a notebook, not a study, and it proves nothing about astrology in general; the most it can do is tell me which placements to lean on less, and telling you when a reading rests on one of them is part of [how I read](/how-i-read/).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Gauquelin proved astrology.”** He reported a link between a few planets' daily positions and eminence, and found nothing for the signs or the aspects. The independent tests that followed didn't find his Mars effect.
- **“Sector 1 is the 1st house.”** Sector 1 lies above the horizon, just after the planet rises; the 1st house lies below the Ascendant.
- **“The key sectors are the angles.”** They lie just past them: in Placidus, in the 12th and the 9th houses.
- **“All the planets in a chart share one set of sectors.”** Each planet's sectors run from its own rising, so each is placed on its own.
