Some relationships commit with a speech; this one commits by doing. Composite Saturn in Aries gives the bond's structure, the part that holds it together over the years, the manner of the first sign: direct, self-starting, and in no hurry to ask permission.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) in [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) is the relationship's frame: what it takes seriously, the responsibilities it carries, the rules it lives by, and the places where it feels slow or tested. [Aries](/astrology/aries/), cardinal fire ruled by Mars, gives that frame a habit of acting first. A pair like this tends to show commitment in deeds rather than declarations: one of you books the removal van while the other is still weighing it up, and the bond counts the van as the promise. The rules it keeps are often rules about independence, the right of each person to act alone, to run their own projects and to have their own say.

In the tradition, Aries is where Saturn has its [fall](/astrology/fall/), across the zodiac from its exaltation in Libra, so a planet that hesitates finds itself in a sign that moves first. In a relationship I read that as a tension to manage, not a flaw: the pair has two speeds, careful and impatient, and has to learn which one a given decision needs. Starting things together is where the lesson sits. Some pairs freeze at the starting line, each waiting for the other to move; others start, stop and start again, as though a beginning could be made perfect by repeating it.

A composite belongs to the relationship rather than to either person, so this Saturn can describe two people who are, on their own, anything but hasty. The midpoint does it. Take a pair born five years apart, in June 1965 and June 1970: their natal Saturns stand near 17° Pisces and 17° Taurus, and the point halfway between is 17° Aries, which appears in neither birth chart. Two people born between March 1967 and April 1969 both have Saturn in Aries, and so does their composite, as it does for nearly every pair born together between April 1937 and March 1940 or between April 1996 and February 1999. Being Saturn's fall makes Aries no rarer: it is level with Pisces as the commonest composite Saturn sign, at about 1 pair in 10 in a count of two million pairs born 1930–2009. For a pair of one generation, the sign mostly describes the years you were born in; what makes the composite Saturn yours is its house, which needs both birth times, and its contacts with the composite's other planets.

A person born with [Saturn in Aries](/astrology/saturn-in-aries/) learns to back their own initiative; a relationship with it in the composite has to learn that as a pair, and each of you may feel the lesson as the other's impatience, or the other's hesitation. Next door, [composite Saturn in Pisces](/astrology/composite-saturn-in-pisces/) builds its structure out of care and shared imagination, and [composite Saturn in Taurus](/astrology/composite-saturn-in-taurus/) out of upkeep and repetition; in Aries, the structure is made of first steps.

The difficulty is usually autonomy turning into separate lives. A relationship that guards each person's right to act alone can end with two people acting alone, side by side, and calling it respect. It helps to make one beginning a shared one on purpose: a project with a date for its first step and a name beside each task. Leadership is the other sore point. Two people who each expect to go first, or who each wait for the other, need a plain rule about who starts what, and this Saturn keeps such rules well once they exist.

Composites aren't part of my readings, so what you're reading is the technique's account of an Aries Saturn, which the astrologers who use it set beside both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). None of it says how long the two of you will last, or whether you should: Saturn describes how a relationship builds, never whether it's allowed to. However eager the sign, every composite is made from two charts, and it waits for two yeses, not one ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
