With the Sun and the Moon both in Aries, what you set out to do and what you need in order to feel all right speak the same quick language. You were born close to a new moon, the two lights side by side in the first sign, and I read the pair as one current running at two depths.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Same sign, same element, same modality: cardinal fire on both sides. There's nothing to translate between purpose and need, because action answers both. The [Sun in Aries](/astrology/sun-in-aries/) finds out who it is by beginning; the [Moon in Aries](/astrology/moon-in-aries/) settles a feeling by doing something with it. Put the two together and the move that gives your life direction is very often the move that calms you down.

Tradition treats the two lights differently here. Aries is the Sun's exaltation, where it is honoured and at ease; for the Moon it is neutral ground, no dignity and no debility. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) rules the sign, so a single planet answers for both lights, and I read its sign and house early. When your Mars has real work to do, your sense of self and your moods tend to steady together; when it has none, they get restless together.

The Moon was somewhere between 29° behind the Sun and 29° ahead of it, which puts the birth within about two and a half days of a new moon. In the eight phases many astrologers use, half of these births fall in the [balsamic moon](/astrology/balsamic-moon/), the closing days of a lunar month, and half in the [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/) phase that opens the next; [your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) explains what each side means. Very close together, the two lights make a [Sun conjunct Moon](/astrology/sun-conjunct-moon/).

About 1 person in 147 is born with this pair. Since the Moon moves on every two and a half days or so, the date usually settles it; on the day it entered or left Aries, only the time can say whether you're a double Aries or have the [Sun in Aries, Moon in Pisces](/astrology/sun-in-aries-moon-in-pisces/) or the [Sun in Aries, Moon in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-aries-moon-in-taurus/) ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). The time also gives the rising sign, which joins the two lights in the first sketch of a chart. Two lights in one sign can look like a whole character, but in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) the Sun sign held about a third of the time and the Moon was not one of the three placements that held far more often, so I read a double Aries as an opening, never as the whole story.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

A partner gets one answer from you, not two. What you want and what you feel tend to arrive together, out loud and in the same minute, so the people close to you rarely have to guess. You propose the plan and you make up after the row, and the bond keeps getting new beginnings. The difficulty is tempo: someone who needs a day to know their own mind can feel rushed by a person whose head and heart have both already decided. Asking *what do you need from me now?*, and waiting for the answer, does more for this pair than any grand gesture.

## At work {#at-work}

Your purpose and your mood lift at the same moments: a launch, a deadline, a problem nobody has touched. That makes you strong in the first week of anything and in emergencies, while others are still working out how they feel. The middle stretch is where a double Aries gets tested, since neither light draws much from maintenance. What keeps you there is a visible next step every week, and a colleague who enjoys finishing as much as you enjoy starting.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Nothing in the pair puts the brakes on. Between impulse, feeling and act, two Aries lights leave very little room, and your energy can run out without warning, since a surge of purpose and a flash of feeling both burn quickly. When Mars has no outlet, self and mood stall at the same moment. What helps usually comes from outside the pair: a slower planet or rising sign elsewhere in your chart, a body that gets moved every day, and a second thought, taken on purpose, before the first one is acted on.
