With a composite Sun in Sagittarius, a relationship's centre points outward, at somewhere neither of you has been yet. The bond knows itself by what it's heading towards: a journey, a question, a way of seeing the world the two of you are still working out.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) of [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) sits halfway between your two natal Suns, the short way round, and that path can cross the turn of the year. Take two birthdays, 15 October and 25 January: one Sun in Libra, one in Aquarius, and a composite Sun at about 14° [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/), two air signs whose midpoint lands in fire. About 1 pair in 12 has a composite Sun in this sign, and for many of them it's a sign neither person was born under, which is part of what makes it the relationship's own.

Mutable fire gives the bond a centre made of perspective. It's revived by plans with a horizon in them, the trip booked for next spring, the course one of you is taking and the other keeps asking about, and it tends to feel most like itself mid-conversation about something larger than the two of you: what's fair, what's true, how to live. Its purpose is often to understand something together, and to keep widening each other's view. It also tends to say what it believes, warmly and out loud. These are the themes of the 9th house, Sagittarius's natural house, where tradition says the Sun rejoices ([composite Sun in the 9th house](/astrology/composite-sun-in-9th-house/)).

[Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) rules Sagittarius, so composite Jupiter is the next thing to read: its sign and house show where this bond finds meaning and room to grow. Jupiter takes about twelve years to go round the zodiac, so two people born twelve years apart often share its sign, and their composite Jupiter sits there with them. In one person's chart, the [Sun in Sagittarius](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius/) describes someone working out what they believe. In a composite, the beliefs being worked out belong to the bond, and they're often ones neither of you held before you met: a way of travelling, of raising a question, of being generous, that only exists between you.

The horizon can come to matter more than the ground. A relationship that knows itself by what's ahead can live in the next plan and miss the present one, and the everyday can feel like waiting. Exploration isn't restlessness, though, and the bond doesn't need a plane ticket to stay itself: a walk somewhere new in your own town, a book read in turns, can do the same work. If the [composite Sun in Scorpio](/astrology/composite-sun-in-scorpio/) asks a bond to go deeper and the [composite Sun in Capricorn](/astrology/composite-sun-in-capricorn/) to build higher, this one asks it to go further.

A sign that likes the wider view deserves this page's: composites are a technique I describe but don't use in my readings, read by those who do with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and they never forecast how long a relationship will last or whether it will succeed. Any journey into two people's charts also starts with both of them agreeing to go ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
