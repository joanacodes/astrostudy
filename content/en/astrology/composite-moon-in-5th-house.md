Some relationships stay alive through play: the game you always lose, the dancing in the kitchen, the trip booked on a whim, the thing you make together just for the pleasure of it. A composite Moon in the 5th house puts the bond's sense of safety in joy.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In the [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), the [Moon](/astrology/moon/), halfway between your two natal Moons, says what the relationship needs in order to feel secure; [the 5th house](/astrology/5th-house/) says where it goes looking: pleasure, play, romance, creativity, everything done for its own sake. The house is succedent: Leo is its natural sign, Good Fortune its old name, and Venus has her joy there. With the Moon here, the relationship refuels by enjoying itself. Fun isn't a reward for getting everything else done; it is how the bond stays well.

That playfulness belongs to the pair. It isn't one person refilling, as with a [Moon in the 5th house](/astrology/moon-in-5th-house/), nor someone bringing out your sense of play, as [their Moon in your 5th house](/astrology/moon-in-5th-house-synastry/) would; it is what the two of you enjoy together, and it can surprise you both: two serious, hard-working people may turn out, together, to be silly, inventive and fond of treats. Creative work counts as much as fun: a band, a garden, a small business built for love more than money, a shared hobby that grows into a little world of its own.

The difficulty comes when the fun runs out. A bond that feels safe in pleasure can struggle through dull or heavy stretches, wondering what has gone wrong when it is only ordinary life. It can also slip into performing happiness for others, posting the good times while the real mood goes unspoken. Play isn't immaturity and joy isn't hedonism, but this Moon needs some practice at sitting through a bad week without treating it as a verdict on the relationship.

Children belonged to the 5th as well, in the old books. No chart can say whether two people will have them, and that isn't a question I read for ([can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/)); a composite Moon here describes the playful, creative life of the relationship, whoever is in it. Romance belongs to this house as well, and a brand-new romance is where any comparison of two charts is best left for a while: the bond isn't lived yet, and both people need to agree to it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#we-just-met)).

Like every composite house, this one needs both birth times and a method: the midpoints of the two charts' cusps, or houses cast from the composite Midheaven for your present home, and the two can disagree near a cusp ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)). The [composite Moon in the 4th house](/astrology/composite-moon-in-4th-house/) finds its comfort at home, the [composite Moon in the 6th house](/astrology/composite-moon-in-6th-house/) in routine; the 5th finds it in delight. Composites are a technique I explain rather than one I draw; whoever reads this Moon should have the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) and both birth charts in front of them.
