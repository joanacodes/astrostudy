Mars sextile Pluto is staying power that waits to be asked. The part of you that acts and the part that transforms are on good terms, two signs apart, and when something matters, you can bring a depth of focus that surprises people.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the function that starts and pushes: initiative, assertion, effort, competition. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) transforms and regenerates: it eliminates what no longer serves, renews what's left, and supports authentic empowerment. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) relates them through opportunity: neither takes over, and the angle opens a channel between drive and depth.

In plain words, you can go deep when you choose to. Faced with a stalled project or a hard situation, you're able to cut back to what matters, find the reserves to keep going, and come out with something renewed. You don't need drama to do it. The sextile asks for engagement, though: without a real task the capacity can sit unused for years, and many people only discover it in a crisis.

Empowerment is not domination, and this aspect tends to know it: its strength works on the problem rather than on the people around it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Water and earth make this a sextile of persistence and repair; fire and air, one of bold renewal and strategy. Mars in Capricorn, its exaltation, sextile Pluto in Scorpio, Pluto's modern sign, gives the renewal a long, patient plan. The houses show the ground: Mars in the 4th sextile Pluto in the 6th, say, might put regenerating effort into home and daily routines.

Counted within 6°, the sextile belongs to about 1 person in 15. When it's tight, the capacity for focused renewal is part of your character; near 6°, it's a reserve you find when you need it.

Mars forms this sextile twice in each two-year cycle with Pluto, for a few weeks of births each time; Pluto's sign, by contrast, is shared by everyone born over twelve to thirty-one years. Pluto was found in 1930 and reclassed as a dwarf planet in 2006; astrologers went on reading it, and so do I. Whatever I see in this sextile, what you do with it is yours to decide, which is the principle behind [How I read](/how-i-read/).

Next to the pair's other aspects:

- **[Mars conjunct Pluto](/astrology/mars-conjunct-pluto/):** drive and depth fused in one push, where the sextile keeps them as partners.
- **[Mars square Pluto](/astrology/mars-square-pluto/):** the same intensity at a right angle, learned through friction rather than taken up by choice.
- **[Mars trine Pluto](/astrology/mars-trine-pluto/):** a fuller, more constant flow of staying power, in one element.
- **[Mars opposite Pluto](/astrology/mars-opposite-pluto/):** intensity met across the chart, often in someone else first.

Between two charts, [Mars sextile Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-sextile-pluto-synastry/) can make a strong working partnership: one person's drive and the other's depth help each other. I read it for both people.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Pluto sextile natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-pluto-sextile-natal-mars/) comes at most twice in a life, and its length varies with your generation, because Pluto's pace ranges from 12 to 31 years a sign: often a year or more near exact. It tends to be a workable season for deep, steady change in how you use your energy.

Mars in the sky sextiles your natal Pluto twice every two years, for a few days: short windows for tackling something you've been avoiding.

Openings for renewal, with nothing more fixed than that: the weather, not a forecast, that these transits describe.
