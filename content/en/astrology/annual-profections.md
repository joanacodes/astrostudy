Of the timing methods I use, profections are the one you can work out in your head. You need your rising sign and your age, and from those two comes a theme for the whole year and a planet to watch while it lasts.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

An annual profection moves the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) forward one sign for every year of life. The year from birth to the first birthday belongs to the rising sign itself, the 1st house; the next year to the sign after it, the 2nd; and so on round the twelve, so the count comes home to the rising sign every twelve years, at 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72 and 84.

The sign a year lands on is its theme. The planet that rules that sign becomes the lord of the year: the planet whose condition in your chart, and whose movements in the sky, weigh more until your next birthday. Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces each have a traditional ruler and a modern one, and I read both ([Traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)).

The method is old. Ptolemy names annual profections among the ways of timing a life at the very end of the *Tetrabiblos* (book IV, chapter 10), beside directions, ingresses, lunations and transits. It belongs to a family of time-lord techniques, where one planet takes charge of a stretch of years. [Firdaria](/astrology/firdaria/), documented in the ninth century by Abu Ma'shar, gives the seven traditional planets and the two nodes fixed periods adding up to 75 years; [Zodiacal releasing](/astrology/zodiacal-releasing/) counts its chapters from a lot.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Divide your age in completed years by twelve and keep the remainder. Nothing left is a 1st-house year, 1 left a 2nd-house year, 11 left a 12th-house year.

Say your rising sign is Gemini and you've just turned 29. Twenty-nine divided by twelve leaves 5, so the year falls on the sixth sign counted from Gemini: Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio. Until your 30th birthday, Scorpio and the house it occupies set the theme, and its two rulers, Mars and Pluto, share the lordship.

Then three questions. Where does the lord sit in your birth chart, by sign, house and aspect? What do the slow planets do to it while it's in charge? Which natal planets already live in the year's sign? A year whose lord is also crossed by a slow transit, or met by a progression, is one where the theme gathers weight.

The count goes sign by sign. In [Whole Sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/), each sign is a house, so the two are the same thing. In [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/), the house system I use, a sign can hold the end of one house and the start of the next, so a year may touch both. Every count starts from the rising sign, which changes about every two hours, and that's why profections need the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

A profection points: this part of the chart, this planet, this year. It says nothing of what will happen there. I read it as emphasis, never as an outcome, and always beside the natal chart.

The houses give the themes. A 1st-house year, at 24, 36, 48 and each twelve years after, brings your own direction and your body's rhythms to the front, with your [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/) as lord. A year on the [7th house](/astrology/7th-house/), at 30, 42, 54 or 66, turns attention to the people across the table: partners, close collaborators, rivals. A 10th-house year, at 33, 45 or 57, does the same for visible work and the responsibilities that come with it.

Some houses carried heavy words in tradition: illness for the 6th, death for the 8th, confinement for the 12th. A year that lands there predicts none of them. A 6th-house year is about routines, work and the care your days need; an 8th-house year about what you share and owe; a 12th-house year about rest and the parts of life no one else sees ([Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)).

## Its limits {#its-limits}

Everyone your age who shares your rising sign is in the same profected sign this year. What makes it yours is the rest of the chart: where the lord sits, what it aspects, and what else is moving.

The technique comes from traditional astrology, and those who use it today disagree on details: whole signs or quadrant houses, traditional rulers alone or modern ones as well. Those choices can change the houses a year touches, or add a second lord, so it's worth knowing which ones a reading has made.

On its own, a profection is a thin thread. I give it weight only when the year's other timing points the same way: a slow [transit](/astrology/transits/) to the lord, a [solar return](/astrology/solar-return/) that stresses the same house, a progression reaching the same planet. Then the profection tells you which of several stirrings is the year's main business.
