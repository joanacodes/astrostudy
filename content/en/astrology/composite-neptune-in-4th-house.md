Some couples could live in a tent and still call it home, as long as they were in it together. A composite Neptune in the 4th house puts the relationship's dream at its foundation: home, roots and the life it keeps for itself.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) draws the relationship as a chart of its own, each planet at the midpoint of its two natal positions, and reads it as a third thing that neither partner owns. Composite [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) is where that third thing idealises. [The 4th house](/astrology/4th-house/), which opens at the composite [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), is its base: the home, the family each partner brings or chooses, the roots the bond grows from, the private life nobody else sees. With Neptune there, home becomes a sanctuary. The two of you may fill it with music, books, candles or rescued animals; leave the door open to anyone who needs a bed; or carry the feeling of home with you, so that a rented room abroad feels as much like it as the house you own.

Roots get the same soft focus. A pair with this Neptune can idealise each other's families, or build a chosen one out of friends; it can feel tied to places or ancestors it has only imagined, or treat the story of how the two of you met as a kind of origin myth. Cancer is the 4th's natural sign, and since [composite Neptune in Cancer](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-cancer/) is out of reach for living couples, this house is where the longing for a harbour mostly shows in practice.

The difficulty is the gap between the dream house and the real one. A bond that idealises home can let the practical side drift: repairs left for another season, bills nobody opens, house rules that were never quite spoken. An open door can also leave no room for the two of you. A home is no less a sanctuary for having a rota on the fridge; for this pair, the plain arrangements are what keep the refuge standing.

In a single chart, [Neptune in the 4th house](/astrology/neptune-in-4th-house/) describes one person's longing for a refuge; in synastry, [their Neptune in your 4th house](/astrology/neptune-in-4th-house-synastry/) is one partner's longing settling where the other lives. The composite 4th is the home the relationship itself imagines, and it can ask for things neither of you would ask for alone.

The house starts at the composite IC, and both of its edges hang on the birth times: each chart's angles turn about a degree for every four minutes, on average. The method adds a twist that suits this house. By the second way of drawing composite houses, from the composite Midheaven, the cusps are cast for the latitude of the place where the two of you live, so the home this house describes also has a say in where its far edge falls ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Both exact times, and an agreed method, come before any reading of it.

The houses around it dream differently: [composite Neptune in the 3rd house](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-3rd-house/) in conversation, [composite Neptune in the 5th house](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-5th-house/) in play and romance. The composite isn't part of my own readings; those who work with it read the house alongside the two natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and it never says whether a couple will move, buy, sell or stay.
