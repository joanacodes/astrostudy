The far horizon, with Taurus on the 9th house cusp, is a place you go back to. Belief, study and long journeys grow here like a garden rather than a voyage: tended, returned to, and richer every year.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The sign on the [9th house](/astrology/9th-house/) cusp is the manner of your search for meaning ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)), and [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/) searches by cultivating. Convictions build up over years, a book, a practice, a conversation at a time, and once they've taken root they carry real weight. You tend to keep up what you study: the language still spoken thirty years on, the course finished even when it took twice as long as planned. Travel often means return: the same valley, the same city, a second home somewhere far away that grows familiar. As a teacher you cultivate rather than dazzle, building a student's skill over seasons. If you write, the book may take ten years and be the better for it: the ideas you publish are ones you've lived with. Faith, for some people with this cusp, is a practice kept up more than a doctrine argued over: the walk, the prayer or the meditation that happens every morning, whatever the mood. What helps is an occasional teacher from outside your tradition, to test what you've built. It's the opposite of the roaming that Sagittarius, the 9th's natural sign, is known for: this cusp goes back to the source.

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) rules your 9th. Libra, its other sign, opens your 2nd in most of these charts, and sometimes your 1st as well, so what you believe and what you value answer to the same planet. You'll spend gladly on the books, journeys and teachers that matter to you, and your beliefs often show in what you choose to own or to do without. Virgo usually rises with this cusp and Gemini holds the Midheaven, so Mercury speaks for how you meet the world and what you do in it, while Venus speaks for what you believe. Your convictions are lived out in the house where Venus stands: the [1st](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-1st-house/), [2nd](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-2nd-house/), [3rd](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/), [4th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-4th-house/), [5th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-5th-house/), [6th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-6th-house/), [7th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-7th-house/), [8th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-8th-house/), [9th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-9th-house/), [10th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-10th-house/), [11th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-11th-house/) or [12th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-12th-house/).

About one birth in eleven has Taurus here, at London and at New York alike, using Placidus, my house system. It holds only this cusp in these charts: the 8th always opens in Aries and the 10th usually in Gemini, which is why [Taurus on the 8th house cusp](/astrology/taurus-on-8th-house-cusp/) and [Taurus on the 10th house cusp](/astrology/taurus-on-10th-house-cusp/) never apply alongside this page. Opposite, Scorpio opens the 3rd: you may dig hard into the questions of an ordinary day and still keep a quiet, settled faith about the large ones. Taurus can also have no cusp of its own, in about 1 London birth in 7, and in about 1 London chart in 70 it lies wholly inside the 9th itself ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)).

A couple of hours either way would put Taurus on another cusp, so I want the birth time before I say anything about this one; that's all it takes for the 9th to open in Aries or Gemini instead ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
