Aquarius ends with two gentler planets in charge, the Moon and Venus, and with Osiris and Proteus among its old names. The water-bearer's own shoulder star shines in these degrees.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

The Moon takes the last face of [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/), following Mercury ([Aquarius, second decan](/astrology/aquarius-second-decan/)) in the [Chaldean order](/astrology/chaldean-order/) ([faces](/astrology/faces/)); the [Moon in Aquarius](/astrology/moon-in-aquarius/) holds a face of its own in these last ten degrees. Libra, the one air sign the modern scheme hasn't yet used in Aquarius, supplies the decan's other ruler, Venus.

Unlike some of the pairs the two systems make, these two get on in the old tables: the Moon is exalted in Taurus, a sign Venus rules. Here they soften the end of a sign that prefers to think things through. The Moon brings what a person needs, Venus what they value, and Aquarius asks how both can work for a whole group. Behind the pair stand Saturn and Uranus, the rulers of Aquarius itself, and I read both.

## Its image {#its-image}

Two later lists give these degrees names with pictures behind them. Cosmas of Maiuma names the decan after Osiris, the Egyptian god of vegetation and of life renewed, shown with crook and flail; Athanasius Kircher, in 1652, after Proteus, the shape-shifting sea god Homer called an Old Man of the Sea. For the last degrees of the water-bearer, a god of renewal and a god of the changing sea make fitting neighbours, whatever the lists intended. Wallis Budge's Egyptian name is plainer: Khukhu. [Firmicus Maternus](/astrology/firmicus-maternus/) gave Tepis or Amasiero, which Joseph Scaliger wrote Tepisatras, and the Hermetic lists have Chonoumous in Greek and Crauxes in Latin.

In the sky, the water-bearer's left shoulder, [Sadalsuud](/astrology/sadalsuud/), whose Arabic name means the luck of lucks, stands at 23°45′ Aquarius, beside the sea-goat's tail, [Deneb Algedi](/astrology/deneb-algedi/), at 23°54′.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun is here from about 9 to 18 February, the last stretch of the [Sun in Aquarius](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius/) season, and a birthday on 18 or 19 February needs the time of birth to settle whether the Sun had reached Pisces ([Pisces, first decan](/astrology/pisces-first-decan/)). Under the Moon's face and Venus's decan, I read the shade as Aquarius's ideals with a warmer hand: the cause, and the people in it. A Moon here holds its own face, and I read it as feelings that steady themselves through friendship; a Venus here rules its own decan, and gives affection as loyalty to a shared idea.

With these degrees on the horizon, the Ascendant moves on within twenty minutes or so: about 20 in London, 22 in Paris ([Aquarius rising](/astrology/aquarius-rising/)). South of the equator the same degrees rise slowly, because the quick-rising signs of the north are the slow ones of the south.

A decan gets little of my time in a reading: the time goes where my tests say it matters most, and the details come after ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

Births from 2000 to 2003 carry [Uranus in Aquarius](/astrology/uranus-in-aquarius/) in this decan, at home there in the modern scheme; Neptune followed between 2007 and 2012.
