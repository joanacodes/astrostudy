Once in each round of the nodes, their axis lies along your horizon with the North Node in the west, on your Descendant, while the South Node stands at the eastern end, on your rising degree. The way you have always come through the door is in plain view, and the less familiar direction stands across the room, among the people you deal with face to face.

## What changes {#what-changes}

Your [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) is your own point of entry, the orientation you start anything from; the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), exactly opposite, is where the other person comes into the picture, as partner, client, friend or rival. The [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) consolidates: it describes what you can do without trying. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) develops: a capacity built by practice. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) keeps two ends in view at once, and under this [transit](/astrology/transits/) your practised way of entering a situation sits at the familiar end, while the newer skill, letting someone else's view shape the opening, sits at the other.

It tends to show in small exchanges. You notice yourself deciding before anyone else has spoken, or arriving with a finished plan where a question would have done. That self-reliance is real and stays yours; the month asks whether it has become the only way you begin.

Nothing in it forecasts a meeting, a partnership or a separation. Some astrologers call contacts with the nodes fated, a modern school's word rather than mine: the Descendant is a way of relating, not a person on the way. If the question is about someone in particular, that's a synastry, which I read only with both people's agreement ([Synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

In Placidus, the house system I use, this crossing ends the North Node's stay in your 7th ([North Node transiting the 7th house](/astrology/north-node-transit-7th-house/)) and begins its stay in your 6th, while the South Node moves from your 1st into your 12th. A planet you were born with near either end of the horizon is crossed in the same weeks. And if your North Node itself was born close to your Ascendant, your natal South Node lies on your Descendant, where the North Node arrives at each [nodal reversal](/astrology/nodal-reversal/): this transit comes with every one of them.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Counted from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the crossing takes about a month in the usual case, and nineteen passes in twenty touch the exact point once. One pass in ten is over within 25 days. Another in ten runs past 100 days because an eclipse season has arrived in the middle of it: while the Sun is near the nodes, the true node hardly moves. Three exact hits come in about one pass in twenty-five.

Those long passes can put an eclipse on your horizon. Take an Ascendant at 20° Taurus: the North Node is within orb of its Descendant, 20° Scorpio, from 4 September to 10 December 2031, exact on 27 September, and the total solar eclipse of 14 November 2031 falls at 22°17′ Scorpio, a little over two degrees from that Descendant. Within about three degrees, I count it ([eclipses on your natal planets](/astrology/eclipses-on-your-natal-planets/)). In other rounds the crossing comes with no eclipse anywhere near.

The opposition comes once a round, about nine years after the North Node crossed your Ascendant ([Transiting North Node conjunct natal Ascendant](/astrology/transiting-north-node-conjunct-natal-ascendant/)), so everyone meets it four or five times by 80. On the way from one to the other, the node passes its sextile, square and trine to your rising degree ([Transiting North Node sextile natal Ascendant](/astrology/transiting-north-node-sextile-natal-ascendant/), [Transiting North Node square natal Ascendant](/astrology/transiting-north-node-square-natal-ascendant/), [Transiting North Node trine natal Ascendant](/astrology/transiting-north-node-trine-natal-ascendant/)).

This one is in the forecast I prepare readings with, since an end of the axis is on a point of your chart. Both ends of your horizon come from your birth time; a few minutes of it move the window by weeks, and without the time there's no reading ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What helps {#what-helps}

Before your usual first move, ask the other person how they see it, and let the answer change how you begin.

Keep your independence in reserve rather than in charge: decide alone where it matters, together where it can.

Look at the manner you lead with as if from across the room. With the South Node on your Ascendant, this is the month it is easiest to see.

If the question is about one person in particular, put it to them.
