A house tells you which part of life is in question; its ruler tells you where in the chart that part of life goes to work. Following the rulers is how a chart stops being a list of placements and starts to connect.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Every house begins with a sign on its cusp ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)), and every sign has a ruler. The planet that rules the sign on the cusp rules the house. It doesn't have to be in that house, and usually isn't: what matters is where it actually sits, because that's where the house's business gets done.

Since most planets rule two signs, one planet often rules two houses, and the two parts of life it rules then share a thread. The ruler of the 1st house has a name of its own, the [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/). And with Scorpio, Aquarius or Pisces on a cusp, a house has two rulers, a modern one and a traditional one, and I read both ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

It takes three steps:

1. **Find the sign on the cusp.** Say Virgo is on the cusp of your 7th house.
2. **Find its ruler.** Virgo's is Mercury, so Mercury rules your 7th.
3. **Find where the ruler sits.** If Mercury is in your 10th house, the business of the 7th, partnership and the people you meet one to one, runs through your work and public life ([Ruler of the 7th in the 10th house](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-10th-house/)).

Then you read the ruler like any planet: its sign gives the manner, its aspects the help or friction it meets, and its condition, whether it's in its own sign or exaltation or in detriment or fall, how easily it does the job. One ruler can lead to another, too: the planet that rules the sign your ruler sits in joins the story, a chain astrologers follow as [dispositors](/astrology/dispositors-and-the-final-dispositor/).

Rulers matter most where a house holds no planet. An [empty house](/astrology/empty-houses/) isn't silent: the sign on its cusp and that sign's ruler speak for it, wherever the ruler happens to be.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I use house rulers to connect the parts of a life. A ruler sitting in another house tells me those two areas lean on each other: the ruler of the 4th in the 11th, and home is where your friends gather, or your friends become a kind of family ([Ruler of the 4th in the 11th house](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-11th-house/)). The ruler of the 1st gets the most attention, since it speaks for you.

The rulers depend on the cusps, and in Placidus, the house system I use, the cusps depend on the birth time. A house that begins at 29° of one sign can begin in the next with a slightly different time, and change its ruler with it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

And I don't read a ruler as fate. A ruler in the 12th doesn't hide away the house it rules, and a ruler in a house with a hard old name doesn't spoil it. It says where the story tends to be lived, in what manner, and alongside which other parts of the chart ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“An empty house has nothing to say.”** Its ruler is somewhere in the chart, and that's where its story is lived.
- **“Each planet rules a single house.”** Most planets rule two signs, so one planet can rule two houses; and with Placidus houses of unequal size, one sign can even sit on two cusps.
- **“Venus always rules the 7th.”** That's the natural ruler. Your 7th is ruled by whichever planet rules the sign on your own 7th cusp.
- **“The ruler of a house tells you what will happen there.”** It tells you how and where that part of life tends to be lived, not what the future holds.
