Most people arrive at a first reading with a question and a little suspicion. I arrived at astrology with a great deal of suspicion, so I'd rather you knew what you're getting before you begin: what I need from you, what happens next, and what no reading of mine will do. If you're still choosing who to read with, [Questions to ask an astrologer](/astrology/questions-to-ask-an-astrologer/) gives you questions to put to anyone, me included.

## Step by step {#step-by-step}

1. **Find your birth time.** I need your date, time and place of birth, and the time isn't optional: it decides your rising sign and your houses, the areas of life each planet speaks to, and on the day the Moon changes sign, your Moon as well. Without it there is no reading ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). If it isn't on the papers you have, [How to find your birth time](/astrology/how-to-find-your-birth-time/) goes through where else to look.
2. **Make sure your question is one a chart can take.** A chart describes how you tend to think, feel, love, work and recover. It can't diagnose anything, foresee a death or a pregnancy, choose an investment or say how a court case will end, and I don't read for anyone under 18, or about them. Each of those has a page of its own: [Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/), [Can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/), [Can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/), [Can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/), [Can astrology predict a court case?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-a-court-case/) and [Children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/).
3. **Choose whose chart.** Your own chart is a [birth chart reading](/birth-chart/). Two charts side by side, for a couple, friends, co-workers or family, make a synastry: the other person has to know you're asking for it, a colleague has to agree as well, and both of you must be adults ([Synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
4. **Tell me what's on your mind, if you like.** The order form has an optional space for anything you'd like me to know, or anything you'd like the reading to look at. A line is enough. You don't need to write to me first, but you can.
5. **Let it be prepared.** Your birth details reach me with your payment, not before. Each reading is prepared with a system I built myself, from the method I tested on my own chart, and I read it before it's sent. It reaches you by email, in a private online space where you can leave notes and highlight what matters, and as a PDF.
6. **Know how it's laid out.** It goes placement by placement: your Sun, Moon and rising sign ([The big three](/astrology/big-three/)), then Mercury, how you love, how you work, and any retrograde planets. The longest part goes to Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, which held 75 to 90% of the time in my notebook, against about a third overall ([The three that held: Saturn, Chiron and Lilith](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)). It's a notebook, not a study, so when something rests on a placement that held less often, I say so.
7. **Read it with a pen.** Go through it the way I went through my own chart: a tick where it describes you in a way it couldn't describe anyone else, a cross where it's vague, wrong, or would fit your neighbour just as well ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). What you keep is your call: a reading is a conversation with yourself, not an instruction ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).
8. **Bring your questions back.** You can write to me after the reading as well as before. With the consulting option, we can talk it through together. And if what you're carrying turns out to be bigger than a reading, I'll tell you plainly, and point you towards someone who can really help ([When to see a professional instead](/astrology/when-to-see-a-professional-instead/)).

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes}

- **Waiting for a date.** People ask when they'll meet someone, or when work will change. I read patterns and seasons, not next Tuesday: a chart can describe how you tend to meet a change, never when one will come.
- **Hoping for a verdict.** A reading won't tell you to stay, leave, forgive or cut anyone off. That decision is yours, and it should be.
- **Rounding the time.** "About three" entered as 15:00 is still about three, and the rising sign changes about every two hours on average. If you aren't sure of the minute, tell me before you order, not after.
- **Wearing it as a label.** "It's because I'm a Scorpio" is an excuse with a vocabulary. A chart describes tendencies, never permissions, for you or for anyone else.
- **Expecting every line to land.** Overall, about a third of what I checked in my notebook held. A line that doesn't fit you deserves a cross, not a struggle to make it fit.
- **Going back to it before every decision.** If you notice you're consulting charts all the time, take a break. The muscle that decides is yours, and it gets stronger with use.
