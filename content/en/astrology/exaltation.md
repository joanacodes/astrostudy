A planet's own sign is where it lives. Its exaltation is somewhere else: the one sign where, by tradition, it is received with honour, the way a household turns out for a guest of note. Each of the seven planets visible to the eye has one.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The seven exaltations are the Sun in Aries, the Moon in Taurus, Mercury in Virgo, Venus in Pisces, Mars in Capricorn, Jupiter in Cancer and Saturn in Libra. A planet standing in that sign is exalted. Mercury is the only one exalted in a sign it also rules, Virgo; for every other planet, exaltation and [domicile](/astrology/domicile/) are two different places.

Each exaltation also has a degree. The Sun's is the 19th degree of Aries, the Moon's the 3rd of Taurus, Mercury's the 15th of Virgo, Venus's the 27th of Pisces, Mars's the 28th of Capricorn, Jupiter's the 15th of Cancer and Saturn's the 21st of Libra, as Otto Neugebauer and H. B. van Hoesen list them in *Greek Horoscopes* (1959).

The idea is older than the zodiac of twelve signs. [Babylonian astrology](/astrology/babylonian-astrology/) already marked special places for the planets, and Francesca Rochberg has pointed out that the system appears in the omen tradition of *Enuma Anu Enlil*, so its roots may go back to the second millennium BCE (*Babylonian Horoscopes*, 1998). Why those places were chosen isn't known. Robert Hand singled out vigorous Mars exalted in cold Capricorn as one of the choices that resist any tidy explanation (*Horoscope Symbols*, 1987).

Uranus, Neptune and Pluto have no exaltation in the tradition. Modern astrologers have proposed several signs for them, never with agreement, and most have let the question drop, so I don't give them one.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

In the medieval ranking, exaltation comes second, just below domicile; [William Lilly](/astrology/william-lilly/) gave it 4 points to domicile's 5 in *Christian Astrology* (1647) ([The table of dignities](/astrology/table-of-dignities/)). A traditional image explains the difference. A planet in its own sign is the host, with the run of the house; an exalted planet is the honoured guest, the centre of attention but with less say over what happens.

Astrologers have never agreed on how far an exaltation reaches. [Al-Biruni](/astrology/al-biruni/), writing in 1029, reported three opinions: that it spreads a few degrees either side of its degree, that it runs from the start of the sign up to that degree, and that it fills the whole sign (*The Book of Instruction in the Elements of the Art of Astrology*). Deborah Houlding, writing on Ptolemy's table, takes the whole sign and the degree together: Mars is dignified anywhere in Capricorn, and most visibly in the 28th degree.

Opposite each exaltation lies the planet's [fall](/astrology/fall/), which for the Sun is Libra. After exaltation, the old tables go on to the [triplicity rulers](/astrology/triplicity-rulers/), planets that preside over a whole element by day or by night.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read every planet as a function and every sign as the manner it works in. An exalted planet is a function whose manner gives it room and an audience. The [Moon in Taurus](/astrology/moon-in-taurus/) regulates feeling through continuity, steadied by what lasts. [Saturn in Libra](/astrology/saturn-in-libra/) brings structure to agreements: it holds the balance and keeps the terms. [Venus in Pisces](/astrology/venus-in-pisces/) values with compassion, generous to what others overlook.

The honoured guest is also watched. An exalted planet tends to be the function other people notice in you and come to expect, which is lovely when it suits you and tiring when it doesn't. That's the side of exaltation I talk about most. Being good at something in public creates its own pressure, and it isn't the same as choosing it.

Mars in Capricorn, the puzzle Hand pointed to, reads well through the same lens. Capricorn starts things strategically, through structure and a long aim, and Mars is the function that acts; together they make effort that is organised and sustained rather than quick. Saturn exalted in Libra gets my full attention too, for a reason of my own: Saturn is one of the three placements that held 75 to 90% of the time in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/).

None of this makes an exalted planet a promise. Exaltation tells me how comfortably a function sits in its sign; the house shows where it works, and the aspects how it gets on with the rest of the chart.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“An exalted planet is stronger than a planet at home.”** The usual ranking puts domicile first and exaltation second. The guest is honoured; the host runs the house.
- **“Every planet has an exaltation.”** The seven traditional ones do. The outer planets have none in the tradition and no agreed one today.
- **“Exaltation only counts at its exact degree.”** Old authors disagreed about its reach, and some already gave it the whole sign, as al-Biruni reported.
- **“The opposite of exaltation is detriment.”** It's fall. Detriment lies opposite the planet's own sign.
