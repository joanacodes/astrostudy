Taurus is where tradition says the Moon is exalted, and Cancer is the sign it rules, so with your Sun in the first and your Moon in the second, the whole pair stands on the Moon's ground. The birth came under a crescent still low in the west at dusk, and I read it as a life built around safety: making it, keeping it, sharing it.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Fixed earth and cardinal water, two signs apart: a sextile by sign, with elements that suit each other, earth giving water a bank to run between. The [Sun in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-taurus/) makes a self out of what it can keep and care for; the [Moon in Cancer](/astrology/moon-in-cancer/) protects and nurtures, and as a cardinal sign it does so by taking the first step. Purpose and need point at the same thing more often than not: a home, a family of your choosing, work that keeps people safe.

The rulers make this pair unusual. [Venus](/astrology/venus/) rules your Sun's sign; your Moon's sign is ruled by the Moon itself, so it answers to no other planet, and its own house and aspects carry the weight. Tradition gives the Moon its [domicile](/astrology/domicile/), Cancer, and its exaltation, Taurus, inside this one pair, and I read that as feelings with a strong, clear voice, which isn't the same as feelings that are always easy.

A crescent hung in the west after sunset when you were born, or soon would: the Moon stood 31° to 90° past the Sun, two and a half to seven days into the month. The eight-phase count calls nearly all of these births [waxing crescent](/astrology/waxing-crescent/) births, leaving one in eight to the [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/) phase, and with a gap close to 60° the chart has a [Sun sextile Moon](/astrology/sun-sextile-moon/). [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) describes what the crescent adds.

Roughly 1 person in 141 is born with both lights on the Moon's ground like this. Each sign gets the Moon for about two and a half days, so a birth near one of its crossings, Gemini into Cancer or Cancer into Leo, could just as well belong to the [Sun in Taurus, Moon in Gemini](/astrology/sun-in-taurus-moon-in-gemini/) or the [Sun in Taurus, Moon in Leo](/astrology/sun-in-taurus-moon-in-leo/); only the time can say ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). The same time sets the rising sign and the houses, in Placidus, the system I use, and next to them the two lights are only a first sketch.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Love, for you, is a shelter you build and keep in good repair. The Taurus Sun brings steadiness and physical care; the Cancer Moon brings memory, protectiveness and a sharp sense of when someone needs looking after. Partners tend to feel very safe with you. The difficulty is that two such protective lights can hold on too tightly, to a person, a routine or an old hurt, and find it hard to let a bond change shape as the people in it grow.

## At work {#at-work}

You work best where effort protects something: a family business, a team you've known for years, a service that looks after people, a craft passed down. The Sun wants a result it can touch, and the Moon wants to feel that the work matters to someone in particular, so purely abstract or transactional roles can leave both lights underfed. Loyal colleagues often become a second family, which is a strength until a reorganisation splits them up.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Holding on, twice over. The Taurus Sun keeps what it has; the Cancer Moon keeps what it remembers. Together they can make change feel like loss even when it's growth, and an old disappointment can sit in you for years, quietly shaping what you're willing to risk. Keep the roots and prune the branches: decide what is truly yours to keep, and let the rest go a little at a time, with some small ritual that thanks it for what it gave.
