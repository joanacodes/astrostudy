The 8th house is where two lives overlap: the trust, the intimacy, the things held in common and the debts between people. Jupiter grows in that overlap, and it tends to find meaning in what others would rather not look at.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 8th house](/astrology/8th-house/) holds intimacy and trust, and what you share with others or owe them: joint money, debts, the closeness that changes you. It's succedent, its natural sign is Scorpio, and its natural rulers are Pluto and, in the tradition, Mars. With [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) here, growth comes through depth and through sharing. You learn by going where things are hidden or difficult, your beliefs are shaped by what you've been through with other people, and your generosity shows in what you're willing to share.

Tradition gave the 8th death and inheritance, and a Jupiter here was once read as gain through other people's deaths. I read neither: a chart can't predict a death ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)), nor what anyone will inherit or owe. What interests me here is how you trust, how you share and how closeness changes you, because that's where this Jupiter does its work.

In Placidus, the house system I use, the 8th's boundaries hang on the birth time and place. A few minutes either way could make this [Jupiter in the 7th house](/astrology/jupiter-in-7th-house/), growth through partners, or [Jupiter in the 9th house](/astrology/jupiter-in-9th-house/), growth through belief and travel ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, you grow through intimacy that goes all the way: sharing what's private, being seen in difficulty, building a trust that can take weight. You can be very generous with a partner, emotionally and practically, and you often help people through their hardest times. The 2nd house opposite is your own worth and resources; with Jupiter in the 8th, the lesson can be keeping a sense of what's yours inside what's shared. The risk is trusting too fast, or making intensity the measure of love.

## At work {#at-work}

Work that deals with what's shared, hidden or difficult often suits this Jupiter: research, psychology, counselling, investigation, insurance, banking, managing other people's resources, crisis work, any field where trust is the real currency. You may also be good at the conversations others avoid: the hard feedback, the negotiation over shared money, the meeting after a crisis. Openings can come through other people's resources or through partnerships built on trust. What to watch is taking on other people's burdens, practical or emotional, as if they were yours to carry.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Depth can become a requirement: a feeling that nothing counts unless it's intense, private or hard-won. Trust may be given in all-or-nothing terms. Shared resources can blur until you aren't sure what's yours. And because this Jupiter can find meaning in difficulty, you may stay in a situation past its time, looking for the lesson when the lesson is to leave.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Make clear agreements about what's shared, and revisit them. Let trust build in stages. Read about what fascinates you, the psychology, the history, the hidden side of things, and share what you learn. Keep something that is simply yours, money, time, a room, while you share the rest. Talk about the subjects people avoid with someone you trust; naming them takes away some of their weight. And when a hard time ends, let it end, and keep what it taught you.

The 8th asks for trust, and [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) asked people for theirs: their charts, with their consent, marked line by line. Jupiter wasn't one of the three that held far more often, Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, and overall about a third held. So I read an 8th-house Jupiter gently, and I tell you plainly when part of a reading rests on it.
