Moon conjunct Algol comes round every month and is over within an afternoon. Every 27⅓ days the Moon crosses Algol's degree, late in Taurus, and the crossing takes three or four hours: a birth inside them puts the Moon in its sign of exaltation, on the star with the darkest name in the old books.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) as your inner housekeeping: needs, moods, attachments, and the habits that bring you back to steady after a hard day. [Algol](/astrology/algol/) is named for the opposite of comfort. Its Arabic name means the ghoul's head, it marks the Gorgon's head in Perseus, and the astrology of fixed stars counted it among the unluckiest stars it knew.

Here the tradition argues with itself. Algol stood at 25°11′ Taurus in 1930 and 26°09′ in 2000, so the Moon on it is always a [Moon in Taurus](/astrology/moon-in-taurus/), and Taurus is where the same tradition placed the Moon in [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/), its most honoured sign. One old rule calls the place unlucky, another calls it the Moon's best. I take that as a reason not to treat either label as a fact about you, and to look at what the sign itself describes: needs met through continuity, steadiness and things that last.

The Moon travels about 13° a day, so it crosses the two-degree window in three or four hours, and the date of a birth can't tell you whether you were born inside them. This is why I won't read a chart without a birth time: here the hour decides the contact outright, and it decides the Moon's house too ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Algol stands too far north for the Moon ever to cover it, so the meeting is one of zodiac degree only, with the Moon passing well to the south of the star itself. Still, two lights that change on a timetable share this degree: the Moon, waxing and waning over 29½ days, and Algol, which fades for about ten hours every 2.87 days as one of its stars passes in front of another.

Algol has a history with calendars as well. An Egyptian calendar of lucky and unlucky days, about 3,200 years old, has been read by some astronomers as a record of the star's blinking, a reading others question. Whatever it tracked, that calendar sorted days into good and bad, which is the kind of sorting I keep out of a chart: the Moon on Algol forecasts nothing about your health or your safety, and the dates of a life, its last one included, are written in no chart ([Can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)).

About 1 person in 186 born between 1930 and 2009 has the contact, close to what a one-degree window gives. Counted in visits, the Moon is this degree's most frequent caller, some thirteen times a year, where the [Sun conjunct Algol](/astrology/sun-conjunct-algol/) and [Venus conjunct Algol](/astrology/venus-conjunct-algol/) each come about once a year, [Mars conjunct Algol](/astrology/mars-conjunct-algol/) about once in two years, and [Saturn conjunct Algol](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-algol/) once in a generation.

My charts carry no stars, so this page describes the contact and leaves the reading to the Moon's own sign, house and aspects. How the tradition counts a star contact is set out in [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/).
