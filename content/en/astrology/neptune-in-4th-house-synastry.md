The kitchen light left on, a door you stop locking when they're due: their Neptune in your 4th house brings another person's dreaming down to the floor of your chart, where home, family and the private self live.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) longs for a belonging larger than everyday life, and softens whatever comes near it. [The 4th house](/astrology/4th-house/) is the ground under you: where you started out and where you live now, your family's story, the part of you only the closest people meet. In this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their longing settles in your private rooms.

With them, home can feel like a refuge rather than an address. You may want to make your place more beautiful, fill it with music, open it to them sooner than to anyone else; old family memories can soften, as if their presence helped you forgive the place you came from.

In your home they may feel they've found the belonging they imagined: your rooms, your rituals, even your relatives can look to them like the family they dreamed of. Angular and private at once, the 4th is the root of the chart, with [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), sign of shelter and memory, as its natural sign. Should their Neptune sit near your [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), the deepest point of your chart, the two make a conjunction, and that contact is the strongest note of the overlay.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

A dream of home meets the washing-up. Who does which chores, whose things live where, how long a visit lasts: with them, the house rules can stay unspoken, and a door left open becomes hard to close. You may also feel that your actual home falls short of the haven they picture.

From their side, idealising your family can hide its real difficulties from them, and they may be hurt when you speak of home with less tenderness than they feel for it.

The 4th describes how home feels from inside, not who your parents were. Their Neptune here says nothing about anyone's family, only about how belonging softens between the two of you.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Make the practical side of shared space explicit, kindly. Agree on keys, chores and visiting hours as you would with anyone, and keep the poetry for the evenings. In a couple, a house that feels like a dream still needs a rota; between friends, staying over works better with an end date; between colleagues working from your home, a clear corner and clear hours help; between adult relatives, sharing memories is lovely if each person's version of the past is allowed to differ.

Starting from their chart instead, it's your Neptune in their 4th house: your longing in their home. [Their Neptune in your 3rd house](/astrology/neptune-in-3rd-house-synastry/) colours daily talk and errands; [their Neptune in your 5th house](/astrology/neptune-in-5th-house-synastry/), romance, play and what you create.

The 4th is where generations sit on top of each other, and a slow planet suits it. Close in age, their Neptune probably lies near yours, and the dream of home is a shared one; between you and someone a generation older, two eras' ideals of family meet at your kitchen table. Their birth date is enough to place Neptune; their hour of birth draws their own houses, and I read neither chart without its time. Your IC, like your Ascendant, turns through the whole zodiac each day, so your birth time decides where your 4th begins, in Placidus, the house system I use, as in every quadrant system ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Home is private ground, which is why I read this overlay only once both of you have agreed ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#a-chart-is-personal)).
