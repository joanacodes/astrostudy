Aquarius widens the circle. Put a composite Neptune there and a relationship's ideal grows larger than the two people in it, and it belongs to the youngest adult couples: at least one partner has to be born from January 1998.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Every planet of [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) is a midpoint of two natal positions, and the chart is read as the relationship's own, a third thing beside the two people who make it. Its [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) is the bond's ideal: where it imagines, where its compassion goes, where it feels part of something larger. [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/), fixed air, points that ideal at the many. Two people with this composite tend to dream of a relationship that is also a friendship, with room for each person to stay themselves; of a circle of friends that feels like chosen family; of a cause, a network or a project that could make the future a little fairer. Its compassion reaches strangers, and its imagination is happiest with what hasn't been tried yet.

Aquarius has two rulers, [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) in modern tropical astrology and [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) in the tradition, and I read both. In a composite, Uranus would show where the bond needs room and change, Saturn where it needs rules and commitment; with Neptune in Aquarius, the pair's ideal tends to ask for both at once: freedom held inside something that lasts.

Two people born between January 1998 and February 2012 both have [Neptune in Aquarius](/astrology/neptune-in-aquarius/), but only those born by late 2008 are adults today, and this site never reads [children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/). Pairs from next door join them: born in June 1994, one partner has Neptune at about 23° Capricorn; born in June 2006, the other has it at about 20° Aquarius; the composite lands at about 6° Aquarius. A random sample of two million pairs born from 1930 to 2009 gives only about 1 pair in 23, but that sample stops just as this generation begins: for couples born in the 2000s, it is the rule.

On one side, [composite Neptune in Capricorn](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-capricorn/) builds its ideal to last; on the other, [composite Neptune in Pisces](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-pisces/) belongs to two people born from 2011 to 2026, all of them children today.

The difficulty is a dream so wide that the two people get lost in it. A bond that idealises community can give its best evenings to the cause and its tired ones to each other, or prize openness so highly that it never quite says what it is. Its gift, at its best, is a love that makes both people kinder to everyone else. Freedom isn't distance, and a fairer world can start with a fair share of the washing-up.

The house, from both birth times, decides where in the pair's life this ideal gathers ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Composites are a technique I describe for readers rather than one I draw; the astrologers who use them read this Neptune with each partner's chart and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) to hand, and none of it tells a young couple how long they will last.
