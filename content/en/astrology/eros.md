In August 1898, an astronomer in Berlin photographed something nobody had seen before: an asteroid whose orbit brings it close to the Earth's. It was named Eros, after the Greek god of desire, and became the first minor planet with a male name. In astrology it also shares its name with a lot of Venus, which is the first thing to untangle.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Eros was found on 13 August 1898 by Carl Gustav Witt at the Urania Observatory in Berlin, and by Auguste Charlois at Nice. No asteroid known until then came inside the orbit of Mars. Eros does, towards the Earth's, and that is why it broke with the custom of giving asteroids female names. It's a stony body shaped rather like a peanut, about 34 km long and 17 km across on average, and it goes round the Sun in about 1.76 years. It belongs to the group of near-Earth asteroids named after [Amor](/astrology/amor/), another name for the same god.

It has a scientific history of its own. Observatories around the world measured its position during its passages of 1900–1901 and 1930–1931 to work out the distance to the Sun, and the second result stood until radar and other methods did better in 1968. In 2000 the NEAR Shoemaker spacecraft went into orbit around it, and on 12 February 2001 it landed there: the first landing on any asteroid.

The god is older than most. In Hesiod's *Theogony*, Eros is one of the first powers to come into being, after Chaos, with the Earth and Tartarus. Later writers made him Aphrodite's son, the winged archer, whom the Romans called Cupid or Amor ([Cupido (asteroid 763)](/astrology/cupido-asteroid/)). His one great story of his own is his love for [Psyche](/astrology/psyche/), whose asteroid has its own page.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Because Eros comes so near, it doesn't move through the zodiac at an even pace. At its closest oppositions, which come round about every 81 years, it can reach the seventh magnitude and seems almost to halt in the sky; at the one of early 2137, its backward loop will cover only 34 minutes of right ascension. Its [retrograde](/astrology/retrograde-explained/) periods are nothing like the steady rhythm of the main-belt asteroids.

Watch the name, too. Venus's own lot among the seven [Hermetic lots](/astrology/hermetic-lots/) is called Eros, and it is worked out from the [Part of Spirit](/astrology/part-of-spirit/): Ascendant + Venus − Spirit by day, the other way round by night. That's a calculated degree, with nothing to do with the asteroid. A program that simply lists "Eros" may mean either, so read its label first.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The system behind my readings calculates no asteroids, Eros included, and it wasn't in the notebook where I [ticked or crossed every placement](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) of my own chart and other people's, so I can't tell you how often an Eros placement holds.

Desire does come into a reading, through two planets the notebook did cover: [Venus](/astrology/venus/), for what draws you and what you prize, and [Mars](/astrology/mars/), for the way you pursue it. Neither is one of the strong results: Venus held about a third of the time, like the placements everyone reads first, and the three that held far more often were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith. I say so in a reading when something rests on Venus or Mars.

Between two people, the same applies. A synastry compares two whole charts, with both people's agreement ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)), and no single contact, asteroid or planet, can prove that two people want each other. If you do add Eros, keep it as a detail beside Venus and Mars, never in place of them ([How to use asteroids without drowning the chart](/astrology/how-to-use-asteroids-without-drowning-the-chart/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Eros in your chart shows who you'll desire.”** A point can't name a person or make a choice for you.
- **“An Eros contact between two charts predicts an affair.”** A contact describes how two people may meet, never what they'll do.
- **“The Lot of Eros is just another name for the asteroid.”** One is a rock in space; the other is a degree counted from Venus and the Part of Spirit.
- **“Eros is the sex asteroid.”** In Hesiod he's one of the first powers of the world, long before he's anybody's lover.
