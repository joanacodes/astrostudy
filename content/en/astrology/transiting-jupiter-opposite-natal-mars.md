Once in every twelve-year round, Jupiter reaches the part of the sky facing your natal Mars. The way you go after things then meets something larger standing across from it: an aim, a principle, and quite often another person's plans.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the part of a chart that acts: it starts things, makes the effort, says what you want and holds your limits. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) widens understanding and looks for the meaning that makes growth worth it. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) relates them through polarity, so for a while you see your own drive most clearly in what it's being asked to serve.

In practice that tends to look like an invitation to do more, or to do it bigger: a partner's venture that needs your energy, a team aiming higher than you'd planned, a cause that wants your weekends. The larger aim stands outside you and looks entirely reasonable, which is why this is the angle at which people most often overcommit. It can also hand effort a reason: the contest worth entering, the work that deserves the push.

Where it lands depends on the house of your Mars, and Jupiter is then crossing the house opposite. With Mars in your 10th, for instance, the opposition comes during [Jupiter transiting the 4th house](/astrology/jupiter-transit-4th-house/): home asks for room while your drive is pointed at work. Mars moves less than a degree a day, so your birth date places it well enough; the house needs your birth time, and without it I don't read the chart at all ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Hard transits to Mars have a reputation for accidents and quarrels. I don't read them for either: a [transit](/astrology/transits/) can't tell you whether you'll be hurt ([Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)) or how a disagreement will end. If your chart has [Mars opposite Jupiter](/astrology/mars-opposite-jupiter/), this one isn't a visitor: each [Jupiter return](/astrology/jupiter-return/) brings it, because Jupiter coming home also faces your Mars.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

With the orbs I use for timing, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, a straight pass across the point opposite your Mars takes about eleven days. About three times in ten, Jupiter turns retrograde nearby and crosses it three times, and the transit stretches to about eight months ([Jupiter retrograde](/astrology/jupiter-retrograde/)). In the site's count for births from 1930 to 2009, everyone meets it six or seven times by the age of 80.

It marks the far point of Jupiter's lap around your Mars. About six years earlier, [transiting Jupiter conjunct natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-conjunct-natal-mars/) set your urge to act and your urge to grow off together; the opposition shows what that start has become. In between, and again on the way back, come [transiting Jupiter sextile natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-sextile-natal-mars/), an opening you have to take, [transiting Jupiter square natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-square-natal-mars/), when effort and ambition grind, and [transiting Jupiter trine natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-trine-natal-mars/), when they run together.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Before you agree to anything large, ask whose plan your energy would be powering, and whether you'd choose it at your own size. Jupiter in the sky makes other people's ambitions look like obviously good ideas; your Mars is the one that will do the work.

Pace yourself on purpose. Put the rest days in before the push starts, not after it: drive running on borrowed enthusiasm burns faster than drive running on its own.

When someone across the table wants more, let the contrast teach you something about how they spend their energy, and what for. An opposition shows you your own habits by setting a different set in front of them, and that is often the most useful thing these weeks leave behind.
