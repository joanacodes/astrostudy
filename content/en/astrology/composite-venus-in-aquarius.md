Separate holidays as well as shared ones, a group of friends who are half the reason you met, a household arranged in a way your parents still don't quite understand: composite Venus in Aquarius puts a relationship's pleasure in being free together.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), the midpoint of two natal Venuses becomes [Venus](/astrology/venus/) for the relationship as a whole, and it speaks for the bond's pleasures, its values and its sense of what's fair. [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/), fixed air, gives Venus neither dignity nor debility. Its rulers are [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), in modern astrology, and [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), in Ptolemy's older scheme; when I read a birth chart, I read both. With roughly seven years per sign, Uranus gives two people of similar age much the same position, so composite Uranus mostly repeats their generation's; composite Saturn, built from a planet that spends only about two and a half years in each sign, more often says something about the pair itself.

Conventional apart, the two of you may run your shared life by rules your friends find unusual: separate rooms for work, an open door for friends at any hour, decisions made by a vote of two. The bond's affection includes room, since it values each person staying fully themselves, and it's often happiest when the two of you belong to something larger: a community garden, a campaign, a circle that meets every month. [Venus in Aquarius](/astrology/venus-in-aquarius/) at birth gives one person a love that begins in friendship; here friendship sits at the heart of the bond itself, whatever else it is. Its share is about 1 pair in 12; natal Venus is in Aquarius a little more often, for about 1 person in 11.

Across the chart from Aquarius lies Leo, the Sun's own sign, and that is where the composite Sun ends up in about 1 pair in 22, facing this Venus. The reason is that each midpoint is taken separately: when two birthdays are months apart, the Suns' halfway point and the Venuses' can come out on opposite sides, and about 1 pair in 10 sees composite Venus stray more than 47° from the composite Sun. For the other 9 in 10, the two stay as close as they would in any birth chart, and the Sun of an Aquarius Venus sits no further back than Sagittarius or further ahead than Aries. It is the calculation at work, not a rift between you.

Independence can look like distance. A bond that values room can forget to say that it also values closeness, and principles about how people ought to treat each other can drift away from the person across the table. Objectivity isn't coldness, and freedom agreed is very different from freedom assumed. It helps to say the warm thing plainly, without irony, and to agree out loud on what each of you is free to do rather than finding out.

[Composite Venus in Capricorn](/astrology/composite-venus-in-capricorn/) builds, [composite Venus in Pisces](/astrology/composite-venus-in-pisces/) forgives; Aquarius befriends. Composites are outside my practice, and I describe the technique here because many astrologers use it, always beside the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) and the two natal charts. A relationship with rules of its own still keeps this one: neither person is charted without saying yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
