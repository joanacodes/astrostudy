Gemini is the sign of the conversation that never quite ends. A composite Neptune there would make talk itself the relationship's dream, and no adult couple alive can have it: Neptune left Gemini in 1902 and comes back in 2051.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Each planet of [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) is a midpoint, and the chart they make is read as the relationship's own, a third presence beside the two people. Composite [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) holds what the bond longs for and imagines: its shared ideal, its compassion, the feeling of being part of something larger. [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/), mutable air ruled by [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), would put that longing into words. A pair with this Neptune would dream through talk: letters kept for years, a private language of jokes and nicknames, the conviction that the two of them could understand anything as long as they kept talking. Gemini is the natural sign of the 3rd house, the house of siblings, so the ideal might look like kinship: two people who feel like twins who found each other late.

The dates rule it out for everyone alive. A sign takes Neptune about fourteen years to cross, which makes a composite Neptune mostly a matter of birth years: partners of one generation share its sign, and partners of two generations meet halfway. Neptune was last in Gemini from August 1887 to May 1902, and nobody born then is alive; adults today were born with Neptune between Cancer and Aquarius, and no midpoint of two such positions reaches Gemini. Draw two million pairs at random from everyone born between 1930 and 2009 and you won't find one. The next couples to share it will be two people born during Neptune's next stay in the sign, from July 2051 to April 2066.

Compare a fast planet. [Composite Mercury in Gemini](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-gemini/), the way a pair talks and thinks together, turns up in about 1 pair in 14, because Mercury goes round the zodiac in a year; Neptune needs about 165. That's why the composite signs of the slow planets say so much about birth years and so little about the two individuals, and why, for Neptune, the house is where a composite becomes personal.

In a single chart, [Neptune in Gemini](/astrology/neptune-in-gemini/) belongs to a generation long gone and to one not yet born. The neighbours are no busier: [composite Neptune in Taurus](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-taurus/) waits for pairs born from 2038, and [composite Neptune in Cancer](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-cancer/) has, in practice, no living couple either.

On paper, this ideal's shadow is a story that replaces the facts: a bond that talks so beautifully about itself that what is actually happening goes unsaid, or that takes being understood for granted and stops checking. When couples with it exist, the astrologers who draw composites will read it with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), to see how the pair dreams, not whether it lasts. Like every reading of two people, it will need a conversation first: both of them knowing, both agreeing ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)). I describe composites without drawing them, and in Gemini this one has no adult couple to describe before 2069.
