Cancer is where the zodiac builds its shelters. A composite Neptune there would make a harbour the relationship's ideal, a home to come back to; in practice no couple alive has it, because Neptune left Cancer in 1916 and won't be back before 2065.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) averages two birth charts, planet by planet, into a single chart that astrologers read as the relationship's: not one person's or the other's, but the bond's. Its [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) shows where the bond idealises and imagines, where compassion flows between the two people and where the edges between them soften. [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), cardinal water ruled by the [Moon](/astrology/moon/), would turn all of that towards shelter. The relationship's dream would be a home that holds more than two people: a door always open to family and friends, a kitchen where nobody stays a guest for long, a sense that the relationship is itself a place to come back to. Its compassion would protect, and at its best it would know that protection is not control and belonging is not owning.

In practice no couple alive has it. With fourteen years or so in each sign, Neptune makes a composite a record of two birth years: partners of the same age inherit their generation's sign, and partners years apart meet in between. Neptune was last in Cancer from July 1901 to May 1916, so this placement would need at least one partner born before May 1916, over a hundred and ten today. Not one of two million random pairs born between 1930 and 2009 has it. Neptune returns to Cancer from July 2065 to July 2079, and same-age pairs born then will be its next couples.

The longing it describes isn't out of reach, though: it comes by house rather than by sign. [Composite Neptune in the 4th house](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-4th-house/), the house whose natural sign is Cancer, gives a relationship a close cousin of this dream of home, and it depends on two birth times, not on two birth years ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Were the sign possible, the Moon would be read beside it, as the ruler of Cancer: in a composite, the Moon shows what a pair needs to feel safe, and with this Neptune that need and the shared dream would point at the same door. In one birth chart, [Neptune in Cancer](/astrology/neptune-in-cancer/) belongs to the generation born from 1901 to 1916. Next door, [composite Neptune in Gemini](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-gemini/) has no adult couple alive, and [composite Neptune in Leo](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-leo/) only the oldest pairs.

Its shadow, on paper, is a harbour that won't let anyone sail: a bond that idealises its home until the world outside looks like a threat, or that keeps everyone close by keeping everyone small. Nobody can test that today. I describe the composite as a technique and leave it out of my readings; in Cancer, the people it would describe were born more than a hundred and ten years ago, or will be born after 2065.
