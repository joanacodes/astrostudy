Some people know exactly when to say "stop for lunch", because they once learned the hard way what happens when nobody does. With their Chiron in your 6th house, the other person's old hurt, and the practical wisdom it gave them, shows up in your ordinary week.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) is adaptation: finding a way to keep going well when something stays difficult, and gaining a craft from it. [The 6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) is your working week seen up close: the tasks rather than the title, the habits that hold you up, the colleagues beside you, and how you look after yourself with sleep, food and rest. In this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their way of keeping going meets your routines.

With them, your days may become more workable. They tend to have practical tricks for doing things within limits: the list broken into smaller lists, the alarm that says stop, the task handed on before it grows. They notice when you're running on empty and say so plainly, and they rarely mistake slowing down for failing.

For them, your way of working can touch something tender. Your perfectionism, or your easy way of taking a break, may recall a time when they felt valued only for being useful, and they may answer by working harder beside you. Cadent houses adjust and keep things running, and this one has [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/) for its natural sign; its old name was Bad Fortune, and Mars was said to rejoice in it. Late in your 6th, their Chiron nears your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), and a working alliance can shade into a one-to-one bond.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Help can become supervision. Tips on how you organise your day, what you eat at your desk or when you go to bed can feel intrusive when you didn't ask, however well meant. And if they quietly take over your tasks, you may lose track of what is yours to do.

From their side, being useful can turn into a hiding place. Helping you may come easier to them than being helped, and they may underplay what their workarounds cost them, until they're tired and you never saw it coming.

Illness was the 6th's in tradition, but I read neither illness nor any other health matter here ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)). Chiron's old hurt isn't a diagnosis, and this overlay describes how two people get through ordinary days together.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Share routines rather than supervise them, and ask what help looks like before giving it. Notice the effort behind their smooth workarounds, and say thank you out loud. A couple can split chores by what each finds easy rather than by habit; friends can swap errands in busy weeks; colleagues often see this overlay at its best in shared projects and clean handovers; adult relatives running a household or a family business together can agree who carries what.

Seen from the other side, you carry the Chiron and they the 6th house: your hard-won know-how then goes into their working day. [Their Chiron in your 5th house](/astrology/chiron-in-5th-house-synastry/) meets your play; [their Chiron in your 7th house](/astrology/chiron-in-7th-house-synastry/), the person across the table.

In Placidus, the house system I use, your 6th ends at your Descendant, and your birth time sets where that line falls, so a Chiron close to it may belong to your 6th or your 7th. Their side is simpler: their date alone is enough, since a day's difference moves Chiron by almost nothing. A reading of the two charts begins once I have both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). A colleague's chart is as personal as a partner's, and it's read only if they've agreed to it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#what-i-ask)).
