Some pairs talk politely in public and properly at home, where the shoes come off and the real subjects come out. Composite Mercury in the 4th house describes a relationship whose thinking lives at its foundation.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Composite [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), the midpoint of your two natal Mercurys in [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), is how the relationship thinks and talks; [the 4th house](/astrology/4th-house/) is its private ground: home, family, roots, the life nobody else sees. Angular, with Cancer as its natural sign, the 4th opens at the composite [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), the lowest point of the chart.

With the bond's mind here, the important conversations happen at home. Plans are made at the kitchen table, the household runs on notes and lists only the two of you can read, and talk returns often to where each of you comes from: family stories compared, old photographs explained, the question of what you'd keep from your own upbringing and what you'd drop. The relationship thinks best where it feels safe, and it may be quieter in public than in private. A natal [Mercury in the 4th house](/astrology/mercury-in-4th-house/) describes one person who thinks best behind a closed door; [their Mercury in your 4th house](/astrology/mercury-in-4th-house-synastry/), one person's voice making the other feel at home. The composite puts the conversation itself at the root of the bond, whether or not the two of you share an address.

Composite Mercury normally travels within about 28° of the composite Sun, so the Sun is often in the 4th as well, or close by. In about 1 pair in 26 it's the other way round: the composite Sun sits near the opposite point, around the 10th, the house of public life, while this Mercury stays home. That's a quirk of the method rather than a division in the relationship.

The composite Imum Coeli lies opposite the composite Midheaven, and the Midheaven, like the horizon, turns through a full circle each day, so both birth times decide where the 4th begins. The method matters too: taken from the midpoints of your two natal cusps, or rebuilt from the composite Midheaven for the place where you live, the 4th can start at different degrees, and a Mercury near its edge can change house ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

The difficulty is the conversation that never leaves the house. A bond that talks best in private can carry every subject home and decide nothing outside, or retell the same family stories until they set like plaster. Privacy isn't isolation. Taking a talk out of the house now and then helps (a café, a bench, a long drive), and so does asking a new question about an old story.

Family composites fit this house, drawn for an adult and a parent, say, or for two grown siblings. Both have to agree, and this site never reads a child's chart ([children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)). I've written this page to explain the technique, not because I draw composites: I don't, and astrologers who do read them with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). On either side, [composite Mercury in the 3rd house](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-3rd-house/) lives in daily messages and [composite Mercury in the 5th house](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-5th-house/) turns talk into play.
