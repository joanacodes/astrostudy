Of the five major aspects the Sun and Mars can make, the sextile is the one people are most often born with. At 60°, purpose and effort are near enough to reach each other and far enough apart to keep their own pace.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

Your [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is the self you're shaping, with its vitality and its sense of direction. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the part of you that starts things on the Sun's behalf: initiative, effort, the willingness to act when it's difficult. The [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) between them is an offer of cooperation, and it pays out only when you engage.

Here that condition has a twist. Most sextiles offer an opening and wait for you to act on it; in this one, one of the two partners is the urge to act. So the opening and the push to go through it arrive together, and what's asked of you is mostly to notice which openings deserve the push. In plain words, when something matters to you, the energy for it tends to be there, steady rather than explosive, neither forced nor in short supply.

That doesn't make effort automatic. Opportunity still requires engagement, and a sextile left alone stays a possibility: you can coast for years on what comes easily and never find out what you could start. I read this aspect at its best in people who pick a direction deliberately and let the drive follow, rather than spending it on whatever happens to be nearest.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Its two signs usually belong to fire and air, or to earth and water. A Sun in Aries with Mars in Gemini moves fast and talks while it moves; a Sun in Virgo with Mars in Cancer works carefully for the people it looks after. The houses show where the cooperation runs: the Sun's house is where you're trying to become yourself, Mars's is where your effort goes, and the sextile lets one feed the other.

About 1 person in 8 has the Sun and Mars within 8° of a sextile, more than for any other of their five major aspects. The reason is in their cycle. The Sun is always the faster, but both sextiles fall on the stretch where Mars is still moving forward at a fair pace, so the Sun gains on it slowly and the aspect lingers. Further round, as Mars slows towards its retrograde, the Sun moves through each aspect more quickly, and the trine and the opposition become much rarer.

A sextile this common is easy to over-read, so in a reading I say what rests on it and what rests elsewhere: one contact never decides a chart ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

The pair's other aspects change the terms of the deal:

- **[Sun conjunct Mars](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mars/):** the two in one place; where your sextile lets purpose call on effort, the conjunction makes them a single move.
- **[Sun square Mars](/astrology/sun-square-mars/):** a sign further apart, and cooperation turns to friction; the push no longer waits to be asked.
- **[Sun trine Mars](/astrology/sun-trine-mars/):** two signs further, in one element; less a door you open than a road already running.
- **[Sun opposite Mars](/astrology/sun-opposite-mars/):** the rarest of the five, with Mars retrograde; the drive stands across from you, often in other people.

Between two people, [Sun sextile Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-sextile-mars-synastry/) makes one person's energy useful to the other's aims: the friend who gets you moving, the colleague whose push suits your plans. I read it from both sides.

## In transits {#in-transits}

In transit, the sextile is one of the mildest contacts the sky makes between these two. Counting both directions, Mars to your natal Sun and the Sun to your natal Mars, it comes round about three times a year, a few days at most each time ([transits](/astrology/transits/)). Mars spends six to seven weeks in a sign, so its own visit is the longer of the two.

They're good days to begin something your purpose already wants, if you choose to. Weather, not a forecast: a small tailwind, never a guarantee.
