Sun conjunct Fomalhaut belongs to two birthdays, 21 and 22 February, when the Sun has just entered Pisces and reaches the degree of Fomalhaut, the star at the Southern Fish's mouth. It is a meeting measured along the zodiac alone, with a star whose royal title is the least certain of the four.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/), as I read it, is the organising centre of a chart: purpose, vitality, the thread that keeps your choices pointing the same way. [Fomalhaut](/astrology/fomalhaut/) brings a title rather than a temperament. R. H. Allen's *Star Names* (1899) counted it among four royal stars of ancient Persia, and Zoroastrian texts do give the southern sky a commander, but a 1942 study by W. B. Henning handed that command to Antares instead. The title survives in astrology books more firmly than in the sources.

That leaves the tradition's reading with a question mark in the middle of it. A royal star on the Sun invites talk of rank and recognition, and I'd keep the Sun's own job in view: recognition can matter to it, but purpose isn't a title. A crown that historians can't quite trace back makes that point better than I could.

The sign deserves more of your attention than the star. Fomalhaut stood at 2°52′ Pisces in 1930 and stands at 4°13′ in 2026, so this contact is always a [Sun in Pisces](/astrology/sun-in-pisces/), two or three days into the sign. The Greek star myths, as Theony Condos collects them, show the Southern Fish swallowing the water that Aquarius pours from his jar, and make the two fish of Pisces its young. Measured along the tropical zodiac, the mouth that drinks that stream now sits just past the boundary between Aquarius and Pisces, where the Sun crosses each February.

In the sky, the two never come near each other. Fomalhaut lies 21.1° south of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), the line the Sun follows through the year, so on your birthday the Sun passed about 21° north of it.

Counted over the births of 1930 to 2009, that comes to about 1 person in 183, and the dates never moved: 21 or 22 February in 1930, in 1970 and in 2009. The window is two degrees wide and the Sun covers about one a day, so its edges belong to the hour: a birth late on 20 February or early on the 23rd may fall inside or out, and the same hour decides which house of the chart the Sun occupies.

Each of the other bodies keeps a calendar of its own on this degree. The [Moon conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/moon-conjunct-fomalhaut/) comes round for a few hours every month; [Venus conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/venus-conjunct-fomalhaut/) needs a birth between early January and early April; [Mars conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/mars-conjunct-fomalhaut/) returns roughly every two years; [Saturn conjunct Fomalhaut](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-fomalhaut/) marked whole seasons of 1935, 1964–65 and 1994.

My notebook gives the Sun sign the same score as the other placements everyone reads first, about a third, and it never tested a star ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). No star appears in the charts I work from either. What I'd read for a 21 February birthday is the Sun in Pisces, its house and its aspects; how astrologers who do use stars measure a contact like this one is explained in [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/).
