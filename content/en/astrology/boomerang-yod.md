Add one planet to a yod, in the right place, and the figure changes direction. A fourth body opposite the apex turns a finger pointing at one planet into a boomerang: what goes out from the apex has somewhere to come back from.

## What it looks like {#what-it-looks-like}

Start from the [Yod (finger of God)](/astrology/yod-finger-of-god/): two planets 60° apart at the base, a third 150° from both at the apex, which lies opposite the midpoint of the base. Put a fourth planet on that midpoint and you have a boomerang yod. The fourth planet makes an [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) to the apex and sits about 30° from each base planet, a [semi-sextile](/astrology/semi-sextile/) to both. That's six aspects among four planets: one sextile, two quincunxes, one opposition and two semi-sextiles.

In signs, the fourth planet usually falls in the sign between the two base planets, and the apex in the sign opposite it. Base planets in Aries and Gemini put the fourth in Taurus and the apex in Scorpio, so the Taurus–Scorpio axis runs straight through the figure, with Aries and Gemini on either side of one end. On the wheel, three planets make a tight fan on one side and the apex stands alone on the other, joined to them by the long line of the opposition, the boomerang's spine.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

A fourth planet exactly opposite a yod's apex is a rarer thing than the yod itself: the count behind this site finds the boomerang in about 1 chart in 27 for births from 1930 to 2009, among the ten planets, with its quincunxes held to 3° and its sextile and opposition allowed 8° near the Sun or Moon, 6° otherwise. The figure needs four planets placed just so, and those tight quincunxes make it sensitive to orbs: a degree's difference either way changes the count a great deal. The count also leaves out the angles, Chiron, Lilith and the nodes, any of which could stand opposite an apex.

The system I built for my readings describes the quincunx as adjustment and recalibration, and the opposition as polarity: two functions made aware of each other, so that each can be integrated rather than fought. In a yod, the apex keeps adjusting with nothing across from it. In a boomerang yod, the planet opposite gives it a counterpart, and I read that as the adjusting becoming visible: something you can see and name, often first in someone else, rather than only feel. The fourth planet also sits close to both base planets, in semi-sextile, a contact of small, steady coordination, so it gathers what the base offers and carries it across the chart.

I still read the four planets one by one before the figure: their functions, their signs, how loudly each speaks. A pattern this neat gets my attention; it doesn't get the last word over the rest of the chart.

The houses decide where the opposition falls, across which two areas of life the boomerang throws and returns, and the houses need the time of birth. A boomerang yod with the Moon in it can form and vanish within a day, as the Moon moves about a sign every two and a half days and the quincunxes allow only 3°. So the boomerang, more than most figures, waits on [the birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/) before I read it.

## What helps {#what-helps}

- **Read the opposition first.** The apex and the planet facing it are the figure's spine. What one does, the other answers; learning both ends is learning the pattern.
- **Let the fourth planet speak for the base.** It sits between the base planets and knows their language, so it's often the easiest way into what the apex keeps adjusting to.
- **Watch for the return.** When the adjusting at the apex shows up across the chart, in a relationship or a role, that's the figure working, not repeating a failure.
- **Keep the name in its place.** A boomerang comes back because of how it's built; so does this pattern, and that is all the name means.

Its neighbour in this family, the [kite](/astrology/kite/), also hangs a planet opposite one corner, of a grand trine this time, and turns ease into direction. A boomerang yod asks for the same patient adjusting as any yod, with one difference that counts: something across the chart answers back.
