Cosmobiology is a German school of astrology with a scientific-sounding name and a narrow way of looking at a chart: through the degrees halfway between planets, and through the hard angles between them, folded onto a dial. It began as one astrologer's revision of another's.

## What it was {#what-it-was}

The word is older than the school. Before Reinhold Ebertin took it up, *Kosmobiologie* had been used by the German medical astrologer Friedrich Feerhow and by the Swiss statistician Karl Krafft for the branch of astrology “working on scientific foundations and keyed to the natural sciences”, in Ebertin's own description.

The method came from Hamburg. Alfred Witte (1878–1941), a surveyor and amateur astronomer, revived the use of midpoints, the degree exactly between two bodies, and his circle published its rules in the *Regelwerk für Planetenbilder* in 1928. His school also worked with hypothetical planets beyond Neptune, eight of them in the end ([The Hamburg School (Uranian astrology)](/astrology/hamburg-school-uranian-astrology/)). The Nazi regime banned the *Regelwerk* on 2 October 1936. Ebertin kept Witte's [midpoints](/astrology/midpoints/) and the 90° dial, left the hypothetical planets out, and called what remained cosmobiology. His handbook, *Kombination der Gestirneinflüsse*, appeared in 1940; its English translation, *The Combination of Stellar Influences*, was last updated in 1972. In it he acknowledges how much he drew on Witte's rulebook, while differing from Witte on method.

A cosmobiologist reads a short, strict list. A planet standing on the midpoint of two others ties the three functions together. Only the aspects of the eighth harmonic count, the ones Witte and Ebertin both judged strongest: the conjunction, the [semi-square](/astrology/semi-square/) of 45°, the square, the [sesquiquadrate](/astrology/sesquiquadrate/) of 135° and the opposition. The chart is drawn as a cosmogram, derived from the Hamburg School's dial ([The Uranian 90° dial](/astrology/uranian-90-dial/)), on which all of those aspects line up. For timing, Ebertin published a graphic ephemeris of 45°, in English as *Transits* in 1973.

He also took the school in two directions of his own. One was medicine: his research in medical astrology, in English as *Astrological Healing* in 1989, and the psychological meanings he added were his most original contributions. The other was a claim to science. He defined cosmobiology as “a scientific discipline concerned with the possible correlation between the cosmos and organic life”, to be pursued with “statistics, analysis, and computer programming”. The name spread in English with the translations of his books, and later authors carried the method on, among them Eleonora Kimmel, with *Cosmobiology for the 21st Century* (2000), and Aren Ober, with *Midpoint Interpretation Simplified* (second edition, 2009).

## What it gave astrology {#what-it-gave-astrology}

First, a method under its own name. The word cosmobiology became current in English through Ebertin's translators, and it now usually means his school: midpoints, the dial and the eighth-harmonic aspects, without the hypothetical planets. A chart read this way is less a list of placements than a web of pairs, each with a point between them that a third planet can occupy.

Second, a family of aspects. Halving the circle, then halving it again, gives the opposition, the square, and the angles of 45° and 135°. Cosmobiology reads all of them as one family, which turns the semi-square and the sesquiquadrate, minor aspects elsewhere, into working tools.

Third, the habit of seeing the whole circle through one of its divisions. That habit has a far older relative in India, where astrologers use sixteen divisional charts, *varga* meaning ‘part’ or ‘division’ ([Vedic astrology (Jyotish)](/astrology/vedic-astrology-jyotish/)); and a chart drawn for a single division of the circle has become a technique of its own ([Harmonic charts](/astrology/harmonic-charts/)).

And a question it couldn't settle. Ebertin wanted statistics. When astrology was put through controlled tests, the results went against it ([Does astrology work?](/astrology/does-astrology-work/)). My own notebook, which went placement by placement through signs and houses, has nothing to say about midpoints either way ([my testing notes](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

In my readings, cosmobiology stays on this page. Midpoints and the dial aren't among the techniques I use: I read the planets by sign, house and aspect ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). Its medical branch is the part I would never take up. No chart, read with a dial or without one, can tell anyone what will happen to their health ([Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)), and I don't read a chart for that.
