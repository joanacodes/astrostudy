Put a number on each essential dignity and a planet's standing in its sign turns into a sum. That's all a dignity score is: arithmetic on an old table, useful when you can see what went into it, misleading when all you see is the total.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The five essential dignities are set out sign by sign in [the table of dignities](/astrology/table-of-dignities/). A dignity score adds up the ones a planet holds at its exact degree, each at the value [William Lilly](/astrology/william-lilly/) gave it in *Christian Astrology*: 5 for [domicile](/astrology/domicile/), 4 for [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/), 3 for triplicity, 2 for a term, 1 for a face. A planet can hold several at once, so the points stack.

Take the Moon at 15° Taurus. The Moon is exalted in Taurus: 4. Taurus is an earth sign, and the Moon rules the earth signs by night ([triplicity rulers](/astrology/triplicity-rulers/)): 3 more, in a night chart. The middle ten degrees of Taurus are the Moon's own [face](/astrology/faces/): 1. The Moon has no terms, so nothing there. That makes 8 in a night chart and 5 in a day chart, where Venus takes the triplicity: the same Moon at the same degree, three points apart because of the hour.

At the other end, a planet that holds none of the five has nothing to add up, and tradition calls it [peregrine](/astrology/peregrine-planets/). The debilities, [detriment](/astrology/detriment/) and [fall](/astrology/fall/), sit on the other side of the table; Lilly's five values describe only the dignities.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A score is only as steady as its inputs, and four of them move.

- **The degree.** The term changes every few degrees and the face every ten, so two planets in one sign can score differently.
- **The sect.** Triplicity depends on whether the chart is a day or a night chart, so a birth near sunrise or sunset can swing a score by 3.
- **The table of terms.** The Egyptian terms and the ones Lilly took from Ptolemy disagree in places, so the same planet can gain or lose 2 points by the choice ([terms](/astrology/terms-bounds/)).
- **The table of triplicities.** For the water signs, Ptolemy and Lilly give Mars the triplicity by day and by night, where Dorotheus gives it by night only.

The values carry the later tradition's order of importance, own sign first and the face last. The Hellenistic authors before it counted sign, exaltation, triplicity and term as about equal, so a score is one reading of the table, not the table itself.

A score also leaves out everything that comes from the chart rather than the sign. A planet's house, its nearness to the angles, its motion and the aspects it receives are accidental dignity, measured apart: a planet can score well and still stand in a cadent house, squared by a malefic, as tradition put it. Adding the points of every planet at one degree answers a different question, which has its own page: the [almuten](/astrology/almuten/).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

When a program prints a dignity score, I take it apart again. A 5 can be a planet in its own sign, or an exalted planet in its own face, or a triplicity ruler in its own term: three different situations behind one number. The parts tell me how comfortably a function works in the manner of its sign; the total tells me very little I can use. It's the same reason I go through a [chart ruler's condition](/astrology/chart-rulers-condition/) one factor at a time.

The birth time matters more than the arithmetic suggests. The Moon crosses a whole face in less than a day, and the triplicity points change hands at sunrise and sunset, so a rounded time can move the Moon's score by several points ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

And I keep the evidence in proportion. [My notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) put a tick or a cross against each placement by sign and house; it never scored dignities, so I have no figure of my own to set beside Lilly's ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The higher the score, the better the person.”** A score describes how one function fits one sign at one degree. It says nothing about character, worth or luck.
- **“Every program scores the same way.”** Tables of terms and triplicity rulers differ, and so do the totals built on them.
- **“The score includes the house.”** Lilly's five values cover essential dignity only. The house, the angles, motion and aspects are measured separately.
- **“The outer planets have scores too.”** Uranus, Neptune and Pluto were found after Lilly wrote, Uranus 134 years later. Any score they get is a modern addition, not his.
