Up before dawn about a week after a full moon, you’ll find a half moon high in the southern sky, lit on the other side from the evening’s half moon. That is the last quarter: the half moon of early risers, and the month’s second turning point.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Like the first, the last quarter is an instant, as astronomers count it: the moment the Moon is exactly 90° behind the Sun, which is 270° ahead of it. It comes about 7.4 days after the full moon on average, and it closes the [waning gibbous (disseminating) moon](/astrology/waning-gibbous-disseminating-moon/) and opens the last, thinning stretch of the month, the one astrologers who count eight phases call the [balsamic moon](/astrology/balsamic-moon/). Some almanacs call it the third quarter, which says the same thing: three quarters of the cycle gone.

Because it sits 90° behind the Sun, a last quarter moon always falls three signs before the Sun’s, at the same degree. With the Sun at 10° Aries, the last quarter Moon is at 10° Capricorn; with the Sun at 10° Cancer, at 10° Aries. As at the first quarter, the two lights then share a modality and differ in element, but the square now comes from behind.

In Dane Rudhyar’s eight phases, from *The Lunation Cycle* (1967), the last quarter is again a phase rather than a moment: the Moon 270° to 315° ahead of the Sun, or 90° to 45° behind it, about three and three-quarter days. It takes in the waning sextile, the Moon 60° behind the Sun, before the balsamic phase begins.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The last quarter Moon rises around midnight, is highest around sunrise and sets around midday. From the northern hemisphere its left half is lit, from the southern hemisphere its right: the mirror image of the first quarter, and in the opposite part of the day.

The square at its heart is the same aspect as at the first quarter, and the system I built for my readings reads every square the same way: friction that makes two functions adapt to each other. What differs is the light. At the first quarter the lit part is growing and the month is heading for its fullest point; at the last quarter it is shrinking, and the month is heading back towards the dark. The friction comes as something closes rather than as something opens.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Born at a last quarter moon, you have the Sun square the Moon, waning, and [Sun square Moon](/astrology/sun-square-moon/) describes the aspect itself. What the phase adds is a direction of travel. I read the last quarter as a square met from the inside: when what you want and what you need pull apart, you tend to settle it by changing your own priorities first, by reviewing, editing, deciding what can go. That’s a description of a rhythm, never a verdict, and the signs and houses of your Sun and Moon give it its colour.

The time can decide the phase. Near the exact quarter, a few hours move your Moon from Rudhyar’s disseminating phase into his last quarter ([the Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)); [your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) explains how to work out the phase you were born in.

As for the sky’s last quarters, they set the pace of the month for me rather than its events. I keep my attention for the new and full moons, and even those count only on points already in play.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The last quarter is the end of the cycle.”** Two stretches remain: the thinning crescent of the balsamic days, then the new moon that ends the month.
- **“The last quarter looks just like the first.”** It is the other half: from the northern hemisphere, the left side lit instead of the right, and in the morning sky instead of the evening’s.
- **“A last quarter birth means a life of letting go.”** No phase dictates a habit. It describes a way of meeting friction, by reworking priorities, which each chart lives differently.
