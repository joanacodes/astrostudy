Every planet in your chart sits in a sign, and the signs make up the zodiac: a circle of twelve, laid along the path the Sun seems to follow through the year. It's astrology's oldest measuring tape, and most of the confusion about it comes from mixing it up with the stars behind it.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Over a year, the Sun seems to travel once around the sky against the background stars. That path is the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), and the Moon and the planets keep close to it, because their orbits lie close to the plane of the Earth's own. The zodiac is that path, divided into twelve signs of 30° each: [Aries](/astrology/aries/), [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/), [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), [Leo](/astrology/leo/), [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/), [Libra](/astrology/libra/), [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/), [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/), [Capricorn](/astrology/capricorn/), [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/) and [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/), always in that order.

The name comes from the Greek *zōidiakos kyklos*, a circle of little figures or animals, since most of the signs carry the name of a creature. Each sign takes its name from a constellation near which it once lay, and that's where the confusion starts. The constellations are patterns of stars, of very different sizes; as astronomers draw them today, the Sun's path runs through thirteen of them, Ophiuchus included. The signs are something else: twelve equal stretches of the path itself, a measuring scale rather than a picture ([Constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A scale needs a zero, and there are two ways of setting it.

- **From the seasons.** The [tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/) starts Aries at the March equinox, the moment the Sun crosses the celestial equator heading north. Cancer then begins at the June solstice, Libra at the September equinox and Capricorn at the December solstice. This is the zodiac of [Western astrology](/astrology/western-astrology/), and the one I use.
- **From the stars.** The [sidereal zodiac](/astrology/sidereal-zodiac/) ties the signs to a point among the fixed stars. Vedic astrology uses it, and so do some Western astrologers.

The two once lined up. Because the Earth's axis slowly wobbles, the equinox slides back against the stars by about a degree every 72 years ([precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/)), and today the two zodiacs sit about 24° apart.

Inside a sign, positions are counted in degrees from 0 to 29, with minutes and seconds for finer detail ([degrees, minutes and seconds](/astrology/degrees-minutes-and-seconds/)). Each sign also has an element, fire, earth, air or water, and a modality, cardinal, fixed or mutable, and both repeat around the circle: the [four elements](/astrology/four-elements/) take turns in order, three times over, and the [three modalities](/astrology/three-modalities/) four times.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

In a chart, the zodiac answers one question about each planet: in what style? A planet's sign shapes how its function comes out, and the system I built for my readings is strict about this. The sign styles a planet; it never replaces it. Mercury in Aries is still Mercury, still taking in and passing on information, only faster and blunter than Mercury in Taurus.

Every chart holds all twelve signs, whether or not planets sit in them, and in Placidus, the house system I use, each house cusp falls in one of them. So a sign with no planets still has a say: it colours the area of life where it falls. That's why I read the zodiac and the houses together, which takes [the birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/).

Bodies also cross the signs at very different speeds. The Sun spends about a month in each, the Moon about two and a half days, Saturn about two and a half years. So the sign of a fast body is personal, while the sign of a slow one is shared by everyone born over a few years, and I read it more through the house it falls in.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“There's a thirteenth sign, and it changes everyone's.”** The Sun does cross the constellation Ophiuchus, but the signs aren't constellations: they're twelve equal sections of the ecliptic, and they haven't moved ([Ophiuchus and the 13th sign](/astrology/ophiuchus-and-the-13th-sign/)).
- **“The zodiac is the stars you'd see behind the Sun.”** Nobody sees stars behind the Sun by day, and in the tropical zodiac the signs are measured from the equinox, not from the stars.
- **“Your sign is your whole zodiac.”** Your Sun sign is one placement. The Moon, each planet and the rising sign sit in signs of their own.
- **“Some signs are better than others.”** Each sign is a style. None is lucky or doomed, and every one of them is somewhere in your chart.
