One of you wants to put the date in the diary; the other would rather keep it open. Mars opposite Uranus in synastry sets one person's Mars about 180° from the other's Uranus, so committing effort and keeping room to change sit at the two ends of one seesaw.

## What flows {#what-flows}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is a person's capacity to go for something: initiative, assertion, effort, nerve. [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) is their capacity to break the pattern: autonomy, experiment, differentiation, reform. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) makes two functions a polarity, each end showing up the other. In [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/), each end is held by a different person.

One person's Mars in Virgo with the other's Uranus in Pisces, both mutable: careful, practical effort faces a wish to dissolve the plan and start again from a different picture. The Mars person writes the list; the Uranus person wants to tear it up and imagine something else. Seen from outside they can look like opposites, the planner and the free spirit, but each is carrying something the other needs.

What flows is a widening. The Mars person learns that commitment can include room to change; the Uranus person learns that freedom gets more done with someone pushing alongside. Neither stays quite as they were.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

If the Mars is yours, the other can feel elusive: plans stay pencilled in, and your push meets a shrug or a fresh alternative. If the Uranus is yours, the other's push can feel like being steered, and a fixed date like a fence.

It's a seesaw, never enmity, and it sticks when each takes the other's need personally: more pushing invites more distance, and the reverse. Autonomy isn't isolation, and commitment isn't a cage; the pair's work is finding both at once.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Mark what's fixed and what's open. Agree a few commitments that hold whatever comes up, and leave the rest free. If the Mars is yours, ask for one firm date rather than ten; if the Uranus is yours, offer that date before you're asked. Saying "I need this fixed" or "I need this open" plainly, without a story about what the other's need means, does most of the work. Between friends, that can be a standing evening with the rest left loose; in a family, a holiday that's booked and an itinerary that isn't; at work, a deadline that stays and a method that can change; in a couple, room for each to have plans of their own.

Count 15 pairs of people and about one of them will have this opposition, either way round, inside 6°. A few hours make no difference to Uranus and little to Mars, but a lot to the houses: the Uranus person's Uranus might fall in the other's 7th house, the house of partnership, or in their 10th, and those are different stories ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). They're stories I tell only to both people ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

In one chart, [Mars opposite Uranus](/astrology/mars-opposite-uranus/) sets one person's push against their own wish for room. Across two charts, the other angles of this pair work like this:

- **[Mars conjunct Uranus in synastry](/astrology/mars-conjunct-uranus-synastry/):** effort and the new idea on one spot, with no distance between them.
- **[Mars sextile Uranus in synastry](/astrology/mars-sextile-uranus-synastry/):** the two ends close up and cooperate when invited.
- **[Mars square Uranus in synastry](/astrology/mars-square-uranus-synastry/):** a corner instead of a line, where plans keep catching on changes of plan.
- **[Mars trine Uranus in synastry](/astrology/mars-trine-uranus-synastry/):** effort and freedom in one element, rarely needing to balance.
