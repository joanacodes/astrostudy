Ask Mars in Aquarius to follow a rule and it will ask what the rule is for. If the answer is good, it will defend that rule better than anyone. If it isn't, it will quietly start redesigning the system, and it won't stop until the new one works.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is a chart's capacity to act: initiative, effort, assertion, the defence of a limit. In [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/), fixed air, it acts on ideas and for the group. Your drive switches on for a principle, a cause, a better way of doing things, and then it holds: fixed signs don't let go easily, and a conviction formed here can last for decades. Competition takes the form of reform. You'd rather change the game than win it as it stands.

Aquarius answers to two rulers; I read both. [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), its ruler in modern tropical astrology, brings innovation and the urge to free things up, so this Mars likes to experiment and to act where systems have gone stale. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), its traditional ruler, brings structure and persistence: the same Mars can be extremely disciplined in the service of a cause. Where each of those planets sits in your chart shows which side leads. In the tradition, Mars itself has no dignity here.

Mars moves quickly through Aquarius, typically in about 39 days, the shortest usual stay of any sign alongside Pisces, and at most 187 when it turns retrograde there. That brevity makes it the least common Mars sign in the count, found in about 1 person in 15 for births from 1930 to 2009. Anyone born a few weeks before or after you will share it; the house says which group or system you're working on. [Mars in Capricorn](/astrology/mars-in-capricorn/) climbs the ladder that exists, Mars in Aquarius wants to rebuild it, and [Mars in Pisces](/astrology/mars-in-pisces/), next, simply flows round it.

Aquarius is the sign of the experiment, so it's a fair place to say how I test my own work. In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Mars went on the list like any other placement and didn't join the three that held far more often than the rest; of everything on the list, about a third held.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, Mars in Aquarius needs freedom and equality. Friendship often comes first, and desire grows out of shared ideas or causes. You argue from principle rather than from feeling, which can leave a partner sensing that you're debating them rather than talking with them. You resist being managed, and you won't manage anyone else. The relationship that suits this Mars is one between two independent people who choose each other, again and again, rather than one that runs on obligation.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, this Mars is an innovator and a reformer: technology, science, research, campaigning, community organising, any team building something new. You see the flaw in a system before others notice the problem, and you're willing to say so. Arbitrary hierarchies irritate you; a flat team with a shared goal gets your best effort. You can be very persistent about a better idea, which is a gift when you're right and a test of everyone's patience when you're not.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Opposing a rule only because it is one. Holding a position long after the evidence has moved, because fixed air grows attached to its ideas. Fighting for humanity while forgetting the human in front of you. And a cool, detached manner in conflict that can feel, to someone who's upset, like not caring at all.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Choose the rules worth challenging and leave the rest alone. Test your ideas on a small scale first, and change your mind out loud when the results say so. Let feelings into the argument: *this matters to me because…* is no weakness. Work in a group with a clear purpose; team sports and shared projects suit this Mars better than solo contests. And now and then, act for one person rather than for everyone.
