The two lights have swapped homes here. Your Sun stands in Cancer, the sign the Moon rules, and your Moon in Leo, the sign the Sun rules, so each is the other's host. The month had only just begun when you were born, the new moon a day or a few days behind you.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Cardinal water and fixed fire, side by side in the zodiac with the Moon one sign ahead: a [semi-sextile](/astrology/semi-sextile/) by sign, neighbours with very different tempers. The [Sun in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-cancer/) is at its best protecting a small circle and keeping its memories warm; the [Moon in Leo](/astrology/moon-in-leo/) settles when its warmth is returned out loud, when the people who matter say they see it. One light gives quietly; the other hopes, not always quietly, to be thanked.

What makes this pair unique is the rulers. The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) rules Leo, where your Moon is, and the Moon rules Cancer, where your Sun is: a [mutual reception](/astrology/mutual-reception/) by rulership, and the only one of its kind the two lights can form. I read it as two lights that can stand in for each other. When your purpose falters, your feelings steady it; when your feelings flag, your sense of purpose warms them up again. Neither light gains a dignity in the other's sign, but each is welcome there.

You were born in the first five days of a lunar month: the Moon had pulled 1° to 60° ahead of the Sun, often still too close to be seen, a thin crescent at most in the evening west. Most of these births fall in the [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/) phase of the eight-phase count and the latest few in the [waxing crescent](/astrology/waxing-crescent/). The new moon just behind you usually fell in Cancer, and a late Cancer Sun with an early Leo Moon can still make an out-of-sign conjunction. A new moon birth, as I read it, starts purpose and need from one point; here they then go two ways, inward to the home and outward to the stage. [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) explains both phases.

About 1 person in 139 has this pair. Leo holds the Moon for some two and a half days, after Cancer and before Virgo, and on a day it crossed either border the birth time chooses between this page, the [Sun in Cancer, Moon in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-cancer-moon-in-cancer/) and the [Sun in Cancer, Moon in Virgo](/astrology/sun-in-cancer-moon-in-virgo/) ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). The rising sign depends on the same moment, and with two lights this bound together, the houses they occupy show where the exchange between them is lived.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You make a home that people want to be in, and you want them to say so. The Cancer Sun cooks, remembers and protects; the Leo Moon brings warmth, play and a generous heart, and needs to feel chosen rather than simply relied upon. When the appreciation dries up, you may keep giving and quietly dim. A partner who notices out loud, *that was lovely*, *I'm proud of you*, keeps both lights burning. It helps to ask for that, too.

## At work {#at-work}

You often thrive where care and visibility meet: hosting, teaching, running a restaurant or a theatre company, leading a team that feels like a family, any work where looking after people is also a performance of sorts. The Sun wants to protect; the Moon wants its contribution recognised by name. Anonymous, thankless roles drain you, however worthy. Choose places where people say thank you, and mean it.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Pride and sensitivity can make a tender combination. The Cancer Sun is easily hurt; the Leo Moon would rather not show it, so a wound may come out as stiffness, a show of being fine, or a grand gesture that asks to be noticed. Sulking can follow, quietly. What helps is saying the plain thing early, *I felt overlooked*, and remembering that asking for warmth is not the same as begging for it.
