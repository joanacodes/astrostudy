Sun conjunct Aldebaran is the birthday contact of the Bull's red eye: born on about 30 or 31 May, you have the Sun within a degree of the brightest star of Taurus, though the Sun itself is in Gemini. The star is the one Ptolemy called the Torch.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) as direction and vitality: what you are trying to become, and the energy you bring to it. Recognition can matter to it, but status isn't its job. [Aldebaran](/astrology/aldebaran/) is a star Ptolemy described by a single planet, Mars, the planet of effort and initiative. Laid over the Sun, that likeness gives your sense of purpose a Mars-like heat in the tradition's terms: a centre that wants to push, start, get things moving. Its place among the four royal stars adds a royal note on top, which I pass on as legend rather than promise.

Its Arabic name, al-Dabarān, means the follower, because the star seems to trail the Pleiades across the night sky, and the Sun keeps the same order every spring. It reaches the Pleiades around 20 or 21 May and the follower about ten days later ([Alcyone and the Pleiades](/astrology/alcyone-and-the-pleiades/)).

That makes the Sun on Aldebaran a [Sun in Gemini](/astrology/sun-in-gemini/), at around 9° or 10° of the sign, although the star belongs to the constellation Taurus. The tropical zodiac follows the seasons, not the star patterns, and precession has opened a gap of most of a sign between the two ([constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/)). Aldebaran stood at 8°48′ Gemini in 1930, 9°47′ in 2000 and 10°09′ in 2026, so the birthday barely moves within a lifetime.

The royal-star story ties Aldebaran to the spring equinox, and that memory is very old: about 5,000 years ago the March equinox lay close to the star ([precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/)). Today the Sun reaches it some ten weeks after the equinox, as spring turns towards summer.

By then the star has been out of sight for weeks. Its Latin name, Palilicium, comes from the Parilia, the feast of Pales on 21 April, around which Aldebaran sank into the evening twilight. On your birthday the Sun passes five and a half degrees north of it: together in the zodiac, well apart in the sky.

The Torch has four other contacts, each with a character of its own: the [Moon conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/moon-conjunct-aldebaran/), which Indian astronomy turned into a love story; [Venus conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/venus-conjunct-aldebaran/), the rarest of the five; [Mars conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/mars-conjunct-aldebaran/), Ptolemy's Mars-like star on Mars itself; and [Saturn conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-aldebaran/), which belongs to three stretches of births since 1942. None of them comes into my readings: the system I read with leaves stars out, so this page describes the contact and stops there ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). The [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/) page explains how astrologers who use stars count it, within the wider tradition of [fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/).
