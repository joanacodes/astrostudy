One of you says exactly what you mean; the other hears what it might mean. Sun square Neptune between two charts places the Neptune of one person about 90° from the other's Sun, and a clear outline keeps meeting an ideal from the side.

## What flows {#what-flows}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) organises identity and direction, and wants a recognisable outline. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) brings imagination, compassion and idealisation, a sense for what lies beyond the literal. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates the two through friction that drives adaptation. In the language of [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/), each function belongs to a different person, so the friction happens in conversation.

What it makes possible is an ideal that gets truer. Pressed against a real person with real plans, the Neptune person's picture is revised, and the compassion that survives the revision is sturdier than the first enchantment. The Sun person, meanwhile, learns to hear what isn't said, and to speak with more care than they might elsewhere.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Holding the Sun, you may feel seen through a filter: an ideal you can't match on an ordinary day, then a quiet disappointment you can't quite answer. Your directness can seem to wound where you meant only to inform, and plans you state firmly may come back softened into hopes.

Holding Neptune, you may feel the Sun person's certainty run over your sensitivity. Their actual choices don't always fit the person you imagined, and you may give more than you can afford, then feel unseen. Saying what you want can feel unkind, so it goes unsaid.

The result is misreadings rather than lies: a maybe taken for a yes, a no taken for rejection. Neptune isn't deception, and the square isn't failure; the work here is translation.

Neptune's sign is shared across some fourteen years of births, a whole generation's backdrop. The square itself comes from where the Sun person's birthday falls, and that is what makes it personal.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Say important things twice: plainly, then kindly. Check what you heard ("when you said that, did you mean...?"), and write down agreements that involve money or time. The Neptune person can remember that compassion isn't self-erasure, and keep their own needs on the table; the Sun person can be specific about intentions as well as plans. In a couple, that might mean a monthly talk about plans that leaves nothing to be guessed; between friends, saying when a hope isn't shared; in a family, letting a relative be who they are rather than who they were imagined to be; at work, a written brief with clear roles.

About 1 pair of people in 7 has this square, one way round or the other, within 6.8°. I don't read it without both birth times: the Sun's daily step of about a degree rarely changes the square, but the houses show where the misreadings tend to gather, around money, home or work ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). And I look at it only with both people in the conversation ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

Elsewhere in the family, [Sun conjunct Neptune in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-neptune-synastry/) fuses the ideal with the identity, [Sun opposite Neptune in synastry](/astrology/sun-opposite-neptune-synastry/) sets the two face to face, and [Sun sextile Neptune in synastry](/astrology/sun-sextile-neptune-synastry/) and [Sun trine Neptune in synastry](/astrology/sun-trine-neptune-synastry/) let imagination help without rubbing. In one chart, [Sun square Neptune](/astrology/sun-square-neptune/) is one person caught between being definite and longing for more; between two charts, the definiteness and the longing belong to different people, and each can take the other's for a criticism.
