“We're both fire signs, so we should be fine.” Sun-sign compatibility is where most people meet relationship astrology, and it isn't wrong so much as small. It compares one placement in each chart, by sign. Here's how it works, what it can say, and why I never stop there.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Sun-sign compatibility takes two people's Sun signs, the signs the Sun was crossing on their birthdays, and compares them. The twelve signs make 78 possible pairings, and tables of them fill magazines and apps. They rest on three properties of the signs:

- **The element.** Fire, earth, air or water ([the four elements](/astrology/four-elements/)). The same element is said to be easiest; fire with air and earth with water, next.
- **The modality.** Cardinal, fixed or mutable: two signs of the same modality share a way of moving, and often disagree about direction.
- **The distance.** Counted in signs, it gives the aspect between two signs: four apart is a trine by sign, two apart a sextile, three apart a square, six apart an opposition.

That last idea is old. Ptolemy described which signs regard each other, by trine, square, sextile or opposition (*Tetrabiblos*, book I), and astrologers still use sign-to-sign aspects. What's newer is reading two whole people through the Sun alone.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

You need only two birthdays. Find each Sun sign, count the signs between them, and note the elements and modalities. A Sun in Aries and one in Leo are four signs apart, both fire: a trine by sign. A Sun in Aries and one in Cancer are three apart, both cardinal, fire with water: a square by sign.

That is the whole method, and its simplicity is the appeal: no birth time, no chart, a result in a minute. It's also the weakness. Everything that depends on the time, the Moon's exact place, the angles and the houses, is missing, which is why I never read a relationship this way.

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) organises identity and purpose, so the relation between two Suns can sketch how two people's sense of direction meets: whether they tend to want to move at a similar pace, and in similar ways. Read gently, it's a first impression, the kind of thing two people notice in a week.

The rest of a relationship lives elsewhere. How you comfort each other is in the Moons; what you value and find attractive, in Venus; how you go after things and argue, in Mars; how you talk, in Mercury; where you're each most sensitive, in Saturn, Chiron and Lilith. Between two charts those are compared aspect by aspect ([synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/)), and many of the contacts people feel most never show up in a Sun-sign table. About 1 pair of people in 12 has one person's Sun conjunct the other's Venus, for instance, usually with birthdays within two months of each other ([Sun conjunct Venus in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-venus-synastry/)).

## Its limits {#its-limits}

It isn't enough, for four reasons.

- **It's one placement.** Even Ptolemy compared both lights, the Sun and the Moon, when he wrote about marriage (*Tetrabiblos*, book IV).
- **It's one of the weaker strands.** In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the Sun sign held about a third of the time, like the rising sign, Venus and Mercury, while Saturn, Chiron and Lilith held 75 to 90%.
- **Signs aren't degrees.** A Sun at 29° Aries and one at 1° Taurus are 2° apart, a conjunction in all but sign; a Sun at 1° Aries and one at 29° Leo sit in a "trine" of signs nearly 150° apart.
- **It decides nothing.** No pairing of signs makes or breaks a relationship; what two adults do with their differences does.

If you want to look properly at two people, the tool is a full synastry, with both birth times and with the other person's knowledge. It works for any adult bond, not only couples ([charts for friends, colleagues and family](/astrology/charts-for-friends-colleagues-and-family/)). And if the other person can't know, your own chart, read in full, will tell you more about how you love than any table of signs.
