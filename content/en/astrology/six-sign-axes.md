Draw a line from any sign through the centre of the zodiac and it lands on the sign opposite. Six lines are enough for all twelve, and those six lines are the axes. They're the frame of a chart: the houses hang on them, the lunar nodes ride them, and most oppositions run along them.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The six axes are Aries–Libra, Taurus–Scorpio, Gemini–Sagittarius, Cancer–Capricorn, Leo–Aquarius and Virgo–Pisces. Each pair shares a modality: two axes are cardinal, Aries–Libra and Cancer–Capricorn, whose signs begin at the equinoxes and the solstices; two are fixed, Taurus–Scorpio and Leo–Aquarius; two are mutable, Gemini–Sagittarius and Virgo–Pisces.

Each pair also joins two elements that complete each other. On the three active axes, fire faces air; on the three receptive ones, earth faces water, so every [water sign](/astrology/water-signs/) looks across at an earth sign: Cancer at Capricorn, Scorpio at Taurus, Pisces at Virgo.

None of this is a coincidence of naming. It's the arithmetic of the circle. The modalities repeat every three signs, so six signs on is two full turns and the modality comes round again. The elements repeat every four, so six signs on lands two elements further, fire to air, earth to water. And because six is even, the alternation between active and receptive signs keeps both ends on the same side. [Polarity](/astrology/polarity/) gives each axis its question in a line; here I'm interested in what holds the pairs together.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The planets' dignities run along the axes too. Every planet's home sign faces the sign of its detriment, so the rulers sit facing each other ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)). With the traditional rulers, the pattern is strikingly neat: Mars and Venus face each other twice, across Aries–Libra and again across Scorpio–Taurus; Mercury and Jupiter twice, across Gemini–Sagittarius and Virgo–Pisces; and each light faces Saturn, the Moon's Cancer across from Capricorn, the Sun's Leo across from Aquarius. The modern rulers, Pluto for Scorpio, Uranus for Aquarius and Neptune for Pisces, add a second voice at three ends, and I read both.

Exaltation and fall work the same way: a planet's fall is always the sign opposite its exaltation, as Ptolemy lists them. Three axes trade places between two planets. On Aries–Libra, the Sun is exalted in Aries and falls in Libra, Saturn the reverse. On Cancer–Capricorn, Jupiter and Mars swap. On Virgo–Pisces, Mercury and Venus swap. Taurus–Scorpio carries the Moon alone, and Gemini–Sagittarius and Leo–Aquarius hold no exaltation of the seven traditional planets.

Old medicine had its own use for the signs. The Zodiac Man, which goes back at least to the Roman poet Manilius, set them down the body from the head, Aries, to the feet, Pisces, so each axis joins an upper part of that figure to a lower one: Cancer's chest faces Capricorn's knees, Virgo's belly faces Pisces's feet ([the signs and the body, in old medicine](/astrology/signs-and-the-body-in-old-medicine/)). It's history, and no reading of mine turns it into a diagnosis ([Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read an axis as one question with two answers, and I look for it in three places.

- **The houses.** In Placidus, the house system I use, opposite cusps always fall on the same degree of opposite signs, so the 1st and 7th, 2nd and 8th, and so on round to the 6th and 12th, lie along sign axes. The rising sign hands its axis to the line between you and other people ([horizon axis](/astrology/horizon-axis/)). All of this depends on the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
- **The lunar nodes.** They always face each other across one axis, which they share for about eighteen months before moving back to the previous pair ([nodal axis](/astrology/nodal-axis/)).
- **Oppositions.** Two planets in opposite signs pull along an axis ([opposition](/astrology/opposition/)). An opposition that straddles a sign boundary is the exception: an [out-of-sign aspect](/astrology/out-of-sign-aspects/), which belongs to no axis.

When a chart crowds one end of an axis and leaves the other empty, I don't read the empty end as missing. It still has a ruler and a house cusp, and it's usually where the chart asks for balance ([empty houses](/astrology/empty-houses/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Your opposite sign is your perfect match.”** Opposites complement each other, but Sun signs alone say little about two people ([sun-sign compatibility, and why it isn't enough](/astrology/sun-sign-compatibility-and-why-it-isnt-enough/)).
- **“Every axis is about relationships.”** Only Aries–Libra is about you and the other person in that sense. Each axis carries its own question, from what's mine and what's shared to the detail and the whole.
- **“An opposition always runs along an axis.”** An orb can stretch it across a sign boundary: 29° Aries and 1° Scorpio are 182° apart, an opposition between two signs that don't face each other.
- **“The exalted and fallen signs are unrelated.”** A planet's fall is always the sign opposite its exaltation, at the other end of the same axis.
