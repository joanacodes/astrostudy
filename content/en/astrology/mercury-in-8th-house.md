Mercury in the 8th house goes where conversations usually stop. The subjects people lower their voices for, money between partners, sex, debt, grief, the family secret, are the ones this mind wants to understand, calmly and properly.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 8th house](/astrology/8th-house/) holds what is shared rather than owned: joint resources and debts, intimacy and trust, and whatever lies beneath the surface of things. This is a succedent house, steadying what the 7th begins, and its natural sign is Scorpio. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) here makes a researcher. You dig, you follow a question to the bottom, you're drawn to psychology, history, mysteries and anything that has been kept quiet. The sign sets the way you dig: in Gemini by asking many questions lightly, in Virgo by keeping careful records, in Sagittarius by looking for the meaning in a loss, in Aquarius by finding the system behind the secret. In Scorpio, the 8th's own sign, Mercury digs deeper still ([Mercury in Scorpio](/astrology/mercury-in-scorpio/)).

Tradition gave this house death and inheritance. A chart can't predict when or how anyone will die, and I don't read it for that ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)). In an adult's life I read the 8th as the house of trust and of what we share, and with Mercury, a mind that isn't afraid of the conversations others put off.

If your birth time is only approximate, I'd rather say so than guess. A Mercury near the cusp of the 7th or the 9th reads very differently from one in the middle of the 8th, and Placidus, the house system I use, needs the minutes to tell them apart ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

[Mercury in the 7th house](/astrology/mercury-in-7th-house/) reasons with a partner; [Mercury in the 9th house](/astrology/mercury-in-9th-house/) wonders what it all means. In between, the 8th asks what the two of you are really sharing.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You want the real conversation, sooner than most: what each of you is afraid of, what you owe and are owed, what you want and haven't said. Trust grows through honesty about difficult things, and you can hold a confidence for life. You may be the friend people call after bad news, because you can listen to the worst without looking away. Light chat early on may feel like waiting at the door. A partner who isn't frightened by depth, and who lets you take your time sharing your own secrets, gives this Mercury what it needs to trust.

## At work {#at-work}

Research, investigation, psychology, counselling, accounting and finance, insurance, archives, crisis work: any job that deals with what's hidden, shared or hard to discuss. You stay steady in conversations that make others uneasy, and people bring you the problem they haven't told anyone else. Confidential work suits you, and so does any role where the question behind the question is the one that matters.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Going straight to the heavy subject when a lighter one would have built the trust first. Keeping your own thoughts locked away while asking others to open theirs. Turning a partner into a research subject, every mood a puzzle to solve. And a mind so used to depth that it can find the surface, the small talk, the easy evening, hard to enjoy.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Let small talk be a way in rather than a waste of time: trust is often built in the light before it's tested in the dark. Share one thing about yourself before you ask for one in return. Write down what you discover, so the digging has somewhere to go. And choose a few people you can tell anything, so that the secrets you keep for others aren't the only ones you carry.
