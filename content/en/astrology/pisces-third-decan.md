The zodiac ends here, and it ends with Mars. The last ten degrees of Pisces carry Mars's final face, the degree of Venus's exaltation and, in one old list, the name of Hope.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

Mars takes the last face of [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/) after Jupiter ([Pisces, second decan](/astrology/pisces-second-decan/)), and with it the last of the thirty-six ([faces](/astrology/faces/)). It is Mars's sixth face, and because the [Chaldean order](/astrology/chaldean-order/) carries straight on, the next ten degrees are Mars's too ([Aries, first decan](/astrology/aries-first-decan/)): the only place in the zodiac where one planet holds two faces in a row. Any [Mars in Pisces](/astrology/mars-in-pisces/) past 20° of the sign is in its own face.

The modern decans give the last third of Pisces to Scorpio, the remaining water sign, and so to its rulers, Mars in the tradition and Pluto in modern tropical astrology. Mars therefore rules this decan in both systems, inside a sign ruled by Jupiter and Neptune, and I read both pairs. I read the decan as compassion that moves: care turned into effort, at the very end of the zodiac.

The decan also carries the degree tradition gives to Venus's exaltation, the 27th of Pisces ([exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/)). Mars's face and the peak of [Venus in Pisces](/astrology/venus-in-pisces/) sit a few degrees apart, the planet of effort and the planet of what we love, side by side where the circle closes.

## Its image {#its-image}

In the list of Cosmas of Maiuma, the last ten degrees of the zodiac belong to Elpis, the Greek spirit of hope. I couldn't have chosen a better last word, and I didn't choose it: it is simply what the list says. Wallis Budge's Egyptian name for the decan is Ḥer-ȧb-khentu; [Firmicus Maternus](/astrology/firmicus-maternus/) gave Uiu or Aatexbui, and Joseph Scaliger Atembui; the Hermetic lists give Syro in Greek and Piatris in Latin.

In the sky, two corners of the Great Square of Pegasus mark these degrees: [Markab](/astrology/markab/) at 23°50′ Pisces, and [Scheat](/astrology/scheat/) at 29°44′, which precession will carry over into Aries around 2044.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun crosses these degrees from about 11 to 20 March and leaves them at the March equinox, when the zodiac starts again at 0° Aries. For a birthday on 20 or 21 March, the time of birth decides whether the Sun was still in Pisces ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). For a [Sun in Pisces](/astrology/sun-in-pisces/) here, I read Mars's double hold as empathy with a will: the urge to help, followed through.

With these degrees rising, the Ascendant is gone in about 17 minutes in London and 19 in Paris, as quickly as any ten degrees of the zodiac rise at those latitudes ([Pisces rising](/astrology/pisces-rising/)).

Neptune, the modern ruler of Pisces, spent 2020 to January 2026 in these degrees, with loops back at both ends, finishing its passage through its own sign; it will not be back in Pisces for about a century and a half ([Neptune in Pisces](/astrology/neptune-in-pisces/)). Uranus had been there from 2008 to 2011, and before that from 1925 to 1928.
