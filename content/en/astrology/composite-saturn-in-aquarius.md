Separate studies, a shared allotment, friends who have known the pair for thirty years, and an arrangement nobody else quite understands but both people keep faithfully. Composite Saturn in Aquarius builds a relationship on rules it wrote for itself.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Through [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) shows the frame a relationship holds itself to. In [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/), fixed air, that frame is a set of principles the pair has worked out on its own terms: how much room each person keeps, how the bond relates to friends and community, and what it's for beyond the two of you. Commitment, for this Saturn, includes freedom. The promise is to stay together while each of you stays yourself, and it's kept with real loyalty, often for decades.

Aquarius has two rulers, and one of them is Saturn itself. The sign was Saturn's in the tradition, and modern tropical astrology gave it to Uranus after that planet was discovered in 1781 ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)). I read both. Applied to a composite, that puts this Saturn in a sign it rules, comfortable with its own rules, while composite Uranus, wherever it falls, shows how and when the bond rewrites them.

Saturn stayed in Aquarius without a break from January 1962 to March 1964 and from February 1991 to May 1993, so every pair born together within those spans has this composite Saturn. The full stretches, with Saturn crossing back and forth at the edges, run from February 1932 to February 1935, January 1962 to December 1964 and February 1991 to January 1994. The commonest Saturn sign in single birth charts of 1930 to 2009, at about 1 person in 10, Aquarius slips a little among composites: about 1 pair in 11 of the two million random pairs in the count, behind Aries and Pisces. For pairs of different ages, Aquarius is where a Saturn in Capricorn and one in Pisces meet halfway, and the bond's principles may then be neither person's inheritance.

[Saturn in Aquarius](/astrology/saturn-in-aquarius/) belongs, in a birth chart, to someone who keeps the rules of a group and learns which ones serve the people inside it. The composite moves that work into the relationship: the two of you decide which conventions apply to you, and keep the ones you choose. Its neighbours frame it. [Composite Saturn in Capricorn](/astrology/composite-saturn-in-capricorn/), Saturn's other home, builds a relationship like an institution, and [composite Saturn in Pisces](/astrology/composite-saturn-in-pisces/) softens its walls for compassion's sake; Aquarius keeps the walls, but puts them where the pair decides.

What can go wrong is freedom turned into a rule. Independence gets scheduled and closeness rationed, and the bond's principles start to matter more than how either person feels. A pair proud of doing things its own way can find it hard to admit a perfectly conventional need. The circle of friends or the shared cause can also get the bond's best energy, leaving the two of you its leftovers. It helps to check the rules now and then against how you actually feel, to let some closeness happen without a plan, and to keep time for just the two of you inside the wider circle.

My own method ([how I read](/how-i-read/)) works from single birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/); composites, this one included, are described here so that you can weigh what others say about them, and none of it predicts whether a relationship lasts, or in what form.
