Stand facing south and imagine a circle drawn across the sky: up from the east point on your horizon, over the top of your head, and down to the west point. Where that circle crosses the zodiac, a chart can mark the Vertex.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

That circle is the prime vertical: the great circle that passes through the east and west points of the horizon and through the point straight overhead, at right angles to the meridian. Like any great circle, it crosses the ecliptic, the Sun's path and the band of the zodiac, in two places, exactly opposite each other. One is the Vertex, usually written Vx. The other is the [Anti-Vertex](/astrology/anti-vertex/).

You won't find anything in the sky at that spot. The Vertex is a point worked out from the time and place of birth, as the angles are. It belongs with them: the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) is where the zodiac meets the horizon, the Midheaven where it meets the meridian, and the Vertex where it meets the third of those circles fixed to the place you were born.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Because it's fixed to the birthplace, the Vertex turns with the sky, as fast as the Ascendant. A few minutes of birth time move it; an hour can change its sign. Latitude matters too: the same moment gives a different Vertex in a northern city and a southern one. So it needs both an exact time and an exact place, and [without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/), there is no Vertex at all. That's one of the reasons I don't read a chart without one.

Changing house system leaves the Vertex's degree alone but can move it into a different house. In Placidus, the system I use, its house comes from that chart's cusps.

The Vertex has neighbours. The [East Point](/astrology/east-point/) is another point worked out from the birth time, and the [horizon axis](/astrology/horizon-axis/) is the line the Ascendant and Descendant make across the chart. Each marks a place where the zodiac meets something fixed to the moment and place of birth.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Astrological traditions differ about the Vertex. Some astrologers give it real weight and read it for encounters that feel significant: meetings and connections that seem to arrive rather than be sought, people who change the direction of a life. Others don't use it at all. Those who do read it much as they read any point:

- **Its sign and house:** the kind of encounter, and the area of life where it tends to show.
- **Planets close to it:** a planet on the Vertex lends it its colour.
- **Other people's charts:** some astrologers give weight to another person's planet falling on your Vertex, as a sign that the connection feels as if it matters.

My notebook covered the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign and each planet; the Vertex wasn't part of it, so I can't tell you how often it held ([what I tested](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). Whatever the Vertex describes, it describes a feeling of significance, not a promise.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Vertex shows your soulmate.”** No calculated point can prove that a relationship was meant to be, or that one is on its way.
- **“A Vertex in the 7th house means marriage.”** It describes a kind of encounter, read with the rest of the chart. It doesn't predict a wedding.
- **“It's a planet.”** It's a crossing of two circles, worked out from the time and place of birth. There's nothing out there that shines, pulls or moves on its own.
- **“Any birth time will do.”** The Vertex moves as fast as the Ascendant: an approximate time gives an approximate Vertex, sometimes in another sign.
