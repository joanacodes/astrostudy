Most centaurs of myth are remembered for fighting. Hylonome is remembered for love: in Ovid she is the devoted wife of the centaur Cyllarus, and the small icy body named after her circles the Sun out beyond Uranus.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Hylonome is a dark, icy centaur about 70 km across. Its orbit takes about 126 years and runs from 18.9 to 31.4 times the Earth's distance from the Sun. At its closest it comes just outside the orbit of [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), which it grazes without crossing; at its farthest it goes beyond [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/)'s. Uranus governs its nearest point and Neptune its farthest, and for a centaur the arrangement is fairly lasting, with an estimated orbital half-life of about 6.4 million years.

David Jewitt and Jane Luu found it at Mauna Kea Observatory, in Hawaii, on 27 February 1995, and its name was published on 26 July 2000. The German astrologer Robert von Heeren had already drawn it a symbol in the late 1990s, from [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/)'s key glyph, with a Greek capital upsilon, the first letter of her name in Greek, where the K had been.

Her husband has a body of his own. 52975 Cyllarus, a very red centaur about 62 km across, was found at Kitt Peak, Arizona, in 1998, and goes round the Sun in about 133 years, on a path of its own.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Hylonome's story is told in book 12 of Ovid's *Metamorphoses*, inside old Nestor's account of the battle between the Lapiths and the centaurs at the wedding of Pirithous, the fight that [Asbolus](/astrology/asbolus/) had warned against. She and Cyllarus are the one loving couple in a scene of slaughter, a relationship one modern scholar calls ideally mutual. In the battle Cyllarus, the handsome centaur, was struck by a spear, and Hylonome, heartbroken, took her own life to be with him.

Readers of Ovid have seen in the pair a study of mixed natures, as centaurs are: human and animal, nature and culture, love and war, the love poem set inside the epic.

The body that carries her name moves too slowly to go even once round the zodiac in a human lifetime, so its sign is shared by whole runs of birth years; its house depends on the minute of birth ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and it singles someone out only on the rare occasions it sits right on a personal planet or an angle. A story of love and grief comes with the name, and nothing tested comes with the story.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

A myth about a devoted couple invites one use above all: looking for Hylonome between two people's charts. I don't. When I compare two charts in my [synastry readings](/synastry/), a contact counts only if at least one personal body is in it, and of all the centaurs, my system knows only Chiron. Nor did the notebook in which I checked the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign, each planet, Chiron and Lilith ever hold a line for her, or for Cyllarus ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

Her story ends in grief and a death by her own hand, and no chart can say that anyone will lose a partner, or what grief will do to them. ## Common myths {#myths}

- **“All the centaurs of myth were male.”** Ovid's Hylonome is a female centaur, and her story is a tender scene inside his battle of the centaurs.
- **“Hylonome and Cyllarus share an orbit.”** They are separate bodies: Hylonome takes about 126 years to go round the Sun, Cyllarus about 133.
- **“Hylonome crosses the orbit of Uranus.”** It crosses Neptune's. It only grazes Uranus's, from outside.
