Saturn conjunct Algol is a contact of school years rather than birthdays: whole classes born between June 1941 and April 1942, or between June 2000 and March 2001, share it, along with those born in a few weeks of May 1971. About 1 person in 191 born between 1930 and 2009 has it.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), as I read it, is the planet of long work: the responsibilities you shoulder, the limits you learn to use, and the structures you build slowly enough to last. [Algol](/astrology/algol/) is the darkest star in the tradition's catalogue, Medusa's head in Perseus, counted among the unluckiest of all.

Medieval magic gave it a surprising owner. In the Behenian list, fifteen stars each tied to planets, a gem and a plant, Algol belongs to Saturn and Jupiter, and a planet within six degrees of one of its own stars was thought to draw on it most strongly. By that scheme, Saturn on Algol is a planet sitting on a star of its own. I mention it for what it shows: one book's curse was another book's source of power, which is reason enough to take neither as a forecast.

All three stretches fall in the closing degrees of Taurus ([Saturn in Taurus](/astrology/saturn-in-taurus/)), a placement shared by those born in roughly 1939–42, 1969–72 or 1998–2001; Algol picks out a few months inside those years. Saturn in Taurus builds security slowly and keeps what has proved its worth.

Each pass ran its own course ([Saturn retrograde](/astrology/saturn-retrograde/)). In 1941 Saturn reached the degree on 11 July, recrossed it in retrograde motion on 15 November, and left it behind for good on 31 March 1942. In 1971 it went straight across, on 17 May, during the very days the Sun passes the star each year, and births of 16 and 17 May 1971 have the Sun and Saturn together on Algol's degree. In 2000 it crossed on 27 June and again, retrograde, on 7 December, turned direct on 25 January 2001 about two degrees on the far side, and made its final crossing on 15 March. The next pass comes in 2030, after Saturn turns back in September 2029 a degree and a half short of the star.

On every pass about 24° of sky lay between planet and star, since in this part of the zodiac Saturn runs a little south of the ecliptic while Algol stands far to the north.

Among everything in my notebook, Saturn sits with Chiron and Lilith in the small group that held 75 to 90% of the time ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)); Algol was never tested, and no star appears in the charts I read ([fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/)). So I'd read the planet rather than the star: Saturn in Taurus, in its house, with its aspects. Saturn is the planet of time, and even it can't tell the length of a life; no chart can ([Can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)).

Saturn's is the contact that whole cohorts share; the others belong to a day or an hour. The [Sun conjunct Algol](/astrology/sun-conjunct-algol/) marks two birthdays a year, the [Moon conjunct Algol](/astrology/moon-conjunct-algol/) a few hours a month, and [Venus conjunct Algol](/astrology/venus-conjunct-algol/) and [Mars conjunct Algol](/astrology/mars-conjunct-algol/) the day or few days of each pass. How the tradition counts all five is laid out in [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/).
