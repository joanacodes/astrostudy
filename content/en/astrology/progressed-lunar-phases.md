Your birth chart caught the Moon at one moment of its month. The progressed chart lets that month play on, one day for each year, so the waxing and waning you could watch in four weeks of sky unfolds over about thirty years of your life.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The [progressed Sun](/astrology/progressed-sun/) moves about a degree a year, the [progressed Moon](/astrology/progressed-moon/) about thirteen. The Moon gains on the Sun by roughly twelve degrees a year and laps it every 29¼ to 29¾ years: the length of the lunar month that followed your birth, counted in years instead of days. Each lap begins at a [progressed new moon](/astrology/progressed-new-moon/) and reaches a progressed full moon about halfway round.

Most astrologers who use the cycle cut it into eight phases of 45°, with the names Dane Rudhyar gave them in *The Lunation Cycle* (1967). Each lasts as long, in years, as the Moon took in days to gain those 45° after your birth: about 3¾ years on average, nearer 3¼ when the Moon was moving fast and 4¼ when it was slow.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Your progressed cycle doesn't start at zero. It carries on from the phase you were born in ([Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/)), so two people of the same age can be in opposite phases, one beginning a chapter and the other closing one.

A program gives the progressed Sun and Moon. Count from the Sun to the Moon in the order of the signs and see which 45° step the distance falls in. There's also a way to see every change in one go: in an ephemeris, find the days after your birth when the real Moon stood 45°, 90°, 135° and so on ahead of the Sun. The number of days after birth is your age, in years, when each progressed phase began.

The birth time barely moves this timing: an hour's error shifts a phase change by about two weeks. It does decide which houses the progressed new and full moons fall in, and in [secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/), as in a birth chart, the houses are where I look for the area of life a chapter is about ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

The phases give the backdrop against which everything else in the progressions plays. I read them as the stages of a chapter, from the inside:

- **[New moon](/astrology/new-moon/)** (0°–45°): something starts before you can name it.
- **[Waxing crescent](/astrology/waxing-crescent/)** (45°–90°): it finds its feet, and the old ways pull back at it.
- **[First quarter moon](/astrology/first-quarter-moon/)** (90°–135°): it has to act, and meets resistance doing so.
- **[Waxing gibbous moon](/astrology/waxing-gibbous-moon/)** (135°–180°): refining, correcting, getting it right.
- **[Full moon](/astrology/full-moon/)** (180°–225°): what you've built is in full view, to you and to others.
- **[Waning gibbous (disseminating) moon](/astrology/waning-gibbous-disseminating-moon/)** (225°–270°): passing on what you learned.
- **[Last quarter moon](/astrology/last-quarter-moon/)** (270°–315°): turning away from forms that no longer fit.
- **[Balsamic moon](/astrology/balsamic-moon/)** (315°–360°): clearing the ground before the next start.

The same aspect lands differently against each backdrop. A progressed Venus meeting your natal Sun in a first-quarter phase can be part of something you're pushing to build; in a balsamic phase, part of what you're learning to let rest.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

The cuts at every 45° are a convention, and so are the eight names. Some astrologers use only the four quarters; others read the phases as gradual shades rather than steps. A phase boundary is a line drawn on a slope, and I don't treat the year it's crossed as a turning point in itself.

A phase lasts years and describes a rhythm, not a result. The full phase doesn't promise that something will come to fruition, and the balsamic phase doesn't mean something will be lost. They're the tone of a long stretch, which you then fill.

They also say nothing on their own about where in life a chapter runs. That comes from the signs and houses the progressed new and full moons fall in, and from the rest of the chart they meet.
