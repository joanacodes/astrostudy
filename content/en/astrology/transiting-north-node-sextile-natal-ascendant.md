A sextile to your Ascendant can only be timed from an exact birth time, because the Ascendant is the degree rising at the minute you were born ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Twice a round, about three years before the North Node reaches that degree and three years after, it stands two signs away, and for a month or so a new way of beginning is offered rather than demanded.

## What changes {#what-changes}

Your [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) is the orientation you start from: how you approach a person, a place, a task. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) is the capacity you are building by repetition. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) relates them through an opportunity that needs a response, so under this [transit](/astrology/transits/) the less practised side of you finds an easy way in: an introduction that goes well, an invitation to start something, a first step that suits you better than your usual one.

The whole axis takes part. The [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) trines your Ascendant from the far side, and the North Node trines your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), so the nodes and your horizon draw the figure astrologers call a [mystic rectangle](/astrology/mystic-rectangle/), the same one the node's trine draws, with the ends swapped: here the familiar end is the one at ease with your rising degree. Your old manner stays comfortable in these weeks, which makes the new opening easy to try and just as easy to leave.

In Placidus, the house system I use, one sextile usually comes while the node is crossing your 3rd or 2nd house on its way up to your Ascendant ([North Node transiting the 3rd house](/astrology/north-node-transit-3rd-house/)), the other while it crosses your 11th or 12th after leaving it ([North Node transiting the 11th house](/astrology/north-node-transit-11th-house/)). From July 2026 to March 2028, with the node in Aquarius, it is the turn of Sagittarius rising, before the crossing, and of Aries rising, after it.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

A sextile to the rising degree usually lasts about a month, counted from 1½° before exact to 1° after. In an eclipse season, while the Sun is near the nodes, it barely moves, and the sextile lasts three months or more; about one pass in 25 brings three exact hits, and the season's eclipses fall two or four signs from your Ascendant. My forecast lists the node only when an end of the axis sits on a point of your chart, so this sextile isn't in it; I mention it when someone asks about those weeks.

An uncertain time spoils the dates quickly. Half an hour of doubt about your birth leaves the Ascendant uncertain by about seven and a half degrees on average, and the node takes some four and a half months to cover that much ground.

Two sextiles a round make eight or nine by 80. The sextile before the crossing leads to [Transiting North Node conjunct natal Ascendant](/astrology/transiting-north-node-conjunct-natal-ascendant/); the one after leads on to [Transiting North Node square natal Ascendant](/astrology/transiting-north-node-square-natal-ascendant/), [Transiting North Node trine natal Ascendant](/astrology/transiting-north-node-trine-natal-ascendant/) and, nine years from the crossing, [Transiting North Node opposite natal Ascendant](/astrology/transiting-north-node-opposite-natal-ascendant/).

## What helps {#what-helps}

Accept one invitation to begin something you would normally watch from the side.

Try a new opening line: a different way of introducing yourself, used three times this month.

Let your old manner make the first contact and the newer capacity carry the conversation. The sextile helps when the two take turns.
