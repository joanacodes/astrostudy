The first full moon after the December solstice falls in Cancer, opposite a Capricorn Sun, and rides high through the long northern nights. You were born close to a full moon, most often that one. I read the pair as the hearth facing the world: a life built outward, and needs that gather everyone in.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

The signs face each other across the zodiac, both cardinal, earth opposite water: an opposition by sign, which I read as two halves asking to be balanced rather than as enemies. The [Sun in Capricorn](/astrology/sun-in-capricorn/) organises a life around responsibility and long work in the world; the [Moon in Cancer](/astrology/moon-in-cancer/), in its own sign, steadies itself by gathering its people in: the Sunday meal, the box of old letters, the call that checks everyone got home. Both signs start things, one by building and one by protecting, so you may find yourself running two households, the one at work and the one at home.

Few axes carry as many dignities. The Moon rules Cancer and is in its [domicile](/astrology/domicile/) there, with its [detriment](/astrology/detriment/) in Capricorn, your Sun's sign; [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), your Sun's ruler, has its detriment in Cancer. So each light's ruler is ill at ease in the other's sign: the Sun's discipline is out of place among the Moon's feelings, and the Moon's tenderness out of place in the Sun's office. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) is exalted on the Moon's side and falls on the Sun's; [Mars](/astrology/mars/) does the reverse. Saturn matters most to me here, and not only as a ruler: in my notebook it was among [the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/) far more often than the rest, 75 to 90% of the time, so its sign and house are where I start.

Your birth came no more than two and a half days from the full moon. The Moon was 151° to 209° from the Sun, twelve and a half to seventeen days after the new moon, so the nearest full moon was most often the [full moon in Cancer](/astrology/full-moon-in-cancer/), the first after the December solstice. The births split evenly between the [waxing gibbous moon](/astrology/waxing-gibbous-moon/) and the [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/) phase, and near 180° the chart has a [Sun opposite Moon](/astrology/sun-opposite-moon/). A full-moon birth, to me, shows both halves of a life in full light at once; [your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) explains which side of the full you fell.

About 1 person in 151 has this pair. On a birthday when the Moon came into Cancer from Gemini, or left it for Leo, only your birth time settles whether this page is yours, or the [Sun in Capricorn, Moon in Gemini](/astrology/sun-in-capricorn-moon-in-gemini/), or the [Sun in Capricorn, Moon in Leo](/astrology/sun-in-capricorn-moon-in-leo/) ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). It also fixes the rising sign and the houses, and on this axis the houses often show the two places that compete for you.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You love by providing and protecting, and you take family seriously in whatever form yours takes. The Cancer Moon remembers birthdays and moods; the Capricorn Sun makes sure the roof is sound. A full-moon pair often meets its other half in a partner: you may choose someone who does the caring while you do the providing, or the reverse, and the arrangement works until one of you wants to swap. Saying out loud what each of you carries keeps the balance fair.

## At work {#at-work}

Institutions that look after people suit this pair: schools, housing, care services, family firms, any organisation that has to be both well run and kind. The Sun brings structure and stamina; the Moon brings loyalty to the team and an instinct for who needs support. You can be fiercely protective of the people you manage. The risk is carrying the workplace home, or treating home as one more workplace.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The opposition can feel like two sets of obligations that never sleep. Work calls when home needs you, home calls when work does, and you may feel you're failing both while doing far more than your share of each. Feelings can be filed under duty for months. What helps is separating the two on purpose: hours that belong to the Moon's people and can't be booked over, and work that ends at a set time, so neither half of the full moon has to eclipse the other.
