For most of its history astrology looked outward, at what would happen. In the twentieth century one school turned it inward, and asked what a chart says about the mind that lives it. Psychological astrology is that turn.

## What it was {#what-it-was}

It starts with [Carl Jung](/astrology/carl-jung/), the Swiss psychiatrist who lived from 1875 to 1961. In June 1911 he wrote to Freud that he was making horoscopic calculations “in order to find a clue to the core of psychological truth”. Studying his patients' dreams and paintings, Jung found recurring mythical figures, the archetypes, and saw in the gods of the planets and signs the same images, projected onto the sky from what he called the collective unconscious. “Astrology represents the sum of all the psychological knowledge of antiquity,” he wrote.

With the physicist Wolfgang Pauli he developed the idea of synchronicity, and he held that “whatever is born or done at this particular moment of time, has the quality of this moment of time”. The links between the planets at birth and a person's development were, for him, acausal: parallels in time, not effects caused by the planets. He also ran experiments with astrology, which Pauli objected to ([Jung’s marriage experiment](/astrology/jungs-marriage-experiment/)).

Astrologers took up his ideas, and in the late twentieth century psychological astrology became an established school. Stephen Arroyo's *Astrology, Psychology and the Four Elements* (1975) grew out of a thesis that won the Astrological Association's prize in 1973, and recast the elements in terms of psychological energy. [Liz Greene](/astrology/liz-greene/)'s *Saturn: A New Look at an Old Devil* (1976) went back to the planet tradition feared most. In 1983 Liz Greene and Howard Sasportas, a psychosynthesis psychotherapist, founded the Centre for Psychological Astrology in London; their joint titles show the programme, *The Development of the Personality* (1987) and *Dynamics of the Unconscious* (1988). From 1985 Greene also worked with the Swiss company Astrodienst on computer-written chart interpretations, and their *Psychological Horoscope Analysis* appeared in 1987, carrying the approach far beyond the consulting room. In Switzerland, Bruno and Louise Huber had founded their own school in 1962, drawing on Roberto Assagioli's psychosynthesis, and in the United States Noel Tyl paired astrological symbols with Maslow's hierarchy of needs.

It also drew criticism, chiefly that it invites confirmation bias: noticing what fits a chart and passing over what doesn't. When Shawn Carlson ran his double-blind test in 1985, several of the astrologers taking part were strongly influenced by Jung, and the astrologers did no better than chance ([Carlson’s double-blind test (1985)](/astrology/carlsons-double-blind-test-1985/)).

## What it gave astrology {#what-it-gave-astrology}

A language for the inner life. Signs became styles of being, the [four elements](/astrology/four-elements/) became kinds of energy, and a planet became a drive within the self rather than an agent of fate.

A second look at the hard planets. Greene's title says it: an old devil, looked at again. I read [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) as a timetable, not a wall, and in my own notebook it was one of the three placements, with Chiron and Lilith, that held far more often than the rest ([the three that held: Saturn, Chiron and Lilith](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)).

And a boundary I keep firmly. A chart can help someone think about their life; it is not a diagnosis and it is not therapy ([astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/)). When what a person carries is psychological or medical, the right door isn't mine, and I say so ([when to see a professional instead](/astrology/when-to-see-a-professional-instead/)).

One of the first astrologers to bring Jung into astrology was the French-born composer Dane Rudhyar, who called his version by another name: [humanistic astrology](/astrology/humanistic-astrology/). The ground had been prepared by Alan Leo's turn to character ([the Theosophical revival](/astrology/theosophical-revival/)).
