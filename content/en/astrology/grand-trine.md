Draw lines between three planets about 120° apart and an equilateral triangle appears across the chart: a grand trine. It's the pattern people hope to find, and it deserves the hope, as long as nobody expects it to do the work by itself.

## What it looks like {#what-it-looks-like}

A grand trine is three [trines](/astrology/trine/) closed into a loop: the first planet trine the second, the second trine the third, the third trine the first. Each corner sits a third of the circle from the other two. Signs four apart share an element, so the three planets usually fall in the three signs of one element, Aries, Leo and Sagittarius for fire, or Cancer, Scorpio and Pisces for water, and the pattern is named after it: a fire grand trine, an earth one ([the four elements](/astrology/four-elements/)). Near the edge of a sign, one corner can slip into the next sign and another element. The circuit then runs between two elements instead of one: an [out-of-sign aspect](/astrology/out-of-sign-aspects/) inside the figure, which takes a little of the smoothness out of it.

The triangle is closed, and that's its character. Each planet receives from the other two and passes it on; nothing in the figure points outside itself. The [grand cross](/astrology/grand-cross/) is its opposite in feel: four planets pulling against each other on every side, friction where the triangle has flow. A grand trine can also hold more than three planets. When one corner is a [stellium](/astrology/stellium/), the whole group joins the circuit.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Among the ten planets, with each trine counted within 8° when the Sun or Moon is a corner and within 6° otherwise, a grand trine turns up in about 1 chart in 10 for births from 1930 to 2009. That's the count behind this site, and it leaves things out: the Ascendant and Midheaven, Chiron, Lilith and the nodes can all close a triangle the planets leave open, and a couple of degrees more or less of orb changes the figure a great deal.

The system I built for my readings describes the trine as flow that keeps each function itself: the two planets help each other without merging into one. Three of them in a loop multiply that. I read a grand trine as a part of you that works without strain, often so smoothly that you don't count it as a gift. But ease isn't a promise that things will turn out well, and flow isn't the same as standing still. The planets decide what circulates: a triangle of the Moon, Venus and Neptune moves feeling around; one of Mercury, Saturn and Uranus, ideas and structure. When Saturn is a corner, I give the figure more time. Saturn was one of the three placements that held 75 to 90% of the time in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), and an easy Saturn is often the steadiness a person leans on without naming it.

The houses of the three corners decide which parts of your life the circuit links, and they need the birth time. So does any triangle that runs through the Moon, which moves about a sign every two and a half days: a Moon corner found from the date alone may not survive the hour. An angle can complete a figure too, and only the time places the angles. A triangle without its houses links three functions but no parts of your life, so I [wait for the time before reading one](/blog/unknown-birth-time/).

## What helps {#what-helps}

- **Give it something to carry.** A grand trine is at its best with a task: a craft, a practice, a responsibility that uses its element. Left alone, it tends to circle.
- **Look for what sets it moving.** A planet opposite one corner turns the triangle into a [kite](/astrology/kite/) and gives it a direction; that planet is also sextile the other two corners, a [minor grand trine](/astrology/minor-grand-trine/) inside the larger one. A square to a corner adds the pressure the figure lacks.
- **Name the element.** Knowing whether your ease is practical, intellectual, emotional or fiery helps you notice where it already works.
- **Don't wait to be pushed.** Nothing in this figure will insist. Choosing to use it is the whole of the work.

Talent that circulates in a closed loop is still talent; the art is to open the loop a little, so the ease reaches something you care about.
