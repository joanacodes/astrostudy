If your birthday sits at the very edge of your sign's dates, you've probably been told you're “on the cusp”, and perhaps that you're a bit of both. I'll be plain about the first part and gentle about the second: you're one sign, and the feeling of being both often has a real source in your chart.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

In everyday astrology, born on the cusp means born near the change from one sign to the next: the last day or so of one sign's dates, or the first of the next. The dates are approximate, because the Sun enters each sign at a precise moment that shifts from year to year and with the time zone ([sign dates, and why they move](/astrology/sign-dates-and-why-they-move/)). Near a change, a list of dates can't tell you which side you're on.

The word has a second meaning in astrology: a cusp is also the line where a house begins. That one belongs to the houses ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)) and has nothing to do with your birthday.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) moves about a degree a day along the zodiac, and each sign is 30° wide. At any instant it stands at one degree of one sign, 29° Scorpio, say, or 0° Sagittarius, never both. So at the minute you were born, the Sun was in one sign or the other. The border between two signs is a line, not a strip.

Three things decide which side of it you're on:

- **The date**, which gets you to within a day or so of the change.
- **The time**, which places your birth before or after the exact moment. A chart program converts your local time, with its time zone and any daylight saving, into Universal Time ([time zones and daylight saving in charts](/astrology/time-zones-and-daylight-saving-in-charts/)).
- **The place**, which tells the program what the clocks were set to.

If you don't know your time and the Sun changed sign on the day you were born, there's no honest way to choose. [What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/) says what else the time decides, and [how to find your birth time](/astrology/how-to-find-your-birth-time/) where to look for it.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Once the chart is calculated, I read the Sun in the sign it's in. A Sun at 29° belongs to its sign as fully as one at 15°.

The feeling of being both usually comes from somewhere else in the chart. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is never more than about 28° from the Sun and [Venus](/astrology/venus/) never more than about 47°, so either may sit in the neighbouring sign, and the Moon or the rising sign can be there too. When someone born at a change tells me they recognise themselves in the next sign, that's where I look first.

The rulers change with the sign, which is one more reason the exact sign matters. Cross from Capricorn into Aquarius and the traditional ruler stays Saturn, with Uranus joining as the modern one; cross from Aquarius into Pisces and both change, Uranus and Saturn giving way to Neptune and Jupiter ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)). A day's step for the Sun can mean a different planet to follow through the chart.

That's [how I read](/how-i-read/): the chart as calculated, with the planets where they actually were, rather than the label on a list.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Born on the cusp, you're half one sign and half the other.”** The Sun is in one sign at any moment. Planets in the neighbouring sign can make you feel like both, and they're worth reading for what they are.
- **“The cusp lasts several days either side.”** Only the day of the change, give or take one, is in doubt, because the moment moves from year to year. A few days away, the date settles it.
- **“On the cusp, you can pick the sign you prefer.”** Your chart has already picked: the Sun's degree is a matter of calculation. What you can do is read the other planets in the sign you feel drawn to.
- **“The last degree of a sign is a weaker version of it.”** Being near the edge changes how much the birth time matters, not how much of the sign is yours.
