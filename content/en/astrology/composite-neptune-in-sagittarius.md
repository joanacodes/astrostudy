Sagittarius always wants to know what lies over the next hill. A composite Neptune there makes that question the relationship's own: the two of you dream of meaning, and of finding it together.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) gives a relationship a chart of its own, built from the midpoints of two birth charts, and reads it as a third thing with its own character. Its [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) is that character's ideal: what the bond imagines, how far its compassion reaches, what it feels part of. [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/), mutable fire ruled by [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/), sends the ideal outward. A pair with this Neptune tends to travel as a kind of devotion, argue about the big questions late into the night, take up a faith, a philosophy or a cause together, and treat a stranger's way of seeing the world as something to learn from. Its compassion is broad-minded; its imagination lives on the horizon.

Jupiter rules Sagittarius, and before Neptune was found the tradition gave Jupiter Pisces too, so here Neptune stands in a sign of the planet that once held Pisces. Both planets widen whatever they touch: in a composite, Jupiter's sign and house show where the bond grows, and with Neptune in Sagittarius the growth and the dream tend to point the same way, outwards.

Two people born between January 1970 and November 1984 both have [Neptune in Sagittarius](/astrology/neptune-in-sagittarius/), and their composite has it too; in two million pairs drawn at random from the births of 1930 to 2009, about 1 in 4 did. Because Neptune moves so slowly and so evenly, a composite Neptune lands close to where Neptune stood on the day halfway between the two births. Born in June 1965 and June 1985, one partner has Neptune at about 18° Scorpio and the other at about 2° Capricorn; the midpoint is about 10° Sagittarius, and on the day halfway between, in June 1975, Neptune stood at about 10° Sagittarius. That day is the one [the Davison chart](/astrology/davison-chart/) is cast for, so for Neptune the two relationship charts nearly agree, always within about 4°.

On either side, [composite Neptune in Scorpio](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-scorpio/), the commonest placement, looks for meaning in depth, and [composite Neptune in Capricorn](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-capricorn/) in building something to last; Sagittarius looks for it in distance.

The difficulty is a horizon that keeps moving. A bond that idealises the search can find the place it has reached less interesting than the next one, treat ordinary life as a waiting room for the next journey, or hold a shared belief so warmly that questioning it feels like disloyalty. Meaning also lives close to home: in the kitchen, at the bus stop, in the town where the two of you actually live.

For a pair of one generation, the sign says most about when they were born, and the composite house, which needs both birth times, says where in their shared life the search takes place ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Composites are a school of thought I explain here without following it in my own readings; those who teach it read them beside both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and never to decide whether a relationship will last.
