Pluto doesn't visit; it moves in. Once it crosses your Ascendant, the planet of depth and power begins a stay that usually outlasts a decade, and it works on the self you lead with until that self fits you better.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) goes to the bottom of things: what you hold on to, what you'd rather not look at, where power sits and who has it. The [1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) is the face and manner you meet the world with, and the way you begin anything. With Pluto there, the outer layer starts to answer to what lies underneath. A manner you adopted to be acceptable can begin to feel like a costume. You may want more say in how you're seen, and less patience with people who decided long ago who you were. Others often sense the shift before you can name it: you come across as more intent, harder to read, and harder to talk out of things.

In Placidus, the house system I use, the 1st starts at the Ascendant, so the stay opens with Pluto on your rising degree ([transiting Pluto conjunct natal Ascendant](/astrology/transiting-pluto-conjunct-natal-ascendant/)), the stretch people tend to remember. Any planet in your natal 1st meets Pluto later, sometimes many years later: a Moon there is asked what it has been guarding, a Venus what it gives up in order to be liked.

Books often promise a rebirth here, or warn of a struggle for control. I don't read either as an event waiting for you. A [transit](/astrology/transits/) brings out what your chart already holds, and Pluto does it at the pace of a glacier. Nothing in this stay forecasts harm, illness or anyone gaining power over you; it describes a long season in which being yourself asks for more honesty and less armour.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Pluto is the slowest body I read, and its stays show it. In Placidus charts drawn at London's latitude, it keeps to a house for about twenty years across all twelve, with most stays between about 10 and 37. The 1st is quicker in the middle, about 14 years, yet its long end stretches to about 46, because at that latitude the 1st can be narrow or enormous depending on the hour of birth, and Pluto lingers in some signs for decades. At New York's latitude the 1st holds Pluto for about 17 years in the middle, most often between about 12 and 40.

With stays this long, Pluto crosses only a few of your houses in a lifetime, and the 1st may or may not be one of them. Where Pluto sat when you were born decides that, and your birth time places the Ascendant it has to reach; without the time I can't say whether or when this begins, so I don't read the chart at all ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Before this stay comes Pluto's long passage through your 12th, all of it below the surface ([Pluto transiting the 12th house](/astrology/pluto-transit-12th-house/)); after it, the turn towards what you own and value ([Pluto transiting the 2nd house](/astrology/pluto-transit-2nd-house/)).

Pluto first entered Aquarius in March 2023, left Capricorn for the last time in November 2024, and is still in Aquarius at the end of 2039, where my count stops. With Aquarius rising, then, the crossing is under way or on its way, and your rising degree gives the year. Capricorn rising had its turn between January 2008 and November 2024. Pisces rising waits until after 2039, and the signs from Aries to Sagittarius lie further off than these figures reach.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Let the outer layer change at its own speed. Pluto in the 1st doesn't ask for a dramatic makeover; it asks you to stop maintaining whatever no longer feels true, one habit at a time.

Notice where power runs through your first impressions: whom you defer to on sight, and who defers to you. The stay is long enough to change both, quietly.

Give the intensity somewhere to go. Work that demands depth, a practice you can go all the way into, one person you can speak to without editing yourself: Pluto here does better with an outlet than with a lid. And if what surfaces feels heavy, someone trained to listen is a better companion for it than any chart.
