Procyon is the star of the Little Dog, and its Greek name says only that it comes before the dog: it rises ahead of Sirius. It stands at 26°08′ Cancer in 2026, the second dog star in the sign of Cancer and one of the Sun's nearest neighbours.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

At magnitude 0.38, α Canis Minoris is usually the eighth-brightest star of the night sky. Its light takes 11.46 years to reach us, and a faint white dwarf companion, Procyon B, circles it every 40.84 years. With [Sirius](/astrology/sirius/) and Betelgeuse it makes the Winter Triangle, and it is one of the six stars of the larger Winter Hexagon. Sirius lies further east, yet from most northern latitudes Procyon clears the horizon first, because it sits further north: hence the name.

For every birth in living memory it has sat in the last third of [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/): 24°48′ in 1930, 25°46′ in 2000, 26°08′ in 2026. Its latitude is 16° south of the ecliptic: well clear of the planets in the sky, though nowhere near as far as [Alkaid](/astrology/alkaid/), the star at the end of the Plough's handle, more than fifty degrees north.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

Ptolemy gives Procyon a nature of [Mars](/astrology/mars/) and [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), in his terms a mixture of the two planets' qualities. Vivian Robson's *The Fixed Stars and Constellations in Astrology* (1923) gives it a mixed name: mostly unfortunate, in the tradition's word, though sometimes bringing wealth, and given to sudden success followed by a fall. Medieval magic set the little dog's star among the fifteen Behenian stars, with agate for its stone and the water crowfoot for its plant.

Older stories are kinder to it. The Babylonians knew Procyon as Nangar, the Carpenter, an aspect of Marduk, who built and ordered the heavens. In Greek myth it is Maera, the hound of Erigone, daughter of Icarius of Athens. Arabic star lore paired the two dog stars as women, Procyon al-ghumaisa’, the bleary-eyed, against Sirius, the teary-eyed, and medieval astrolabes in England and western Europe wrote its name as Algomeyza.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

About 17–18 July, the Sun arrives on Procyon's degree, a [Sun in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-cancer/) in its last decan, and in a recent chart any planet from about 25° to 27° Cancer sits on the same degree. Only that conjunction counts for the tradition, along the zodiac and nowhere else ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)).

Procyon's reputation is the kind I won't hand on. A rise and then a fall is a story, and a chart that tells it about a person has stopped describing and started predicting. I don't place Procyon in my readings at all, and stars were never among the placements I tested ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). If you meet the little dog's star on one of your planets, take Ptolemy's pairing at its plainest, Mars for effort and Mercury for thought and speech, and leave Robson's rise and fall with Robson.
