Procyon is the star of the Little Dog, and its Greek name says only that it comes before the dog: it rises ahead of Sirius. Cancer holds both dog stars, and Procyon, at 26°08′ in 2026, is the later of the two.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

At magnitude 0.38, α Canis Minoris usually ranks eighth among the stars of the night sky. Its light takes 11.46 years to reach us, and a faint white dwarf companion, Procyon B, circles it every 40.84 years. With [Sirius](/astrology/sirius/) and Betelgeuse, Orion's red shoulder, it makes the Winter Triangle, and it is one of the six stars of the larger Winter Hexagon. Its white light has a faint yellow tinge, and it stands highest at midnight in mid-January. Sirius lies further east, yet from most northern latitudes Procyon clears the horizon first, because it sits further north: hence the name.

Since 1930, precession has carried it 1°20′ along [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), from 24°48′ to 26°08′ in 2026 (25°46′ in 2000), nearly twelve degrees after Sirius. Its latitude is 16° south of the ecliptic: well clear of the planets in the sky, though nowhere near as far as [Alkaid](/astrology/alkaid/), the star at the end of the Plough's handle, more than fifty degrees north.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

Ptolemy gives Procyon a nature of [Mars](/astrology/mars/) and [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), in his terms a mixture of the two planets' qualities. Vivian Robson's *The Fixed Stars and Constellations in Astrology* (1923) gives it a mixed name: mostly unfortunate, in the tradition's word, though sometimes bringing wealth, and given to sudden success followed by a fall. Agate and the water crowfoot were its stone and plant as one of the fifteen Behenian stars of medieval magic.

Older stories are kinder to it. The Babylonians knew Procyon as Nangar, the Carpenter, an aspect of Marduk, who built and ordered the heavens. In Greek myth it is Maera, the hound of Erigone, daughter of Icarius of Athens. Arabic star lore paired the two dog stars as women, Procyon al-ghumaisa’, the bleary-eyed, against Sirius, the teary-eyed, and medieval astrolabes in England and western Europe wrote its name as Algomeyza.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Procyon's turn with the Sun comes twelve days after Sirius's, on about 17 or 18 July, with a [Sun in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-cancer/) in its last decan. The tradition asks only that a planet fall within about a degree of 26°08′ Cancer; the star's sixteen degrees of southern latitude do not enter into it ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)).

Procyon's reputation is the kind I won't hand on. A rise and then a fall is a story, and a chart that tells it about a person has stopped describing and started predicting. I don't place Procyon in my readings at all, and stars were never among the placements I tested ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). If you meet the little dog's star on one of your planets, take Ptolemy's pairing at its plainest, Mars for effort and Mercury for thought and speech, and leave Robson's rise and fall with Robson.
