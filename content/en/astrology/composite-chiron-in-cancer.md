Every relationship has to work out where it lives, in a flat, in two families, in a circle of friends, and some find that question harder than others. For a pair with composite Chiron in Cancer, belonging is the sore question, and shelter is something the two of them learn to build by hand.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Read as a chart of the relationship itself, [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) puts [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) where the bond keeps meeting a limit and has to find its own way round. [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), cardinal water ruled by the [Moon](/astrology/moon/), makes that limit a question of home: whether the relationship has a place, whether its families make room for it, whether care can be asked for as easily as it is given. A pair like this may feel slightly out of place at both families' tables, or find that the home it shares never quite feels finished.

Holidays tend to show it: each of you may be welcome at your own family's table and still feel, as a couple, like guests at both. Belonging, for such a pair, is made rather than found: traditions of their own instead of borrowed ones, a door open to friends who need somewhere to land, a habit of noticing who did the looking-after this week. A composite can describe a couple, two friends, or a parent and a grown child, and in each the shelter is built, not inherited.

Chiron moves faster through Cancer than through the signs before it, so fewer birth charts have it there, and fewer midpoints: about 1 pair in 14, among two million pairs of birth dates drawn from 1930 to 2009. Its adult generations are short ones, the births of August 1937 to June 1941 and of June 1988 to July 1991; pair two people from the same one and the composite keeps their Chiron in Cancer. A square can make it too. Someone born in June 1984 has Chiron at about 5° Gemini, someone born in June 1994 at about 5° Virgo, a right angle apart, and the point halfway between them, about 20° Cancer, is their composite Chiron.

Someone born with [Chiron in Cancer](/astrology/chiron-in-cancer/) searches for a place to belong on their own account. Shared, the search often pulls two ways at once, towards more closeness and away from being swallowed by it, and a pair can feel both pulls in the same evening.

Care can tip into control. Over-protecting, keeping score of who looks after whom, withdrawing into the shell when a family remark stings: these are the bond's defences, and they cost it. Asking for care in plain words beats waiting for it, and a home can be imperfect and still be home.

The neighbouring placements describe other tender places: [composite Chiron in Gemini](/astrology/composite-chiron-in-gemini/) the words between you, [composite Chiron in Leo](/astrology/composite-chiron-in-leo/) the wish to be seen together.

Composite charts never come into my own readings. Here they are described as a technique, the way astrologers who draw them read this Chiron: next to both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and never as a prediction of a home, a wedding or a separation. Care starts with asking, so a chart of two people needs both of them to have said yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
