After the push of Aries, the progressed Moon slows into Taurus, the sign where tradition says the Moon is strongest. For about two years and four months, what you need tends to be less about starting and more about keeping: a rhythm, a place, a few things that last.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[The progressed Moon](/astrology/progressed-moon/) is the quickest point of [secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/), the day-for-a-year chart, and I read it as the mood of a chapter. [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/) is fixed earth, ruled by Venus, and the Moon is exalted there: needs find solid ground, and comfort comes from continuity, from the steady routine, the familiar room, the people who are still there next year. The question of this chapter is the one Taurus always asks: how does this become durable? Feelings may move more slowly, and that slowness is a way of making sure, not a refusal to change. In practice it can look like wanting fewer changes at once, saying no to a rush you would once have joined, or caring more about how a day feels than about how much it holds.

Exaltation is the tradition’s word for a planet especially well received in a sign, and I read it gently: it means needs are easier to meet in these years, not that nothing will be asked of you. Where they gather depends on the house the progressed Moon crosses, which only [your birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/) can show; I draw houses in Placidus, and I don’t read a chart without the time. With a natal [Moon in Taurus](/astrology/moon-in-taurus/), the progressed Moon comes back to its birth sign here, and the chapter can feel like a return to your own way of being comforted.

Inside the chapter there are shorter passages. Crossing Taurus, the progressed Moon reaches any planet you were born with there, squares the planets you hold in Leo and Aquarius, and stands opposite those in Scorpio; I read each passage as a brief stretch in which that planet’s function asks for steadier ground.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Steady as it feels, the Taurus chapter is no slower than the others: for births from 1930 to 2009, the progressed Moon spent between two and two and a half years there, usually about two years and four months. What sets your own length is the real Moon’s speed in the days after you were born, a day of its travel for each year of the chapter.

It comes in from the [progressed Moon in Aries](/astrology/progressed-moon-in-aries/) and moves on to the [progressed Moon in Gemini](/astrology/progressed-moon-in-gemini/), and it won’t be back in Taurus for about 27 years, the time the progressed Moon needs to go round the whole zodiac. That long return is one reason to notice the chapter while you are in it.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Protect a rhythm you can keep. A Taurus chapter tends to sit best with one or two steady things at its centre, a routine, a place, a practice, rather than with constant reinvention. Venus rules Taurus, and the house Venus occupies in your birth chart shows where comfort and value tend to meet in these years.

Possessions and money are Taurus’s familiar ground, but I don’t read this chapter as a forecast for either; what it describes is the need to feel secure, whatever shape that takes for you. When change does come, give it time to become yours: Taurus needs to know a thing will hold before it leans on it. And notice what you would like to carry into the livelier chapter that follows, because the steadiness you build now is what it will stand on.
