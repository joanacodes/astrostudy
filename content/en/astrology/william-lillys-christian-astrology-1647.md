*Christian Astrology* is the manual [William Lilly](/astrology/william-lilly/) published in London in 1647: three books that take a reader from setting up a chart to answering a question to reading a birth. Astrologers had learned their trade from Latin books, as Lilly himself had. Nothing on this scale had yet been printed in English.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

John and Humphrey Partridge published it in 1647, and a second edition followed in 1659. Lilly dedicated it to Bulstrode Whitelocke, the Lord Keeper of the Great Seal, whose support had won him friends in Parliament. The three books build on one another: *An Introduction to Astrology*, *The Resolution of all manner of Questions and Demands*, and *An Easie and Plaine Method Teaching How to Judge upon Nativities*.

In an appendix Lilly lists the books he worked from, and it reads like a map of the tradition. There is Ptolemy's *Quadripartitum*, the Latin name of [Ptolemy’s Tetrabiblos](/astrology/ptolemys-tetrabiblos/); Bonatti's ten treatises, [Guido Bonatti’s Book of Astronomy](/astrology/guido-bonattis-book-of-astronomy/); Agrippa's *De occulta philosophia*; John Dee's aphorisms; and works by Robert Fludd, Johannes Kepler and Paracelsus ([Renaissance astrology](/astrology/renaissance-astrology/)).

Its second life began in 1980, when the astrologer Olivia Barclay obtained an original copy; the facsimile she arranged in 1985 brought astrologers back to the old techniques ([The traditional revival](/astrology/traditional-revival/)). Deborah Houlding has since edited the text, and in 2019 the historian Jeffrey Kotyk set its doctrine of the twelve houses and the Lot of Fortune beside that of a Chinese compendium of the previous century, Wan Minying's *Xingxue dacheng*.

## What it says {#what-it-says}

The first book is a course in tools: how to use an ephemeris, how to set up the figure, the natures of the signs, the planets and the twelve houses. It is also where Lilly defines a planet's essential dignity as “the strength, fortitude or debility of the Planets [or] significators”, and sets out the table that scores it ([The table of dignities](/astrology/table-of-dignities/)).

The second book teaches horary astrology: questions on illness, wealth, choice of marriage, travel, each answered from the chart of the moment it was asked, with worked examples ([Horary astrology](/astrology/horary-astrology/)). Its method turns on choosing the right significator: the house a question belongs to, and the planet that rules it.

The third book reads the birth chart, and it asks of a nativity almost everything a client of the 1640s might want to know: body and build, colour, wit, wealth, illnesses, the manner of death, marriage and the number of spouses, children, the best career. Many of those are questions I won't put to a chart ([Can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/), [Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)). The timing that follows, primary directions, solar returns and transits, is closer to my own work: I read transits for the seasons of a year, as weather rather than events.

Lilly also shows how to correct a chart whose birth time isn't exactly known, and thought it of major importance for accurate prediction. Here we part. A time worked backwards from events is a hypothesis, and I would rather start from the time on the record, or not start at all ([Rectification](/astrology/rectification/), [What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

In one chapter, on the Part of Fortune and how to take it by day or by night, he counts it the same way for both, as far from the Ascendant as the Moon is from the Sun. That was Ptolemy's rule, and Ptolemy's authority had carried it through medieval and Renaissance astrology, though most Hellenistic astrologers had reversed the count at night ([Part of Fortune](/astrology/part-of-fortune/)).

Lilly's book judges questions and events. Almost three centuries later, [Dane Rudhyar’s The Astrology of Personality (1936)](/astrology/dane-rudhyars-the-astrology-of-personality-1936/) would come at the chart from the other end, as a picture of the person.
