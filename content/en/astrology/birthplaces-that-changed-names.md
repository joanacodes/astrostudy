A chart is drawn for a point on the Earth, not for a word on a certificate. When the word has changed since you were born, the work is to get from the old name back to the right point, and to the clock that was kept there.

## Step by step {#step-by-step}

1. **Copy the place exactly as the record gives it.** Keep the spelling, the language and the region or country named at the time, and don't modernise anything yet. “Leningrad, USSR” and “Saint Petersburg, Russia” are the same city; “Berlin, Ontario” is not Berlin at all ([How to find your birth time](/astrology/how-to-find-your-birth-time/)).
2. **Find what the place is called now, and since when.** Saint Petersburg became Petrograd in 1914, Leningrad in 1924, and Saint Petersburg again on 6 September 1991. Tsaritsyn became Stalingrad in 1925 and Volgograd on 10 November 1961. Bombay officially became Mumbai in November 1995. Set the date of the change beside your birth date: it tells you which name was official when you were born, a useful check that you have the right town.
3. **Rule out the namesakes.** Many places share a name, and some were named after others: St. Petersburg in Florida was named after the Russian city, and the Ontario town of Berlin became Kitchener in 1916. Make sure the region and the country on the record match the place you've chosen before you trust it. A city and its region can part ways too: Saint Petersburg took its name back in 1991, but the region around it is still Leningrad Oblast.
4. **Use the coordinates, not the name.** A chart is calculated from a latitude and a longitude; the name only helps the program find them. Look the coordinates up, compare them with the program's match, and enter them by hand if it offers the wrong town. One degree of longitude moves the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) and the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) as much as four minutes of clock time would ([Born at sea or in flight](/astrology/born-at-sea-or-in-flight/)).
5. **Check the clock that was kept there.** A town that changed country may have changed its clocks too, and the chart needs the offset in force at that place on that date ([Time zones and daylight saving in charts](/astrology/time-zones-and-daylight-saving-in-charts/)). The public time-zone database known as tz names each zone after a representative city and keeps old spellings as aliases: Asia/Calcutta was renamed Asia/Kolkata in 2008, and both still work. It aims to record every change of clocks since 1970, so for older births, check the offset your program applied: in Brazil in 1963, only five states kept summer time between 23 October and 9 December, yet a request to split the São Paulo zone was turned down in 2010 because its clocks have agreed since 1970. Before standard time, towns kept their own ([Local mean time for old births](/astrology/local-mean-time-for-old-births/)).
6. **Write down what you used, and why.** Note the record's wording, the modern name, the coordinates and the offset, with where each came from. The place can't make up for a missing time: the hour sets the rising sign and the houses, and I don't read a chart without it ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). If you're unsure whether what you've found is enough for a [birth chart reading](/birth-chart/), write to me before you order.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes}

- **Taking the program's first match.** Type an old or shared name and a program may offer a town of the same name in another country, with other coordinates and another clock. Check the region before you accept it.
- **Applying today's map to an old date.** A place now in one country may have kept another country's time when you were born; the offset follows the date, not the present border.
- **Overwriting the record.** Keep the place as written beside the modern name rather than replacing one with the other: if a question comes up later, the original wording is your evidence.
- **Rounding to the nearest big city.** The capital of the region, or the city a time zone is named after, can lie far from the town you were born in, and every degree of longitude counts.
- **Using where you live now.** A birth chart is drawn for the place of birth; a chart recast for another place is a different technique ([Relocation charts](/astrology/relocation-charts/)).
