Nearly everyone meets this square once, and for about two and a half years what you love and what you long for stop lining up. Of Neptune's contacts with Venus, it's the one that asks most plainly whether you're seeing a person, a thing or a plan, or the hope you've hung on it.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) values and reciprocates: it knows what it likes and keeps a fair exchange between people. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) idealises and gives without counting. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates them through friction, the kind that asks you to adjust rather than pick a side, and during this [transit](/astrology/transits/) the adjustment is between the real and the ideal in everything Venus touches.

It can feel like taste going out of focus: things you loved look ordinary, and something you can't name looks better. In relationships the friction tends to gather around reciprocity. Neptune makes it easy to give without counting and to forgive before anything is said; Venus, which keeps the balance, notices when the giving runs one way. Neither is wrong. Squares resolve, as far as they do, through love that keeps its warmth and checks its facts: asking instead of guessing, saying what you need instead of hoping it will be sensed.

I don't read this square as deception, disappointment or a parting, and nothing in it decides how a relationship goes. If a blur of feeling turns into distress that stays, please take it to a doctor or a therapist; a chart can keep you company in a hard season, never treat it ([astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/)).

The house of your Venus says where the friction is felt, and the house Neptune is crossing says where it comes from. With Venus in your 7th, the square often arrives while Neptune crosses your 4th or your 10th, so home life, or the shape of your working life, starts asking something of the partnership ([Neptune transiting the 4th house](/astrology/neptune-transit-4th-house/)). Placing your Venus in the 7th, and Neptune in the 4th or the 10th, takes your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

[Venus square Neptune](/astrology/venus-square-neptune/) in a birth chart makes this friction a lifelong teacher; the transit is a stretch of it with an end date.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

From 1½° before exact to 1° after, the orbs I use, Neptune holds the square for about two years and seven months, seldom fewer than twenty-two. Crossing the exact point three times, or five in about one pass in four, it stays long enough for the friction to change how you choose, and not only what.

Your Venus has two square points, a quarter of the zodiac on either side, and they lie half a circle apart. In eighty years Neptune covers almost half a circle, so one of the two comes to almost everyone: nearly all the people born between 1930 and 2009 meet this square before 80, and a very few meet it twice.

Born with Venus close to Neptune, or opposite it, you meet the square around 41, in the same years as the [Neptune square](/astrology/neptune-square/), when Neptune squares its own birth position; the two then read as one season.

About fourteen years on either side of a square lie [transiting Neptune sextile natal Venus](/astrology/transiting-neptune-sextile-natal-venus/) and [transiting Neptune trine natal Venus](/astrology/transiting-neptune-trine-natal-venus/). [Transiting Neptune conjunct natal Venus](/astrology/transiting-neptune-conjunct-natal-venus/) and [transiting Neptune opposite natal Venus](/astrology/transiting-neptune-opposite-natal-venus/) are each about forty-one years away, which is why many people meet one of them and this square in the same life.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Ask rather than guess. Under this square, sensing what someone feels is easy and often half right; a plain question gets the other half.

Keep the exchange visible. Notice who gives what, in time, care and attention, and say so kindly when it has drifted one way.

Give old loves of taste a second look before you drop them. Some things only look ordinary in fog, and some were never really yours; the season is long enough to tell which.
