A lot turns a relationship into a place. Take how far one planet stands from another, carry that distance over to a third point, and you have a new degree of the zodiac standing for what you measured. The doctrine of the lots is the set of rules for building those degrees and reading them.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The lots are among the oldest tools of horoscopic astrology. Where they came from is unclear, and Babylonian, Egyptian, Magian, Persian and Hermetic origins have all been suggested, but by the 1st century AD Dorotheus of Sidon was using them as a matter of course ([Hellenistic astrology](/astrology/hellenistic-astrology/)). In AD 378, Paulus Alexandrinus worked with a dozen or so main lots, led by the [Part of Fortune](/astrology/part-of-fortune/), the Moon's lot, and the [Part of Spirit](/astrology/part-of-spirit/), the Sun's.

Paulus also gives a set of seven, the [Hermetic lots](/astrology/hermetic-lots/), one for each traditional planet. Set beside the [Thema Mundi](/astrology/thema-mundi/), the legendary horoscope of the world's beginning, where every planet stands in a sign it rules, they share its shape: in both, the Sun and Moon come first and the five planets are placed from them. In the Thema Mundi the planets take their signs outward from the lights' Cancer and Leo; among the Hermetic lots, the five planets' lots are counted from Fortune and Spirit.

Later centuries kept adding. Arabic manuscripts of the Abbasid era show their number exploding, and Abu Ma'shar (787–886) described no fewer than 55, only the ones he counted as significant. When their books reached Europe in Latin, medieval astrologers such as Guido Bonatti took the lots for their invention, hence the name Arabic parts. By William Lilly's time, in the 17th century, the Part of Fortune was the only one still in common use.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Every lot follows the same rule: the distance from a first point to a second, counted in the order of the signs, is counted again from a third. The third is usually the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/).

Here is the arithmetic once, with Fortune. Say the Ascendant is at 12° Virgo, the Sun at 25° Taurus and the Moon at 3° Cancer. The Moon stands 38° ahead of the Sun, so by day Fortune falls 38° on from the Ascendant, at 20° Libra. By night many astrologers swap the Sun and Moon, and Fortune lands 38° before the Ascendant, at 4° Leo: the same distance, on the other side. The reason given is [sect](/astrology/sect-day-and-night-charts/), the light in charge by day or by night, and the seven Hermetic lots all have a day formula and a night one.

Lots can be built on other lots. The Hermetic set does it openly: the lots of the benefics, Venus's lot of Eros and Jupiter's lot of Victory, are counted from Spirit, while the lots of Mercury, Mars and Saturn, called Necessity, Courage and Nemesis, are counted from Fortune.

All of this hangs on the Ascendant, which moves a degree every four minutes or so, and on whether the Sun was above the horizon. Change the birth time and every lot moves with it; [without the time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/), there are no lots to read.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Astrologers who work with the lots read each one much as the Part of Fortune page describes: by its sign and house, the planet that rules it, and any planet close to it, always for the topic the lot was made for. A lot of marriage speaks of partnership ([Part of Marriage](/astrology/part-of-marriage/)), a lot of courage of daring, and so on.

In my own work, the lots enter through timing. [Zodiacal releasing](/astrology/zodiacal-releasing/), one of the techniques in my forecasts, divides a life into chapters counted from the Lot of Spirit and related lots, and reads them as changing emphasis, never as fixed events.

Two limits I keep. The old lists include lots of death, of illness and of children, and I don't read a chart for any of those ([lots of death and illness, in history](/astrology/lots-of-death-and-illness-in-history/); [can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)). And the more lots you calculate, the more of them will land on something striking by chance, so a single lot is never the basis of anything I tell you. My notebook tested placements of planets by sign and house; the lots weren't part of it.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The more lots you use, the more precise the reading.”** More points mean more coincidences. A few lots, read in their context, say more than fifty read at once.
- **“A lot of marriage tells you when you'll marry.”** It describes a theme in a chart. No point can date a marriage or promise one.
- **“Lots are a medieval add-on.”** They are among the oldest tools of horoscopic astrology: Dorotheus used them in the 1st century, long before anyone wrote of Arabic parts.
- **“A lot works without the birth time, like a planet.”** Every lot moves with the Ascendant, so a lot without the birth time is a guess.
