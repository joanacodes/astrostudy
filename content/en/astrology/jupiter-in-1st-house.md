When you were born, Jupiter was just below the eastern horizon, in the stretch of sky about to rise. Whatever Jupiter means in your chart, you lead with it: the growth and the beliefs show in how you arrive, before you've said very much at all.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) is the self you lead with: your manner, your body, the way you begin things and meet people. It's angular, one of the four houses that start at an angle, and its natural sign is Aries. With [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) here, the planet of growth and meaning becomes part of your presence. You tend to learn by showing up, people often come to you for perspective or encouragement, and your beliefs can be read in the way you carry yourself. Your growth is personal in the most literal sense: it happens as you become more fully yourself.

In Placidus, the house system I use, the 1st house begins at the Ascendant, and the Ascendant changes sign about every two hours on average. A planet this close to the horizon is the one most sensitive to the birth time: a quarter of an hour can move Jupiter between the 1st and the 12th, which read very differently ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). If your time was rounded, I'll say how sure I can be.

Just above the horizon, [Jupiter in the 12th house](/astrology/jupiter-in-12th-house/) grows in private; a little further down, [Jupiter in the 2nd house](/astrology/jupiter-in-2nd-house/) grows through what you value. Here, in the 1st, the growing is on show.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You tend to arrive in people's lives with encouragement: they feel larger in your company, more hopeful about their own plans. Generosity is part of how you introduce yourself. The 7th house sits opposite the 1st, so partners meet this Jupiter head on. Some are drawn to the warmth; some feel a little crowded by it. A partner who can enjoy your enthusiasm without having to match it is often a good fit. The work is leaving room: letting the other person's growth be theirs, and letting them see your doubts as well as your confidence.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, your presence is often part of the job. Roles where you represent, host, teach, hold a room, launch a project or make the first contact tend to suit you, because people take you on trust quickly. You may also be the one asked to speak for a team, which is a responsibility as much as a compliment. All this opens doors, though an open door isn't a result. A first impression can promise more than the next six months deliver, so the follow-through matters as much as the arrival.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The expectation that you'll always be the hopeful one. People may lean on your good humour and be surprised when you need something yourself. You may come to believe that being generous, upbeat or wise is who you have to be, and find doubt or tiredness hard to show. Growth can get confused with image: getting bigger in other people's eyes rather than more yourself.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Let people see you uncertain sometimes; it makes your encouragement easier to believe. Keep some of your growing private: a subject studied for its own sake, a practice nobody watches. Match what you promise in person with what you can deliver later. And look at the sign on your Ascendant and the planet that rules it, your [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/), since a 1st-house Jupiter works in that sign's style.

Jupiter's house went into [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) with everything else, and Jupiter wasn't one of the three that held far more often; overall, about a third held. The rising sign, which a 1st-house Jupiter colours, was itself among the placements that held about a third of the time. So I read this one with care, and I'm honest about how much of it rests on your birth time.
