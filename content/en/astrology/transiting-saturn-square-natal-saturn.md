Saturn squares its own birth place about every seven years, taking turns with the opposition and the return: a first time at about seven, then in the early twenties, the mid-thirties, the early fifties and the mid-sixties. Each square is a checkpoint on the long project your natal Saturn describes.

## What changes {#what-changes}

Your natal [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) describes where you build slowly and carry responsibility. A [square](/astrology/square/), 90°, relates two functions through constructive friction, and here Saturn's function meets itself at right angles: the structure you have now against the structure you started from. Something no longer fits, and adjusting it is the work.

I read the two kinds of square differently ([Saturn squares and opposition](/astrology/saturn-squares-and-opposition/)). The waxing ones, about seven years after a return, at about 36 and 66, test what that return set up: the commitment is real now, and so is its weight. The waning ones, about seven years before a return, at about 22 and 51, begin a sorting the next return will finish: what you carry because you chose it, and what you carry from habit. Friction isn't failure in either case; a square is how a structure finds out where it's weak while there's still time to mend it.

Natal Saturn is the placement I trust most in a chart ([the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)), so I read its checkpoints closely, and gently. The first square, at about seven, belongs to childhood, and this site never reads children's charts ([children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)); I mention it only to place the adult ones.

The houses decide where the friction sits. With natal Saturn in the 7th, transiting Saturn squares it from your 10th ([Saturn transiting the 10th house](/astrology/saturn-transit-10th-house/)) or your 4th, so commitments to a partner meet the demands of work or of home. The 7th, the 10th and the 4th all hang on your birth time, and so does my reading of this square ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

A square of Saturn to itself usually holds for about nine months with my orbs (1½° before exact, 1° after): a checkpoint long enough to notice the strain, find its cause and mend it. Saturn's retrograde loop turns a little over half of its [transits](/astrology/transits/) into three exact passes over about nine and a half months; without one, a crossing can be over in three weeks.

Counted for births every fifth day from 1930 to 2009, the first square came between 5.7 and 8.2, at 7 in the middle: at 6.5 for someone born on 1 January 1965, at 6.8 for 1 January 1975, at 8.2 for 1 January 1985. The later ones come at about 22, 36, 51 and 66, and some people meet a sixth just before 80.

Each waxing square follows [transiting Saturn sextile natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-saturn-sextile-natal-saturn/) by about two years and leads on to [transiting Saturn trine natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-saturn-trine-natal-saturn/) and [transiting Saturn opposite natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-saturn-opposite-natal-saturn/). Each waning one comes after the opposition and the second trine, about seven years before the [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/).

## What helps {#what-helps}

Change the part that no longer fits, not everything. A square asks for an adjustment, not a demolition.

At a waxing square, check the load: which commitments from your last return would you still choose, and what support do they need?

At a waning square, start the list for the return: what you'll keep carrying, and what you'll put down.
