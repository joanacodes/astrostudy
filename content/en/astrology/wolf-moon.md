The wolf moon is January’s full moon under its almanac name: a cold, bright Moon over the northern winter, and the first of the year in the set of full moon names that fills calendars and weather pages every month.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

There is nothing special about this full moon in the sky: it is simply the full moon that falls in January. In the dates I use, that is 3 January 2026, at 13°02′ Cancer; 22 January 2027, at 2°14′ Leo; 12 January 2028, at 21°27′ Cancer, which is also a partial lunar eclipse; 30 January 2029, at 10°38′ Leo; and 19 January 2030, at 29°37′ Cancer.

Names like this one became familiar through American almanacs. According to NASA’s monthly full moon guides, the *Maine Farmers’ Almanac* began publishing what it called Indian names for the full moons in the 1930s, and the names have been widely used since. NASA’s own account adds that their original source isn’t completely clear, and lists other names for January’s full moon: the Moon after Yule, the Old Moon, the Ice Moon. So I won’t tell you which people first called it the wolf moon. I haven’t found a source that shows where the name began, and I won’t repeat lists that pin it on one nation without saying how they know.

The almanacs tie the name to wolves heard in the depths of winter. It is a northern-hemisphere name for a northern winter: in the southern hemisphere, January’s full moon rises over midsummer.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The names follow the calendar month, not the sky, so the wolf moon can fall on any date in January. Its sign follows the date. The Sun is in Capricorn until about 20 January and in Aquarius after, and a full moon puts the Moon opposite the Sun, so a full moon early in January is in Cancer and a late one in Leo, as the dates above show.

Year by year it moves: the full moons come about eleven days earlier each year, until one slips back into December and January’s becomes the next, late in the month. In 2028 the January full moon is on the 12th; a year on, the full moon eleven days earlier falls on 31 December 2028, so January 2029’s comes on the 30th. That December one, a blue moon in the calendar sense, is the kind of thing these names can’t settle: almanacs that give each month one name have to decide what to call a second.

The next name in the list is the [snow moon](/astrology/snow-moon/), for February. Like the [black moon (the calendar one)](/astrology/black-moon-calendar/), all of these are names laid over the lunar cycle by calendars, not by astronomy.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The name adds nothing I read. A January [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/) is the Moon in Cancer or Leo with the Sun opposite in Capricorn or Aquarius, a [Full moon in Cancer](/astrology/full-moon-in-cancer/) or a [Full moon in Leo](/astrology/full-moon-in-leo/), and I read it like every full moon: only within a degree of a natal point, or of the point opposite, that something slower is already working on. The full moon of 12 January 2028 is also a lunar eclipse, which in the system I built for my readings counts within about 3° of a natal point, at either end of its axis.

Born at a January full moon, you have the Sun opposite the Moon across Capricorn and Cancer or Aquarius and Leo, and that axis, not the almanac’s name, is what I read. The houses it falls across need your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). As weather, a wolf moon is a fine, cold full moon to look at, and nothing it does is written in its name.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Wolves howl at the full moon.”** Wolves howl to keep in touch over long distances and to hold a pack together, whatever the Moon is doing. The International Wolf Center treats the idea that they howl only at a full moon as folklore.
- **“The wolf moon is a name from one Native American nation.”** No source I trust shows that. NASA’s account traces the names’ spread to an American almanac in the 1930s and says their original source isn’t clear.
- **“The wolf moon is a special kind of full moon.”** It is whichever full moon falls in January, and its sign is Cancer or Leo every time.
- **“It’s the wolf moon everywhere.”** The names belong to a northern calendar. In the southern hemisphere, January’s full moon rises over summer.
