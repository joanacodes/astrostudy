A chart wheel packs a lot into a small circle, and the first time you see one it can look like a clock someone dropped. It has a logic, though, and once you know which way it turns, you can read most of it in a couple of minutes.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The wheel is a picture of the sky at a birth, drawn flat. Most programs draw it in three rings.

- **The outer ring** is the zodiac: the twelve signs, 30° each, marked with their glyphs ([Glyphs: the cheat sheet](/astrology/glyphs-the-cheat-sheet/)).
- **The inner ring** holds the twelve houses, numbered from 1 to 12, with lines from the centre marking their cusps.
- **The middle** carries the planets, each glyph with its degree and minute beside it, and the lines drawn between them for the aspects.

Across it runs one horizontal line: the horizon at your birth. Its left end is the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), marked AC or ASC, where the eastern horizon met the zodiac; its right end is the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), DC. Near the top sits the Midheaven, MC, and near the bottom the Imum Coeli, IC. The wheel is drawn as if you were facing south from the northern hemisphere, which is why east is on the left and why the top of the chart points south, not north ([Horizon axis](/astrology/horizon-axis/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Two motions share the same wheel, and they run in opposite directions.

The zodiac and the houses both run anticlockwise. The 1st house lies just below the Ascendant, the 2nd below that, and so on round to the 7th, which begins at the Descendant, and the 12th, which ends back at the Ascendant. The signs go the same way: with a Cancer Ascendant, Leo comes next, below it.

The sky's daily turn runs clockwise. In the course of a day, every planet rises at the Ascendant, climbs to the Midheaven, sets at the Descendant and passes under the Imum Coeli. So the Sun's place on your wheel tells you roughly when you were born: close to the Ascendant, around sunrise; near the top, around the middle of the day; near the Descendant, around sunset; near the bottom, in the middle of the night. A planet drawn above the horizon line, in houses 7 to 12, was up in the sky at your birth; below it, in houses 1 to 6, it was under the earth.

The houses depend on the house system. In Placidus, the house system I use, they're unequal, the Midheaven always sits on the cusp of the 10th, and the top of the chart can lean to one side. In [Equal houses](/astrology/equal-houses/), every house is 30° wide and the Midheaven floats somewhere near the top. Two wheels of the same birth can look different for that reason alone ([Which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Here's the order I'd suggest for reading a wheel:

1. **Check the settings.** Zodiac, house system, which node and which Lilith: a wheel is only as good as what it was set to. Mine are the tropical zodiac and Placidus, with both nodes, mean and true.
2. **Find the Ascendant**, on the left, and note its sign.
3. **Check the Sun.** If you were born at night and the Sun is drawn above the horizon line, or at midday and it's below, look again at the time and the time zone.
4. **Go round the houses**, anticlockwise from the 1st, noting which planets sit where, and in which sign.
5. **Read the lines last.** The aspects matter, but their colours are each program's choice, not a verdict; many people find the [aspect grid](/astrology/aspect-grid/), a table of the same aspects, easier to follow.

Then comes the part no wheel does for you: putting it together. The positions come from an ephemeris ([Ephemeris, and how to read one](/astrology/ephemeris-and-how-to-read-one/)); the reading comes from knowing what each planet does, in what style, and in which area of life. And the wheel needs your birth time to place the houses at all ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The top of the chart is north.”** The top is the Midheaven, which for a birth in the northern hemisphere lies to the south.
- **“Red lines are bad aspects.”** Colours are a program's convention. A square is friction, never failure, and a trine's ease can hide things too.
- **“An empty house means nothing happens there.”** Every house has a sign on its cusp and a ruling planet somewhere in the chart ([Empty houses](/astrology/empty-houses/)).
- **“If two wheels look different, one of them is wrong.”** They may use different house systems or a different zodiac. Check the settings before you decide.
