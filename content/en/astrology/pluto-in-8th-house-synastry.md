In the house of its own modern sign, Pluto speaks with its fullest voice. Their Pluto in your 8th house takes another person's depth to the place where trust, intimacy and shared resources are decided, and what passes between you there rarely stays on the surface.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) brings to light what is buried and renews what has run its course. [The 8th house](/astrology/8th-house/) holds what two people build and risk once they let each other in: intimacy, trust, joint money and debts, and the way you come through crises together. Through this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their depth reaches what you hold in common.

With them, trust may become total or nothing. Intimacy can feel transforming, a hard time faced together can renew you, and shared money turns into a matter of real trust. You may find yourself telling them things you've never said aloud.

Your depths can feel like home to them. They may sense what you've lived through, be drawn to share your hardest moments, and feel at ease with an intensity other people shy away from. The 8th is one of the succedent houses, where bonds store up what they make. [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/), its natural sign, was Mars's first and was given to Pluto by modern tropical astrology; I read both rulers, and here Pluto stands in the house of its own sign.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Privacy and secrecy can blur. Who knows what about the joint accounts, which confidences are kept, how much each of you reveals: intensity can be mistaken for truth, and control over shared matters for safety. Hard times can start to feel like tests of loyalty.

From their side, being kept out of part of your life can feel like a betrayal of the bond, and they may push for an openness that comes too soon for you.

The old books made the 8th the house of death, and I read no death in any chart ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)). Their Pluto in your 8th describes depth, trust and what two living adults share; in intimacy, everything rests on a clear yes from both.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Keep transparency where it protects the bond, and privacy where it protects each of you. Write down what is shared, lent and owed, and keep some resources apart. In a couple, talk about desire, consent and money plainly, even when it's hard; between friends, a confidence is a trust, not a currency; between colleagues sharing a risk, clear terms stop intensity from becoming control; between adult relatives managing family property together, written agreements keep old loyalties from being tested.

Turn it round and it's your Pluto in their 8th house, your depth meeting their trust. [Their Pluto in your 7th house](/astrology/pluto-in-7th-house-synastry/) makes partnership serious; [their Pluto in your 9th house](/astrology/pluto-in-9th-house-synastry/) questions belief and meaning.

Ideas of intimacy and of what couples share change between generations, and Pluto's sign marks some of that. Close in age, your charts hold Pluto at almost the same degree ([Pluto in the 8th house](/astrology/pluto-in-8th-house/)), and this intensity is your generation's as much as theirs; with a generation between you, two sets of rules about privacy and sharing meet, and saying them out loud helps. The day they were born is all Pluto needs, though their houses need the hour, and I don't read a pair with one time missing. In Placidus, the house system I use, your 8th can be broad or slim depending on your latitude and the hour, and only your birth time draws it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). What two people share here is the most private part of a chart, and I read it only with both people's yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#a-chart-is-personal)).
