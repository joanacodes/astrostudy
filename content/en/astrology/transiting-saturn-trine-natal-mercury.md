Twice in each 29½-year round, Saturn trines the degree your Mercury held at birth: about ten years after crossing it, and again about ten years before coming back. It's one of the quietest Saturn seasons. Thinking slows down, and for once that's useful.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is the part of a chart that takes in information, makes sense of it and passes it on: how you learn, reason, write and talk. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) builds structure that lasts: method, responsibility, the long view. A [trine](/astrology/trine/), 120°, lets two functions help each other while each keeps its own job. The transit adds nothing your chart doesn't already hold; it lends your Mercury some of Saturn's patience, without much of Saturn's weight.

In practice, attention holds. A subject you'd put off for years becomes an hour of study a day; notes turn into a system; a long piece of writing finds its plan. People tend to hear more weight in what you say, mostly because you say it after thinking it through. Reasoning isn't certainty, though, and this transit makes it easy to sound surer than you are: leave a little room for being wrong.

Trine transits are easy to miss, because nothing forces the issue. Ease isn't inevitability, and if no work is started, the season simply passes as a calm one.

Your Mercury's house says which part of life the steadier thinking serves; the house Saturn is crossing says where the structure comes from. Saturn moving through your 10th house ([Saturn transiting the 10th house](/astrology/saturn-transit-10th-house/)) while it trines a Mercury in your 6th might bring professional standards into the way you organise a working week. Whether your Mercury sits in the 6th or across a cusp can hang on a few minutes of birth time, and without that time I don't read the transit at all ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Counting 1½° before exact and 1° after, as I do for timing, Saturn's trine to your Mercury usually holds for about nine months. In a little over half of Saturn's [transits](/astrology/transits/), a retrograde loop takes it over the exact trine three times, and those stretch to about nine and a half months; a single crossing at full speed can be over in three weeks. Nine months is long enough to begin and finish a course, which is roughly the scale of work this transit favours.

By 80, everyone born between 1930 and 2009 has met it five or six times. On the way out, it falls between [transiting Saturn square natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-saturn-square-natal-mercury/) and [transiting Saturn opposite natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-saturn-opposite-natal-mercury/), and on the way back between the same two in reverse. [Transiting Saturn sextile natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-saturn-sextile-natal-mercury/) is its lighter cousin, two signs from the point instead of four, and [transiting Saturn conjunct natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-saturn-conjunct-natal-mercury/) opens and closes each round.

With Mercury in Gemini, both trines usually come from signs tradition gave to Saturn: Libra, its exaltation, and Aquarius, its home before Uranus was found. And if your chart has [Mercury trine Saturn](/astrology/mercury-trine-saturn/), each Saturn return rebuilds the trine from Saturn's side, so this transit keeps that appointment too.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Choose the long version. Enrol in the course, start the manual, plan the thesis in chapters: this transit backs things that take months.

Keep a fixed hour for it. Saturn rewards repetition more than inspiration, and an hour kept daily outlasts a weekend of zeal.

Spend the steadiness on something you haven't mastered yet. The trine is wasted on what you already do well, and well used on a skill that still feels awkward.
