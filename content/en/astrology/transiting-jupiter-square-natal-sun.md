Jupiter squares your Sun twice in each twelve-year round, about three years after crossing it and about three years before crossing it again. It's the angle at which wanting more starts to cost something, and proportion gets learned by practice rather than admired from a distance.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) enlarges understanding, plans and the sense of what's possible. Your [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is the centre those plans are meant to serve: identity, vitality, the direction you've chosen. At 90°, a [square](/astrology/square/) makes them rub until something adjusts: Jupiter's appetite for more meets the shape your life really has, and one side has to yield a little ground.

It often shows as a season of overreach: a yes to a role bigger than the time you have, confidence a few steps ahead of preparation, impatience with a life that felt the right size last year. None of that is failure. A square is how a chart grows by adjusting, and plenty of people outgrow something real under this one, a title, a habit of thinking small, a version of themselves they'd kept out of loyalty.

The Sun stands for vitality as well as purpose, and vitality isn't a constant supply. A Jupiter square tends to forget that, so the right adjustment is often fewer commitments rather than more effort.

Where it lands depends on two houses: your Sun's, and the one Jupiter is crossing. If Jupiter squares your Sun from your 6th house ([Jupiter transiting the 6th house](/astrology/jupiter-transit-6th-house/)), the friction tends to sit in daily work and routines, with more tasks accepted than a week can hold. Neither house can be drawn without your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), so without it this [transit](/astrology/transits/) stays unread. Born with [Sun square Jupiter](/astrology/sun-square-jupiter/), you already know this gap between who you are and what you aim at: each Jupiter return rebuilds your birth square from the same side.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Each square is brief unless Jupiter turns round near it: about eleven days within my orbs, which run from 1½° before exact to 1° after, and about eight months with three exact hits when a retrograde loop falls on the spot, as it does about three times in ten.

The two squares of a round don't feel alike. The first, three years after [transiting Jupiter conjunct natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-conjunct-natal-sun/), tests what you began then against your real hours and strengths. The second, nine years in, comes while you're clearing ground for the next crossing, and asks what you've outgrown. Halfway between them lies [transiting Jupiter opposite natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-opposite-natal-sun/). If either square is exact near your birthday, Jupiter stands a quarter of the sky from the Sun, at its highest around dusk for the first and around dawn for the second.

This square comes back thirteen or fourteen times by 80 for everyone born between 1930 and 2009, so nobody reaches that age without a few lessons in how far confidence can run ahead of the hours. [Transiting Jupiter sextile natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-sextile-natal-sun/) and [transiting Jupiter trine natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-trine-natal-sun/) give your sense of direction the same room without the pressure; the square is the visit that leaves a lesson in scale behind it.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Count before you promise. Under this square, ask of every new yes what it will push out of your week.

Keep the ambition and trim the size. The friction is useful when it makes a plan sound, and wasted when it only makes you tired.

Show the plan to someone who isn't excited about it. And if you can, finish one thing before Jupiter moves on: this transit rewards completion more than expansion.
