Mercury sextile Neptune is one of the quieter contacts between the mind and the imagination. Nothing about it insists. It waits until you need a metaphor, a softer way of putting something, or a sense of what a room is feeling, and then it's there.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is the part of a chart that learns and explains: it gathers information, makes sense of it, and passes it on. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) is the part that imagines and empathises: it reads symbols, feels what others feel, and dissolves boundaries that don't need to be there. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) relates them through opportunity: the two cooperate as soon as you give them a reason to.

Put simply, your words can borrow from your imagination whenever you ask them to. When you're explaining something hard, an image arrives to carry it; when someone is struggling to say what they feel, you can often find the sentence for them. It's a light touch rather than a flood, so your plain thinking stays plain until you invite the poetry in.

An opportunity has to be taken, though. The sextile is the easiest of the helpful aspects to overlook, and people with it sometimes don't notice their own knack until they start writing, teaching or simply listening more. Astrologers count it a soft aspect ([hard and soft aspects](/astrology/hard-and-soft-aspects/)); I'd add that it's a soft aspect that rewards effort.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Two signs apart, the sextile pairs elements that suit each other, fire with air and earth with water. For the generation born between 1957 and 1970, Neptune was in Scorpio, so the sextile came from Mercury in Virgo or Capricorn: a precise, practical mind that can, when it chooses, find words for what runs deep. That half belongs to everyone born in those years; your own part is Mercury's sign and the two houses. Mercury in the 3rd sextile Neptune in the 5th might show as playful, imaginative writing ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/) explains why houses need the hour).

About 1 person in 15 is born with Mercury and Neptune within 6° of a sextile. Close to exact, the door into imagination is easy to find; at 5° or 6°, it's there, but you have to know to look for it. Neptune barely moves in a year, so the sextile is Mercury's doing: a few days either side of your birth date can take it in or out of orb.

The same two functions meet differently at the other angles:

- **[Mercury conjunct Neptune](/astrology/mercury-conjunct-neptune/):** imagination in every thought rather than on request; the mind and the dream share one voice.
- **[Mercury square Neptune](/astrology/mercury-square-neptune/):** fact and image don't take turns politely, and the friction trains you to check as well as to imagine.
- **[Mercury trine Neptune](/astrology/mercury-trine-neptune/):** a wider, steadier flow; the poetry tends to come whether or not you call it.
- **[Mercury opposite Neptune](/astrology/mercury-opposite-neptune/):** the imaginative pull sits across the chart, and often comes through other people's visions first.

For two people, [Mercury sextile Neptune in synastry](/astrology/mercury-sextile-neptune-synastry/) is an easy link between one person's way of putting things and the other's imagination: a conversation that drifts into stories and brings back something useful. Each of you feels a different side of it.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Neptune sextile natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-neptune-sextile-natal-mercury/) is one of Neptune's mildest seasons. At Neptune's speed the sextile stays near exact for a long while, often more than a year, as the planet turns retrograde and back. It's usually read as a time when imagination is easy to bring into words: a good season to start the journal, the course or the creative project you'd been putting off, if you choose to.

Like the natal sextile, it opens a door and leaves you to walk through it. Weather, not a forecast: a Neptune sextile to your Mercury describes a gentle climate for imaginative thinking, never what you'll make of it.
