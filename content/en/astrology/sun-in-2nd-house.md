With the Sun in the 2nd house, the question *who am I?* keeps running into another one: *what am I worth?* I read this placement as an identity that grows by building something it can stand on, and that learns, often slowly, that worth and price are not the same thing.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) holds what you have and what you value: money, belongings, the talents you could live from, and the sense of your own worth underneath them. It's succedent, a house that gathers and keeps what the 1st began, and its natural sign is Taurus. The house doesn't hand the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) anything; it gives it a setting. Here the Sun's identity, vitality and purpose are worked out through resources and values: what you build, what you keep, and what you think is worth having.

In Placidus, the house system I use, a 2nd-house Sun was deep below the horizon when you were born, in the small hours, on its way back towards the east. Nearer dawn, it would be the [Sun in the 1st house](/astrology/sun-in-1st-house/); a little earlier, closer to the middle of the night, the [Sun in the 3rd house](/astrology/sun-in-3rd-house/). Those are times by the Sun rather than the clock, and the houses stretch and shrink with the season, so only your exact [birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/) and place settle it.

This Sun says nothing about how much you'll earn or own. No chart can. What it describes is how closely your sense of self is tied to feeling resourced, and how much of your purpose goes into making something that lasts and supports you.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, you may show who you are through what you provide: the meal, the home, the practical help, the present chosen with care. Security matters, and so does being valued for more than what you bring. The opposite house, the 8th, holds what is shared, so joint money and possessions can be tender ground; talking about them early, plainly and without blame helps a great deal. The lesson is that you are the gift, more than anything you bring with you.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, this Sun wants its talents to become something solid: a skill you can live from, a craft that keeps its value, a practice or a body of work that is yours. Purpose sharpens when effort turns into resources you can see, and fades when the work feels unpaid in every sense of the word. Recognition means being valued, plainly and fairly, and it's worth asking for in concrete terms. People with a 2nd-house Sun often do their best work once they stop underpricing themselves.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Worth and net worth can blur. When your sense of self rests on what you have, a lean month can feel like a question about who you are, and fear of scarcity can keep you in a job, a house or a habit long after it has stopped serving you. Some people with this Sun grip tightly; others undersell themselves so as never to ask for too much. Both come from the same place: a self still learning that its value doesn't rise and fall with the account.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Name three things you value that would still matter if they earned nothing, and give them time every week. Build at least one resource that nobody can take away: a skill, a practice, a friendship of many years. Keep a budget of energy as well as money, since vitality is a resource too. Spend, now and then, on what feeds your sense of self rather than your sense of safety. And read the Sun's sign alongside the house: an Aries Sun here builds very differently from the [Sun in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-taurus/), which shares this house's natural sign.
