Libra is the sign of partnership, so when a relationship chart puts Uranus there, the subject of the chart and the thing that wants changing are the same: the partnership itself. Two homes and one relationship, roles swapped with the seasons, an agreement rewritten every anniversary because something has changed on both sides.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Read in [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) asks how two free people stay free inside one bond. [Libra](/astrology/libra/), cardinal air ruled by [Venus](/astrology/venus/), brings the question home to the partnership: the pair changes by renegotiating what being a pair means. Uranus gains no dignity in Libra, and composite Venus, the ruler, sets the two measures Libra applies to everything: what the two of you consider fair, and what you find lovely. Tradition also exalts [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) in Libra, which suits this placement: the freedom Uranus negotiates holds best when it is written into commitments both of you keep.

In the relationship's own chart, this reads as equality treated as an experiment. The pair is likely to question every inherited rule of coupledom: who earns, who cooks, who moves for whose work, whether you live together at all. Each question gets settled by agreement rather than custom, and freedom is negotiated in the open, with both people expecting the same amount of it. [Uranus in Libra](/astrology/uranus-in-libra/) belongs to a generation that rewrote the rules of being together; in a composite, rewriting them is the bond's ordinary work.

The sign of partnership is no more common in partnership charts than any other: about 1 pair in 12 in my count of two million random pairs born 1930–2009. Couples the same age take their generation's sign, and Uranus was in Libra from September 1968 to September 1975: those couples are in their fifties now. Between ages it works by halves. Someone of 61 and someone of 48 today, born in mid-1965 and mid-1978 with Uranus at about 11° Virgo and 13° Scorpio, have a composite Uranus at about 12° Libra: Uranus was crossing Libra in the years halfway between their births.

The trap is a relationship that is always in negotiation. Fairness can turn into a ledger, and a cool, reasonable distance can pass for objectivity when one of you simply needs closeness. What helps is to let some agreements stand for a year without review, and to keep a place for warmth that nobody has to earn. The gift is the other side of the same coin: a Libra composite notices imbalance early, and when one of you has quietly given up more freedom than the other, the bond itself starts to feel wrong long before anyone says so.

Since the sign comes with the birth years, the house says more about the two of you, and it needs both birth times: [composite Uranus in the 7th house](/astrology/composite-uranus-in-7th-house/), Libra's natural house, sets the whole partnership on new terms. [Composite Uranus in Virgo](/astrology/composite-uranus-in-virgo/) reforms routines; [composite Uranus in Scorpio](/astrology/composite-uranus-in-scorpio/), trust. None of this predicts a separation or a wedding. I describe composites without using them in readings; their users set one beside both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and a relationship chart, made from two people's data, needs both people's yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
