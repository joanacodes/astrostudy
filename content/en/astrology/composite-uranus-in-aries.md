Some relationships mend what breaks; this one tends to start again. Composite Uranus in Aries makes the bond itself the part that wants a fresh beginning, and among couples alive today it belongs mostly to the very old, or to pairs born decades apart.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) is the planet of change in a chart: innovation, independence, release from whatever no longer fits. In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), set at the midpoint of your two natal Uranus positions, it describes how the relationship itself changes and how much room it keeps for two free people. [Aries](/astrology/aries/), cardinal fire, gives that change the shape of a first move: the bond acts, then adjusts. The tradition never saw Uranus and gave it no dignity, and Aries is neither Aquarius, its modern sign, nor Leo opposite, so the colour comes from the ruler of Aries: composite [Mars](/astrology/mars/), by sign and house, shows what drives this pair to begin again.

In two million random pairs of birth dates from 1930 to 2009, about 1 pair in 12 has it, as with most signs. Couples are seldom random pairs, though. Uranus spends about seven years in a sign, so two people born in the same stretch share it, and their composite simply repeats it. Uranus crossed Aries from April 1927 to March 1935 and again from May 2010 to March 2019: same-age couples with this composite are in their nineties, and the younger generation is still at school: the site reads no [children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/).

Wide age gaps reach it another way. Past about four decades apart, the two natal positions lie more than half the zodiac from each other, the nearer midpoint swings to the far side, and the composite lands across the circle from where Uranus stood halfway between the births. A grandparent born in June 1946, with Uranus at about 17° Gemini, and a grandchild born in June 2004, at about 7° Pisces, share a composite Uranus at about 27° Aries, although in June 1975, halfway between them, Uranus was finishing Libra. A composite can be drawn for any close bond, family included, and both of these people are adults.

Read as a third thing, the placement says the urge to begin again may belong to neither of you. Two people who each like a plan can find that their relationship keeps choosing the untried option: the first of their friends to swap a fixed address for a year of travel, the first in either family to arrange a shared life in a way nobody has tested. The freedom it asks for is the freedom to act without waiting for permission, granted to each person and to the bond. The natal page, [Uranus in Aries](/astrology/uranus-in-aries/), describes a generation's wish to change things by acting first; in a composite that wish is no longer one person's temperament but the relationship's habit.

The trap is a restart used in place of a repair. When something chafes, a bond like this can reach for a new city, plan or rule rather than mend the old one, and the slower of you may be cast as the obstacle when they are only adjusting at their own pace. Before each new start, say what it is meant to change, and let the one who needs more time set part of the speed.

With the sign held by so few living couples, and always by an age group rather than a pair, the house is where this Uranus becomes yours, and composite houses need both birth times: [composite Uranus in the 1st house](/astrology/composite-uranus-in-1st-house/), for instance, makes the fresh starts the first thing others see. [Composite Uranus in Pisces](/astrology/composite-uranus-in-pisces/), before it, changes by imagining; [composite Uranus in Taurus](/astrology/composite-uranus-in-taurus/), after it, by rebuilding what lasts. My own readings leave composites out; this page explains the method, which belongs beside both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and to two people who have both agreed to share their birth data ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
