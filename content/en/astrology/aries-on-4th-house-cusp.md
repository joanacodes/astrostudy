Under everything else in a birth chart lies the [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), the IC, where the 4th house begins, and with Aries on the 4th house cusp that root is in the first sign. Home, family and the private ground you stand on all start, here, from Aries.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read the sign on the IC as the kind of ground you need in order to feel at home ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)). [Aries](/astrology/aries/) at the root of the chart makes that ground something you claim and build rather than inherit: the flat you found and furnished yourself, the move you decided on, the wall you knocked down because the room felt wrong. You recharge by doing, and a free day at home tends to turn into a project. Belonging comes from taking part in the decisions, so a home where everything is settled for you rarely feels like yours. The [4th house](/astrology/4th-house/) says nothing about who your parents were. It describes the private self underneath, and with Aries there, that self is frank: often more direct at home than anywhere else.

That last point has a reason in the geometry. The IC and the Midheaven are exactly opposite, so with Aries below, Libra is above: in public you weigh, consult and keep things even; at home you say what you want. Neither is the false one. They're the two ends of one axis, and this cusp often needs both, a public life with room for fairness and a private one with room for frankness.

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) rules the 4th when Aries is on its cusp. Tradition gave Mars its fall in Cancer, the 4th's natural sign, as if drive and shelter sat awkwardly together; with this cusp they have to get along, because the planet of effort runs your home. In practice that often means a home that's lived in actively, and a private life that needs room to act. Scorpio nearly always opens the 11th as well, and often the 12th, so Mars, its traditional ruler, shares those houses with Pluto. Where Mars itself sits shows where home life spills into the rest of the chart: the [1st](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-1st-house/), [2nd](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-2nd-house/), [3rd](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/), [4th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-4th-house/), [5th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-5th-house/), [6th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-6th-house/), [7th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-7th-house/), [8th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-8th-house/), [9th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-9th-house/), [10th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-10th-house/), [11th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-11th-house/) or [12th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-12th-house/) house.

The IC is in Aries for about 1 birth in 13, and on that figure London and New York agree. In Placidus, the house system I use, the IC is the cusp of the 4th, and like the Midheaven it takes its sign from the time of day rather than from the latitude, so the share hardly moves from one city to the other. Aries crosses the meridian a little faster than the average sign, which keeps it just under 1 in 12. Sagittarius usually rises with it. With this cusp Aries never opens the 3rd as well, so [Aries on the 3rd house cusp](/astrology/aries-on-3rd-house-cusp/) describes other charts; but in about 1 London chart in 10 it runs on to open the 5th ([duplicated signs](/astrology/duplicated-signs/)), Mars then rules home and play together, and [Aries on the 5th house cusp](/astrology/aries-on-5th-house-cusp/) is yours as well. Aries can also miss the cusps altogether, lying inside one house ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)), in about 1 London birth in 6.

The IC turns through the whole zodiac in a day, so only the birth time can put Aries here, and without it I don't read the chart ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
