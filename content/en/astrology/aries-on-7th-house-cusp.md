With Aries on the 7th house cusp, the zodiac's first sign sits at the point where your chart turns to face someone else. The 7th begins at the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), opposite the Ascendant, so this cusp always comes with [Libra rising](/astrology/libra-rising/): the zodiac's opening pair, Aries and Libra, laid across your horizon the other way round.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The sign on the Descendant describes what you look for, and tend to meet, in the people you face one to one ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)). With [Aries](/astrology/aries/) there, it's the person who acts: the friend who books the tickets, the partner who says what they want before you've finished weighing it up, the colleague who takes the risk you were still pricing. Libra on your Ascendant meets the world through tact and balance, and Aries opposite brings the push, so partnership is often where you find your own directness, by seeing it in someone else first. The [7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) covers business partners and close collaborators as well as love, and the pattern holds there too: you tend to work best beside someone with ventures of their own. Tradition also gave the 7th open rivals; with Aries on its cusp, disagreements with the people closest to you tend to be said out loud, which I count as a mercy rather than a flaw.

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) rules your 7th. Its house is where you tend to meet people one to one and where partnerships do their work; its sign, how they go about it. Scorpio usually opens the 2nd in these charts, in about 4 London charts out of 5, so Mars, its traditional ruler, shares that house with Pluto: what you share with a partner and what you count as your own answer to the same planet, and I read both of Scorpio's rulers. Where your Mars stands, partnership follows: the [1st](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-1st-house/), [2nd](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-2nd-house/), [3rd](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/), [4th](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-4th-house/), [5th](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-5th-house/), [6th](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-6th-house/), [7th](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-7th-house/), [8th](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-8th-house/), [9th](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-9th-house/), [10th](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-10th-house/), [11th](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-11th-house/) or [12th](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-12th-house/) house.

This is the cusp where Aries is most common: about 1 birth in 8 at London's latitude, 1 in 9 at New York's, in Placidus, the house system I use. At London, Aries clears the eastern horizon in about 52 minutes, but it takes nearly three hours to go down in the west, the time Libra needs to rise, and the longer a sign lingers on the Descendant, the more births it collects. In about half the London charts with this cusp, Aries opens a second house as well ([duplicated signs](/astrology/duplicated-signs/)): the 6th, so that one planet runs both your working day and your partnerships ([Aries on the 6th house cusp](/astrology/aries-on-6th-house-cusp/)), or the 8th, where what you share with them lives ([Aries on the 8th house cusp](/astrology/aries-on-8th-house-cusp/)). In about 1 London chart in 6, on the other hand, Aries opens no house at all ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)).

None of this says whether or when you'll marry, stay or part: the 7th describes a way of relating, never its outcome. And the Descendant moves with the Ascendant, a degree every few minutes, so it needs your birth time as much as any house does; without it, I don't read the chart ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
