At the moment you were born, Uranus was beneath your feet, on the far side of the Earth, nearing the lowest point of its daily circle. In the house of home and roots, it asks for a private life arranged your own way, and a home that can change as you do.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 4th house](/astrology/4th-house/) is home, roots and family: where you come from, what you build in order to belong, and the private self underneath. Angular, it rises from the [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), the point opposite the Midheaven, and takes Cancer, the Moon's sign, as its natural sign. With [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) here, independence and reform work at the base of the chart: a home life that has to make room for change, and a family story you may need to rewrite in your own words before it can support you. Because the 4th is the most private of the angular houses, this Uranus often works out of public view: friends may not guess how unconventionally you live.

In Placidus, the house system I use, that angle shifts quickly with the time of day, so a birth time off by a few minutes can move Uranus into the 3rd house or the 5th ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). The sign Uranus was in speaks for your generation; this house speaks for your own front door. [Uranus in the 3rd house](/astrology/uranus-in-3rd-house/) and [Uranus in the 5th house](/astrology/uranus-in-5th-house/) describe the houses on either side, the everyday mind before it and the pleasures after it.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, a 4th-house Uranus needs a home with room in it: your own space, your own hours, a way of living together that suits you both rather than the one you grew up with. You may be drawn to unconventional arrangements, living apart together, shared houses, a life that moves. What makes a partner feel like home is often that they don't try to pin you down. Family history may hold a pattern you want to change rather than repeat, and saying so gently helps everyone, including the relatives who did things the old way.

## At work {#at-work}

The 10th house, opposite, is public life, and a 4th-house Uranus tends to want the base it works from to stay flexible: working from home, irregular hours, a job that can move with you. Some people with this placement build homes or communities for a living, in architecture, housing, care or design. Whatever the work, you do better when your private life isn't dictated by your job, and when home remains the place where you recharge rather than one more office.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The hard part is restlessness where you most need rest. You may move often, redecorate, or rearrange life at home whenever it starts to feel fixed, and wonder why you never quite settle. Old family patterns can make independence feel like disloyalty, or belonging feel like a trap. Autonomy is not isolation: the work is building a home you don't need to escape, and a relationship with your roots that leaves you free.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Make one corner of your home entirely yours, and let it change as you do. Talk with the people you live with about what freedom looks like for each of you. Read the Moon, natural ruler of this house, alongside Uranus. For those born from 1948 to 1956, whose Uranus is in Cancer, the sign of this house, home is underlined twice ([Uranus in Cancer](/astrology/uranus-in-cancer/)). As for [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), it held Uranus to the same tick or cross as every placement: not one of the three that held far more often, while a third or so held across the board.
