Mercury sextile Venus in synastry puts about 60° between one person's way of thinking and the other's idea of what's worth having. The two aren't fused, as in a conjunction, and they don't argue, as in a square: they stand at a comfortable distance and keep finding things to say to each other.

## What flows {#what-flows}

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is the part of a person that notices, wonders and explains, and finds words for it all. [Venus](/astrology/venus/) is the part that judges what's worth having: what pleases them, what they'd pick, what feels like a fair exchange. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) relates the two through opportunity, an opening each person can use or leave.

Holding Mercury, you find the other's tastes interesting to think about. You ask why they love what they love, and their answers give you something to chew on. With your Mercury in Virgo and their Venus in Scorpio, your careful, practical questions draw out what they value deeply and rarely explain.

Holding Venus, you get a hearing for your preferences without having to defend them. Explaining what you love to someone who listens closely is a pleasure in itself, and you come away with better words for your own taste. You can admire the other's mind from a little distance, which keeps the admiration honest.

What comes easily is an exchange of enthusiasms: recommendations that land, a shared list of things to read or see, two colleagues who brainstorm happily, relatives who always know what to give each other.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Ease can stay shallow. A sextile offers an opening and nothing more, so two people can keep to pleasant small talk for years, each enjoying the other and neither asking the next question. The Venus person may never say what they actually think of an idea; the Mercury person may never risk an opinion the other wouldn't like.

The narrow orb matters too: at 3.6°, the contact is often faint, a background warmth rather than a strong current, and it's easy to overlook when other contacts are louder.

How the rest of the pair's angles change the conversation:

- **[Mercury conjunct Venus in synastry](/astrology/mercury-conjunct-venus-synastry/):** words and taste fused, a month or so between birthdays.
- **[Mercury square Venus in synastry](/astrology/mercury-square-venus-synastry/):** impossible within one chart; reasoning and preference rub.
- **[Mercury trine Venus in synastry](/astrology/mercury-trine-venus-synastry/):** also impossible in one chart; the two styles flow together, birthdays about four months apart.
- **[Mercury opposite Venus in synastry](/astrology/mercury-opposite-venus-synastry/):** the widest gap, birthdays about five months apart, and two people on opposite sides of a question.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Put the opening to work. Make a thing together: a reading list, a trip, a playlist, a project at work where one person drafts and the other judges what's good. Ask "what do you like about it?" and mean it; answer honestly when you're asked back. Between friends or siblings, a standing swap of recommendations keeps the line open.

About 1 pair of people in 13 has this sextile, one way round or the other, within 3.6°, and their birthdays usually fall about two months apart, whatever the years, rarely more than four. Inside one chart, a Mercury–Venus sextile is the widest angle the two planets can make, and it needs the Sun between them; among [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/), it's an everyday contact, set mostly by the calendar. In a few hours Mercury and Venus barely shift, so the birth hour seldom settles it, but the houses do depend on it, which is one reason both times come first ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

[Mercury sextile Venus](/astrology/mercury-sextile-venus/), inside one chart, gives a single person thought and taste on good terms, with the Sun sitting between them.
