Wanting to understand your child is one of the most loving reasons anyone has for opening a birth chart. It's also a request I always turn down, and this page explains why, and what I'd suggest instead.

## The short answer {#the-short-answer}

I don't read children's charts. There are no readings for anyone under 18, and none about them either, including inside a [family synastry](/synastry/), where both people must be adults. Once a child has grown up, the chart is theirs: they can come for a reading of their own or, if they're happy with it, sit down with you for a family synastry done together. That's what I can offer a parent, and it's all I offer.

The same principle keeps the charts of real people off this site ([Reading public figures](/astrology/reading-public-figures/)): a chart belongs to the person whose birth it records, and they should have a say in who reads it. A baby can't have that say, and a fifteen-year-old shouldn't have to defend it against a parent.

## Why it matters {#why-it-matters}

- **A chart can become a label.** Read at the wrong age, it can turn into something a child grows into. Tell a child, or the adults around them, that they're stubborn because of their Moon or difficult because of a square, and people start looking for the stubbornness and the difficulty, and so does the child. Adults can argue with a reading. Children tend to believe it.
- **It holds the most personal parts of a person.** In [my own tests](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the placements that held best were Saturn, Lilith and Chiron, 75 to 90% of the time: [the hardest lesson, the wound a person keeps circling, the part of them they were told to hide](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/). Those are exactly what a child hasn't chosen to show anyone yet, and a parent reading them alone holds a strange kind of power over that child's story.
- **A child can't say no.** Consent is what everything else stands on, in one chart as in two ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#your-child)). A child's birth details belong to the child as well, and [Your birth details are personal data](/astrology/your-birth-details-are-personal-data/) says why that matters at any age.

Tradition gave children to the [5th house](/astrology/5th-house/) of a parent's chart, and some astrologers still look for them there. I don't: a chart can't say whether or when anyone will have a child ([Can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/)), or what that child will be like. And when I describe the signs the slow planets have crossed in recent years, I describe the sky of a time, shared by everyone born under it, never a particular child. The house that would make it personal can wait until they're old enough to ask.

## What to do instead {#what-to-do-instead}

- **Get to know the child you have.** Watch what absorbs them, what frightens them, how they recover from a bad day. They'll tell you more than a chart could, and they'll correct you when you're wrong, which a chart never will.
- **If you're worried, ask the people who see them.** Sleep, mood, learning, friendships, health: their doctor, their teachers and, where it helps, a specialist who works with children. A worry about a child deserves a real answer.
- **Read yourself.** If you're thinking about your child because you're thinking about how you parent, your own chart is the place to look: how you love, what you need, what you protect. That's the half of the story you can change.
- **Keep their birth details safe.** Write the time of birth down with the date and place, somewhere it won't get lost ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). One day they may want their chart read, and the choice should be theirs, with everything they need to make it.
- **Wait for them.** At 18 or later, if they're curious, they can come for a reading of their own, or with you, if that's what they'd like. A chart doesn't expire, and it will be all the better read by someone old enough to say what fits.
