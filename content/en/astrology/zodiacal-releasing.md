Some timing techniques mark a year. Zodiacal releasing marks chapters: it takes one point of your chart, a lot, and counts the long stretches of a life from it, sign after sign, each under the planet that rules its sign. It is one of the techniques I use when I look ahead.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A lot is a point you calculate rather than observe ([doctrine of the lots](/astrology/doctrine-of-the-lots/)), and zodiacal releasing is a way of telling time from one. The lot most often named is the Lot of Spirit, the Sun's lot, which tradition read for the initiative a person takes and the use they make of what they're given ([Part of Spirit](/astrology/part-of-spirit/)). Its partner, the Lot of Fortune, is the Moon's lot, read for the body and the circumstances that come to you ([Part of Fortune](/astrology/part-of-fortune/)). Counting from one or the other tells two different stories about the same life.

The method goes back to [Vettius Valens](/astrology/vettius-valens/), a working astrologer of the second century whose *Anthology*, written in Greek between about 150 and 175, is the longest and most detailed treatise on astrology to survive from that period, with more than a hundred sample charts from his own case files ([Vettius Valens’ Anthology](/astrology/vettius-valens-anthology/)). The name *zodiacal releasing* is a modern one. Astrologers read the technique again today from the translations of the [traditional revival](/astrology/traditional-revival/).

It belongs with the time-lord techniques, where one planet at a time takes charge. [Firdaria](/astrology/firdaria/) hand out fixed periods by a table; [decennials](/astrology/decennials/) are another scheme of the same family; zodiacal releasing takes its chapters from the signs of your own chart.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

First, the lot. The Lot of Spirit is counted from the Ascendant using the distance between the Sun and the Moon, one way for a day birth and the other for a night birth, so the [sect](/astrology/sect-day-and-night-charts/) of the chart comes into it before anything else. Programs don't all reverse the formulas at night, so check which one yours uses.

Then the count. The sign the lot falls in opens the first chapter, and the chapters follow in the order of the signs. Each one goes to the planet that rules its sign. For Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces there are two rulers, a traditional one and a modern one, and I read both ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)). The timing of each chapter follows the rules Valens set out; a program gives you the dates once it has your birth details.

All of it hangs on the birth time. The lots move with the Ascendant, round the whole zodiac in about a day, so an hour's error can put the Lot of Spirit in another sign and shift every chapter after it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

A chapter is a change of emphasis. Counted from Spirit, it describes what you are working towards in that stretch of years; counted from Fortune, the conditions you're living in. It never names an event.

When I read a chapter, I start with its sign, the manner of the period, and then its ruler: where that planet sits in your birth chart, by sign, house and aspect, and how comfortably. Planets already in the chapter's sign add their own functions to it. Because the count goes sign by sign and I use [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/) houses, a chapter's sign can hold the end of one house and the start of the next, and I read both.

Zodiacal releasing is one strand of my forecasts, beside [annual profections](/astrology/annual-profections/), [transits](/astrology/transits/), secondary progressions, solar arcs and the solar return. Its chapters give the long-term background; the shorter techniques show when something in that background is stirred. A chapter whose ruler is also the lord of the profection year, or is met by a slow transit, is one I give real weight to.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

Astrologers who use zodiacal releasing don't all interpret it the same way, so two readings of the same chart can describe a chapter differently. Ask which lot a reading counts from, and how it treats day and night births.

It is symbolic time from start to finish. Nothing moves in the sky when a chapter turns, and nothing in the technique guarantees success, recognition or failure in any period. A chapter read on its own, without the birth chart and the rest of the timing, says very little ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).
