Venus in Taurus, Saturn in Capricorn, the Moon in Cancer: some placements read as if the planet had come home, and the tradition agrees. Its word for a planet in its own sign is domicile, the first of the old measures of how well a planet suits the place where it stands.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A planet's domicile is a sign it rules. Stand it in that sign and it is in domicile, or, as most astrologers put it now, in its own sign. In the traditional scheme, the one [Ptolemy](/astrology/ptolemy/) sets out, the Sun rules Leo and the Moon Cancer; Mercury rules Gemini and Virgo, Venus Taurus and Libra, Mars Aries and Scorpio, Jupiter Sagittarius and Pisces, Saturn Capricorn and Aquarius. How the signs came to be shared out that way, and how three more planets joined in, is told in [traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/).

Modern tropical astrology adds three homes: Uranus in Aquarius, Neptune in Pisces, Pluto in Scorpio. Those signs then have two rulers each, and I read both. The tradition never saw the outer planets, so an old table of dignities is simply silent about them.

Domicile heads the five essential dignities, the tradition's measures of how much at home a planet is at a given degree: domicile, [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/), triplicity, terms and face. When [William Lilly](/astrology/william-lilly/) put points on them in *Christian Astrology* (1647), he gave domicile 5, more than any of the others ([The table of dignities](/astrology/table-of-dignities/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Rulership runs in two directions. A planet rules its sign wherever the planet happens to be, so the sign's business follows it round the chart: with Taurus on the cusp of your 2nd house, Venus carries that house's story from wherever it stands ([house rulers](/astrology/house-rulers/)). When the planet is in its own sign, the two meet, and the ruler sits among the affairs it rules.

It also answers to no other planet. Astrologers trace a chain from each planet to the ruler of the sign it occupies, then on to that planet's ruler: Mercury in Libra hands over to Venus, and Venus in Sagittarius hands over to Jupiter. A planet in domicile is its own ruler, so the chain stops there ([dispositors and the final dispositor](/astrology/dispositors-and-the-final-dispositor/)).

Two planets can also stand in each other's homes, as Mercury in Capricorn and Saturn in Virgo do. That pairing has a name and a page of its own: [mutual reception](/astrology/mutual-reception/).

Opposite every domicile lies the planet's [detriment](/astrology/detriment/): Scorpio and Aries for Venus, Capricorn for the Moon. The twelve signs that give each planet a home also mark the place furthest from it.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The system I built for my readings treats every planet as a function and every sign as a manner: the sign changes how a function works, never what it is. Domicile is the one case where the two were cut from the same cloth. Saturn in Capricorn builds the way Saturn builds, patiently and to last; Mercury in Gemini thinks the way Mercury thinks, in several directions at once. Nothing needs translating.

So I read a planet at home as fluent rather than fortunate. Fluency is a real strength, and it has a blind spot: what comes naturally is seldom questioned. A Moon in Cancer can look after everyone so readily that its own needs go unsaid; a Jupiter in Sagittarius can find meaning so easily that it stops checking it. The dignity tells me how well the function fits its sign. The house, the aspects and the life you bring to the reading tell me what you do with that fit, which is why no single factor decides a reading ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

The outer planets' homes belong to whole generations. Everyone born between April 1995 and December 2003 has Uranus in Aquarius; between November 1983 and November 1995, Pluto in Scorpio; between April 2011 and January 2026, Neptune in Pisces. For those, the house the planet falls in is what makes it personal, and the house needs an exact birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A planet in its own sign is lucky.”** It's comfortable, which isn't the same thing. Dignity describes fit, not fortune, and no placement promises how a life will go.
- **“Every planet has one home.”** The Sun and Moon have one sign each; Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn have two in the traditional scheme. Uranus, Neptune and Pluto have one each, and only in the modern scheme.
- **“A planet in domicile is the strongest planet in the chart.”** Sign is one measure among several. A planet at home can still be [retrograde](/astrology/retrograde-explained/), lost in the Sun's light or tucked away in a quiet house, and each of those changes how much it shows.
- **“Domicile and exaltation are the same thing.”** They are two dignities, usually in two different signs. Domicile is the planet's own sign; exaltation is the sign where it is treated as an honoured guest.
