Of all the angles the node makes to your Moon, the sextile is the lightest, and the easiest to let pass. For a month or so the point of growth sits two signs away from your Moon, while the South Node, across the axis, trines it.

## What changes {#what-changes}

The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) describes what you need and how you settle; the [North Node](/astrology/north-node/), a capacity that grows the more you practise it. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) relates them through opportunity: the two can cooperate, on condition that you do something with the opening. Under this [transit](/astrology/transits/), a new way of meeting a need comes within easy reach, and nothing makes you take it.

It tends to arrive small. An invitation to a group you'd normally skip; a routine that suddenly looks possible; a conversation in which you say what you need and it's heard. Because the South Node trines your Moon in the same weeks, the old comforts stay close and easy, which helps: you can try something new without giving up what steadies you. The risk is only that comfort wins by default, and the month goes by as months do.

With your [Moon in the 3rd house](/astrology/moon-in-3rd-house/), which tradition named the Goddess and made the Moon's joy, the opening is likely to come through everyday words and the people close at hand: a neighbour, a brother or sister, a message you finally answer. Your houses are drawn from your birth time, and the Moon's degree depends on it too: each hour moves your Moon about half a degree, and this transit by some ten days. That is part of why the birth time matters so much to me ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Within my orbs, 1½° before exact and 1° after, the sextile usually lasts about a month. Now and then it stretches: a tenth of passes run to three and a half months or more; about one in twenty-five goes back and forth over the exact sextile three times. An eclipse season may fall in those weeks, and the node slows almost to a stop in it; but its eclipses then land a couple of signs from your Moon, and the rule I use takes an eclipse into account only on a natal point or the degree opposite. My forecast leaves the sextile itself out on the same principle: it reads the nodes as one axis and counts them only by conjunction or opposition.

Two sextiles come in each round of the nodes. The first falls about three years after the node's conjunction with your Moon ([Transiting North Node conjunct natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-north-node-conjunct-natal-moon/)), the second about three years before the next one, so they arrive alternately some twelve and six years apart, and by 80 most people have had eight or nine. Between the two come [Transiting North Node square natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-north-node-square-natal-moon/), [Transiting North Node trine natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-north-node-trine-natal-moon/) and, halfway round, [Transiting North Node opposite natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-north-node-opposite-natal-moon/). With [Moon sextile North Node](/astrology/moon-sextile-north-node/) in your chart, your nodal return, every 18.6 years, sets the node at this same sextile to your Moon.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Say yes to one small invitation you would usually turn down for being unfamiliar, and see how you feel about it a week later.

Start a habit of care small enough to keep: ten minutes, at the same time each day, for the length of the month.

Tell someone near you what you need, plainly, once. A sextile rewards asking more than hoping.
