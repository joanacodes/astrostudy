Ceres was the first body found between Mars and Jupiter, on the first night of 1801, and for half a century it was listed as a planet. Today it's a dwarf planet, and the one astrologers turn to for care: who feeds whom, and what happens when something loved is taken away for a season.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Ceres takes its meaning from its goddess. The Romans honoured her as the goddess of grain crops, fertility and motherly relationships. In the Greek story of her counterpart, Demeter, the daughter is carried off to the underworld and must spend a third of every year there, and the growing season follows her return. Astrologers who use the asteroid read care in it, and the cycle of losing and getting back. It was the first of four bodies found within six years; [Pallas](/astrology/pallas/) came next, in 1802.

The system I built for my readings doesn't compute the asteroids, so Ceres is something I describe here rather than read in your chart. Those who do read it look at three things:

- **Its sign:** the manner of your care, whether practical, spoken, protective or playful.
- **Its house:** the part of life where you give care, or most want to receive it.
- **Its aspects:** which other functions your care has to work with.

It's usually read beside the [Moon](/astrology/moon/), which describes your needs and what comforts you; Ceres is the act of tending, given and received. With [Venus](/astrology/venus/) it touches pleasure and food shared; with [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), duty and the limits of what one person can carry. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) fuses care with the other planet's function. A [square](/astrology/square/) makes the two rub until care finds a better form, and an [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) sets giving against needing, two ends to balance rather than choose between.

## Ceres through the signs {#through-the-signs}

With an orbit of 4.6 years, Ceres moves far more slowly than the Moon, so many people born close to you share its sign. This site has no ephemeris for the asteroids, so I give no dates; a chart program that includes Ceres will show yours, and the house is what makes it particular.

- **Ceres in Aries:** care as action. You fix, defend and get people moving, and feel looked after when someone takes your side at once.
- **Ceres in Taurus:** care through food, comfort and steady presence; a good meal says what words might not.
- **Ceres in Gemini:** care through talk. You explain, check in, pass on what you've read, and need to be listened to in return.
- **Ceres in Cancer:** care through home and belonging, warm and protective, with a habit of holding on.
- **Ceres in Leo:** care through attention and praise; you nourish people by letting them shine.
- **Ceres in Virgo:** care through useful help, routines and small repairs done properly.
- **Ceres in Libra:** care through company and fairness: the shared table, the effort split evenly.
- **Ceres in Scorpio:** care that stays through a crisis, loyal and private, sometimes braced for the next loss.
- **Ceres in Sagittarius:** care through encouragement and room to grow; you feed people with horizons.
- **Ceres in Capricorn:** care as provision and reliability, the structure others lean on.
- **Ceres in Aquarius:** care for groups and causes, with respect for everyone's independence.
- **Ceres in Pisces:** care through empathy and imagination, at the risk of dissolving into other people's needs.

## Ceres in the houses {#in-the-houses}

The house needs the [birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/): in Placidus, the house system I use, the Ascendant moves about a degree every four minutes, and every cusp moves with it. Tradition gave the 5th, 6th, 8th and 12th houses children, illness, death and confinement. Read for Ceres, they describe where care is given, and no line below predicts any of those ([Can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/)).

1. **1st house:** care is part of how you come across; people bring you their troubles before they know you well.
2. **2nd house:** looking after yourself through resources and bodily comfort, with your worth tied to what you can provide.
3. **3rd house:** care through words and daily errands, with siblings, neighbours and the people you see every day.
4. **4th house:** home as the place where people are fed; you make a home for others wherever you live.
5. **5th house:** care through play and making things, and through encouraging what others create.
6. **6th house:** the daily round of meals, routines and work that looks after people.
7. **7th house:** care inside partnership, and the open question of who tends whom.
8. **8th house:** care through hard passages and shared resources; you stay with someone through a loss.
9. **9th house:** care through teaching and ideas, feeding minds rather than mouths.
10. **10th house:** care as a public role, or the vocation people know you for.
11. **11th house:** care for friends, groups and the communities you choose.
12. **12th house:** quiet care behind the scenes, and needs you find hard to ask for.

## Ceres in synastry {#in-synastry}

In my [synastry readings](/synastry/) I count only contacts that involve at least one personal body, and the asteroids aren't among the bodies I use. Astrologers who do bring Ceres into a comparison read it both ways round. When your Ceres meets someone's Moon or Venus, you tend to look after them in a way they recognise, and they tend to feel fed by you, in every sense. The same contact can tip into care that manages the other person, and that is worth naming early. Whatever two charts show, both people should agree to the comparison first: [why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/).

## Transits and the Ceres return {#ceres-return}

Ceres comes back to the place it held at your birth roughly every four and a half years: the Ceres return. My forecasts use the transits of Jupiter to Pluto, Chiron and the true node, and Ceres isn't among them. Astrologers who follow it read a transit of Ceres over the Moon or Venus as a time when care, given or needed, comes to the front. It's weather, not a forecast: a theme to notice, never an event. Before adding Ceres and its companions to every chart you look at, read [How to use asteroids without drowning the chart](/astrology/how-to-use-asteroids-without-drowning-the-chart/).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Ceres is just an asteroid.”** Since 2006 it has been classed a dwarf planet, the only one in the asteroid belt.
- **“Ceres is the mother asteroid.”** Its goddess was a mother, but the care it describes is given by anyone, to anyone, yourself included.
- **“A prominent Ceres means you'll end up a carer.”** It describes a way of caring, not a duty or a job.
- **“Ceres replaces the Moon.”** The Moon is what you need; Ceres is how care passes between people. Astrologers who use it read the two together.
