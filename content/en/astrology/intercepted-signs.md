Count the signs round a chart drawn in Placidus and you may find one missing from the cusps: it's in the chart, planets and all, but no house begins in it. That sign is intercepted. It's a matter of geometry, not of fate, and it says as much about where you were born as about you.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Each house runs from its own cusp to the next, and each takes its sign from the degree on its cusp ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)). When a house is wider than a sign, it can hold the whole of one, from 0° to 30°, with cusps of other signs on either side. That sign has no cusp of its own: it is intercepted.

Take a birth in London with about 7° Sagittarius rising and 5° Libra on the Midheaven. In [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/), the 3rd house cusp falls at about 27° Aquarius and the 4th at about 5° Aries, so the whole of Pisces lies inside the 3rd house. Opposite cusps always mirror each other, so Virgo lies inside the 9th. Intercepted signs always come in pairs, on one axis.

And they never come alone. Twelve cusps share out twelve signs; if two signs get none, two others get two each. In the same chart, Taurus opens both the 5th and the 6th houses, and Scorpio both the 11th and the 12th ([duplicated signs](/astrology/duplicated-signs/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Interceptions come from unequal houses, and unequal houses come from the slant of the zodiac. The Ascendant moves through some signs quickly and others slowly, and Placidus divides the sky by time, so some houses stretch while others shrink ([which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)). Near the equator, Placidus houses stay close to 30° and interceptions are the exception. At the latitude of London, a house can be under 20° wide or over 50°, and most Placidus charts drawn there have an intercepted pair.

Two house systems never produce one. In [Whole Sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/) each sign is a house; in [equal houses](/astrology/equal-houses/) each house is exactly 30° from the Ascendant's degree, so no sign can fit wholly inside. The other quadrant systems, Koch and Porphyry among them, intercept signs as Placidus does, though not always the same ones.

Because cusps turn with the sky, a degree every four minutes or so, an interception depends on the birth time. A few minutes can create one or remove it, which is one more reason I don't read a chart without the time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read each house from its cusp: the sign there, then its ruler, then any planets inside ([house rulers](/astrology/house-rulers/)). An intercepted sign adds a second voice to the house that holds it. In the London example, the 3rd house speaks first through Aquarius, its cusp sign, and its rulers, Uranus and Saturn; Pisces sits inside it as well, with its own two rulers, Neptune and Jupiter. I read both rulers for each, and the house's story through all of them.

Planets in an intercepted sign are read like any others: by sign, house and aspects. Nothing in the geometry holds them back. If the intercepted sign sits in a house you came with a question about, the 7th for partners or the 2nd for money, it's one more strand in that topic, not a missing one ([houses by topic](/astrology/houses-by-topic-love-money-work-home/)).

What an interception does change is the bookkeeping of rulers: the intercepted sign's ruler governs no house by its cusp, while each duplicated sign's ruler governs two. I mention that in a reading when it matters, as one strand among several ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“An intercepted sign is blocked or locked away.”** It's an effect of house sizes. The sign and any planets in it work as fully as any others.
- **“Interceptions mean the chart was drawn wrong.”** They're normal in quadrant systems, and common at the latitudes of Britain and France.
- **“Switch to Whole Sign houses and the problem goes away.”** It does, and so does every Placidus house. Choose a house system for its reasons, then keep to it.
