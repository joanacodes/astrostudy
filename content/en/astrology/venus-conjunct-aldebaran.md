Venus conjunct Aldebaran puts the planet of taste and affection on the Bull's red eye, a star of the constellation that shares its name with Venus's own sign. It is the rarest of Aldebaran's contacts: about 1 person in 187 born between 1930 and 2009 has it.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read [Venus](/astrology/venus/) as what you value and how you choose: the things and people you find beautiful, what you want to keep, how you like affection to go back and forth. In Ptolemy's account [Aldebaran](/astrology/aldebaran/) has nothing of Venus; he likens it to Mars alone. Medieval magic saw it differently. Its list of fifteen Behenian stars, discussed by Agrippa, pairs Aldebaran with Mars and Venus together, so the later tradition gives the star a share of this planet's nature. Laid over Venus, the old picture is affection with some fire in it, a taste that goes after what it likes.

The sign holds a twist. Venus rules [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), the sign, and Aldebaran is the eye of Taurus, the constellation; but the star now stands at 10° of Gemini, and stood at 9°47′ there in 2000. A Venus on the Bull's eye is therefore a [Venus in Gemini](/astrology/venus-in-gemini/), drawn first by conversation and wit rather than settled comfort, and it is the sign, not the constellation, that I read ([constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/)).

Venus never strays more than about 47° from the Sun, so it can sit on Aldebaran only for births between about 12 April and 16 July, with the Sun anywhere from late Aries to late Cancer. Each year it passes the star once. Only one summer in these eighty years did it turn round there. Venus passed between the Earth and the Sun on 8 June 2004, came back over the star's degree on 26 June, stood still on 30 June about a fifth of a degree from it, and crossed it forward again on 3 July. Births in those days have Venus slow, and for some of them retrograde, on the Bull's eye ([Venus retrograde in Gemini](/astrology/venus-retrograde-in-gemini/), [natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)).

The two can come close in the sky as well. Aldebaran lies five and a half degrees south of the ecliptic, and Venus, which strays several degrees from it when nearest the Earth, passed the star within about a degree in some years and nine degrees to the north in others.

Whatever the star adds, Venus keeps its own job. In my readings it describes what you value and enjoy, and how you go about having it; a Mars-tinged star may sharpen that picture in the tradition's eyes, but it makes no one luckier in love, and I don't read it as if it did.

Venus is the rarest of the five bodies on this star. The [Sun conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/sun-conjunct-aldebaran/) comes to about 1 in 176, the [Moon conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/moon-conjunct-aldebaran/) to 1 in 178, [Mars conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/mars-conjunct-aldebaran/) to 1 in 181 and [Saturn conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-aldebaran/) to 1 in 186. My readings leave stars out altogether, so I set this one down as a description; [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/) has the rule, and [fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/) the history.
