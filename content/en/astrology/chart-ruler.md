Your rising sign describes how you come through the door. Its ruler, the chart ruler, shows where you go once you're inside, and it's the planet astrologers look for straight after the Ascendant.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A sign's ruler is the planet at home in it. The chart ruler is simply the ruler of the sign on your [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), the one that was rising in the east when you were born; since the Ascendant opens the [1st house](/astrology/1st-house/), it rules the 1st as well. Every rising sign gives one: Mars for Aries, Venus for Taurus and Libra, Mercury for Gemini and Virgo, the Moon for Cancer, the Sun for Leo, Jupiter for Sagittarius, Saturn for Capricorn. Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces each give two: Pluto and Mars, Uranus and Saturn, Neptune and Jupiter.

So any body from the Sun to Pluto can rule a chart, the [luminaries](/astrology/luminaries/) included.

It takes a birth time. The Ascendant changes sign about every two hours on average, so two people born on the same day can have different chart rulers, and a time rounded to the hour can hand you the wrong one ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Once you know which planet it is, you read it like any other planet, with one difference: whatever it touches is tied back to you.

- **Its house** is where your life tends to gather, whatever the rising sign promised. With the chart ruler in the 10th, your sense of self and your public direction are hard to pull apart ([Ruler of the 1st in the 10th house](/astrology/1st-house-ruler-in-10th-house/)); in the 7th, you come to know yourself through the people you pair with ([Ruler of the 1st in the 7th house](/astrology/1st-house-ruler-in-7th-house/)).
- **Its sign** is the manner in which it gets there: Mars in Taurus takes its time, Mars in Gemini argues its way.
- **Its aspects** show which other parts of the chart it has to work with.
- **Its condition** is how comfortable it is where it stands, in its own sign or exaltation or in detriment or fall ([the chart ruler's condition](/astrology/chart-rulers-condition/)).

The planet keeps its own character, too. Tradition sorted the planets into [benefics and malefics](/astrology/benefics-and-malefics/), and some people are alarmed to find Saturn or Mars ruling their chart. A Saturn-ruled chart tends to meet life carefully and for the long haul, a Mars-ruled one directly. That's a way of going about things, not a forecast of how life will go.

The same logic applies to every house: the planet that rules the sign on a cusp carries that house's business to wherever it sits ([house rulers](/astrology/house-rulers/)). The 1st simply happens to be about you.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

With Scorpio, Aquarius or Pisces rising there are two rulers, a modern one and a traditional one, and I read both ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)). The modern one is slow, so many people born in the same years share its sign, and its house is what makes it personal; the traditional one moves faster and changes more from one person to the next. When they point to different houses, the chart has two threads to follow, and I follow both.

The chart ruler also extends the [big three](/astrology/big-three/): the Sun, the Moon and the rising sign give a first sketch, and the chart ruler shows where the rising sign's energy goes. In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the rising sign held about a third of the time, so I hold this thread with some care. The exception is Saturn, ruler of Capricorn and traditional ruler of Aquarius: it was one of the three placements that held 75 to 90% of the time, and wherever it sits gets the closest reading ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Your ruling planet is the ruler of your Sun sign.”** That's how many horoscope columns use the phrase. The chart ruler belongs to the rising sign, and the two coincide only when your Sun sign and rising sign share a ruler.
- **“A chart ruled by Saturn or Mars is a hard chart.”** Tradition called both malefics. As a chart ruler, each describes a way of meeting life, carefully or directly, never a sentence passed on it.
- **“With two rulers, you have to pick one.”** You can read both, and I do: the slow one through its house, the fast one as the more personal voice.
- **“You can work out your chart ruler from your birthday.”** Only the time and place of birth give the rising sign, and the ruler follows from it.
