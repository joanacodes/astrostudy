Saturn keeps a structure standing; Uranus asks whether it should be standing that way. Twice in most lives the two meet at right angles across your chart, Uranus in the sky squaring your natal Saturn, and for about twenty months what you've built has to adjust while it's still carrying weight.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) establishes structure, responsibility and the boundaries that make long work possible. [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) frees what has grown too tight and tries other arrangements. A [square](/astrology/square/), 90°, relates two functions through constructive friction, so under this [transit](/astrology/transits/) the urge to reform presses on the very things you've made stable.

The friction usually gathers around one structure: a job's routine, a family rule, a role you've held so long it has become part of you, a self-discipline that has turned into a cage. Something in it no longer fits, and the pressure comes from both ends at once, the wish to hold on hard and the wish to be rid of the whole thing.

Both ends have a blind spot. Saturn believes control guarantees safety; Uranus assumes disruption brings progress by itself. The square asks for a third way: change made under load, one beam at a time, so the structure keeps working while it's rebuilt. That's slower than either end wants, and it's what tends to hold.

With Saturn in your 1st and Uranus crossing your 10th ([Uranus transiting the 10th house](/astrology/uranus-transit-10th-house/)), the way you hold yourself together meets a push to work and be seen differently. Your birth time places both houses; when it's missing, there is no reading from me ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). With [Saturn square Uranus](/astrology/saturn-square-uranus/) in your own chart, from a birth in 1930–32, 1950–53, 1975–77 or 1999–2001, the friction is familiar: the transit gives it a date.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Within my orbs, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the square lasts about twenty months, and its quickest passes about eleven. In more than nine passes out of ten Uranus crosses the exact square three times, and each crossing tends to bring the question back.

The two squares to Saturn face each other across the chart, so they come about forty years apart, a few more or less with Uranus's changing speed. In the site's count, someone born at the start of 1955 met the first before the age of 5, someone born at the start of 1975 at about 39; by 80, every birth from 1930 to 2009 has had at least one, and most have had both.

Born between March 1964 and March 1967, with Saturn in Pisces opposite Uranus ([Saturn opposite Uranus](/astrology/saturn-opposite-uranus/)), you meet these squares within a few years of your own Uranus squares ([transiting Uranus square natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-uranus-square-natal-uranus/)): the first between about 16 and 22, the second in your early or mid sixties, while Uranus crosses Gemini, until 2033.

From the first square, [transiting Uranus trine natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-uranus-trine-natal-saturn/) is about seven years on and [transiting Uranus opposite natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-uranus-opposite-natal-saturn/) about twenty-one; [transiting Uranus sextile natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-uranus-sextile-natal-saturn/) and [transiting Uranus conjunct natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-uranus-conjunct-natal-saturn/) sit on the far side of the circle, between the second square and the first.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Find the beam that's actually cracked. Under this square the whole structure can feel wrong; usually one part of it is, and that's where the work goes.

Negotiate the pace. Saturn wants slower and Uranus faster, and a date for each step settles more arguments, inside you and around you, than a decision to change everything at once.

Let go of one rule on purpose. A square to Saturn often eases once you choose what to drop, rather than waiting for it to be pulled away.
