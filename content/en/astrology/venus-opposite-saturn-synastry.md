In many pairs, one person is the one who suggests the treat and the other the one who checks the calendar. Venus opposite Saturn in synastry is one reason it can fall out that way: one person's Venus and the other's Saturn sit on opposite sides of the zodiac, and pleasure and prudence end up seated at either end of the same seesaw.

## What flows {#what-flows}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) is how someone loves and what they prize: affection, taste, the feeling that a give-and-take is even. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) is how they hold things together: limits, duty, plans made for the long run. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) sets two functions across from each other, so that each shows up the other; among the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/), the two ends are lived by two different people.

Between them, a pair with this opposition often covers both ends of a good life: something to enjoy, and the structure that keeps it possible. A friendship has its outings and someone who remembers to book them. The Venus person sees what a limit protects by seeing it held; the Saturn person sees what a pleasure is worth by seeing it enjoyed.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Seesaws work by contrast, and contrast can harden into roles. If the Venus is yours, you may become "the fun one" by default, and start to feel the other weighs down every plan; it's tempting to play lighter still, just to keep the balance. If the Saturn is yours, you may become "the sensible one", the adult in every room, holding limits for both of you and getting little thanks; it's tempting to grow stricter in answer. Each can start to blame the other for the end they carry. An opposition is a seesaw, never enmity, and the push from one side is what keeps the other in the air.

The pair's four other contacts arrange the same ends differently:

- **[Venus conjunct Saturn in synastry](/astrology/venus-conjunct-saturn-synastry/):** the treat and the timetable in one place, with no seesaw to ride.
- **[Venus sextile Saturn in synastry](/astrology/venus-sextile-saturn-synastry/):** two ends that cooperate as soon as someone invites them to.
- **[Venus square Saturn in synastry](/astrology/venus-square-saturn-synastry/):** a quarter turn, where terms get argued rather than roles.
- **[Venus trine Saturn in synastry](/astrology/venus-trine-saturn-synastry/):** pleasure and prudence sharing an element, so nobody has to hold only one.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Swap ends sometimes, on purpose. Let the Saturn person plan a pleasure from start to finish, and let the Venus person hold a limit the other would normally guard. Name the roles out loud, with some humour, and check now and then that the division of labour is chosen rather than assumed. Between flatmates, that might mean trading who books and who budgets; between a parent and a grown child, who hosts and who helps; between colleagues, who pitches the idea and who costs it.

About 1 pair of people in 15 has this opposition, one way round or the other, within 6°. Put the Saturn person beside anyone born within a few months of them and Saturn has barely moved; put the Venus person beside their own siblings and Venus could sit anywhere within two signs of each one's Sun. So the slow end belongs to a cohort, and the fast end is what makes the opposition theirs. The hour of birth hardly moves either planet, but the houses hang on it, and with an opposition they show which two parts of life face each other: home and career, say, or self and partnership ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

My notebook explains where I slow down rather than what this contact does: Saturn, Lilith and Chiron held 75 to 90% of the time, each in someone's own chart, and no opposition between two charts was ever counted ([the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)). [Venus opposite Saturn](/astrology/venus-opposite-saturn/) in one chart is a single person holding both ends. Shared between two charts, the ends are split, and a seesaw needs both riders on board, as the reading does ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
