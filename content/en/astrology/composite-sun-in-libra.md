Libra is the sign tradition calls the Sun's fall, and it's also the sign of relationship itself, which makes a composite Sun here less of a paradox than it sounds. The bond the two of you make has its centre in balance, and it knows itself by how fair things feel between you.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

The composite Sun is the midpoint of two natal Suns, taken the shorter way round, and sometimes the scales are almost level. Take someone born on 1 January 1991 and someone born on 3 July 1990, both at midday, Universal Time: their Suns are almost exactly opposite, and [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) puts its [Sun](/astrology/sun/) at about 11° [Libra](/astrology/libra/). Had the July birth come a day earlier, the nearer midpoint would flip to about 10° Aries, giving the [composite Sun in Aries](/astrology/composite-sun-in-aries/) instead. Pairs born about six months apart live on that edge, and for them even the birth times can decide the sign. Most of the 1 pair in 12 with a Libra composite Sun are nowhere near it.

Read as the relationship's own centre, this puts its identity in reciprocity: turns taken, favours returned, a sense that over a month things come out even. It's revived by harmony and by things that look right, a well-laid table, a room arranged together, and it often finds a purpose in being fair to others too: the pair friends bring their disputes to, or the two who make an awkward gathering easy. The weight is shared, and the bond tends to feel most like itself when neither of you is carrying it alone.

In the tradition, Libra is the Sun's [fall](/astrology/fall/), across the zodiac from Aries, where it is exalted. In one person's chart, the [Sun in Libra](/astrology/sun-in-libra/) describes a self that finds its shape in relation to others. A relationship already lives between two people, so in a composite the fall reads less as a weakness than as a centre that sits in the balance rather than in either of you. [Venus](/astrology/venus/) rules Libra, and composite Venus, by sign and house, shows what this bond values and how it likes things done.

Keeping things even has a cost. A bond organised around harmony can put off the disagreement it needs, and fairness can slide into keeping score. Harmony isn't the absence of conflict, though: a Libra bond can hold a real argument if it's conducted fairly, and it's often better for it. Saying out loud when a week has been uneven helps more than quietly balancing the books. Its neighbours ask other questions of a relationship: Virgo, in the [composite Sun in Virgo](/astrology/composite-sun-in-virgo/), whether it works; Scorpio, in the [composite Sun in Scorpio](/astrology/composite-sun-in-scorpio/), whether it's true. Libra asks whether it's fair.

A composite weighs nothing about the future, neither how long a relationship lasts nor how well it goes, and I don't use the technique in my own readings; astrologers who do weigh it against both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). It would hardly be fair to draw one for a single person's curiosity: the chart takes both people's birth data, so it needs both people's yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
