One of them keeps asking how, with what and by when; the other keeps asking why not. In Saturn square Neptune in synastry, one person's Saturn and the other's Neptune sit about 90° apart, and neither question is wrong; they simply arrive at right angles.

## What flows {#what-flows}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) is the function of limits: what can be held, afforded, finished. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) is the function of ideals: imagination, compassion, the urge to give beyond what's measured. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates them through constructive friction, and among the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) this one gives each to a different person, often of a different generation.

Most squares keep to one modality. Say one chart has Saturn in Libra and the other Neptune in Capricorn, two cardinal signs: a careful sense of what's fair, crossing a generation's ideal of institutions that would actually serve people. Both want to set things going, which keeps the argument active rather than sullen.

What the friction makes possible is an idealism that has been tested, and a realism that hasn't forgotten what it's for. Pairs who keep talking end up with hopes that survive a budget and rules that remember people.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

If the Saturn is yours, the other's hopes can seem to skip the hard parts: who pays, who does it, what happens in winter. You may find yourself cast as the voice of disappointment, a role you never asked for, and you may tighten your limits further each time they're ignored.

If the Neptune is yours, the other's caution can seem to drain the colour from what you care about. Every hope comes back with a "but", and you may start keeping your ideals to yourself, or giving without limits just to prove a point. None of that makes the square a fault line: it rubs because both are trying to protect something.

- **[Saturn conjunct Neptune in synastry](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-neptune-synastry/):** conjunct, the limits and the hopes stop being separable.
- **[Saturn sextile Neptune in synastry](/astrology/saturn-sextile-neptune-synastry/):** an exchange of steps and reasons, offered politely.
- **[Saturn trine Neptune in synastry](/astrology/saturn-trine-neptune-synastry/):** caution and faith trust each other.
- **[Saturn opposite Neptune in synastry](/astrology/saturn-opposite-neptune-synastry/):** facing each other across the zodiac, each holding one end.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Agree on what you're protecting before you argue about how. The Saturn person can learn to say "yes, if"; the Neptune person, "then a smaller version". Partners deciding how much to give to family, colleagues in a school or a hospital, a parent and an adult child arguing about a life in the arts: each can turn the crossing into a plan that keeps its soul.

My two examples involve the same two signs, with the roles swapped. Take a person born in mid-1965, with Saturn in Pisces: everyone born between November 1976 and January 1980, Neptune in Sagittarius, has this square with them. Turn it round, and a person born in mid-1975 has it with everyone born from March 1994 to February 1995, Saturn in Pisces squaring the 1975 Neptune. Since everyone born across those spans shares it, I leave it out of the contacts a synastry counts, which all touch the Sun, the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, the Ascendant or the Midheaven. I read it through the houses, as two eras disagreeing about the possible, more than two people.

Both times are needed to place the houses, while the square is the same at any hour of either day ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Whatever my notebook says about Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, that they held 75 to 90% of the time, it says about placements in one chart; I never tallied a square between two people, so this one has no figure ([the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)).

[Saturn square Neptune](/astrology/saturn-square-neptune/), in one chart, is a person testing their own faith against their own limits. Across two charts, one person brings the faith and the other the limits, and the reading needs both their consent ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
