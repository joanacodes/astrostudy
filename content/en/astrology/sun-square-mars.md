Sun square Mars is the aspect of a strong will that hasn't yet found every use for its strength. The two sit a quarter of the circle apart, purpose on one side, drive on the other, and the friction between them is where you learn, over years, how hard to push and where.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is the centre you organise your life around: identity, vitality, the direction you're growing in. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) is how you go at that life: initiative, assertion, effort, competing, holding a boundary. Set at a [square](/astrology/square/), they rub: constructive friction, in which neither replaces the other and each keeps nudging it.

In plain words, your drive tends to move before your purpose has finished deciding. You push, then check whether it was the right wall; you take on a challenge because it's there, then find out whether it was yours. Effort comes in bursts, sometimes more than a situation needs, and holding back can feel harder than charging. That isn't aggression, and it isn't a temper: assertion is not attack. It's force that hasn't yet been given a clear address.

Friction is not failure, and this square is one of the clearest examples I know. Each time the drive overshoots or the purpose stalls, you learn a little more about aim, timing and what deserves your energy. Over the years that becomes a rare kind of capability: knowing how hard to push, and when not to. I read it as a tendency you work with, never a verdict ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The two signs normally share a modality. Cardinal, as with a Sun in Libra and Mars in Capricorn, it's one way of starting meeting another; fixed, as with a Sun in Taurus and Mars in Leo, two kinds of stubbornness; mutable, as with a Sun in Pisces and Mars in Gemini, effort that scatters until it finds a focus. The houses name the two areas of life that rub: the Sun's house is where you want to become yourself, Mars's is where your energy goes, and the square keeps sending one into the other.

About 1 person in 11 is born under this square, counting within 8°. There are two squares in each cycle of a little over two years, and at both, Mars is still moving forward, so the Sun needs a few weeks to pass through the orb: people born weeks apart in the same year often share it, with very different houses. The Sun is the faster of the two, so it makes and breaks the square; born before exact, you have the Sun still closing in ([applying and separating aspects](/astrology/applying-and-separating-aspects/)).

Set beside its siblings, the square is the one that asks for aim:

- **[Sun conjunct Mars](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mars/):** no angle at all; purpose and drive move as one, and the question becomes rest rather than aim.
- **[Sun sextile Mars](/astrology/sun-sextile-mars/):** a sign closer; the same two functions cooperate, and the drive waits to be called.
- **[Sun trine Mars](/astrology/sun-trine-mars/):** a sign further, in one element; effort runs with purpose instead of across it.
- **[Sun opposite Mars](/astrology/sun-opposite-mars/):** the far end, with Mars retrograde; the friction moves outside and often arrives in other people.

Between two charts, [Sun square Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-square-mars-synastry/) is one person's drive pressing on the other's sense of direction: energising or wearing, depending on how both of you handle friction, and on whether you share a project to aim it at.

## In transits {#in-transits}

In the sky, Mars squares your natal Sun roughly once a year and holds the angle for a few days, and energy tends to run ahead of plans ([transits](/astrology/transits/)). If Mars turns retrograde close to the square, the visit can stretch over weeks.

Those days suit a push you've been putting off, less so patience. Weather, not a forecast: a temperature, never an event.
