Pluto can take from twelve to more than thirty years to cross a sign, so the degree of your birth Pluto is shared with everyone born within a year or two of you. When the North Node slides over it, the direction your chart grows in meets the place where you let go and rebuild, for a month or so, and for a whole age group in turn.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) is where a chart transforms: it reorganises what has become unsustainable, releases what is finished and returns a deeper kind of strength. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) marks the capacity you are building through practice. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) fuses the two, so under this [transit](/astrology/transits/) growth asks for a change at the root rather than at the surface.

It often shows as something you have outgrown and kept: a role, a pattern of control, an arrangement that costs more than it gives. Pluto's need is to see the truth about what no longer works and to reorganise around it; its blind spots are mistaking intensity for truth and control for safety. Across the chart the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) sits opposite your Pluto, on strengths you can lean on while the change happens. Pluto and the nodes are the two placements [evolutionary astrology](/astrology/evolutionary-astrology/) put at the centre, read as the soul's past; I read them in this life. And because Pluto carries old associations with death, I'll say it plainly: no transit foretells a death, an illness or a loss ([Can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)).

The generation sets the calendar. Those born with [Pluto in Scorpio](/astrology/pluto-in-scorpio/), from November 1983 to November 1995, met this conjunction while the node last crossed Scorpio, from August 2012 to February 2014, and meet it again from March 2031 to December 2032, the youngest first. The house sets the rest, and the house needs the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

From 1½° before exact to 1° after, the true node usually takes a month or so to cross your Pluto. If the crossing falls in an eclipse season, the node stalls while the Sun goes by and the pass runs three months or more; about one pass in 25 makes three exact hits. The year or so around this transit, and around the opposition nine years later, is when an eclipse can fall on your Pluto.

The first came at 4.7 for someone born on 1 January 1990 and at 17.7 for 1 January 1995; then every 18.6 years, four or five times by 80. With [Pluto conjunct North Node](/astrology/pluto-conjunct-north-node/) in your chart, each nodal return lays the node on your Pluto again.

About three years later the node sextiles your Pluto ([Transiting North Node sextile natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-north-node-sextile-natal-pluto/)), then squares it ([Transiting North Node square natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-north-node-square-natal-pluto/)) and trines it ([Transiting North Node trine natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-north-node-trine-natal-pluto/)), until the South Node arrives ([Transiting North Node opposite natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-north-node-opposite-natal-pluto/)).

## What helps {#what-helps}

Name one thing that has become unsustainable, and write down what it would take to change it at the root.

Tell the truth about it to one person you trust. Pluto's changes go better in the light than in secret.

Let the change take its time. A deep reorganisation done in stages tends to hold better than one done in a single night.
