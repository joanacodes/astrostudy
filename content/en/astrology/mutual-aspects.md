"Mutual aspect" sounds like a single idea, but it means different things depending on who's counting. In Indian astrology it's a precise term; in Western astrology it's almost a repetition; between two charts it names something you can actually look for.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

In Indian astrology, a mutual aspect is two planets that each cast a full aspect on the other. That needs saying because Indian aspects can run one way: one planet can look at another without being looked at in return ([Vedic astrology (Jyotish)](/astrology/vedic-astrology-jyotish/)).

In Western astrology, every aspect is mutual by definition. Aspects are measured from degree to degree, and a distance is the same from either end: if [Mars](/astrology/mars/) is 120° from [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/), Jupiter is 120° from Mars. The count by degree is also what makes out-of-sign aspects possible, where the degrees and the signs disagree ([out-of-sign aspects](/astrology/out-of-sign-aspects/)).

Between two charts, direction comes back. Your Sun square their Moon isn't the same contact as their Sun square your Moon, and when both happen at once, the contact runs both ways round: the nearest thing synastry has to a mutual aspect ([synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Indian astrology counts aspects from sign to sign, and each planet casts its own, at strengths that differ by planet and by distance. In the Parashari system, three planets have special full aspects: Mars on the 4th and 8th signs from its own, Jupiter on the 5th and 9th, [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) on the 3rd and 10th. So with Jupiter in Aries and Mars in Leo, Jupiter's aspect on Mars is full and Mars's on Jupiter only partial. With Saturn in Aries and Mars in Capricorn, Saturn aspects the 10th sign from it and Mars the 4th from it: each looks at the other in full, a mutual aspect. Classical texts list it among the five main associations, or *sambandha*, between two planets, beside the exchange of signs that Western astrologers call [mutual reception](/astrology/mutual-reception/).

Western aspects are mutual, but motion isn't. The faster planet does the moving, so one applies to the other and later separates from it ([applying and separating aspects](/astrology/applying-and-separating-aspects/)). The aspect still belongs to both.

In synastry, the counts show how often a contact runs both ways. Across random pairs of people born from 1930 to 2009, by The Astro Study's count, one person's Sun squares the other's Moon in about 1 pair in 7, one way round or the other, and both ways at once in about 1 pair in 200. Venus conjunct Mars both ways is rarer still, about 1 pair in 650. A contact between the same planet in both charts, Venus to Venus, can only run both ways.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

In a birth chart, I read every aspect as a conversation between two functions, both of them yours: Mars trine Jupiter is your drive and your sense of scope getting along, whichever you name first. I measure aspects in degrees and don't use Indian aspects, so the one-way aspect, and the mutual aspect in its Indian sense, aren't part of my readings ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

In synastry, direction matters, because each contact has two sides and a good reading says who tends to feel what ([Sun square Moon in synastry](/astrology/sun-square-moon-synastry/)). When the same contact runs both ways, each of you is both the Sun and the Moon in that square. I read it as a theme you share from both sides, so neither of you carries all of it. It isn't a verdict on the relationship, either way. And I only compare two charts when both people know ([Synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Some Western aspects run one way.”** Measured in degrees, an aspect is the same from both ends. Only the motion has a direction.
- **“A mutual aspect is the same as mutual reception.”** Reception is about signs and their rulers; an aspect is an angle. They can come together, and the Indian list of associations names both.
- **“A contact that runs both ways means two people are meant to be.”** It repeats a theme; it doesn't decide a relationship.
