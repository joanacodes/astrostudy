Sun conjunct Mars between two charts puts one person's Mars on the other's Sun. The Mars person's drive lands right where the other's sense of self lives, so being together tends to get things moving. The question this contact keeps asking, in friendships and families as much as in couples or at work, is whose initiative it is.

## What flows {#what-flows}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is the self that does the acting: identity, purpose, agency. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the push: initiative, assertion, effort, the courage to start. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) makes two functions act as one without merging them, and in [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) the two have different owners: here, one person's purpose and the other's push.

If it's your Sun, the other person sets you in motion. Plans that were sitting still start to happen around them; you feel bolder, quicker, more willing to try. Their energy seems aimed at what you are, which can feel like the best kind of encouragement.

If it's your Mars, the other person gives your drive a target worth having. You want to act with them, for them, alongside them; their aims make your effort feel meaningful, and a challenge from them is one you're glad to take up.

It's the contact of training partners, of people founding something together, of siblings renovating a house, of a couple at their best mid-project. As I read Mars, assertion is not aggression, and here it mostly looks like two people getting on with things.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

At a conjunction two things blur, and here it's initiative. The Mars person can take over the Sun person's projects, setting the pace and starting before the other has decided; the Sun person can start borrowing the other's push, and find it hard to move without it. A purpose run on someone else's drive slowly stops feeling like your own. And a challenge meant playfully can land on the Sun person's sense of self rather than on the task.

Spread the two further apart, and the energy finds other routes:

- **[Sun sextile Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-sextile-mars-synastry/):** help that answers when called, with room between the two people.
- **[Sun square Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-square-mars-synastry/):** drive cutting across purpose; contests over pace, and the stamina they build.
- **[Sun trine Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-trine-mars-synastry/):** effort and aims in step, in signs of one element; momentum without collision.
- **[Sun opposite Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-opposite-mars-synastry/):** the drive faces the self from across the zodiac, like a sparring partner.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Agree who leads what, out loud: "this one's yours, I'll back you" settles a lot. The Mars person can ask before acting on the other's behalf; the Sun person can make a point of starting some things alone, to keep their own engine running. Competition is not hostility, so keep contests where they belong, on the court, over a board game, in a race to finish the shelves, and away from who either of you is.

This conjunction joins about 1 pair of people in 12, in one direction or the other, within 8°. Your birthday places your Sun; their Mars depends on the season of the year they were born, since Mars usually spends six or seven weeks in each sign.

A few hours make little difference to where either body stood, except right at the edge of the orb; the times settle the houses, and so where in each life the push is felt, and I read nothing without them ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Held by one person alone, [Sun conjunct Mars](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mars/) is purpose that arrives already moving. Here the movement comes from someone else, and that's why both people need to have said yes before I read it ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
