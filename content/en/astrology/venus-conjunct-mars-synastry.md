Of all the contacts between two charts, this is the one people ask me about first, usually about someone they have only just met. In Venus conjunct Mars in synastry, one person's Venus sits within 6° of the other's Mars: what one finds attractive, enjoyable or worth having is exactly where the other puts their drive. I read it from both ends, and well beyond romance.

## What flows {#what-flows}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) is how a person values and relates: what attracts them, what they prefer, what feels fair in an exchange. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) is how they act: initiative, effort, the push that gets something started and the line they hold. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) joins two functions in one place, and in the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) the two functions come from two different people, so liking and doing arrive from two directions at once.

If the Venus is yours, the other person's energy goes straight to what you care about. Your enthusiasm doesn't stay an enthusiasm for long; they pick it up and act on it, and it can feel like being taken seriously, or being swept along.

If the Mars is yours, your effort finds an audience that delights in it. What you start, they value; where you push, they are glad you did. That kind of appreciation is a strong motivator, and it tends to make you braver around them.

Who carries which has nothing to do with gender: I don't read Venus as feminine or Mars as masculine, and either person in any pair can hold either. With one person's Venus and the other's Mars both in Capricorn, a sign where tradition exalts Mars, the Venus person values reliability and long effort, and the Mars person shows interest by working steadily: affection arrives as practical help. Between friends, this is "you love the idea, I'll book it"; at work, a colleague whose drive lands exactly on what you'd judge worth doing; in a family, the sibling who runs with your enthusiasm.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

When taste and drive sit in one place, it can get hard to tell whose wish is whose. The Mars person may act on the other's preferences before they've been stated, and anticipating slides into presuming. The Venus person may let the other's pursuit define what they like, and only later ask whether they wanted it. Pace is the other snag: one person is already moving while the other is still weighing, and an assertion is not an aggression, but it can feel like pressure if nobody says "wait".

The same two functions at other angles keep more distance:

- **[Venus sextile Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-sextile-mars-synastry/):** taste and drive cooperate through invitations, taken up or left.
- **[Venus square Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-square-mars-synastry/):** what one values and how the other acts rub, and terms get negotiated.
- **[Venus trine Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-trine-mars-synastry/):** the two keep pace in one element, with little to discuss.
- **[Venus opposite Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-opposite-mars-synastry/):** taste and drive face each other, each showing the other what it doesn't do.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Say the yes and the no out loud. The Venus person's sense of what they actually want is the steering here; the Mars person's job is to ask before acting on it, and to hear "not yet" as information. Slow beginnings help: a first project at work scoped together, a friendship that grows over several plans rather than one big one, a couple that lets interest settle before it decides anything.

About 1 pair of people in 15 has this conjunction, one way round or the other, within 6°, far too many for it to mark anything rare. Venus covers about a degree a day, a little more at its fastest, and Mars less, so the hour rarely makes or unmakes it; the houses where it lands require both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Within a single birth chart, [Venus conjunct Mars](/astrology/venus-conjunct-mars/) fuses liking and going after in one person. Between two people they belong to different lives, which is why they need talking about. If you're reading this about someone you've only just met, I'd wait: the first weeks are for questions, not charts, and a synastry needs both people to know ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
