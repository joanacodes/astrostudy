Venus at 20° Taurus and Mars at 5° Leo are 75° apart, too far from 90° for most astrologers to call it a square. Count their signs instead, Taurus to Leo, three signs on, and they are square. Whole-sign aspects are that second count: no orbs, no exact moment, only the signs.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A whole-sign aspect is an aspect between two signs, and so between any planets standing in them. Count from one sign to the other, in either direction: two signs on makes a [sextile](/astrology/sextile/), three a [square](/astrology/square/), four a [trine](/astrology/trine/), six an [opposition](/astrology/opposition/). Every planet in Taurus is then square every planet in Leo and in Aquarius, trine those in Virgo and Capricorn, sextile those in Cancer and Pisces, and opposite those in Scorpio, at any degree.

Four of the other eleven signs are left out: the two neighbours, and the two signs five away. Taurus makes no aspect to Aries or Gemini, nor to Libra or Sagittarius. The old texts say that planets in such signs can't see each other, and the relationship has a name and a page of its own, [aversion](/astrology/aversion/).

The count carries the elements and the modalities with it. Signs four apart always share an element; signs three or six apart share a modality, cardinal, fixed or mutable; signs two apart pair fire with air, or earth with water. A whole-sign aspect says something about two manners before it says anything about two planets.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Most of the time the two counts agree. They part company when planets sit near the edges of their signs. A planet at 1° Taurus and another at 29° Leo are 118° apart, a trine by degree; by sign, Taurus and Leo are square. An astrologer counting by degree calls that an [out-of-sign aspect](/astrology/out-of-sign-aspects/). An astrologer counting by sign simply calls it a square.

The sign count has no use for [orbs](/astrology/orbs/), since every degree of a sign stands in the same relation to every degree of another. It has no exact moment either, and so none of what the tradition built on that moment, from [applying and separating aspects](/astrology/applying-and-separating-aspects/) to [refranation](/astrology/refranation/). Those questions need degrees, and a chart that only counts signs can't ask them.

Neither habit is a modern invention. Both are already there in the *Tetrabiblos*, where [Ptolemy](/astrology/ptolemy/) grounds the aspects in the angles between planets and between signs, measured in degrees of the ecliptic. The sign count fits most naturally beside [Whole Sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/), where every house is one sign: there, an aspect between two signs is also an aspect between two houses. And the old test of whether two planets can help each other, in a [mutual reception](/astrology/mutual-reception/) for instance, asks whether their signs see each other, not how many degrees apart the planets are.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I count aspects by degree, within orbs, because that's how the system I read with defines an aspect: Venus at 20° Taurus and Mars at 5° Leo make none in my reading. The sign count still tells me something. Taurus and Leo are both fixed signs, so whatever Venus values and whatever Mars goes after, each is held to in a manner that doesn't easily give way. I describe that as the signs' relationship, never as a square.

When I read a chart drawn by an astrologer who counts by sign, I translate rather than correct. Their squares will include some of my trines and leave out some of my squares, and both lists are faithful to their own rule ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

A sign count looks like something a birth date could settle, and for the slow planets it usually is. The Moon changes sign about every two and a half days and the Ascendant about every two hours, so their aspects by sign can turn on the hour. That's one more reason I don't read a chart without the time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Neighbouring signs are in aspect: that's the semi-sextile.”** By degree, some astrologers use a [semi-sextile](/astrology/semi-sextile/) of 30°. By sign, neighbours make no aspect at all.
- **“Whole-sign aspects and out-of-sign aspects are the same thing.”** An out-of-sign aspect is one the degrees make and the signs don't. A whole-sign aspect is one the signs make, whatever the degrees.
- **“A sign count is a sloppy degree count.”** It answers another question: whether two signs are configured, not how close two planets are to an exact angle.
- **“One count is right and the other wrong.”** Each is consistent within its own rule. What goes wrong is mixing them without saying so.
