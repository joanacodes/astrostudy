Some pairs are introduced by their conversation before anyone says their names: a running joke, a question the two of them always ask, a way of finishing each other's explanations. Composite Mercury in the 1st house puts the relationship's mind at its front door.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) gives the relationship its own [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), the midpoint of your two natal Mercurys, and the house it falls in shows where that shared mind does its work. [The 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) is where the relationship shows itself: its first impression, its initiative, the face it turns to strangers. Angular, with Aries as its natural sign, it is also where tradition gives Mercury its joy ([planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/)), the one house among the twelve where the planet of thought is happiest.

With the bond's thinking here, its talk is the first thing people meet. Friends know the pair by its questions and its humour, by the way the two of you tell a story together or argue cheerfully in public. The relationship tends to start things by talking about them, and it explains itself well: ask how you met and the answer comes as a double act. A natal [Mercury in the 1st house](/astrology/mercury-in-1st-house/) puts one person's curiosity on show; in synastry, [their Mercury in your 1st house](/astrology/mercury-in-1st-house-synastry/) is one person's words shaping how the other comes across. Here neither of you owns the voice, and two reserved people can still be known, as a pair, for their conversation.

Composite Mercury usually keeps close to the composite Sun, within about 28° in all but about 1 pair in 18, so the Sun is often in the 1st as well, or in a house next to it. In about 1 pair in 26 it sits across the chart instead, around the 7th: an effect of midpoints, not a second story.

The 1st begins at the composite Ascendant, and both birth times decide where that is. The two ways of drawing composite houses also part company here. Taken from the midpoints of your natal cusps, the composite Ascendant is fixed once your times are known. Built from the composite Midheaven for the place where you live, it depends on that place's latitude too, so a pair who moves north or south can see the Ascendant shift, and a Mercury near it change house ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)).

What to watch is the gap between the public conversation and the private one. A bond known for its talk can start performing it, and the slower exchange, the doubt or the half-formed worry, gets less room than the witty one. It also helps to notice whether one of you has quietly become the pair's spokesperson. Some conversations are better had before you go out, with nobody listening.

Before it comes [composite Mercury in the 12th house](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-12th-house/), whose thinking happens out of sight; after it, [composite Mercury in the 2nd house](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-2nd-house/), which talks about what the two of you value and keep. My own readings don't include composites: what's here is the technique as astrologers use it, with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and only when both people have agreed ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
