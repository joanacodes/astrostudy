After the elements, the second count many astrologers make is by modality: how many planets stand in cardinal signs, how many in fixed, how many in mutable. It answers a different question from the first, not what a chart is made of but how it moves.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Each sign has one of [the three modalities](/astrology/three-modalities/): cardinal for Aries, Cancer, Libra and Capricorn, fixed for Taurus, Leo, Scorpio and Aquarius, mutable for Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius and Pisces. Sort the ten planets by the modality of their signs and you get three numbers adding up to ten. Usually one leads, sometimes by a long way; now and then one is zero, and astrologers speak of a missing modality.

When I read modalities, cardinal means initiating, fixed means stabilising and sustaining, mutable means adapting and adjusting. They describe how a planet goes about its work, never the work itself, so their balance describes the tempo of the whole chart: where it tends to begin, where it holds on, where it bends.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Three planets of the ten are tied to the season of your birth. In the tropical zodiac the Sun enters a cardinal sign at each equinox and solstice, spends about a month there, then a month in a fixed sign, then a month in a mutable one, so the Sun's modality tells you which third of a season your birthday fell in. Mercury, never more than about 28° from the Sun, can only stand in the Sun's sign or the one next to it; Venus, never more than about 47° away, can reach two signs either side. Part of any modality count is set by the calendar before the chart says anything personal.

The four signs of one modality sit a quarter of the circle apart. Planets that share a modality therefore tend to make [squares](/astrology/square/) and [oppositions](/astrology/opposition/) by sign, and a chart crowded in one modality often carries a fair amount of tension: planets pulling in different directions at the same tempo. That isn't misfortune. In the charts I read, that friction is often where the drive comes from.

The houses repeat the pattern. Taken in order from the 1st, the houses match the signs in order from Aries, so the angular houses (the 1st, 4th, 7th and 10th) echo the cardinal signs, the succedent houses the fixed ones and the cadent houses the mutable ones ([Angular, succedent and cadent houses](/astrology/angular-succedent-and-cadent-houses/)). Some astrologers count planets by house family as well as by modality. That second count, like the modality of the Ascendant and the Midheaven, rests on the birth time, and it's one of the reasons I never begin a reading without it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read the leading modality as the chart's habitual tempo, and the planets carrying it tell me where that tempo shows. A cardinal emphasis made of the Moon, Venus and Mars says something warmer and more personal than one made of Saturn, Uranus and Pluto, which everyone born in the same few years would share. And an emphasis gathered in the angular houses comes out more visibly than one spread through the cadent houses.

Element and modality read best as a pair ([Element balance and missing elements](/astrology/element-balance-and-missing-elements/)). They're independent of each other: each element owns one sign of each modality, so a chart can be crowded in water and evenly spread by pace. Fixed water and mutable water are both water, but one gathers depth and the other moves where it's needed.

An empty modality I take as a pace that doesn't come by default, not one that's out of reach. With no planet in a cardinal sign, beginning may need a deliberate push, a date in the diary, someone to start with. The four cardinal signs still lie across the houses, and the planets that rule them are placed somewhere, so the chart still says where the push is most likely to come from.

Both these counts sort a chart by sign. The next ones, by halves and quarters of the wheel, sort it by house instead, which ties them to the birth time from the start ([Hemisphere emphasis](/astrology/hemisphere-emphasis/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“No cardinal planets means you never start anything.”** It means none of your planets stands in Aries, Cancer, Libra or Capricorn. People begin things for reasons no count can see.
- **“Mostly fixed planets means you can't change.”** Holding is a pace, not a cage. Fixed planets change too, usually once the new ground feels solid.
- **“Modality and element say the same thing.”** They're two separate sorts of the same signs: fire comes cardinal, fixed and mutable.
- **“A crowded modality means a hard life.”** It makes squares and oppositions more likely, and those are tension to work with, not a sentence.
