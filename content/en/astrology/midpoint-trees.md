Open a midpoint report and you'll find, under each planet, a short column of pairs: Venus/Mars, Sun/Saturn, Moon/Ascendant. That column is a midpoint tree. It's how midpoint astrologers read a chart one point at a time, and it reads better once you know how it's built and how much of it chance would put there anyway.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Any two points of a chart have a degree halfway between them, their [midpoint](/astrology/midpoints/). A tree turns the idea round. Instead of starting from a pair and asking what sits between them, it starts from one point, the trunk, and lists every pair whose midpoint that point touches: those are the branches.

Here is one branch, worked out. Say Mars is at 18° Aries and Venus at 2° Leo. The shorter arc between them is 104°, so their midpoint falls 52° on from Mars, at 10° Gemini, with its opposite at 10° Sagittarius. A Moon at 11° Gemini sits a degree from it, so Venus/Mars goes into the Moon's tree ([Venus/Mars midpoint](/astrology/venus-mars-midpoint/)). A Jupiter at 10½° Virgo, square to that midpoint, would list Venus/Mars in its own tree too. The first contact is what midpoint books call direct, with a body on the midpoint itself; the second is indirect, a body in aspect to the midpoint without anything sitting there.

The vocabulary is Hamburg's. Alfred Witte worked out these combinations on movable dials, and the rules his school published in 1928 call three factors read as one a planetary picture ([the Hamburg School](/astrology/hamburg-school-uranian-astrology/)); each branch of a tree is one such picture. Reinhold Ebertin's [cosmobiology](/astrology/cosmobiology/) built its main reference book, *The Combination of Stellar Influences* (1940), largely on early versions of those rules, but without the hypothetical planets Witte's school had invented.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A branch counts when the point is in one of the hard aspects of the eighth harmonic to the midpoint: the conjunction, the [semi-square](/astrology/semi-square/), the square, the [sesquiquadrate](/astrology/sesquiquadrate/) or the opposition. Those fall every 45° around the circle, eight positions in all, which is why the Hamburg School folded the zodiac onto a [90° dial](/astrology/uranian-90-dial/), where all of them line up.

Eight positions all round the circle mean branches come easily. Take the ten planets with the Ascendant and the Midheaven: twelve points. For any one of them there are 55 midpoints of the other pairs. With an orb of 1½° either side, each of those midpoints has about one chance in fifteen of touching that point, so a tree carries three or four branches on average, in any chart, by chance alone. At 2° it's about five. A full tree isn't remarkable in itself; tight branches, and branches that repeat a theme the chart already shows, are what would make one worth reading ([orbs](/astrology/orbs/)).

Speed matters as well. A midpoint covers half the ground its two bodies cover together, so one involving the Ascendant moves about a degree every eight minutes of birth time. A quarter of an hour's error shifts it by nearly two degrees, more than the orb, and can rewrite every branch that involves the Ascendant or the Midheaven. Midpoints of the Moon move far more slowly, a quarter of a degree or so an hour. So the trees of the angles are the first a rounded birth time spoils ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I work without midpoint trees. The system I built prints none, and I read the planets themselves, by sign, house and aspect ([how I read](/how-i-read/)). My notebook has nothing to say about them either. The placements that held best in it, [Saturn, Chiron and Lilith](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/), are single bodies in signs and houses; a branch joins three factors at once, and nothing I counted tells me how often such a combination holds.

If you read your own trees, start with the [Sun/Moon midpoint](/astrology/sun-moon-midpoint/) and the trees of the Sun and Moon, keep the orbs small, and give weight only to branches that echo what the planets already say. Read every branch as a link between functions, never as a forecast.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A tree with many branches belongs to a powerful planet.”** With a 1½° orb, chance alone gives most trees three or four.
- **“Everyone born that day has the same trees.”** The branches that involve the Ascendant, the Midheaven or the Moon change with the hour.
- **“A branch predicts an event.”** In a birth chart, a branch describes three parts of you acting as one, not something waiting to happen.
- **“Midpoint trees came with computers.”** Witte worked with movable dials long before software; programs only made the lists faster to print.
