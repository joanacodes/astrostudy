Low in the western sky and close to setting: that's where Saturn was when you were born. It puts Saturn in the 7th house, opposite your Ascendant, where a chart looks at the person across the table, and it makes every close bond something you take seriously.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Partners in love and in work, close collaborators, the open rivals who stand in front of you rather than behind: [the 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) is everyone you meet one to one, as an equal. It's angular, beginning at the Descendant, so what sits here shows in the people you choose and the ones you clash with. With [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) here, partnership becomes a place of responsibility and slow growth: commitment taken very seriously, often later than others make it, and bonds that strengthen with time.

Libra is the house's natural sign, and Libra is Saturn's exaltation. Exaltation belongs to signs rather than houses, but the echo is real: fairness, agreements kept, a partnership of equals. Saturn in the 7th doesn't say whether you'll marry, or when. I don't read a chart for that. It describes how you approach commitment, and what you tend to meet in other people.

That second part matters. The 7th shows what we often see in others before we see it in ourselves, so Saturn here can arrive in partners who seem serious, reserved or exacting. Part of the work is recognising your own Saturn in them, the caution, the standards, the fear of getting it wrong, and taking it back.

The Descendant is always exactly opposite the Ascendant, so the same birth-time error that blurs a rising sign blurs this placement too. Placidus, the system I read in, needs an accurate time before I'll place Saturn here ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Before Saturn reaches the 7th, in [Saturn in the 6th house](/astrology/saturn-in-6th-house/), love shows as practical help; past it, in [Saturn in the 8th house](/astrology/saturn-in-8th-house/), as trust given slowly. Here it shows as commitment itself. You may take a long time to say yes, test the ground carefully, and then stay, loyal through difficulty. You may be drawn to steadiness and maturity, whatever the person's age. The risk is staying out of duty in a bond that has become unequal, or holding back so long that nobody gets close enough to stay. Comparing your chart with a partner's is something I do only when you've both agreed to it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)), and Saturn's contacts are the ones I read most slowly.

## At work {#at-work}

One-to-one work suits you: business partnerships, clients, contracts, negotiation, any role where you deal with people across a table. You take agreements seriously and expect others to. In a business partnership it's usually you who reads the contract, and you who honours it when it becomes inconvenient. Open rivals can be good teachers here; a competitor who sets the bar high can sharpen you more than any friend.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Fear of commitment, or its mirror, commitment held onto too long. Seeing partners as judges whose approval has to be earned. Loneliness inside a relationship, when you've made yourself responsible for keeping it going on your own. And a quiet belief that love has to be earned through good behaviour, which turns every disagreement into an exam you might fail.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Make agreements explicit, and revisit them as you both change. Give bonds time, but notice when patience has turned into endurance. Look for a partner who is a teammate, not an examiner. Saturn held 75 to 90% of the time in my notebook, which is why I read a 7th-house Saturn with care: slowly, and never as a sentence passed on your love life.
