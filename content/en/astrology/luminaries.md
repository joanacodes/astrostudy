Every chart holds two bodies that astrology has always set a little apart: the Sun and the Moon, the luminaries. They're the lights of the day and of the night, and a reading starts with them, as a pair, rather than ending there.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

*Luminary* comes from the Latin for a light, or a lamp, and the old texts simply call them the lights. Astronomy files them differently: the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is a star, the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) is Earth's natural satellite, and neither is a planet. Astrology counts them with the planets because, seen from Earth, both travel round the zodiac against the stars, as the planets do.

Their speeds are very different. The Sun takes a year to go round, about 30 days in each sign; the Moon about 27⅓ days, two and a half days in a sign. Neither is ever retrograde. Tradition gave each of them a single sign, where the five planets it knew had two apiece: Leo to the Sun and Cancer to the Moon, side by side in the middle of the northern summer. The Sun is exalted in Aries, the Moon in Taurus.

Sorted by speed, the lights head the list of the [personal, social and generational planets](/astrology/personal-social-and-generational-planets/): they change sign far too often to belong to a generation. Two people born a week apart share most of their planets' signs, but never the Moon's.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The relationship between the two lights is written into the sky every month. When the Moon catches up with the Sun, there's a [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/); when it stands opposite, a [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/); when either falls close to the lunar nodes, an eclipse. So the angle between your Sun and Moon is also the phase you were born under ([your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/)).

In plain words, the Sun is the part of you that gathers itself around a purpose and puts itself forward. The Moon looks after how you feel and what you need: it's how you settle, attach and find comfort, and how you respond when things shift. One is a direction, the other a home base.

They also take turns. Tradition sorted charts into day and night ones by whether the Sun was above the horizon at birth, a doctrine called [sect](/astrology/sect-day-and-night-charts/): by day the Sun leads, by night the Moon, and the one leading is [the sect light](/astrology/sect-light/).

And they sit on either side of an old line. In Ptolemy's account, the Moon counts among the beneficent planets, with Jupiter and Venus, while the Sun is one of the common ones, which borrow their character from the planets beside them. The same chapter sets out tradition's best-known labels for the planets, [benefics and malefics](/astrology/benefics-and-malefics/).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read the lights together before I read either alone. What you're aiming for and what you need to feel steady can pull the same way or not, and that conversation runs through the rest of a chart. Their signs give the style, their houses the parts of life where each is busiest, and the aspect between them how easily they agree: [Sun square Moon](/astrology/sun-square-moon/), for instance, describes a life of adjusting between the two.

The Moon is the one to check. It changes sign about every two and a half days, so on the day it changes, only the birth time can say which sign is yours ([the Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

I also keep them in proportion. When I tested charts in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the Sun sign held about a third of the time. The Moon went through the same checks as everything else and wasn't one of the three that held far more often, which were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith. So the lights, with the rising sign, make the [big three](/astrology/big-three/), a first sketch I hold lightly, and the closest reading goes where the notebook points. That's [how I read](/how-i-read/).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Sun and the Moon are planets.”** One is a star and the other a satellite. Astrology reads them with the planets because they move through the zodiac like planets, not because they are any.
- **“Your Sun sign is your whole astrology.”** It's one light of two, and a chart also has eight planets, the angles and the houses. The Moon alone can change what the same Sun sign looks like in two lives.
- **“The Moon is your mother.”** The Moon describes how you look after your own feelings and needs. It isn't a portrait of anyone in your family, and it can't tell their story.
- **“Born at night, your Sun matters less.”** Sect changes which light leads; it doesn't switch the other one off.
