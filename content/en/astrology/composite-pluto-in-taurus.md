Composite Pluto in Taurus needs a partner born before 1884, or one born after 2095. Pluto spent 1851 to 1884 in Taurus and won't return until the very end of this century, so no couple living today has this composite, and none will for decades.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), fixed earth ruled by Venus, is the sign that asks how a thing becomes durable. Put [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) in that sign in [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) and deep change would come slowly, through what the pair keeps: the home lived in for forty years, the garden dug over every spring, the habits of a shared table, the body's rhythms learnt side by side. Such a bond wouldn't transform by announcement. It would change the way a riverbank does, almost invisibly, until one day the shape is different; and what it finally let go of, it would let go of completely.

Modern tropical astrology gives Pluto its home in Scorpio, so Taurus, opposite, is its [detriment](/astrology/detriment/): the planet of elimination in the sign of preservation. In a relationship I'd read that as friction between keeping and clearing, not as a flaw. The trap would be Pluto's old confusion of control with safety: holding on to every shared object, routine and grievance as if loosening any of them would sink the bond. A reading would also have to ask what composite Venus says, because Venus rules Taurus and shows what a pair values and enjoys; here, what the two of you treasure would be exactly what the bond finds hardest to release.

No one can have it now. A composite Pluto is the halfway point between two natal Plutos, and Pluto lingers so long in each sign that a birth year all but fixes each of them. For the halfway point to land in Taurus, at least one of the two has to be born before April 1884 or after June 2095. Two million random pairs born 1930–2009 were checked, and none had it. [Pluto in Taurus](/astrology/pluto-in-taurus/) belongs to the charts of people born in those years, the first group long gone and the second not yet born.

The pages either side are just as empty for now: [composite Pluto in Aries](/astrology/composite-pluto-in-aries/) last belonged to pairs with someone born before 1853, and [composite Pluto in Gemini](/astrology/composite-pluto-in-gemini/) needs one partner born before 1914.

Taurus asks what lasts, and so did my notebook: I tested birth charts one placement at a time, never composites, and Pluto wasn't one of the three that held far more often ([the invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). A placement nobody alive has is one I can only describe, so take this page as the sign's grammar applied to a relationship, not as anyone's story.
