Every sign ends with a degree that has picked up a reputation: 29°. Some astrology writing calls it the anaretic degree and treats a planet there as urgent, unfinished or fated. The geometry is simpler, and kinder. A planet at 29° is about to change sign.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The anaretic degree runs from 29°00′ to 29°59′ of a sign. One minute of arc further and the planet stands at [0° of the next sign](/astrology/0-of-a-sign/). Because degrees inside a sign are counted from 0, this stretch is the thirtieth degree, not the twenty-ninth, even though it's often called the 29th degree ([degrees, minutes and seconds](/astrology/degrees-minutes-and-seconds/)). When you read about it, check which way the writer counts.

It isn't on the usual list of [critical degrees](/astrology/critical-degrees/), the one taken from the 28 lunar mansions, but many astrologers watch it separately. Those who do read a planet there as carrying its sign's themes to their end: pressing, ripe, or not quite resolved, depending on the book. That is an interpretation; the arithmetic underneath only says that a change of sign is close.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

How long a planet spends in its last degree depends on its speed, and that decides who shares the placement:

- **The Moon:** about two hours, so the birth time decides whether it is at 29° of one sign or 0° of the next.
- **The Sun:** about a day, near the end of each sign's dates, which shift a little from year to year ([born on the cusp](/astrology/born-on-the-cusp/)).
- **Mercury, Venus and Mars:** from under a day to about two, much longer if one of them stops to turn around there.
- **Jupiter and Saturn:** roughly twelve days and a month, on average.
- **Uranus, Neptune and Pluto:** months at a time, a placement common to everyone born in those months.

Retrograde motion can stretch the stay or bring a planet back to it ([retrograde, explained](/astrology/retrograde-explained/)). [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) entered Aries on 30 March 2025, slipped back into the last degree of Pisces on 22 October and stayed there until 26 January 2026. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) did the same at the end of Capricorn, returning to it from 1 September to 19 November 2024 before settling in Aquarius. A slow planet that turns at the edge of a sign can hover over its last degree for months ([stations](/astrology/stations/)).

The angles move fastest of all. The [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) crosses a degree in about four minutes, so an Ascendant at 29° is a few minutes from the next sign, and a birth time rounded to the quarter-hour can put it on either side. In Placidus, the house system I use, the same goes for every house cusp ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

A planet at 29° belongs to its sign as fully as one at 15°, and I read it that way: by sign, house and aspects. What the late degree changes for me is practical. If it's the Moon or an angle, I check how the birth time was recorded before I trust the sign. If it's a slow planet, I know a whole cohort shares it, so I read it through the house it falls in.

Some people come to me frightened by what they've read about the 29th degree. It is a late degree, not a fatal one. No degree of the zodiac predicts death, illness or catastrophe, this one included ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)), and a reading that leans on a degree to tell you otherwise isn't reading your chart. Degrees were never part of my notebook, which counted signs and houses, so I have no figure for this one either way ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A planet at 29° is already half in the next sign.”** It is wholly in its own sign until it crosses. The next sign may show elsewhere in the chart, not through this planet.
- **“The anaretic degree is a death degree.”** No degree predicts death or disaster. The idea frightens people and proves nothing.
- **“29° means unfinished karma.”** No chart can show karma. The degree says the planet is late in its sign, nothing more.
- **“If my Ascendant is at 29°, I'm really the next sign.”** With an exact birth time, the Ascendant is in the sign the chart shows. With a rounded one, check the time before you decide.
