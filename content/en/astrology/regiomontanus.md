Regiomontanus is the house system of the horary astrologers, and for a long stretch of European history it was simply the house system. It takes its divisions not from the zodiac, and not from time, but from the sky's own equator.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Regiomontanus is a quadrant system: the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) is the cusp of the 1st house, the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) the cusp of the 10th, and their opposite points open the 7th and 4th. The circle it divides is the [celestial equator](/astrology/celestial-equator/), the Earth's equator projected onto the sky. Starting from the meridian, the equator is cut into twelve equal arcs of 30°, each two sidereal hours of the sky's rotation. Each dividing point is then joined to the north and south points of the horizon by a great circle, and where that circle crosses the zodiac is a house cusp.

Two of those circles are familiar. The one through the meridian's point on the equator is the meridian itself, so the 10th cusp is the Midheaven; the one through the east point of the horizon, which lies on the equator, is the horizon, so the 1st cusp is the Ascendant. The other eight fall in between. Because the zodiac is tilted against the equator, equal arcs of the one become unequal stretches of the other, and away from the equator the houses can differ a great deal in size, with whole signs inside a single house ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)).

The system is named after Johannes Müller von Königsberg (1436–1476), a German mathematician, astronomer and astrologer who worked in Vienna, Buda and Nuremberg. Regiomontanus is a Latin form of his home town, Königsberg in Lower Franconia, first used for him by Philipp Melanchthon in 1534. In Hungary he drew up trigonometric tables designed for astrology, including the finding of houses, printed after his death, in 1490, as the *Tabulae directionum profectionumque*.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Set it beside its neighbours and the family likeness shows. [Porphyry](/astrology/porphyry/) cuts each quarter of the zodiac into three equal stretches. [Campanus](/astrology/campanus/) uses the same circles through the north and south points but divides a different circle, the prime vertical, which runs overhead from east to west. [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/) divides time, each degree's journey from the horizon to the meridian. Regiomontanus is the equator's version: equal steps of the sky's rotation, two sidereal hours apiece, turned into cusps by circles through the north and south points of the horizon.

It became one of the most popular house systems in Europe, until Placidus largely replaced it. It kept one stronghold. Horary astrology, which casts a chart for the moment a question is asked, still commonly uses Regiomontanus, following [William Lilly](/astrology/william-lilly/), the 17th-century English horary astrologer ([horary astrology](/astrology/horary-astrology/)).

Its maker's influence ran further than his houses. In the 17th century Jean-Baptiste Morin, whose name another house system carries, built his method of prediction, directions "in mundo", largely on Regiomontanus's work ([Morinus](/astrology/morinus/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I use Placidus, and not because Regiomontanus is wrong. Both keep the Ascendant and the Midheaven on the cusps, which matters most to me, and they part only on the houses in between. Regiomontanus measures equal arcs of the equator, a division of space; Placidus measures each degree's own climb, a division of time. A birth time records a moment in the turning of the sky, and I want houses built from that turning, degree by degree ([which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)).

If your chart was drawn in Regiomontanus, perhaps by a horary astrologer, your signs, degrees, aspects and angles are the ones I'd read. Only the cusps between the angles move, so look first at any planet close to one ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)). And because every cusp is fixed by the meridian and the horizon of one moment, the time has to be right ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Regiomontanus was the man's name.”** He was Johannes Müller; Regiomontanus is a Latin form of his home town, Königsberg in Lower Franconia.
- **“It's only for horary astrology.”** For a long time it was one of Europe's main systems for birth charts too, until Placidus took over.
- **“Its houses are equal because the equator is cut equally.”** The arcs are equal on the equator. Carried onto the tilted zodiac, the houses come out unequal.
