Mars in Sagittarius needs to know *why* before it will run. Give it a reason it believes in and it will cover astonishing distances; take the reason away and it simply stops, however much is left to do.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the drive to act: initiative, effort, courage, competition. [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/) looks far: mutable fire, the sign of travel, learning, belief and the horizon. In Sagittarius, Mars works on conviction. You act fast for what you believe in, argue openly for it, and look for adventure rather than comfort. If Aries is fire's spark and Leo its hearth, Sagittarius is the wildfire: mutable fire travels and spreads more than it holds, and its enthusiasm can leap to a new cause once the first has stopped meaning anything.

Sagittarius gives Mars no dignity, good or bad, so the sign is read for its manner. Its ruler, [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/), the planet of meaning and growth, is where I look next: its sign and house show what kind of cause or horizon this Mars is really chasing.

Mars generally spends about 43 days in Sagittarius, and up to 208, nearly seven months, when it turns retrograde there. Count the births of 1930 to 2009 and about 1 person in 13 has it, alongside everyone born in your few weeks. The archer's target, though, is set by the house, and the house can't be found without your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). [Mars in Scorpio](/astrology/mars-in-scorpio/) keeps its plans hidden; [Mars in Capricorn](/astrology/mars-in-capricorn/), the next sign on, turns enthusiasm into a long climb.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, this Mars wants a fellow traveller. You're drawn to people with a life and opinions of their own, and a relationship that feels like an adventure keeps you longer than one that feels like a cage. Honesty comes easily, sometimes too easily: you may say exactly what you think and be surprised that it landed hard. Arguments tend to be about principles rather than feelings. Shared plans, a trip, a course, a cause you both care about, do more for this Mars than any amount of reassurance. The skill is letting a partner disagree with you without needing to win them over.

## At work {#at-work}

Put Mars in Sagittarius on a team and it becomes the enthusiast who gets everyone moving. Teaching, publishing, travel, sport, campaigns, anything with a mission and room to roam suits it. You sell an idea you believe in better than anyone, and the start of something large is where you shine. Bureaucracy, narrow tasks and rules nobody can explain drain you quickly. So does work you no longer believe in: when the meaning goes, most of your energy goes with it.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Righteousness. Convinced you're right, you may press the point long after it's been heard, or treat a disagreement as a question of truth rather than of taste. Promises made in the heat of enthusiasm can outrun what any one person could deliver. And the pull to move on, to the next place, project or cause, can mean leaving before the work is done. From outside, that restlessness can look like a fear of commitment; more often it's a fear of the meaningless.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Choose the cause, then the next small step, and write both down. Ask one real question for every opinion you offer. Move the body over distance: long walks, cycling, days in the hills, travel with a purpose. Before you promise, sleep on it. Plan the adventures properly: courage isn't recklessness, and the best expeditions are the ones that come home. And look for teachers who argue with you well; this Mars grows most when its beliefs are tested by someone it respects.
