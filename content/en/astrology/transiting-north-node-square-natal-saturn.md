Halfway between the North Node's crossing of your Saturn and the South Node's, the axis of the nodes lies across your Saturn at a right angle. It happens twice a round, nine years or so apart, and for about a month your responsibilities stand in the middle of the road, where neither the old way nor the new one can go round them.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) holds a chart's structure: what you have promised, what you carry, the limits time imposes. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) marks the capacity you are developing; the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/), opposite, the one you developed long ago. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates through friction that pushes adaptation, and because whatever is 90° from one node is 90° from the other, this [transit](/astrology/transits/) squares your Saturn from both ends at once.

Lived, it tends to feel like a duty arriving in the middle of a plan. You want to push on with what you are learning, and a commitment you made years ago asks for its hours; you want to fall back on the old routine, and the same commitment won't let that be restful either. Saturn's growth edge is to replace fear-based control with stewardship, and a square from the axis gives it practice: you work out what the plan must make room for, rather than abandoning one or the other.

If your Saturn is in the 10th, the squares tend to come while the axis runs near your 1st and 7th houses: the role you carry in public meets the question of how you stand with the people closest to you. Those houses come from your birth time, as does everything else here that is personal ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Saturn's square from the node holds for about a month as a rule, measured from 1½° before exact to 1° after. An eclipse season in the same weeks, with the Sun close to the nodes, can hold it nearly still and stretch the square to three months or more; about one pass in 25 crosses the exact degree three times. The eclipses of such a season land at right angles to your Saturn, which my eclipse rule leaves aside: it counts an eclipse only on a natal point or opposite it.

Neither end of the axis sits on your Saturn, which is also why the forecast I prepare readings with doesn't list this square: there the node counts only head on, by conjunction or opposition. I read it as the background to whatever the slower planets are doing to your Saturn at the time.

The two squares fall about four and a half years either side of the opposition, halfway between [Transiting North Node conjunct natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-north-node-conjunct-natal-saturn/) and [Transiting North Node opposite natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-north-node-opposite-natal-saturn/), with [Transiting North Node sextile natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-north-node-sextile-natal-saturn/) and [Transiting North Node trine natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-north-node-trine-natal-saturn/) on either side. By 80 people meet eight or nine of them. Two examples: born on 1 January 1955, the first square at 7.0; born on 1 January 1995, at 8.0. [Saturn square North Node](/astrology/saturn-square-north-node/) in a birth chart is the same right angle, built in.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Put the duty in the plan. Write the commitment that keeps interrupting into the timetable, with its hours, instead of treating it as an obstacle.

Re-pace rather than quit. Ask how much of the new thing you can do each week alongside what you carry, and do that much.

Hand on one responsibility that is no longer yours to hold. Saturn finds delegating hard, and a square from the nodes is a good month to try.
