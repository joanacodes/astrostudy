Their Mars in your 9th house sends another person's drive towards your horizons: what you believe, what you study, where you'd go if you dared. Conversations with them don't stay small; they become arguments about how the world works, and plans to go and see.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is courage and initiative, the will to act on what matters to you. [The 9th house](/astrology/9th-house/) holds your beliefs and the search behind them: long study, travel, other cultures, teaching, the ideas you'd defend. This [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/) puts force behind your convictions, and sometimes against them.

With them, you may finally book the trip, sign up for the course, or say in public what you actually think. They challenge your views and, in doing so, make you work out why you hold them. Belief stops being a private habit and turns into something you're ready to act on.

For them, your horizons are a cause worth taking up. They may love planning journeys with you, arguing philosophy late into the night, or championing an idea that first came from you. Cadent, the 9th turns what has been lived into meaning; [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/), its natural sign, belongs to Jupiter, and the Sun has its joy in this house, which tradition called the God. If their Mars falls late in your 9th, it nears your [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/), and belief starts to push towards public action.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

A debate can turn into a crusade. You may feel argued with, preached at, or pushed to adopt a view before you've made up your mind, and your quieter convictions can seem to need defending at every meal. If you travel together, their pace may leave little room for wandering.

From their side, your beliefs may not move when pushed, and they can take that as a reason to push harder. They may also feel that you treat their passion as zealotry, when to them it's simply caring.

A conviction isn't a battle line. Mars gives the 9th courage; it doesn't make anyone right, and the house itself asks for understanding more than certainty.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Argue to understand, not to win. Ask each other why you believe what you believe, and agree that changing your mind is allowed, even welcome. In a couple, plan trips that give each of you a day of your own; between friends, a running debate can be one of the best things you share; between colleagues, training and research go well when you challenge the ideas and spare the person; between adult relatives with different beliefs, it may help to name the subjects you'll leave for another day.

In the other direction, your Mars lands in their 9th house, and your convictions push at theirs. [Their Mars in your 8th house](/astrology/mars-in-8th-house-synastry/) is where shared experience goes deep; [their Mars in your 10th house](/astrology/mars-in-10th-house-synastry/) takes the push into your public life.

Your Midheaven, the cusp of the 10th and the far edge of your 9th, turns with the clock like every angle: an hour's error in your birth time can shift it by about fifteen degrees, and in Placidus, the house system I use, every cusp of your 9th hangs on that time. When [Mars retrograde](/astrology/mars-retrograde/) comes round, about every two years and lasting some two and a half months, Mars can stay in one stretch of the zodiac for months, so people born weeks apart may put their Mars in the same house of yours. Your time draws the cusp, theirs counts only on it, and I hold the reading until both are known ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Beliefs are personal ground too, read only when both people are willing ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
