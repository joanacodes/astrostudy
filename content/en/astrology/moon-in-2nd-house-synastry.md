Their Moon in your 2nd house is an overlay about feeling safe with what's solid. Another person's need for comfort settles around the things you have, the way you look after them, and the steadiness people can count on in you.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[The 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) answers the question *what can I count on?*: your resources, your belongings, the skills you could live from, and, underneath them, the worth you feel you have. The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) is how a person looks after their feelings, and what they need in order to feel secure. In this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their need for security comes to rest on your ground.

For you, the effect is often practical tenderness. They notice what you need and provide it without fuss: the right food in the cupboard, the repair done, the thing you'd lost found. Being looked after in concrete ways can make you feel valued, and over time it can steady your sense of your own worth.

For them, your tangible world can be a refuge. Your reliability, your routines and the things you keep around you soothe them; with you they feel there is something to hold on to. Succedent houses gather and keep, and the 2nd's natural sign is [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), the sign where tradition says the Moon is exalted. That's a rhyme rather than a rule, but this overlay does often feel steady.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

When someone's sense of safety rests partly on your resources, you may start to feel you have to keep everything steady for them: the money, the plans, the home, your own moods. Spending and saving can carry feelings that have little to do with numbers, and an object you'd happily give away may turn out to mean a great deal to them, or the other way round.

From their side, your caution or your generosity with what you have can touch their sense of security directly. If your circumstances wobble, they may feel it as their own unease, and find it hard to say so without sounding as if they're counting your money.

No chart says what either of you will have, and I don't read one for money ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)); this overlay describes how two people's feelings meet around what matters.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Talk about what makes each of you feel secure, in plain terms: a savings habit, a full fridge, a home that stays the same, a promise kept. Then decide what's shared and what isn't. In a couple, money talks go better when they start with feelings rather than figures; between friends, gifts and loans can be lovely as long as nobody keeps score; between colleagues, being the reliable one is a strength until it becomes the only role you're allowed.

The mirror image, your Moon in their 2nd house, puts your peace of mind in their hands and among their things, with the same need to keep it light. A house before, [their Moon in your 1st house](/astrology/moon-in-1st-house-synastry/) meets your presence; a house after, [their Moon in your 3rd house](/astrology/moon-in-3rd-house-synastry/) meets your everyday talk.

A day's travel for the Moon is about 13°, nearly half an average house, so without their birth time I couldn't tell you whether their Moon sits in your 1st, 2nd or 3rd. In Placidus, the house system I use, your own time draws those houses, and I wait for both before I begin ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). Their sense of security is theirs to share, so I read it only with their agreement ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
