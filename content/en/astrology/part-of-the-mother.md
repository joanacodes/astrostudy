Of all the lots, the Part of the Mother is the one most often mixed up with something else: the Moon. They're two different things. Keeping them apart is the first step to reading either of them honestly.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

It's a lot ([doctrine of the lots](/astrology/doctrine-of-the-lots/)), and tradition kept it for questions about the mother. It belongs to a small family of lots for relatives, beside the [Part of the Father](/astrology/part-of-the-father/), with the [Part of Siblings](/astrology/part-of-siblings/) one branch further along the family tree.

Many astrology books take a shortcut that's easy to fall into: the Moon is the mother. The idea is widespread enough that a general encyclopaedia records it, carefully, as an association made "for some". I don't read the Moon that way. In the system I read with, the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) is how you process feeling and attachment: what makes you feel safe, the habits that settle you, how you look after a need. Some of that you learned from whoever looked after you, but the Moon describes you, not her.

The lot isn't the Moon either. It's a separate degree, produced by arithmetic, which can land in any sign and any house, far from wherever your Moon happens to be.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Lots of this kind follow one pattern: the distance from one point to another, counted on from the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/). For a night birth, many astrologers count the other way, from the second point to the first. That reversal leaves a recognisable trace, because the night version is the day version reflected in your Ascendant. So if two programs give you two Parts of the Mother on either side of your Ascendant, at equal distances, one has reversed the count for a night birth and the other hasn't.

As for which two points go into it, this page leaves them out: I'd rather send you to your program's documentation than copy out a rule I haven't been able to verify.

The Ascendant settles the rest. It carries the lot round the zodiac in about a day, so [the birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/) sets the lot's degree, and its house as well. In Placidus, the house system I use, the house also depends on where the cusps fall for that day and that latitude.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

My readings leave this lot out, since the system I built calculates no lot but Fortune and Spirit, so if your mother comes into a reading, it's through your own Moon and your 4th house.

She often does. Your Moon shows how you take care of feeling and how you let yourself be cared for; the [4th house](/astrology/4th-house/) shows home and roots as you lived them. Together they describe being mothered, which isn't the same as your mother. Plenty of people were raised by a grandmother, a father, an aunt, a stepmother or foster parents, and a chart has no idea who did the raising. Reading care rather than a person leaves room for every one of those stories.

Beyond that, I keep to three rules:

- **She has her own chart.** If your mother ever wants hers read, that's a reading for her, with her agreement ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
- **No child's chart.** I read adults only, so I won't read a baby's chart, or compare a mother's chart with a young child's ([children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)).
- **No forecast of motherhood.** Pregnancy is a subject I never read, from this lot or from any other point ([can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Your Moon sign is your mother's personality.”** Your Moon describes how you meet your own feelings and needs. Your mother's temperament is in her chart, not yours.
- **“The Part of the Mother shows whether you'll have children.”** It was never a pregnancy point, and no point is one.
- **“Your Part of the Mother is wherever the first app put it.”** Check how that app treats a night birth. If a second one mirrors it around your Ascendant, the difference is the reversal, not a mistake.
- **“A tense placement means you weren't loved.”** No placement measures the love you received. What a chart can show is how care felt, and what you need from it now.
