A chart can give you words for what you feel. It can't tell you what's wrong, and it can't make you well. Both halves matter, and this page is about keeping them in the right order.

## The short answer {#the-short-answer}

Astrology can be a way of reflecting: a vocabulary for your moods and needs, a sense of how you tend to cope, the reminder that hard times come in seasons. It is never a diagnosis and never a treatment. No placement causes, predicts or explains a mental health problem, and a reading can sit beside care, never in place of it.

Used gently, that vocabulary has real uses: noticing what drains you and what restores you, how you react under pressure, which stretches of your life have tended to be harder. It can make it easier to describe what you're going through to someone trained to help.

In my own readings, the line is clear. Sometimes what someone carries is psychological, sometimes medical, and then [the right door isn't mine](/how-i-read/): I'll say so plainly, and point you towards someone who can really help. A therapist isn't a sign that your chart has failed; it's the right tool for a different job.

## Why it matters {#why-it-matters}

Astrology's vocabulary can slide into medical language without anyone meaning it to, and that's where care is needed.

- **No placement is a symptom.** I read the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) as the way you process feelings and needs, not as moodiness; [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) as imagination and compassion, not addiction; [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) as the place where you adapt around something that doesn't go away, not as trauma, and not as a promise of healing either. Tradition tied the [12th house](/astrology/12th-house/) to confinement and hospitals. I read it as the quieter, more private rooms of a life, and a planet there is never a diagnosis.
- **A transit can become a reason to wait.** ‘It's my [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/).’ ‘It'll lift when Pluto changes sign.’ A transit can name a hard season, and transits, to me, are weather rather than a forecast. But low mood, anxiety or sleeplessness that keeps going, or gets in the way of your days, isn't weather to sit out. It belongs with a doctor, whatever the sky is doing.
- **A label can stick.** ‘I'm anxious because I'm a Virgo.’ A chart describes tendencies, never permissions and never sentences. Used to explain suffering, it can make the suffering feel fixed, which is the opposite of what it's for.
- **Checking can become a habit you can't stop.** Reading your transits every morning, looking up the Moon before every conversation: when consulting the sky becomes something you can't put down, it's worth noticing. Take a break. The part of you that decides gets stronger with use.

## What to do instead {#what-to-do-instead}

- **If you're in danger now,** or thinking about ending your life, call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department, and tell someone near you. My Disclaimers page, linked at the foot of every page of this site, lists helplines for the UK and Ireland, and for the US and Canada.
- **For anything that keeps going,** start with a doctor. Low mood, worry, panic, sleep, eating, drinking, the sense that you're not yourself: a doctor can listen, check what else might be going on, and point you to the right help.
- **Look for a qualified therapist.** Ask what their training is and which professional body they belong to. A good one won't mind the question.
- **Keep astrology where it helps.** If your chart gives you words for what you're feeling, take those words into the room with you. Never stop a treatment or a medication because of something in a chart; talk to your doctor first.
- **After a reading,** if something in it weighs on you, say so, to me or to someone you trust. Nothing in a chart is a sentence.
