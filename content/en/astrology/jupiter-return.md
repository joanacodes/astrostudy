About every twelve years, Jupiter comes back to the degree it held when you were born. It's the most frequent of the slow planets' returns, and the gentlest: the close of one round of growth and the opening of the next.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

[Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) takes about twelve years to go round the Sun, and so about as long to go round the zodiac. When it reaches its birth position again, it completes a cycle. In the site's count for births from 1930 to 2009, the first exact return came between 11.3 and 12 years of age in every one of the 160 birth dates computed. That first one falls in childhood, and this site doesn't read children's charts; the returns I read come later.

They don't come at exact multiples of twelve. Jupiter's orbit is a little shorter than twelve years, so each return arrives slightly earlier than the last one's age plus twelve. Born on 1 July 1965, you met Jupiter at 12, 23.9, 35.9, 47.2, 59.1, 71.1 and 83: by the seventh return, a year ahead of 84. Across all the births computed, the second came between 23.2 and 23.9, the third between 35.2 and 35.9, the fourth between 47.1 and 47.8.

Halfway round, about six years after each return, transiting Jupiter opposes its birth place ([transiting Jupiter opposite natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-opposite-natal-jupiter/)), with a square on either side ([transiting Jupiter square natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-square-natal-jupiter/)): four checkpoints in every twelve years.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A return brings a planet's function back to the place where your chart first set it. For Jupiter, that function is expanding understanding, making meaning and supporting growth, so the return is read through your natal Jupiter: its sign, the manner of your growing; its house, the area of life where it happens, which needs your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)); and its aspects, which transiting Jupiter repeats on its birth degree.

With the orbs I use for timing, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, a Jupiter transit lasts about twelve days when the planet passes straight over the point, and about eight months when a retrograde loop takes it over three times, which happens about three times in ten ([Jupiter retrograde](/astrology/jupiter-retrograde/)). Jupiter also spends about a year in each sign, so some astrologers count the whole year it spends in your Jupiter's sign as the return.

The return keeps time with another twelve-year count. [Annual profections](/astrology/annual-profections/) move your rising sign on one sign a year and bring it home at 12, 24, 36 and so on. In every birth the site computed, the returns at about 23½, 35½ and 47½ fell in the last year of a profection round, the 12th-house year, just before the count comes home to the rising sign.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read a Jupiter return as a new chapter of growth. Jupiter's natal house says where you tend to find room and meaning; the return asks what you want more of there, what you have outgrown, and what you now understand that you didn't twelve years ago. It is one of the transits I follow when I look ahead, never alone: in a year with a return, I read it with the [solar return](/astrology/solar-return/) of that year, the progressions, and whatever the slower planets are doing.

The ages line up with other cycles in ways worth knowing. The third return, near 35½, falls about six years after the first [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/), and the seventh, between about 82 and 84, lands near the once-in-a-lifetime [Uranus return](/astrology/uranus-return/), at about 84.

Tradition called Jupiter the greater benefic, and the return has a hopeful reputation. In the system I built for my readings, Jupiter's function is growth and understanding, not luck, wealth or success, and opportunity is never a guarantee. A return opens a door; it doesn't decide what's behind it. In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), natal Jupiter was checked like every placement and wasn't one of the three that held far more often, so I read its return as one theme among several. Weather, not a forecast.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A Jupiter return brings luck or money.”** It describes room to grow, read through your natal Jupiter. No transit promises a windfall.
- **“It comes exactly every twelve years.”** A little less: by the seventh return, about a year early.
- **“Everyone's Jupiter return feels the same.”** The ages are shared; the sign, house and aspects of your Jupiter are yours.
- **“Nothing happening means it was wasted.”** Many returns are quiet. Growth in understanding seldom makes noise.
