For most of its history, astrology and medicine shared a room. Physicians consulted the sky before they bled a patient, and astrologers wrote manuals on disease. This page tells that story as history, because that is all it is on this site: I don't read health in a chart.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Medical astrology, traditionally called iatromathematics, applied astrology to the body. Its core was melothesia, the matching of body parts, illnesses and remedies to the Sun, the Moon, the planets and the twelve signs. Its best-known image is the Zodiac Man, a human figure with each sign set on the part it was thought to govern, from Aries at the head to Pisces at the feet, which appeared in medieval calendars, Books of Hours and medical treatises ([The signs and the body, in old medicine](/astrology/signs-and-the-body-in-old-medicine/)).

Its roots are old. Greek medicine in the Hippocratic tradition taught a doctrine of critical days, *dies decretorii*. Galen believed that the heavenly bodies influenced human life, yet distrusted the horoscope-casters. The earliest exposition of the Zodiac Man is in the *Astronomica* of Manilius, in the first century, though a cuneiform tablet of unknown date gives an almost identical list of the body's divisions. [Ptolemy’s Tetrabiblos](/astrology/ptolemys-tetrabiblos/) includes a chapter on bodily injuries and diseases, and when it entered the universities of Western Europe, it was often taught alongside medicine ([Medieval European astrology](/astrology/medieval-european-astrology/)). The literature grew long: [Marsilio Ficino](/astrology/marsilio-ficino/)'s *Three Books on Life* (1489), [William Lilly’s Christian Astrology (1647)](/astrology/william-lillys-christian-astrology-1647/), Richard Saunders's *The Astrological Judgment and Practice of Physick* (1677).

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Or rather, how it was done. Several practices ran side by side:

- **Timing treatment by the Moon.** The foremost rule of the Zodiac Man, attributed to Ptolemy, was not to cut a part of the body while the Moon was in the sign that ruled it. Surgery, medication and bloodletting were timed by it.
- **Charts of an illness.** Physician-astrologers cast charts to judge a sickness. Nicholas Culpeper's *Astrological Judgement of Diseases from the Decumbiture of the Sick* (1655), one of the most detailed works on medical astrology in early modern Europe, took the moment the patient lay down, the decumbiture of its title ([Event charts](/astrology/event-charts/)).
- **Planetary remedies.** In his herbal, Culpeper paired plants and diseases with planets and treated an illness with a remedy under an opposing planet, within the humoral medicine inherited from Galen.
- **Casebooks.** In London in the 1590s, Simon Forman built a practice as an astrologer-physician and recorded his clients' questions in detailed casebooks; his pupil Richard Napier kept records that a team led by Lauren Kassell at the University of Cambridge has since digitised.

In Italy, astrology was a routine part of medical training at Bologna. In England it was taught less, though astrological circumstances were sometimes invoked to clear surgeons of blame, and in 1629 Robert Fludd described medicine, theology and astrology as a single discipline.

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

How deeply astrology was once woven into ordinary life, and how much of its vocabulary still carries that past. The [6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) was given illness; the tradition kept lots of death and illness ([Lots of death and illness, in history](/astrology/lots-of-death-and-illness-in-history/)). Those meanings come from medicine's astrological centuries, not from anything a chart can see.

Astrological medicine declined after the seventeenth century. The historian Mark Harrison has traced what followed in British medicine, “from medical astrology to medical astronomy”, between about 1700 and 1850. Other branches of astrology went through their own histories of prediction ([Financial astrology, in history](/astrology/financial-astrology-in-history/)).

## Its limits {#its-limits}

There is no scientific basis for its core beliefs. No planet governs an organ, no sign weakens a limb, and no transit can make anyone ill or well.

That's why health stays out of my readings, for everyone, whatever the chart ([how I read](/how-i-read/)). I read the 6th house as daily work and daily care, never as a forecast of illness. If you're worried about a symptom or a diagnosis, the person to see is a doctor, and if it's urgent, the emergency services; [Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/) says more about where a chart's usefulness ends.
