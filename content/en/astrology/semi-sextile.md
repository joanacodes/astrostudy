The semi-sextile joins two planets in signs side by side, 30° apart. Neighbours on the zodiac but strangers in temperament, the two keep making small adjustments to each other, so quietly that the aspect is easy to overlook.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

A semi-sextile is an angle of 30°, a twelfth of the circle: exactly one sign. Aries and Taurus, Taurus and Gemini, and so on round to Pisces and Aries: every pair of neighbouring signs is a semi-sextile apart. Close as they are, they have nothing in common. Fire is followed by earth, cardinal by fixed, and polarity alternates from one sign to the next, so two signs side by side never share an element, a modality or a polarity.

I read a semi-sextile as adjustment in small steps. Each planet keeps its own job; what the aspect adds is a running awareness of the other and a habit of small corrections. It's subtle, which isn't the same as insignificant, and it isn't easy, whatever its name suggests: it's half a [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) only in degrees. The sextile joins signs that get on, fire with air, earth with water; the semi-sextile joins signs that have to learn each other's ways.

It's given a degree or two of orb either side (see [orbs](/astrology/orbs/)). Near the edge of a sign, it can fall between two planets in the same sign, or two signs apart, and the strangeness between neighbours is gone.

Thirty degrees is a third of a [square](/astrology/square/) and a quarter of a [trine](/astrology/trine/), but the semi-sextile isn't counted among the five major aspects, and Ptolemy left it out: in the *Tetrabiblos*, signs side by side have no aspect at all, as if turned away from each other. It's the first step away from a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) into a different sign. Its mirror, 30° short of the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/), is the [quincunx](/astrology/quincunx/), which also joins signs with nothing in common, but across the chart rather than next door. Where the quincunx asks for recalibration, the semi-sextile asks for something smaller and more frequent: coordination.

## Between which planets {#between-which-planets}

The site has no pages for a semi-sextile between two named planets. I read it by the speed of the two bodies, quick or slow.

- **Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus and Mars among themselves:** the coordination is daily and nearly invisible. The Sun can make it with Venus, which strays up to about 47° from it; Mercury, never more than about 28° from the Sun, falls just short of the exact angle.
- **One of them with Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune or Pluto:** the personal planet keeps adjusting, by small degrees, to a larger and slower demand. This is where I look most closely, because the small steps add up over years.
- **Two of the slow planets:** everyone born over several years shares it, so it's the houses it touches that tell me how to read it.

One step further round, at 45°, the [semi-square](/astrology/semi-square/) turns the small steps into small frictions: a local tension that asks for a correction rather than a quiet shift.

## The semi-sextile in synastry {#in-synastry}

Between two charts, a semi-sextile puts one person's planet in the sign next to the other's. It seldom shows as attraction or conflict, more in the small coordinations of shared life: one of you notices what the other has just walked past, or finishes neatly what the other started. With the Moon or Venus and a tight orb, that can add up to a real sense of being looked after; with Mars or Saturn, to a running negotiation over details. I read it both ways round and only after the major aspects, as in any of my [synastry readings](/synastry/).

## The semi-sextile in transits {#in-transits}

A transiting semi-sextile comes from a planet passing through the sign next to one of yours. The Sun makes one to your natal Sun about a month before your birthday and again about a month after. Kept to a degree, it passes quickly: hours for the Moon, a day or two for the Sun, Mercury and Venus, a few days for Mars; only the slow planets linger, for weeks or months. It tends to bring small things into view, a detail to adjust, an awareness that sharpens. Weather, not a forecast: it shows where a small step is wanted, never what will happen.
