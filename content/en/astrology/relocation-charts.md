Move to another city and the sky of your birth stays behind: it belongs to one minute and one place. A relocation chart asks a different question. What would that same minute have looked like from somewhere else?

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A relocation chart keeps the moment of your birth, the same instant in universal time, and changes only the place: the latitude and longitude of where you live now, or where you're thinking of going. Everything that depends on the moment stays put. Your planets keep their signs and degrees, and every aspect between them is unchanged. Everything that depends on the place is drawn again: the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) and the twelve houses.

The geometry is simple. The Earth turns 15° an hour, so as far as the angles are concerned, moving 15° of longitude east is like being born an hour later, and moving west an hour earlier: the Midheaven turns by about half a sign. Paris and New York are about 76° of longitude apart, so one birth minute gives them Midheavens about two and a half signs apart. Moving due north or south leaves the Midheaven where it was and tilts the horizon instead, so the Ascendant and the cusps between the angles shift, more and more as you go towards the poles ([Houses at high latitudes](/astrology/houses-at-high-latitudes/)).

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Enter your birth date, and the clock time and time zone of your birth, exactly as for your birth chart; then give the new place. The program turns the birth time into universal time and casts the angles and houses for the new coordinates. The mistake to avoid is typing the new place's local time: the moment has to stay the moment you were born ([Time zones and daylight saving in charts](/astrology/time-zones-and-daylight-saving-in-charts/)).

Astrologers who use the method then compare the two charts. Which sign rises in the new place? Which planets fall in a different house? Above all, which ones now sit close to an angle, the positions tradition gives the most weight ([Angular planets](/astrology/angular-planets/))? A planet tucked in the 12th house at your birthplace can sit on the Midheaven a few time zones away.

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

Those who work with relocation read the new houses as the way the same chart is lived somewhere else: the same planets, with different areas of life brought forward. It is a reading of emphasis, never a new chart. Relocation can't give you a planet you don't have, or change the way your planets talk to each other.

I don't draw relocation charts in my readings. I read the chart of the minute and place you were born, wherever you live now. If you've moved, what the move means to you belongs in the conversation, and the houses of your birth chart still describe where in life it lands.

The same question comes up each year with the [solar return](/astrology/solar-return/), which some astrologers cast for wherever you spend your birthday rather than for your birthplace. A world map of the same idea, showing where each planet would be angular, is [Astrocartography](/astrology/astrocartography/).

## Its limits {#its-limits}

Because only the angles and houses change, everything a relocation chart says rests on the house system. Two systems can disagree about which house a planet falls in at home; abroad, they can disagree more ([Which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)).

It also inherits every uncertainty of the birth time. An error of four minutes shifts the angles by about a degree, at the birthplace and in every relocated chart alike, so a time rounded to the hour leaves the new houses as uncertain as the old ones ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

And no chart, relocated or not, can say where you'll be happier, earn more, meet someone or stay well. Those are the questions a move raises, and they're the ones no chart can settle. A move is decided with your feet on the ground.
