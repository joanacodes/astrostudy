Polarity is the simplest pattern in the zodiac, and the one most often misread. Every other sign is active and every other receptive, and every sign has a partner across the circle: two ideas, both about balance, and neither about who's better.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The word carries two meanings in astrology, and they fit together.

The first is an alternation. Go round the zodiac and the signs switch between two kinds at every step: Aries active, Taurus receptive, Gemini active, Cancer receptive, and so on to Pisces. The active signs are the fire and air signs, Aries, Gemini, Leo, Libra, Sagittarius and Aquarius; the receptive ones are earth and water, Taurus, Cancer, Virgo, Scorpio, Capricorn and Pisces ([The four elements](/astrology/four-elements/)). Ptolemy called the first six masculine and diurnal and the second six feminine and nocturnal, beginning the count at Aries, and noted that some astrologers of his day counted from the rising sign instead. Later writers have also said positive and negative.

The second meaning is a pairing. Each sign faces another across the circle, 180° away, and the two make an axis: Aries and Libra, Taurus and Scorpio, Gemini and Sagittarius, Cancer and Capricorn, Leo and Aquarius, Virgo and Pisces. Opposite signs share a modality and a polarity, both active or both receptive, and they pair complementary elements, fire with air, earth with water.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The alternation runs through the aspects. Signs two apart share a polarity, which is part of why the [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) feels friendly. Signs four apart share their element too, and make the trine, the basis of the [triplicities](/astrology/triplicities/). Signs three apart, in [square](/astrology/square/), differ in polarity but share a modality ([The three modalities](/astrology/three-modalities/)). Neighbouring signs, and signs five apart, differ in polarity, element and modality all at once, which is why the semi-sextile and the quincunx are aspects of adjustment.

The axes work as two ends of one question. I read them like this:

- **Aries–Libra:** myself and the other person.
- **Taurus–Scorpio:** what is mine and what is shared.
- **Gemini–Sagittarius:** the facts close at hand and the meaning further off.
- **Cancer–Capricorn:** home and the world, care and responsibility.
- **Leo–Aquarius:** the single heart and the group.
- **Virgo–Pisces:** the detail that can be fixed and the whole that has to be accepted.

The houses carry the same axes, the 1st facing the 7th and the 4th the 10th, and the lunar nodes always lie along one of them ([Nodal axis](/astrology/nodal-axis/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I say active and receptive, because the old labels describe two directions of energy, not anyone's gender. The system I built for my readings is explicit that Mars isn't masculinity and Venus isn't femininity, and the same goes for the signs: a receptive sign takes in, absorbs and consolidates; an active sign reaches out, starts and asserts. A chart weighted towards one side tells me something about rhythm, whether someone tends to go towards things or to let them come, and nothing about worth.

The axes matter more in practice. A planet in one sign always has the opposite sign in view, and an [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) across an axis describes a polarity to integrate, never an enmity. When a chart leans hard into one end of an axis, the other end is often where growth lies, which is also how I read the two nodes, always facing each other across the circle. And the house axes, which need [the birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/), show where in a life each pull is felt.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Feminine signs are for women and masculine signs for men.”** Everyone has planets in both kinds. The words describe energy, not gender.
- **“Negative signs are bad signs.”** Negative works here like the pole of a battery. Receiving is as necessary as reaching out.
- **“Opposite signs are incompatible.”** They share a modality and a polarity, and their elements complete each other. An opposition is a seesaw, not a war.
- **“Polarity means conflict.”** It means two ends of one thing. Tension can come with it, but balance is the point.
