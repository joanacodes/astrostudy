It's the question under all the others, and it deserves a straight answer from someone who reads charts, not a shrug.

## The short answer {#the-short-answer}

If you mean, does the sky at your birth describe who you are: controlled tests haven't found that it does. When astrologers have been tested blind, they've done no better than chance, and no mechanism has been proposed that fits what physics and biology know. If you mean, is astrology a real practice with a real history: yes, a very old one, and historians take it seriously as part of the history of science. I read anyway, for the reasons I give below, and I'd rather you heard the evidence from me first.

## What the research found {#what-the-research-found}

Start with the practice. Forms of astrology have been used since at least the second millennium BCE. Until the Enlightenment it was generally considered a scholarly tradition, common in learned circles and closely tied to astronomy, meteorology, medicine and alchemy; it has been regarded as pseudoscientific since the 18th century. Historians such as Tamsyn Barton and Roger Beck argue that applying that label wholesale to earlier centuries is anachronistic, and stress how much the discipline mattered to the development of medieval science. Roy Willis and Patrick Curry argue that astrology's central concern "is not knowledge (factual, let alone scientific) but wisdom (ethical, spiritual and pragmatic)". Whether any of this makes it a science is a question with its own page: [Is astrology a science?](/astrology/is-astrology-a-science/)

Then the claim. Scientific testing has found no evidence for the premises or the effects that astrological traditions describe, and where astrology has made predictions that could be checked under controlled conditions, they have failed. The best-known test, [Carlson's double-blind test (1985)](/astrology/carlsons-double-blind-test-1985/), published in *Nature*, was designed with astrologers nominated by the National Council for Geocosmic Research, who helped draw up the central claim of natal astrology to be tested. Then 28 astrologers tried to match more than a hundred birth charts to personality profiles, and did no better than chance. The other tests, and exactly what they asked, are on [Does astrology work?](/astrology/does-astrology-work/)

Could it work at all? In 1975 *The Humanist* published "Objections to Astrology", a statement signed by 186 scientists, which centred on the lack of any mechanism by which the planets and stars could shape a life. The astronomer Carl Sagan declined to sign it. Not because he thought astrology valid: he found the statement's tone authoritarian, and he thought that rejecting astrology for lack of a mechanism, while "certainly a relevant point", wasn't convincing in itself. A statement that described and refuted the principal tenets of astrology, he argued, would have persuaded more. Carlson's test, ten years later, did something close to that. Today, no proposed mechanism by which the positions of the planets could affect people fits basic, well-understood biology and physics.

Why, then, can it feel so real? Psychology has well-studied answers, each with its own page: [confirmation bias](/astrology/confirmation-bias/), [subjective validation](/astrology/subjective-validation/), [cold reading](/astrology/cold-reading/) and the [self-fulfilling prophecy](/astrology/self-fulfilling-prophecy/). None of them proves astrology false. Together, they explain why feeling that it works is not the same as finding that it does.

## What I found {#what-i-found}

I came to astrology to mock it, and it answered back, so I tested it. I went through my own chart placement by placement, the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign, each planet in its sign and house, with a tick or a cross, then did the same, with their consent, for family, friends and strangers. About a third held, 30 to 40%, the Sun sign, the rising sign, Venus and Mercury among them, which is close to what a flattering horoscope scores. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), [Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/) and [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) held 75 to 90% of the time. It's [a notebook, not a study](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), and it proves nothing about astrology in general.

So is it real for me? I don't claim to know how astrology works, and I don't claim a gift: there's nothing in a reading you couldn't learn yourself, with time. My readings are offered for reflection and entertainment. A chart describes tendencies, not fate, and a reading is a conversation with yourself, not an instruction; at its best, it gives you who you already are, in more words. When something rests on a placement that held less often in my tests, I tell you. And when what you're carrying needs a doctor or a therapist rather than a chart, I tell you that too: [Astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/) says more.
