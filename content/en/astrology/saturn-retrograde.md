Saturn was the farthest planet the ancient astrologers knew, and it spends more of the year going backwards than any planet they could see. Its loops are short, about seven degrees, but they keep it on the same stretch of the zodiac for the best part of a year.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Every 377 to 380 days Earth overtakes [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), which needs about 29½ years to go round the zodiac, and each loop starts about two weeks later in the calendar than the one before. For 134 to 141 days Saturn seems to move backwards, and in the middle of that stretch it stands opposite the Sun, up all night and as close to us as it comes that year.

Four loops, from the summer of 2025 to the start of 2029:

- **13 July to 28 November 2025**, from 1°55′ Aries back to 25°09′ Pisces;
- **26 July to 10 December 2026**, from 14°44′ to 7°55′ Aries;
- **9 August to 24 December 2027**, from 27°52′ to 21°01′ Aries;
- **22 August 2028 to 5 January 2029**, from 11°18′ to 4°24′ Taurus.

Aries is the sign of Saturn's [fall](/astrology/fall/), and the loops of 2026 and 2027 both turn back there ([Saturn in Aries](/astrology/saturn-in-aries/)). The 2026 loop turned at 14°44′ and stopped at 7°55′, never reaching back to 0°45′ Aries, where Saturn had met Neptune on 20 February that year ([The Saturn–Neptune conjunction of 2026](/astrology/saturn-neptune-conjunction-of-2026/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The length of the loops drifts over the decades. In the dates from 2000 to 2040, the longest, 141 days, came between 2015 and 2021, with Saturn in Sagittarius, Capricorn and Aquarius; the shortest, 134 days, in 2001 to 2005 and again from 2030, between Gemini and Leo.

A loop of seven degrees crossed three times keeps Saturn near any point inside it for months. With the orbs my forecast uses, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, just over half of Saturn's transits to a point in a chart touch it three times, and even the single passes tend to linger, since Saturn moves so slowly near its turning points. Either way, a typical Saturn transit lasts about nine months. That is why a [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/) so often comes in three passes rather than one.

The neighbours frame it. [Jupiter retrograde](/astrology/jupiter-retrograde/) is a little shorter, about four months, and backs over about ten degrees; [Uranus retrograde](/astrology/uranus-retrograde/), the next planet out, lasts about five months and covers only about four. The farther the planet, the longer the loop lasts and the less ground it covers.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The system I built for my readings defines Saturn as the function that establishes structure, responsibility and boundaries over the long term. More than a third of people are born with it retrograde, and I read that as structure built from the inside first: rules set privately, responsibility taken on before it's asked for, an authority that comes from inner reckoning rather than titles. It's the inward rhythm I describe in [Natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/), applied to the planet of patience.

Saturn is also one of the three placements that held 75 to 90% of the time in my notebook, so I never read a Saturn lightly ([The three that held: Saturn, Chiron and Lilith](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/#saturn)). The retrograde is another matter. Everyone born over the same four and a half months shares it, and I read it through the house it falls in, which takes the birth time.

Moving through your chart as a [transit](/astrology/transits/), a retrograde Saturn spends about four and a half months reversing over one house, and I read those months as an inspection of what you have built there: a commitment re-examined, a structure reinforced or quietly let go. It is a timetable, not a wall. Nothing about it says a plan has failed; it says a plan is being checked.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Saturn retrograde brings hard times.”** It describes a season of review. What you meet in those months depends on your chart and your life, not on the loop.
- **“Born with Saturn retrograde, you'll lack discipline.”** It describes discipline that starts inside, and is often stricter for it.
- **“Saturn retrograde delays everything.”** Review isn't delay. Some things simply get done twice, and better.
- **“Saturn retrograde is a test you can fail.”** Checking what you've built isn't an exam, and nobody is marking it.
