Alkaid is the star at the tip of the Plough's handle, the last one the eye reaches as it follows the curve away from the bowl. Its zodiac degree in 2026 is 27°18′ Virgo, less than three degrees short of the September equinox point.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Of the Great Bear's stars, only two outshine η Ursae Majoris, Alkaid, at magnitude 1.86. It is a hot, young, blue-white star, only a few million years old, and unlike most of the Plough's stars it doesn't travel through space with the others: it is not a member of the Ursa Major moving group, just a star that lines up with them from where we stand. Since 1943 astronomers have used its spectrum as one of the anchor points for sorting other stars into types. Its other traditional name is Benetnasch. Its declination, more than 49° north of the celestial equator, keeps it above the horizon all night, all year, anywhere north of about 41°, which takes in the whole of Britain.

At precession's pace of a degree in 72 years, it has gone from 26°55′ [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/) in 2000 to 27°18′ in 2026 and will reach 27°38′ by 2050, crossing into Libra only around 2220 ([precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/)). A planet "on" Alkaid is more than fifty degrees from it in the sky, since the star lies 54.4° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/): the two share nothing but a number.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

Its Arabic name is short for qā'id bināt naʿsh, the leader of the daughters of the bier. Arab sky-watchers saw the Plough as a funeral: the four stars of the bowl are the bier, the three of the handle, Alkaid, Mizar and Alioth, are the mourning daughters, and Alkaid walks at their head. In Hindu tradition the star is Marīci, one of the seven sages, the Saptarshi; in Chinese astronomy it is the Seventh Star of the Northern Dipper.

Medieval magic, which set the fifteen Behenian stars each under one or two planets, gave the mourners' leader to Venus and the Moon, with the magnet as its stone and succory, or chicory, as its plant. Along the zodiac the next Behenian star is [Algorab](/astrology/algorab/), the crow's star, in mid-Libra.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Alkaid's degree belongs to the last days of a [Sun in Virgo](/astrology/sun-in-virgo/), about 19–20 September. The tradition's one-degree rule ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)) stretches thinnest here: [Procyon](/astrology/procyon/), sixteen degrees south of the Sun's path, meets a planet more closely than this star ever can.

For the Ascendant or the Midheaven to fall on Alkaid's degree, the birth time has to be right to within a few minutes, the time an angle takes to sweep across the two degrees around 27° Virgo ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). I don't place Alkaid in my readings, and of all the stars it is the one I'd least want to: a degree of late Virgo tied to a star more than fifty degrees away is where description and reading most need keeping apart.
