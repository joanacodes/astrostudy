If you follow Mercury retrograde online, you’ll meet the warnings before it has even started: Mercury is entering its shadow. You’ll meet them again for weeks after it ends. The shadow is a real stretch of the zodiac, but the period is an astrologers’ way of counting, and the sky itself doesn’t mark it.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Every retrograde covers a stretch of the zodiac, from the degree where the planet stations retrograde back to the degree where it stations direct. The shadow is that same stretch, crossed while the planet is moving forward: once before the retrograde, once after.

Take Mercury in the autumn of 2026, in the dates I use. It stations retrograde on 24 October at 20°58′ Scorpio, backs up, and stations direct on 13 November at 5°02′ Scorpio. The shadow is everything between, from 5°02′ to 20°58′ Scorpio:

- **The pre-shadow** begins the day Mercury, still moving forward, first reaches 5°02′ Scorpio, and runs until the station retrograde.
- **The retrograde** takes Mercury back over the same degrees, from 20°58′ to 5°02′.
- **The post-shadow** begins at the station direct and ends when Mercury, moving forward again, gets back to 20°58′ Scorpio.

The convention belongs to modern astrologers. I can’t point you to whoever first drew it: it has passed from one astrologer to the next, mostly in writing about [Mercury retrograde](/astrology/mercury-retrograde/), and it is applied to Venus and Mars as well. Astronomy marks the two [stations](/astrology/stations/) and nothing else. At the edges of the shadow, the planet doesn’t change speed or direction, and nothing at all happens in the sky.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

What the shadow does capture is a real piece of geometry: every degree inside it is crossed three times, forward, backward and forward again. Outside it, the planet passes once.

The width of the stretch changes with every retrograde. Between 2000 and 2040, Mercury’s covers between 8½° and 16°. In 2026 alone it is about 14° in Pisces in February and March, 10° in Cancer in June and July, and 16° in Scorpio in the autumn. Venus’s retrogrades cover about 15° to 16½°, and Mars’s between 10° and 19½°, the widest of them being the one of 2027, from 20°55′ Leo to 10°25′ Virgo.

Because the shadow is measured in degrees, its length in days depends on how fast the planet moves either side of its retrograde, and that varies too. Any fixed number of days you read about is a rule of thumb. The honest way to find a shadow is to take the two station degrees from an ephemeris and look for the days the planet crosses them.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I don’t read the shadow as a period with a mood of its own. What I look at is whether a point of your chart sits inside it. If your natal Sun is at 12° Scorpio, say, Mercury crosses it three times in the autumn of 2026, within a couple of months: once in the pre-shadow, once retrograde, once in the post-shadow. Each pass is a transit, and a theme that comes round three times tends to be one you look at more than once: Mercury’s work of taking in, sorting and passing on, turned to whatever that point stands for in your chart.

If none of your points sits there, the shadow is just a stretch of sky. Which of your houses it falls in depends on your birth time, like every house of the chart ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Either way, it is weather, not a forecast: three passes over one degree describe a theme coming back for another look, never something bound to go wrong.

In a birth chart I read the retrograde itself, when a planet was born moving backwards, as I describe in [Natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/). A natal shadow I don’t read at all: the system I built for my readings doesn’t mark one.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The shadow is part of the retrograde.”** In the shadow, the planet is moving forward. Only the stretch between the two stations is retrograde.
- **“The shadow is astronomy.”** It is a way of counting. Astronomy has the two stations; the edges of the shadow are degrees that astrologers mark, where nothing happens in the sky.
- **“Anything begun in the shadow will have to be redone.”** No transit decides that. A shadow describes a stretch the planet will cross again, not the fate of what you start in it.
- **“The shadow always lasts two weeks.”** It is set by degrees, not days, and the degrees change with every retrograde: about 10° for Mercury in Cancer in 2026, 16° in Scorpio a few months later.
