Before Lilith was a point in the Moon's orbit, it was a moon. Dark Moon Lilith is that older Lilith: a second satellite of the Earth that one astronomer announced, one astrologer named, and nobody ever found.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

In 1898 Georg Waltemath, an astronomer from Hamburg whose name is also spelt Waldemath, announced that the Earth had more than one moon. He had started from the idea that something was tugging at the [Moon](/astrology/moon/)'s orbit, and he described a system of small moons, one of them about 1,030,000 km away and 700 km across, going round the Earth in 119 days. He said it was normally invisible to the naked eye, cited earlier sightings by others, and predicted that it would show itself between 2 and 4 February 1898. Twelve sightings were reported on those days. Two astronomers who had been watching the same part of the sky, W. Winkler and Baron Ivo von Benko, showed the reports to be mistaken or fraudulent. Waltemath then announced a third moon, a "real weather and magnet moon", and the journal *Science* wondered whether it was "also the moon presiding over lunacy".

In 1918 the English astrologer Walter Gorn Old, who wrote as Sepharial, claimed to have confirmed Waltemath's moon. He named it Lilith, after the figure of medieval Jewish legend who was Adam's first wife, and said it was dark enough to stay invisible except when it crossed the face of the Sun, as he claimed to have seen it do. By then astronomers had already set Waltemath's moon aside.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A chart that shows the Dark Moon places it from an orbit that was described but never observed. On Waltemath's own figure, a moon circling the Earth in 119 days would go round the whole zodiac in about four months, roughly ten days to a sign: a quick point, nothing like the nine months mean Lilith spends in each sign.

The name, meanwhile, moved on. When astrologers say Lilith today, they generally mean the Black Moon: the far end of the real Moon's orbit, sometimes taken instead as the empty second focus of that ellipse, which turns round the zodiac in about 8 years and 10 months ([Black Moon Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/)). It's a point, not an object, and its versions differ only in how they follow the Moon: the mean one, the true one, the [interpolated one](/astrology/interpolated-lilith/). The Dark Moon belongs to none of them.

The asteroid that carries the name is something else again: a real body, found in 1927, going round the Sun on an orbit of its own ([Asteroid Lilith (1181)](/astrology/asteroid-lilith-1181/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read one Lilith, the mean Black Moon, and the Dark Moon isn't part of my readings. Its position rests on figures for a moon that failed the one test it was given, and a point that has to be taken on trust gives me nothing to check against a life.

Mean Lilith has earned its place in a way the Dark Moon never could. My notebook put it with Saturn and Chiron among [the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/), 75 to 90% of the time, against about a third for placements overall; the Dark Moon was never in the count ([my testing notes](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

If a reading or an app speaks of your dark moon, look at the label beside the glyph before going further: the words Dark Moon point to Waltemath's moon, while mean, true or interpolated point to the Black Moon ([which Lilith is mine?](/astrology/which-lilith-is-mine/)). ## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Dark Moon and the Black Moon are the same.”** One was meant to be an object that was never found; the other is a point of the real Moon's orbit.
- **“Waltemath saw his moon.”** He worked it out from the Moon's motion and predicted when it would show; the sightings reported for February 1898 didn't stand.
- **“Sepharial discovered Lilith.”** He claimed to have confirmed Waltemath's moon, and gave it its name.
- **“The Dark Moon is asteroid 1181.”** The asteroid is real and goes round the Sun; the Dark Moon was supposed to go round the Earth.
