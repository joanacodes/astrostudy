Venus conjunct North Node puts what you value, and the way you relate, right on the growing edge of your chart. Learning to choose, to receive and to make fair exchanges is part of the less familiar capacity your life keeps practising.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) is the part of a chart that values and relates: what attracts you, what you prefer, how you balance give and take. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/), where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's going north, points to a less familiar capacity that grows through practice and feedback; the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/), opposite, to the established one you start from. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) fuses Venus with the growing end, so valuing and relating become the very things you're asked to practise.

In plain words, relationships are where you grow, and also how. Whatever your more familiar habits are, on the South Node's side, this conjunction keeps inviting you to say what you like, to receive without paying it back at once, and to let other people matter. None of that arrives ready-made: development isn't automatic, and a new way of relating can feel clumsy at first. It isn't destiny either. Venus on the North Node doesn't promise a person; it describes a capacity you strengthen by using it, without giving up what you already know how to do.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The nodes move backwards, about 18 months in each sign, so the North Node's sign is shared by everyone born in that stretch, and Venus passes over it for a week or two each year. With the North Node in Taurus or Libra, Venus is in its own sign on the growing end: a feel for steadiness, or for fairness, that comes naturally and still asks to be lived more fully, while the South Node opposite, in Scorpio or Aries, is the familiar ground the growth starts from. The houses matter most here, because they say where the practising happens, and they need a birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

About 1 person in 29 is born with Venus within 6° of the North Node, counted with the true node; the mean node is never more than a degree or two away, and I look at both.

The pair's other angles:

- **[Venus sextile North Node](/astrology/venus-sextile-north-node/):** Venus a friendly step from the growing end, and in easy trine with the familiar one.
- **[Venus square North Node](/astrology/venus-square-north-node/):** Venus at right angles to both ends, asking each to make room for what you value.
- **[Venus trine North Node](/astrology/venus-trine-north-node/):** Venus in easy step with the direction of growth, and sextile to where you started.
- **[Venus opposite North Node](/astrology/venus-opposite-north-node/):** Venus on the familiar end, a well-practised way of relating that the growth builds from.

Between two charts, [Venus conjunct North Node in synastry](/astrology/venus-conjunct-north-node-synastry/) sets one person's Venus on the other's growing edge: their way of loving can feel like an invitation to develop. I read it from both sides, and never as a sign that two people are fated.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting North Node conjunct natal Venus](/astrology/transiting-north-node-conjunct-natal-venus/) comes once in each 18.6-year round of the nodes and lasts a month or two, the true node wobbling back and forth as it goes. In the year around it, eclipses fall in the stretch of the zodiac near your Venus, and opposite it, which is why astrologers watch this transit: a season when what you value and how you relate come up for review. Born with the conjunction, you meet it at every [nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/), around 18½, 37 and 56.

Venus itself crosses your natal North Node about once a year, a day or two of noticing what you'd like to practise.

Weather, not a forecast: a review of what you value, never an appointment with anyone.
