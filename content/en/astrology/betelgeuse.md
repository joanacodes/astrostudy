Betelgeuse is the red shoulder of Orion, a star so restless that it visibly faded between October 2019 and February 2020. In the zodiac it sits on the last degree of Gemini, a few decades from crossing into Cancer, and on almost exactly the same degree as the pole star.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Betelgeuse is α Orionis, a red supergiant. The Yale catalogue gives its magnitude as 0.50, but it never keeps still: it varies between about 0.0 and 1.6 over a main cycle of roughly 400 days, the widest range of any first-magnitude star. In its fading of 2019–2020 it dropped from 0.5 to 1.7, which a study with the Hubble Space Telescope traced to a cloud of dust the star had thrown off.

Betelgeuse has reached the last degree of [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/): 29°07′ in 2026, after 27°46′ in 1930 and 28°45′ in 2000. At about a degree every 72 years ([precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/)), it will cross into [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/) around 2090.

Its latitude is 16° south of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), far beyond the 5° or so that the Moon ever strays, so the Moon never passes in front of it and no planet comes near it. [Polaris](/astrology/polaris/), the pole star, sits on almost the same zodiac degree, 28°55′ Gemini, at the other end of the sky.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

The name is a misreading. The Arabic was yad al-jawzā', the hand of al-Jawzā', the old Arabic name for Orion; a medieval copyist read the first letter as a b, and Europe got Betelgeuse. The meaning "armpit", still repeated, comes from a later false reading of the same name.

[Ptolemy](/astrology/ptolemy/) described its colour as hypókirrhos, roughly "tawny orange", and that redness has long linked it with [Mars](/astrology/mars/), the planet named after the Roman war god: the star lore Richard Hinckley Allen gathered in *Star Names* (1899) ties the red star to conquest. That is a tradition's picture, said as such.

In India it is Ārdrā, "the moist one", which gives its name to a lunar mansion presided over by Rudra, the storm god of the Rigveda ([the 28 lunar mansions](/astrology/28-lunar-mansions/)). In Japan the Heike clan took the red star as its emblem while the Genji took white [Rigel](/astrology/rigel/), at Orion's foot: two families at war, kept apart in the sky by the three stars of the belt.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The tradition's measure is a planet or angle within about a degree, by zodiac position ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). For a birth in 2026 that window runs from 28°07′ Gemini to 0°07′ Cancer, so a planet that has only just entered Cancer can still be on Betelgeuse; the orb is counted in degrees, not in signs. For a birth in 1930 the window is 26°46′ to 28°46′ Gemini.

Birthdays on about 19 and 20 June bring the Sun to the star, at the very end of a [Sun in Gemini](/astrology/sun-in-gemini/), a day or two before the solstice. The same birthdays put the Sun on Polaris's degree too, which shows what a contact by longitude is: one number shared by two stars some 82° apart.

Read by an astrologer who uses stars, Betelgeuse would lend a planet on its degree something of the red star's martial lore. My readings compute no stars, so I keep that lore here, as history worth knowing and never a verdict about anyone born with a planet at 29° Gemini ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).
