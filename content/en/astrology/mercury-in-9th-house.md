Far places, other languages, other people's beliefs, the questions with no quick answer: Mercury in the 9th house wants the long view, and will travel, on foot or on paper, to get it. *What does it all mean?* is this mind's favourite question, and it rarely tires of asking.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 9th house](/astrology/9th-house/) is the house of the far horizon: long journeys, foreign places and languages, philosophy, belief, publishing, the search for meaning. The 9th is cadent; the old books called it the God and gave the Sun its joy there. Its natural sign is Sagittarius, the sign of Mercury's detriment, but a house is not a sign. The 9th gives [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) a subject, not a condition, and Mercury's strength still comes from the sign it's actually in; in Sagittarius itself, sign and house share one horizon ([Mercury in Sagittarius](/astrology/mercury-in-sagittarius/)).

Here the planet of learning turns to the kind of learning that changes how you see the world. You may study widely, out of love more than for a certificate, collect languages or ways of thinking, and enjoy a good argument about principles. Belief, for this Mercury, is something to examine and talk over rather than to receive. How the search goes depends on the sign: a 9th-house Mercury in Cancer looks for meaning in family and history, in Libra in fairness and justice, in Capricorn in what has stood the test of time, in Aquarius in the shape of the future.

When you were born, a 9th-house Mercury had not long crossed the meridian, the highest point of its daily path, and was beginning to descend in the western sky. In Placidus, my house system, the cusp of the 10th is that crossing, the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/), so a few minutes decide whether Mercury is 9th or 10th ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

[Mercury in the 8th house](/astrology/mercury-in-8th-house/) looks underneath; [Mercury in the 10th house](/astrology/mercury-in-10th-house/) speaks in public. The 9th looks further off, towards the horizon.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You fall for minds: people from elsewhere, people who know something you don't, people who'll argue with you about what life is for. Long conversations about big questions are a form of intimacy for you, and a shared love of travel or learning keeps a bond fresh. Some of your closest bonds may begin far from home, or be kept alive across a border. Differences in belief can be a joy or a strain. This Mercury is at its best when it's curious about a partner's view rather than set on converting it.

## At work {#at-work}

Teaching, publishing, translation, research, journalism, travel and tourism, international work, religion and philosophy: work that crosses borders or explains the world. You're often the colleague who brings the bigger context into the meeting, or who can explain a complicated idea to people from a different background. A job that never lets you learn anything new can feel like a small room with no window.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Abstraction: living in the big question and missing the small answer that was needed today. Preaching, when you meant to share. Restlessness for the next horizon before you've finished the journey you're on. And a habit of trusting ideas from far away more readily than the wisdom close to home.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Bring ideas home: for each big principle, find one small, practical use. Teach what you learn, which keeps it honest. Travel when you can; when you can't, read someone from a culture you don't know. Ask about other people's beliefs before offering yours. Keep a note of where your own beliefs came from, and when: it's the kind of study this house enjoys most. A mind that loves meaning does its best work when the meaning reaches somebody else.
