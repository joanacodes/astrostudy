This is the Neptune contact with Saturn that nobody born between 1930 and 2009 escapes: everyone meets it at least once before 80, for about two and a half years, at an age their birth chart sets.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) builds and holds: commitments, routines, rules, the long effort that makes a life dependable. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) loosens certainties and asks what they're for. A [square](/astrology/square/) sets the two in friction, so under this [transit](/astrology/transits/) what you've built is tested by doubt, and the doubt by what you've built.

Plans can resist for reasons that are hard to name. A commitment may feel heavier, or emptier, than it used to; a routine you relied on may stop working without any obvious cause; you may wonder whether the effort is worth it. Saturn's reflex is to tighten its grip, which rarely helps here, and its blind spot is mistaking flexibility for irresponsibility. The square asks for the opposite skill: to keep your commitments while letting their form change, and to tell a structure that protects you from one that only protects your fear.

Doubt is part of this transit; despair isn't. If low mood stays for weeks, or the ground seems to give way, please talk to a doctor or a therapist and let astrology wait ([astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/)).

With Saturn in your 7th, the square often comes while Neptune crosses your 4th or your 10th: home life ([Neptune transiting the 4th house](/astrology/neptune-transit-4th-house/)), or your public direction, goes soft just as your commitments to others ask for steadiness. The 7th, the 4th and the 10th are angular houses, and only your birth time places them ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Born with [Saturn square Neptune](/astrology/saturn-square-neptune/), as at the start of 1945, you had this square in your first weeks; its second point then lay about eighty years away.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Within my orbs, 1½° before exact and 1° after, the square lasts about two years and seven months, twenty-two at the least. The friction gathers at the exact contacts, three in most passes and five in about one in four, with breathing space between them.

Of the people born between 1930 and 2009, not one reaches 80 without it, and a handful meet it twice. The ages range widely: in the first weeks for someone born at the start of 1945, about 5 for 1965, 23 for 1985, 30 for 2005, 32 for 1935, 50 for 1955, 56 for 1975 and nearly 60 for 1995. For that 2005 birth the square follows the first [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/) by less than a year, so the review the return brings runs straight into this softer questioning.

The same 2005 birth meets [transiting Neptune trine natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-neptune-trine-natal-saturn/) at about 17, this square at about 30, [transiting Neptune sextile natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-neptune-sextile-natal-saturn/) at about 43½ and [transiting Neptune conjunct natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-neptune-conjunct-natal-saturn/) at about 70½. The opposition, [transiting Neptune opposite natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-neptune-opposite-natal-saturn/), would come only after 80.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Keep the commitment and renegotiate its form. Ask what a duty is for, and whether there's a lighter way to serve that purpose.

Shorten your plans' horizon. A year ahead may be all this square lets you see clearly, so plan that year well.

Let someone check the structures you can't see past: a friend, a colleague or a professional who can say which of your rules still serve you.
