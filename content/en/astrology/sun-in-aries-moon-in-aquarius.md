Both of these signs guard their independence, for different reasons. The Aries Sun wants freedom to act; the Aquarius Moon wants a little distance so it can feel clearly. You were born in the last week of a lunar month, under a Moon that rises before dawn.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

The Moon sits two signs behind the Sun, in an air sign that fans the Aries fire: a sextile by sign, the kind of help that has to be taken up. Aries is cardinal and starts things; Aquarius is fixed and holds a position once it has thought it through. The [Sun in Aries](/astrology/sun-in-aries/) acts on instinct; the [Moon in Aquarius](/astrology/moon-in-aquarius/) understands a feeling once it has stood back far enough to see it. Together they tend to make someone who moves fast and feels from a slight distance, which others can mistake for coolness.

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) rules your Sun's sign. Aquarius has two rulers, and I read both: [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), the modern one, for the part of life where this Moon insists on its own way of doing things, and [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), the traditional one, for the structures it chooses to keep. Saturn's fall is in Aries: the Moon's older ruler, set down in your Sun's sign, is out of its element there, and patience, for this pair, tends to be learned rather than given. The Sun keeps its exaltation; Aquarius gives the Moon no dignity.

Seen from the ground, every one of these births comes after the last quarter. The Moon was 271° to 329° ahead of the Sun, which is 31° to 89° behind it, twenty-two to twenty-seven days after a new moon: a waning crescent that rises in the small hours. Of the eight phases, the [last quarter moon](/astrology/last-quarter-moon/) takes seven of these births in eight and the [balsamic moon](/astrology/balsamic-moon/) the rest, and with the Moon near 60° behind, the chart has a waning [Sun sextile Moon](/astrology/sun-sextile-moon/). [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) sets the eight out in order.

About 1 person in 142 is born with it. The Moon walks through Capricorn, Aquarius and Pisces in about a week, some two and a half days apiece, and a birthday that saw it cross from one into the next can't be placed without the time: the [Sun in Aries, Moon in Capricorn](/astrology/sun-in-aries-moon-in-capricorn/) and the [Sun in Aries, Moon in Pisces](/astrology/sun-in-aries-moon-in-pisces/) are only hours away ([the Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). Nor can the rising sign, which completes the opening sketch of a chart; a sketch, and Aquarius likes to see the whole system before judging.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Love works best for you between equals. The Aries Sun likes directness, the Aquarius Moon wants a friend first, and both want room to stay themselves inside the bond. Separate interests, honest arguments and a partner who isn't threatened by your circle of friends suit the pair. What needs watching is the habit of handling closeness the way you handle a problem: efficiently, quickly, and a little from the outside.

## At work {#at-work}

You're often the colleague who spots a better way and says so, early. The Sun supplies the nerve to propose it; the Moon supplies the detachment to see the whole system and a steady loyalty to the people and principles involved. Rigid hierarchies and unexplained rules wear both lights down. Teams with a shared aim and a real say in how it's reached tend to suit you, as does work where new ideas are the job rather than a nuisance.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Independence can turn into distance without anyone deciding it should. The Aries Sun handles things alone; the Aquarius Moon processes feelings at arm's length; together they can leave you capable, principled and quietly lonely. What helps is a habit of reporting in: telling the people close to you what you feel while you're still feeling it, not once it has been understood and filed away. It feels clumsy at first, and a good deal of closeness grows out of exactly that clumsiness.
