One of you wants to get going; the other wants to know what lies underneath first. In Mars square Pluto in synastry, a right angle joins one person's Mars to the other's Pluto, and pace and depth keep pressing on each other from the side.

## What flows {#what-flows}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) moves: it initiates, asserts, competes, defends a line. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) goes deep: it transforms, regenerates, removes what has become obsolete and grows real strength from what's left. A [square](/astrology/square/) sets them at right angles, and the friction makes both adapt; among the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) this angle is where questions of say and power tend to arrive early.

What the pressure makes possible is proportion. The Mars person finds out that more force isn't always the answer, and that some situations need waiting until their depth shows. The Pluto person learns that change can begin before everything underneath has been examined, and that someone else's speed can be useful rather than careless. Trust that has come through this kind of testing is rarely naive.

Both signs usually belong to one modality, and that modality names the theme. Mars in Aquarius square Pluto in Scorpio, both fixed: neither keen to give way, so patience becomes the skill. Mars in Cancer square Pluto in Libra, both cardinal: the question is who starts, and on whose terms.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

As the Mars person, you may feel examined, slowed or second-guessed: every plan gets asked what it's really for, and your energy can seem to hit something immovable. It's easy to push harder just to get past it.

Holding the Pluto, you may feel hurried, or that your caution is being overruled: their momentum arrives before you've seen the bottom of a situation, and your depth can be taken for control. It's easy to dig in just to slow things down.

Both reactions are understandable, and neither proves bad intent: not every uneven moment is someone taking charge on purpose. Friction is not failure, and nobody has to win this square.

Set beside the square, the pair's other contacts:

- **[Mars conjunct Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-conjunct-pluto-synastry/):** no corner to press against; drive and depth fuse.
- **[Mars sextile Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-sextile-pluto-synastry/):** a sign closer, the same strengths cooperate instead of testing each other.
- **[Mars trine Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-trine-pluto-synastry/):** a sign further, stamina flows without the push and pull.
- **[Mars opposite Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-opposite-pluto-synastry/):** the pressure turns face to face, as a balance of power.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Name what you want, plainly, before it turns into a test of will. Divide the territory: who has the say over what, at home, at work, in a shared project. Agree a pause rule, so that when the intensity climbs, either person can stop the conversation and come back to it. Competitive friends can keep it a game with agreed limits; colleagues can write down who decides; adult siblings sorting out a family matter can take turns leading.

By my count, about 1 pair of people in 9 has this square, one way round or the other, within 5.1°. Strip out the Mars, and what's left belongs to a generation: Pluto stays in one sign for 12 to 31 years, which makes the Pluto person's side of the square the side of all their contemporaries, and only a birthday brings the other's Mars to it. The time of day shifts neither body enough to count, but the houses show where the pressure lands in each life, and I won't guess at them ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Since it touches how much say each has, the reading has to be wanted by both, never requested by one about the other ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

Alone in a birth chart, [Mars square Pluto](/astrology/mars-square-pluto/) is someone learning to fit their force to the task. Here, two people learn it together, and can tell each other when it doesn't fit.
