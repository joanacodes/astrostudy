For months at a time, Venus is the bright light low in the west after sunset, the evening star. Then, every year and a half or so, it slides back towards the Sun, vanishes into its glare and comes back before dawn as the morning star. The weeks of that crossing are Venus retrograde.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) goes round the Sun in about 225 days and passes between us and the Sun every 580 to 588 days. Each time, seen from here, it seems to back up through the zodiac for 41 to 43 days: about six weeks, every year and a half or a little more. In the dates I work from, that happens 25 times between 2000 and 2040, and three times between 2026 and 2030:

- **3 October to 14 November 2026**, from 8°29′ Scorpio back to 22°51′ Libra ([Venus retrograde in Scorpio](/astrology/venus-retrograde-in-scorpio/), then in Libra);
- **10 May to 22 June 2028**, from 19°41′ to 3°10′ Gemini;
- **16 December 2029 to 26 January 2030**, from 24°01′ to 8°37′ Capricorn.

Venus’s retrogrades keep a calendar you could learn by heart. Five of its cycles with the Sun take almost exactly eight of our years, so every eight years it turns back in nearly the same place, about 2½° earlier each time. The Scorpio retrograde of 2026 echoes those of 2002, 2010 and 2018, which turned back at 15°36′, 13°13′ and 10°50′ Scorpio; the one of 2026 turns at 8°29′, and the next of the series comes in 2034. In any eight years, Venus turns back in five places round the zodiac, in Aries or Pisces, Scorpio or Libra, Gemini, Capricorn and Leo, and then begins the round again.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Halfway through the retrograde, Venus is lined up with the Sun on our side of it, which is also when it comes nearest to Earth. Before that moment it sits east of the Sun and shines in the evening; after it, west of the Sun, in the morning. The turn at each end is slow: Venus eases to a halt over several days, and in all it backs over about 15° to 16½° of the zodiac.

In rhythm, Venus sits between the other two personal planets. [Mercury retrograde](/astrology/mercury-retrograde/) comes three or four times a year and lasts about three weeks; [Mars retrograde](/astrology/mars-retrograde/) comes about every two years and lasts two months or more. Venus’s six weeks are long enough to stay a while with one area of your chart, and they come round only every year and a half or so.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Venus is retrograde about one day in fourteen, so about one person in fourteen is born with it. I read Venus as the way you value: what attracts you, what you prefer, what you think a fair exchange is. Retrograde, that valuing works inward first. Preferences are arrived at slowly, by comparison and second looks; generosity checks that the give-and-take is mutual before it trusts it; taste ignores the fashion of the day until it has made up its own mind. That isn’t less love. It is affection with its own timing, the rhythm I describe in [Natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/). Venus is also one of the placements that held about a third of the time in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), so I weigh its retrograde against the rest of the chart rather than on its own.

As a transit, Venus retrograde backs for about six weeks through one house of your chart, and your birth time says which. I read those weeks as a season for reassessing: what something is worth to you, whether an exchange is still mutual, whether a taste still fits the person you are now. It’s weather, not a forecast, and I don’t give dates for love, reconciliations or spending.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Don’t change your look while Venus is retrograde.”** No transit rules on haircuts. If you want a change, the sky isn’t the one to ask.
- **“A wedding under Venus retrograde is doomed.”** A transit describes weather, not an outcome. The two people in a marriage decide how it goes, whatever Venus was doing on the day.
- **“Born with Venus retrograde, you’ll struggle to be loved.”** It describes valuing that takes its time, not love that can’t come. The warmth is all there; it arrives on its own schedule.
- **“Venus retrograde comes at random.”** It keeps a strict calendar: every eight years, it turns back in almost the same place.
