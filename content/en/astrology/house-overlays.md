Synastry has two halves. One is the aspects between two charts. The other is quieter and often more telling: where each person's planets land in the other's houses. Astrologers call these house overlays, and they answer a simple question: which part of my life does this person tend to touch?

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Your birth chart divides the sky into twelve houses, each an area of life: the self, resources, siblings and daily surroundings, home, and so on round to the 12th. The other person's planets have positions too, and each of those positions falls somewhere in your houses. Their Venus in your 7th house, their Saturn in your 1st, their Moon in your 4th: each of these is an overlay.

The grammar is simple. Their planet brings its function, the house brings an area of your life, and you tend to feel that area lit or stirred when you're with them: [their Venus in your 7th house](/astrology/venus-in-7th-house-synastry/), for instance, tends to colour what partnership feels like with them. Then you turn the whole thing round. Your planets fall in their houses too, and what they feel there is the other half of the story.

Overlays stay inside the two real charts. That's what separates them from the [Davison chart](/astrology/davison-chart/) and the composite, which build a third chart for the relationship.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Place each of their planets in your chart by its degree, and note the house it falls in; then do the same with your planets in theirs. In Placidus, the house system I use, some houses are much wider than others, and the wide ones tend to catch more planets.

Two birth times are needed, one for each set of houses. Their planets mostly need only their birth date, with two exceptions: the Moon, which moves about 13° a day, and any planet within a few degrees of one of your house cusps, where their time decides which side it falls. A planet near one of your angles does double work: it also meets your Ascendant, IC, Descendant or Midheaven, and I read it as a contact as well as an overlay ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

The slow planets need care. Everyone born within a few years shares Uranus, Neptune and Pluto, so their slow planets falling in your houses describe their generation meeting your life more than them personally. Between two slow bodies I count no aspect, only these house positions, read as a difference between two eras. The aspects I count are on [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

Overlays show where two people meet in each other's lives. [Their Moon in your 4th house](/astrology/moon-in-4th-house-synastry/) tends to feel like comfort at home, a person you can be ordinary with. [Their Saturn in your 1st house](/astrology/saturn-in-1st-house-synastry/) can steady the way you present yourself, or make you feel watched, depending on how each of you holds structure. Their Mars in your 10th can spur your ambitions, or compete with them.

Tradition counts the angular houses (1st, 4th, 7th, 10th) as the strongest, so an overlay there tends to be noticed first; succedent and cadent houses work more quietly. And an overlay always depends on the two charts: the same planet in the same house reads differently for two different pairs.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

An overlay describes how one person tends to experience the other, never what will happen between them. The 5th, 6th, 8th and 12th houses were once read for children, illness, death and confinement, and I never read them that way, in synastry or anywhere else ([can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/), [can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)). Their planet in your 8th describes trust and what you share, not an ending.

And because overlays read the other person's chart as closely as yours, they need the other person's knowledge. I read two charts only between adults who both know it's happening ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
