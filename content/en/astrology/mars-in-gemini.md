Give Mars in Gemini a closed door and it will knock, phone, send a message and look for a window, possibly within the same hour. Other placements of Mars push; this one asks, argues and tries another route, and it treats an obstacle as a puzzle with more than one answer.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is effort and assertion, the part of you that moves towards a goal and stands up for it. [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/) is mutable air, ruled by Mercury: curious, quick, many-sided. In Gemini, Mars works through the mind and the voice. Your drive is fed by interest, your courage often takes the form of saying the thing, and competition happens in conversation: a debate, a quick reply, a better argument.

Gemini is neither one of Mars's own signs nor one where tradition says it struggles, so there's no dignity to weigh, only a manner to read. That manner is plural. This Mars likes two projects better than one and changes approach easily, which makes it adaptable on a good day and scattered on a bad one.

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), Gemini's ruler, is the planet to look at next: its sign and house say how this Mars thinks before it acts, or while it acts. I read the two together, and with the rest of the chart, which is the only way I read anything ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

Its passage through Gemini normally takes about 44 days, though a retrograde turn can keep it there for 217, just over seven months. That adds up to about 1 person in 12 among those born from 1930 to 2009. People born a few weeks apart share it; the house shows which conversations your drive takes on, and that's where it becomes yours. After the slow push of [Mars in Taurus](/astrology/mars-in-taurus/), Gemini gives Mars several directions at once; [Mars in Cancer](/astrology/mars-in-cancer/), next, gathers that energy round the people it protects.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, Mars in Gemini flirts with words. Banter, teasing, long conversations at odd hours: desire often starts in the mind, and someone who can't keep up with the talk may lose your interest faster than someone who isn't your type on paper. Disagreements turn into debates, which you may enjoy more than your partner does. The trap is winning the argument and losing the evening. You also need movement and variety, and a relationship with room for your many interests holds you better than one that asks you to choose a single one.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, this Mars is quick on its feet. It handles several tasks at once, switches between them without fuss, and thrives wherever the job is talk: pitching, negotiating, teaching, writing, selling, sorting out a problem over the phone. It's at its best in a crisis that needs fast thinking more than force. Repetition is what drains it. If the same task comes back every day, your energy will drift towards something newer, so build variety into the week, or rotate the work with a colleague.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Spreading yourself thin. Ten open projects can feel like energy while working as a way of not committing to any of them. Words can become the weapon: a sharp line, delivered fast, lands harder than you meant, and in an argument you may score points rather than settle anything. And when nothing new comes along, restlessness can start a debate just to have something to push against.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Choose a main project and allow yourself one side project, not five. Before a difficult conversation, write down what you want from it, so that cleverness serves the goal. Move while you think: walking or cycling gives the restless energy a route. Turn debate into a practice with rules, a club, a course, a podcast with friends, so the urge to argue has a proper place. And ask one more question before you answer; this Mars is never short of answers.
