Altair is the flying eagle, the bright star of Aquila. Its astrological reputation can be traced to named writers: a military star, in the tradition's word, and a star of boldness and risk-taking for a modern author.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Altair is α Aquilae, magnitude 0.77 in the Yale catalogue, the twelfth-brightest star of the night. Only 16.7 light-years away, it is one of the Sun's nearest bright neighbours. It is the southernmost corner of the Summer Triangle, whose other corners are [Vega](/astrology/vega/) and [Deneb Adige](/astrology/deneb-adige/), high on summer evenings in the northern hemisphere.

Altair has been in [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/) only since the 1870s, when the [precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/) carried it over from [Capricorn](/astrology/capricorn/): it was at 0°47′ Aquarius in 1930, 1°46′ in 2000 and 2°08′ in 2026.

The Eagle flies 29.3° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), too far for any planet to come near it in the sky; a planet "on" Altair shares only its degree.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

The name is Arabic, al-nasr al-ṭā'ir, the flying eagle, translated into Latin as Vultur volans; across the Milky Way, Vega was the falling one. The Babylonians had already called Altair the eagle star, and for the Greeks the Eagle was the bird that carried Zeus's thunderbolts. The Koori people of Victoria knew Altair as Bunjil, the wedge-tailed eagle, with β and γ Aquilae as his two wives, the black swans; the Māori call it Poutu-te-rangi, the pillar of heaven.

In China and Japan it is the Cowherd, Niulang or Hikoboshi, with a child on each side of him, the stars β and γ Aquilae. Their mother, the Weaving Girl, is across the river of the Milky Way, and the family may cross to her once a year.

Western astrology calls Altair a military star, a view whose sources include the Roman poet Manilius, and [Bernadette Brady](/astrology/bernadette-brady/) ties it to boldness, determination and risk-taking. Military is the tradition's word, said as such; read gently, it is a tone of daring, not a calling.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

For a birth in 2026, a planet or angle between 1°08′ and 3°08′ Aquarius is conjunct Altair in the tradition's terms ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). For a birth in 1930 the window ran from 29°47′ Capricorn to 1°47′ Aquarius, so in older charts a planet in the last degree of Capricorn can be on Altair. The Sun touches it on about 21 and 22 January, the first days of a [Sun in Aquarius](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius/), and the Moon crosses the same degree every month, always more than 20° south of the star.

A star astrologer would add Altair's daring to the planet on it. The planet's own function still leads: [Mars](/astrology/mars/) there would still be about action and effort in the manner of Aquarius, with boldness as a colour rather than a promise.
