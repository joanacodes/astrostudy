Their Mercury in your 8th house asks the questions most people leave alone. Talk with them goes below the surface quickly: what you're afraid of, what you owe, what you've never quite said, and the paperwork of a life you share.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[The 8th house](/astrology/8th-house/) is where a bond deepens into what's shared: intimacy, trust and what it costs, joint money, debts, the closeness that changes people. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) investigates, interprets and names. As a [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), this one sends their curiosity into your most private arrangements.

With them you may say things you haven't said to anyone. They listen for what's underneath, ask the second question, and don't flinch at the answer, so confiding comes more easily than you expected. They can also be the clear head you need when a subject is practical and heavy at once: a loan, a joint account, a document you'd rather not read alone.

For them, your depths are worth understanding. They want to know what drives you, and being trusted with what you keep back matters to them. The 8th is succedent, a house that holds what a bond creates, and its natural sign is [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/), which has two rulers, Pluto and, traditionally, Mars; I read both.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Questions can start to feel like an investigation. Curiosity this keen may press for more than you're ready to tell, and you might end up explaining feelings you'd rather simply have. Conversations about shared money can turn tense when each of you remembers the arrangement differently.

From their side, your silences invite more digging. If you go quiet, they may assume there's something to find; if you change the subject, they can feel shut out of a trust they thought you shared. Being handed a secret can weigh on them more than you know.

The old books listed death and legacies among the 8th's matters. I leave both out of every reading, synastry included ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)); between the two of you, this overlay is about how you speak of trust, money held in common and the things people avoid saying.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Agree what stays between you, and keep to it. Let "not yet" be a full answer, and set a time for the practical conversations instead of letting them ambush an evening. In a couple, closeness of every sort grows when both can say what they want to talk about, and what they don't; between friends, this is the confidant who saw you through a hard year; between colleagues, research, audits and confidential files suit you, with clear rules about who sees what; between adult relatives, sorting out what a family holds in common goes better with notes and a calm afternoon.

If the Mercury is yours and the house theirs, your Mercury in their 8th house, you're the one asking what lies under the surface. One house earlier, [their Mercury in your 7th house](/astrology/mercury-in-7th-house-synastry/) meets you face to face; one house later, [their Mercury in your 9th house](/astrology/mercury-in-9th-house-synastry/) turns what you've lived through into ideas and meaning.

In Placidus, the house system I use, the 8th can be narrow in one chart and very wide in another, and its cusps move with your birth time and the latitude you were born at. Over a single day their Mercury travels a degree or two at most, which leaves their birth date to settle the house unless a cusp is very near; I wait for both times all the same ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Confidences belong to the person who made them, so this overlay is read only with both people's agreement ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#a-chart-is-personal)).
