Orion's belt is three stars of nearly equal brightness in a short straight line, about 2⅓° from end to end. Astrology has no single position for a group like this, so this page gives each star its own degree: together they cover a little over two degrees of late Gemini.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

The belt runs across the middle of Orion. Mintaka (δ Orionis, magnitude 2.23) is its western end and the star closest to the celestial equator; Alnilam (ε, 1.70) is the middle and the brightest; Alnitak (ζ, 2.05) closes it to the east. They look like three single stars, but only Alnilam is one: Alnitak is a triple system and Mintaka a fivefold one.

In the tropical zodiac, for 2026, Mintaka stands at 22°43′, Alnilam at 23°49′ and Alnitak at 25°02′ [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/); in 2000 they were at 22°21′, 23°27′ and 24°40′. The belt sits between [Bellatrix](/astrology/bellatrix/), at 21°18′, and [Betelgeuse](/astrology/betelgeuse/), at 29°07′.

All three lie between 23.5° and 25.3° south of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), out of reach of the Moon and every planet. Whatever touches the belt in a chart touches its longitude, and only that ([celestial latitude](/astrology/celestial-latitude/)).

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

The three were once one Arabic name, al-niẓām, the string of pearls, and their modern names are Arabic too. English folk names run to measuring rods and processions: Jacob's Rod, Peter's Staff, the Ell and Yard, Our Lady's Wand, the Three Kings, the Three Marys. Spanish keeps Las Tres Marías, and in Mexico they are Los Tres Reyes Magos.

In the Chinese *Classic of Poetry* the belt, called Shen, is paired with [Antares](/astrology/antares/), called Shang, as an image of two people who could never be together, perhaps because Antares rises only once the belt has set. The Māori call it Tautoru, the string of three, the stern of a great canoe whose prow is Matariki, the Pleiades. And the Book of Job asks who can "loose the bands of Orion".

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Astrologers who use the belt take its stars one at a time, each with the tradition's orb of about a degree ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). For a birth in 2026 the three windows join into one stretch from about 21°43′ to 26°02′ Gemini: a planet there is within a degree of at least one of the three, and in the overlaps, of two. A contact names one star, never "the belt".

The Sun walks along it from about 12 to 16 June each year, Mintaka first and Alnitak last, so a mid-June birthday puts a [Sun in Gemini](/astrology/sun-in-gemini/) on one of the three. For the Moon or an angle, which of the stars it was depends on the exact time of birth ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

[Pollux](/astrology/pollux/), the next star on this site, repeats Alnilam's degree one sign later, at 23°34′ Cancer. I describe the belt and its names here; my readings leave fixed stars out, and three stars 24° below the Sun's path are a clear example of why I'd want the sky to agree before the arithmetic counts.
