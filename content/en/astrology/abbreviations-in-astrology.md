Beside the glyphs of the planets and signs, a chart uses a handful of letters: two for each angle, two for the nodes, one or two for motion, and one for time. Spelled out, each is a single, ordinary idea, and this is the order I'd decode them in.

## Step by step {#step-by-step}

1. **Start with the horizon: AC or ASC, DC or DSC.** AC, also written ASC, is the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), the degree rising on the eastern horizon at your birth: the left-hand end of the horizontal line on most wheels. DC, or DSC, is the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), the degree setting in the west, exactly opposite. Together they make the [horizon axis](/astrology/horizon-axis/). In Placidus, the house system I use, they are the cusps of the 1st and 7th houses.
2. **Then the meridian: MC and IC.** MC is Medium Coeli, Latin for the middle of the sky: the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/), where the zodiac crossed the meridian above the horizon, near the top of the chart. It isn't the point straight overhead. IC is Imum Coeli, the bottom of the sky, exactly opposite ([Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), [Meridian axis](/astrology/meridian-axis/)); French charts write it FC, for Fond du Ciel. In Placidus they are the cusps of the 10th and 4th houses. All four angles need a birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
3. **Find the nodes: NN and SN.** NN is the [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) and SN the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/), the two points where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's; their glyphs are ☊ and ☋. They always sit exactly opposite each other ([Nodal axis](/astrology/nodal-axis/)). Look for the word beside them, mean or true: the two versions are a degree or two apart at most, and I look at both ([Mean node and true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/)).
4. **Read the motion letters: R or ℞, and D.** An R, often written ℞, beside a planet in your chart means it was retrograde when you were born: seen from Earth, it seemed to move backwards ([Retrograde, explained](/astrology/retrograde-explained/)). In an ephemeris, R or ℞ marks the day a planet stations retrograde and D the day it turns direct ([Stations](/astrology/stations/), [Direct motion](/astrology/direct-motion/)). In a birth chart, I read a retrograde planet as a function that works inward first ([natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)).
5. **Check the time: UT.** UT is Universal Time, the time at Greenwich without daylight saving. Ephemerides usually list positions for midnight or noon UT ([Ephemeris, and how to read one](/astrology/ephemeris-and-how-to-read-one/)), and a chart's details may give your birth time in UT beside the local time, so the two figures needn't match. Converting local time to UT, with the time zone and daylight saving in force at the birth, is part of [Calculating a chart by hand](/astrology/calculating-a-chart-by-hand/) too.
6. **Read the figures beside them.** Each letter or glyph comes with a position such as 16°53′ Scorpio: 16 degrees and 53 minutes of the sign ([Degrees, minutes and seconds](/astrology/degrees-minutes-and-seconds/)). Programs also add short labels of their own, and they don't all agree: the legend or settings says what yours mean. The symbols themselves are on [Glyphs: the cheat sheet](/astrology/glyphs-the-cheat-sheet/).

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes}

- **Taking two spellings for two points.** AC and ASC name the same point, and so do DC and DSC; IC and FC are one point in Latin and in French.
- **Reading the MC as the point overhead, or as north.** The Midheaven is where the zodiac crossed the meridian, not the point straight above you, and seen from Britain or France it always lies to the south.
- **Taking ℞ for a warning.** It describes how a planet seemed to move, seen from Earth. Nothing turns back in space, and in a birth chart a retrograde is a rhythm, not a fault.
- **Worrying about an R beside the node.** The mean node always moves backwards, so the letter tells you nothing unusual.
- **Reading UT as your birth time.** If a chart's details show a time that isn't the one on your certificate, check whether it's given in UT before deciding the chart is wrong.
- **Treating NN as good and SN as bad.** They're the two ends of one line: where you're heading and where you started, and neither cancels the other.
