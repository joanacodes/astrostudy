The square is the most common of Neptune's contacts with your Mercury: by 80, almost everyone has had one. For roughly two and a half years, the facts you sort and the meanings you sense stop agreeing easily, and each can teach the other something.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) blurs edges and widens meaning; [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) analyses, compares and communicates. A [square](/astrology/square/), 90°, puts the two under strain, and the strain asks for adaptation: under this [transit](/astrology/transits/), details that used to stay put drift, a message lands differently from the way you meant it, and an idea can feel true before you can show why. The work is telling a real intuition from a wish. Learning may come more easily through stories than through rules for a while, and that's worth using rather than fighting.

Neptune's blind spots are sharpest here: confusing resonance with truth, and imagination with evidence. A useful habit for these years is to treat an important hunch as a question to check rather than an answer to act on. Misunderstandings, when they come, can be about tone as much as facts; saying the same thing twice, in plain words, saves a lot of guessing.

Say your Mercury is in the 6th while Neptune crosses the 9th ([Neptune transiting the 9th house](/astrology/neptune-transit-9th-house/)): the way you organise your days rubs against a belief that's changing. With Mercury in the 2nd and Neptune in the 11th, your thinking about what matters to you and a group's ideals start to diverge. Both houses hang on your birth time, so I leave them alone until I have it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Within my orbs, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the square spans about two and a half years, and even the quickest passes last about twenty-two months. Neptune usually crosses the exact square three times, and five times in about a quarter of passes, with the middle passes made while Neptune is retrograde.

The gap between Mercury and the Sun never exceeds about 28°, and Neptune needs about thirteen years to cover that distance, so the square to your Mercury and [transiting Neptune square natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-neptune-square-natal-sun/) tend to come within about a dozen years of each other; by 80, hardly anyone born between 1930 and 2009 has gone without one. The squares sit between the conjunction ([transiting Neptune conjunct natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-neptune-conjunct-natal-mercury/)) and the opposition ([transiting Neptune opposite natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-neptune-opposite-natal-mercury/)); the sextile ([transiting Neptune sextile natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-neptune-sextile-natal-mercury/)) lies on the conjunction's side of each, the trine ([transiting Neptune trine natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-neptune-trine-natal-mercury/)) on the opposition's. In a birth chart, [Mercury square Neptune](/astrology/mercury-square-neptune/) sets up this friction for life; the transit version ends, and knowing that makes it easier to work with.

Right now the square falls on Mercury in Cancer or Capricorn, and will until Neptune leaves Aries in 2039.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Put decisions in writing, with the reasons beside them. Reading them a week later shows which were intuition and which were hope.

Check what matters with a second source, and a second person, before you sign or send.

Give imagination its own notebook, separate from the to-do list, so each can do its job.
