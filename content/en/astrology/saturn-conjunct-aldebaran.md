Saturn conjunct Aldebaran belongs to three stretches of birth dates: July 1942 to April 1943, May to June 1972, and June 2001 to April 2002. About 1 person in 186 born between 1930 and 2009 has Saturn within a degree of the Bull's red eye, a little less than a one-degree contact usually gives.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) as the part of a chart that takes its time: structure, responsibility, the limits you keep, and the long work that ends in something solid. Where Saturn sits, life tends to ask for patience first and reward it later. [Aldebaran](/astrology/aldebaran/) has an unusual relationship with this planet in Ptolemy's account. He likened the bright star itself to Mars, and the fainter stars of the Hyades around it, the V-shaped group that outlines the Bull's head, to Saturn and, more mildly, to Mercury. Saturn on Aldebaran is the planet those faint stars were said to resemble, sitting on the one light of the group Ptolemy gave to Mars.

Astronomy has since pulled the group apart. Aldebaran only lies in front of the Hyades: the cluster is more than twice as far away, and the red star is no member of it. The tradition's picture, a Mars-like torch set among Saturn-like stars, is a picture of how the sky looks from here, which is all an astrologer's star ever is.

The three stretches differ. In 1942–43 and 2001–02, Saturn crossed the star in July, turned retrograde a few degrees further on, came back over it in December, and crossed it a third time in the spring: a stretch of nine or ten months, though Saturn was within the degree for only part of it. In 1972 Saturn went straight over, at the end of May, and its retrograde loop fell well past the star, so the contact lasted barely a few weeks ([Saturn retrograde](/astrology/saturn-retrograde/)). All three put Saturn at around 9° or 10° Gemini ([Saturn in Gemini](/astrology/saturn-in-gemini/)).

In the sky they share a degree, not a place: Aldebaran lies five and a half degrees south of the ecliptic, and Saturn went by about four degrees north of it on every pass.

Saturn is one of the three placements I trust most in a chart, for reasons set out in [The three that held: Saturn, Chiron and Lilith](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/). That trust belongs to Saturn's sign, house and aspects, not to the star; I don't read stars at all, since the charts I work from are drawn without them. If you were born in one of these stretches, what I'd look at is the part of life Saturn occupies: where you were asked to grow up early, and what you have built there since.

The star's four other contacts are personal rather than generational: the [Sun conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/sun-conjunct-aldebaran/) on a birthday at the end of May, the [Moon conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/moon-conjunct-aldebaran/) on a few hours each month, [Venus conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/venus-conjunct-aldebaran/) between mid-April and mid-July, and [Mars conjunct Aldebaran](/astrology/mars-conjunct-aldebaran/) on a few days about every two years. [Conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/) sets out the one-degree rule they share, and [fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/) the tradition behind it.
