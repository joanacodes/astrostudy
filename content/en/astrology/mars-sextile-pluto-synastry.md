Give this pair a problem and they tend to solve it from both ends: one person gets things moving, the other sees what has to go. In Mars sextile Pluto in synastry, about 60° separates one person's Mars from the other's Pluto, and the gap works like a handshake.

## What flows {#what-flows}

With [Mars](/astrology/mars/), a person starts, pushes, competes and keeps going. With [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/), they get to the root of things: they let go of what has become obsolete, renew what's left, and grow into their own strength. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) is a contact of opportunity, effective once someone takes it up, and in the language of [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/), it turns these two functions into allies rather than rivals.

Bring the Mars to this pair and your energy gains aim. The other person helps you see which part of a job really matters, so effort stops scattering. Bring the Pluto, and your sense of what needs changing finds someone willing to begin: the reorganisation you'd been turning over for months gets its first afternoon of actual work.

The two signs usually get on by element. Mars in Cancer with Pluto in Virgo joins a protective drive to a methodical urge to overhaul, good at repairing a household's routines; Mars in Aquarius with Pluto in Sagittarius, an inventive push to a wish to renew beliefs and plans.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

What a sextile's ease can hide here is time. Because the alliance works so well under pressure, the pair may only call on it in emergencies: the Pluto person's depth when something breaks, the Mars person's energy for heavy lifting. In calm periods, neither mentions what they'd like to change, and small adjustments wait until they've become large ones. Gradual change is easy to underestimate.

From the Mars side, there's also a risk of becoming the engine for someone else's overhauls and never your own. From the Pluto side, of handing the hard part of starting to the other person every time, and losing the habit of beginning.

How the pair's other contacts compare:

- **[Mars conjunct Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-conjunct-pluto-synastry/):** the alliance becomes a fusion, all-in and harder to pace.
- **[Mars square Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-square-pluto-synastry/):** a sign further round, cooperation gives way to testing each other's force.
- **[Mars trine Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-trine-pluto-synastry/):** the same help, wider and more constant, without needing an occasion.
- **[Mars opposite Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-opposite-pluto-synastry/):** drive and depth across from each other, where power needs balancing.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Give the alliance a standing job, not just a crisis. A monthly look at what could change, a shared project with a long horizon, a decision made in calm weather about how you'll decide in rough. Colleagues can use it to fix a process before it fails; flatmates, to sort out a house; friends, to train for something hard; a parent and adult child, to rethink a family arrangement that no longer fits anyone.

Within 3.6°, and one way round or the other, about 1 pair of people in 12 has the sextile. Half of it belongs to an age group: anyone born within a few years of the Pluto person has Pluto in nearly the same place, since a sign holds it for 12 to 31 years. The other half, Mars, picks out a few weeks of birthdays every couple of years. In a tight orb like this, a birth right at its edge is the one case where the hour can tip the sextile in or out; and for the houses, where the alliance does its work, I need the two times in any case ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Held by one person, [Mars sextile Pluto](/astrology/mars-sextile-pluto/) is a reserve they draw on when it counts. Between two people, the reserve has two keys, and it opens best when both turn them.
