*Linda Goodman's Sun Signs* is a book of popular astrology about the Sun signs, and the first book on astrology to make *The New York Times* best-seller list. [Linda Goodman](/astrology/linda-goodman/) published it in New York in 1968. Its subject is the sign the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) was in on the day you were born, which a birth date alone usually settles.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Taplinger published it in New York in 1968, and Pan Books published it in London, in an edition of 1982 that I cite below. In *Occult America* (2009), his history of the occult in the United States, Mitch Horowitz records it as the first astrology book ever to earn a place on *The New York Times* list. Ten years after it, Goodman's *Love Signs*, from Harper & Row, made the same list.

Its place in history isn't only astrology's. In the history of the New Age, its publication, with that of *Love Signs*, is counted among the events that brought the movement to wide public notice, alongside Shirley MacLaine's *Out on a Limb* in 1983 and the Harmonic Convergence of August 1987.

It also stands at the opposite end of twentieth-century astrology from [Dane Rudhyar’s The Astrology of Personality (1936)](/astrology/dane-rudhyars-the-astrology-of-personality-1936/). Rudhyar's book reads a whole chart, drawn for one birth, as the picture of one person ([Humanistic astrology](/astrology/humanistic-astrology/)). *Sun Signs* starts from a single placement, shared by everyone born in the same month or so. That kind of astrology already had a mass readership in the newspaper columns, which in Britain go back to the 1930s ([Horoscope](/astrology/horoscope/)); Goodman's book took it onto the best-seller list.

## What it says {#what-it-says}

Beneath its signs runs a theory of how new planets enter astrology. In the book, Goodman explains that a planet, or any other body in the sky, has no effect at all until it is discovered, and that each is found when its time has come.

From that theory the book draws a forecast. In time, it expects, every one of the twelve signs will have a ruler of its own, which leaves two planets still to be found: the true rulers of Taurus and Gemini. The names it gives them are Pallas, for Taurus, and Apollo, for Gemini. Both names already belonged to asteroids, Pallas found in 1802 and Apollo in 1932 ([Pallas](/astrology/pallas/), [Apollo (asteroid)](/astrology/apollo-asteroid/)); the planets the book looks forward to were still to come.

This is where the book and I part company. In every chart I read, [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/) belongs to Venus and [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/) to Mercury, and I keep no place for a planet nobody has found. The modern rulers I use arrived with real discoveries: Uranus for Aquarius, Neptune for Pisces, Pluto for Scorpio. For those three signs I read both, the modern ruler and the traditional one ([Traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)).

A book can be written for a Sun sign because a birthday is enough to find one, except near the change of sign ([Born on the cusp](/astrology/born-on-the-cusp/)). A reading can't. The rising sign and the houses come from the minute and the place of birth, and without a birth time I don't read at all ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). With one, the Sun sign takes its place beside the Moon, the rising sign and every planet in its house ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

What readers take from a description of their sign has been studied too. People familiar with what their Sun sign is supposed to mean tend to rate themselves in line with it, more so when they know astrology is the subject, while people who don't know the descriptions show no such pattern ([Sun signs and personality: the studies](/astrology/sun-signs-and-personality-the-studies/)). That doesn't settle what a chart can do. It does mean that what we read about our sign can shape how we see ourselves, which is one more reason I would rather read the chart than the sign.

If your sign is where you started, the rest of your chart is where I would go next.
