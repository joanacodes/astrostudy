Every year, on your birthday or very close to it, the Sun comes back to the exact degree it held when you were born. A chart cast for that moment is a solar return. It's the chart astrologers use to describe the tone of a year, a little like a birthday portrait taken against the same background each time.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) goes round the zodiac once a year, so it meets its own birth degree every year, give or take a day. The solar return chart is drawn for that minute: the Sun sits exactly where it sat at your birth, and everything else has moved on. The Ascendant and the houses are new, the Moon is somewhere else entirely, and the slow planets have advanced by a year.

The return moment drifts. The Sun takes about 365 days and a quarter to come back, so each year it arrives nearly six hours later by the clock, and a leap day pulls it back again. That's why your return usually falls on your birthday but sometimes the day before or after, and at a different hour each year.

The technique is old. The French still call it a *révolution solaire*, from its old name, the revolution of the years of a nativity, and Abu Ma'shar, writing in Baghdad in the 9th century, gave it a whole book, read together with the year's other timing methods (*On the Revolutions of the Years of Nativities*, translated by Benjamin Dykes, 2019).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A return is a full chart, but it's never read alone. Its meaning comes from how it sits on the birth chart: which natal house the return's Ascendant falls in, which natal planets the return's angles touch, which house of the return the Sun lands in this year, where the Moon is and what it meets. Those placements describe what the year emphasises and in what manner, not what will happen.

Two practical questions decide what you'll see:

- **The place.** Astrologers disagree about where to cast it. Some use the birthplace, as for the natal chart; others use the place where you spend the birthday, since that's where the year is lived. The Sun's degree doesn't change, but the Ascendant and houses do, sometimes by a lot. Some astrologers go further and travel for their birthday to change the return's angles on purpose. The year is still lived in your natal chart, and no journey changes that.
- **The birth time.** The return's moment depends on the Sun's exact natal degree, and the Sun moves about a degree a day. An hour's error in your birth time moves that degree by about two and a half minutes of arc, which moves the return by about an hour, which moves its Ascendant by around 15° on average. A return is as sensitive to the birth time as the birth chart itself ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

When I look at a year, the solar return is one of the charts I use, with [transits](/astrology/transits/) from Jupiter to Pluto, Chiron and the true node, [secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/), [solar arcs](/astrology/solar-arc-directions/), [profections](/astrology/annual-profections/) and [zodiacal releasing](/astrology/zodiacal-releasing/). None of them is read on its own. A return that puts the emphasis on the 10th house, say, while slow transits and progressions also touch your Midheaven, describes a year where work and public life are likely to ask for attention. The same return with nothing else stirring describes a lighter accent.

Returns come in many lengths. The Sun's comes every year; the Moon's every month ([lunar return](/astrology/lunar-return/)); Jupiter's about every twelve years ([Jupiter return](/astrology/jupiter-return/)); Chiron's around 50, usually once in a life ([Chiron return](/astrology/chiron-return/)). The solar return is the one that frames a single year, which is why people tend to look at it around their birthday.

I read it as weather. It can tell you which parts of life are likely to ask for attention this year and in what tone, and that's often useful to know. It can't tell you whether the year will be good, and it doesn't decide anything.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Your solar return is always on your birthday.”** The exact moment drifts by hours each year and can fall the day before or after.
- **“The return chart replaces your birth chart for the year.”** It's read on top of the natal chart, never instead of it.
- **“A difficult-looking return means a bad year.”** It describes emphasis and tone, not outcomes.
- **“You can fix a year by spending your birthday somewhere else.”** Travelling changes the return's angles; it doesn't change the chart you were born with, or the life you bring to the year.
