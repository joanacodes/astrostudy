Every twenty years or so, Jupiter catches up with Saturn and the two planets meet in the sky. Astrologers have tracked these meetings for well over a thousand years, sign by sign and element by element, and called them great conjunctions.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

[Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) goes round the zodiac in about twelve years, [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) in about 29½, so Jupiter overtakes Saturn on average every 19.86 years, in practice anywhere from 18 years 10 months to 20 years 8 months after the previous [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/). They are called great because, of all the meetings between planets visible to the naked eye, they are the rarest. Now and then a retrograde loop turns one meeting into three in a single season: in the dates this site keeps, that happened in Taurus in 1940–41 and in Libra in 1980–81.

Here is every one from 1802 to 2100, in the tropical zodiac:

- **Earth signs:** 1802, 5°07′ Virgo · 1842, 8°54′ Capricorn · 1861, 18°22′ Virgo · 1881, 1°36′ Taurus · 1901, 13°59′ Capricorn · 1921, 26°35′ Virgo · 1940–41, three times in Taurus, from 14°27′ to 9°07′ · 1961, 25°11′ Capricorn · 2000, 22°43′ Taurus.
- **The exceptions:** 1821, 24°39′ Aries · 1980–81, three times in Libra, at 9°29′, 8°06′ and 4°56′.
- **Air signs:** 21 December 2020, 0°29′ Aquarius · 31 October 2040, 17°55′ Libra · 7 April 2060, 0°46′ Gemini · 15 March 2080, 11°52′ Aquarius · 18 September 2100, 25°32′ Libra.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Each meeting falls about 120° short of the one before, so three in a row draw a triangle across the zodiac, and the triangle sits in signs of one element, a trigon ([triplicities](/astrology/triplicities/)). It turns slowly: about every 220 years the meetings move on to the next element, and they come back to the first after some 800 or 900 years. That is the pattern in the list. Apart from the Libra meetings of 1980–81, every great conjunction from 1842 to 2000 fell in earth; the one of 2020, on the first degree of Aquarius, opened a run in air.

The tradition behind this is old. Masha'allah, the eighth-century astrologer who helped choose the moment for founding Baghdad, held that these meetings time great changes on Earth, that they stay in one triplicity for about two centuries, and that the shift to a new one matters most. The doctrine reached Europe through Latin translations of Arabic texts, above all [Abu Ma'shar](/astrology/abu-mashar/)'s book on conjunctions ([Persian and Arabic astrology](/astrology/persian-and-arabic-astrology/)), and medieval astrologers gave 960 years to the full round of the four elements; [Johannes Kepler](/astrology/johannes-kepler/) later reckoned 794. It has also known its embarrassments. The conjunction of 1583, the last in the water trigon, was widely expected to herald upheaval; when nothing came of it by the feared year 1603, public interest faded fast.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Great conjunctions belong to [mundane astrology](/astrology/mundane-astrology/), the astrology of nations and eras, and I read people, not nations. In a birth chart, a great conjunction matters where its degree falls. The house it lands in needs your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)); a natal planet within a few degrees of it meets transiting Jupiter and Saturn together, growth and structure arriving at once. Someone with a planet near 0° Aquarius met the 2020 meeting head on; the 2040 one, near 18° Libra, will touch different charts.

If you were born within a few months of a meeting, you carry it in your chart for life: that is a natal aspect with its own page ([Jupiter conjunct Saturn](/astrology/jupiter-conjunct-saturn/)).

Rarer meetings exist. Saturn and Neptune meet only about every 36 years, and they did so in February 2026 ([the Saturn–Neptune conjunction of 2026](/astrology/saturn-neptune-conjunction-of-2026/)). I read all of them as I read any transit: weather in your chart, never a prophecy ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A great conjunction predicts world events.”** The tradition read it for nations, and the record includes predictions that came to nothing, 1583 among them.
- **“It happens once in a lifetime.”** It comes about every twenty years; most lives see three or four.
- **“The element never changes for centuries.”** The meetings stay in one element for about two centuries, with exceptions: Aries in 1821, Libra in 1980–81.
- **“You have to be born at one for it to matter.”** Its degree falls somewhere in every chart. The house it lands in is yours to read.
