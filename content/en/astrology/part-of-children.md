The old lists of lots had one for children. This page says what that lot was, and why it plays no part in how I read.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Every lot is a piece of arithmetic on a chart: one distance, measured between two of its points, laid off again from a third, usually the Ascendant ([doctrine of the lots](/astrology/doctrine-of-the-lots/)). Seven lots belong to the planets, from Fortune for the Moon to Nemesis for Saturn ([the Hermetic lots](/astrology/hermetic-lots/)). The Part of Children belongs instead to a subject of life, like the [Part of Marriage](/astrology/part-of-marriage/) and the [Part of the Father](/astrology/part-of-the-father/), which have pages of their own. Paulus Alexandrinus, in the 4th century, already used his dozen or so main lots for almost every part of his analysis, and lots named for subjects kept multiplying after him.

Other old lots dealt with illness and death, and this site treats them as history ([lots of death and illness, in history](/astrology/lots-of-death-and-illness-in-history/)). Ptolemy, writing in the 2nd century, ranked children among the questions an astrologer was expected to answer after a birth, with marriage, illness and the length of life. That list is a record of what astrology once claimed, not a guide to what a chart can do.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

No formula for this lot appears here. The lists that carry lots of this kind combine more than one tradition, and I couldn't trace a formula for the Part of Children to a source I could check, so I'd rather leave the arithmetic out than hand you a degree that nobody can vouch for.

Whatever its formula, it would share the frailties of every lot. It turns with the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), about a degree for every four minutes of birth time; for many lots a night birth reverses the count; and a chart without an exact time has no Part of Children at all ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). A point that sensitive to the clock is a poor place to hang a question as large as this one.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I don't read this lot, and not only because the system I built draws no lots beyond Fortune and Spirit. Children are not something I read a chart for: not whether they'll come, not when, not how many, not whether a baby will be a girl or a boy ([can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/)). Those questions belong to the people living them, and the medical ones to their doctors. If anyone offers, for a fee, to read from your chart whether children will come, or to change what it is said to show, walk away: no chart can tell, and no ritual can alter it ([paid “spiritual work”](/astrology/paid-spiritual-work/)).

Nor do I read children's own charts. A child's chart belongs to the child, to open or not as an adult ([children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)).

Tradition gave the [5th house](/astrology/5th-house/) children, along with its old name, Good Fortune, and placed the joy of Venus there ([planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/)). In an adult's chart, I read that house for what the adult makes, enjoys, plays at and falls for; it doesn't count anyone's children, born or not. That's [how I read](/how-i-read/).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Part of Children shows how many children you'll have.”** No point in a chart can, and I won't read one for it.
- **“It's one of the Hermetic lots.”** It isn't among the seven; those belong to the planets.
- **“It describes your children.”** A lot in your chart is about your chart. Your children's lives are their own, and so are their charts.
- **“The old books agree on its formula.”** The lists that carry it combine more than one tradition, and I've found no formula I could check.
