The North Node in the 7th house puts your growth across the table, in the person facing you. Sitting in [the 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/), the [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) says that much of what you're growing into is learnt with someone, not alone.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Angular like the 1st, the 7th begins at the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), the degree setting in the west as you were born, and its natural sign is Libra. It covers the people you meet one to one: partners in love and in business, clients, close collaborators and, in the old books, open rivals. The [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) sits on the other side of the horizon, in [the 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/), and describes a strength you bring with you: self-reliance, initiative, a clear sense of who you are and what you'd do about it. That's yours to keep. The 7th-house end asks what happens when someone else has a say.

In Placidus, the house system I use, the 7th starts at the Descendant, which moves with the Ascendant, so the house needs an accurate [birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/). With the node near the cusp, a few minutes either way decide between this house and the 6th; deeper in, it can reach towards the 8th. See [North Node in the 6th house](/astrology/north-node-in-6th-house/) and [North Node in the 8th house](/astrology/north-node-in-8th-house/) if yours sits near an edge. The mean and the true node, at most a degree or two apart, can fall either side of a cusp; I look at both, and tell you when they do.

The angle my [testing notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) reports is the rising sign, which faces this house across the chart; about one time in three it held, and among all the placements, the three that stood out were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

This is the house of the committed partner, so love is often where the practice runs deepest. The 1st-house end is used to making up its own mind and presenting the result; the 7th-house end learns to arrive at decisions together, and to be changed a little on the way. You may also notice that you look for qualities in partners that you haven't yet claimed in yourself, which the 7th is famous for. Often the growth is in recognising them as yours too.

Nothing here says whether or when you'll commit to anyone. It describes how you grow when you do.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, partnerships count as much as love: a business partner, a co-author, a client you work closely with, an agent. The North Node points to the kind of work that only happens in pairs: consulting, negotiation, mediation, coaching, any role where your success is tied to someone else's. Contracts and agreements belong to this house, and learning to read them well and keep them is part of the growth. Even a rival can teach you something here: the competitor who makes you sharpen your offer, the colleague whose objections improve the plan.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Self-reliance is the old safety. When things get tense, you may take everything back into your own hands, the 1st-house way, and treat a partner's input as interference. You may also choose people who can't commit, which quietly protects your independence. Or you hand a partner the qualities you'd rather not develop yourself, and then resent them for having them.

The overcorrection is losing yourself in the other person: their plans, their opinions, their life. That isn't partnership either; a partner needs someone to meet.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Choose one partnership, in love or at work, and invest in it on purpose: regular check-ins, shared goals, honest talks about what each of you needs. Read agreements carefully, then keep them. Notice what you admire in others, and ask whether some of it is yours to grow.

Your independence is still a gift. Bring it to the partnership instead of guarding it from the partnership.
