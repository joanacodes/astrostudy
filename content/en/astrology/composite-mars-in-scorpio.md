With composite Mars in Scorpio, a relationship's drive goes deep and stays there. This pair rarely does anything halfway: it commits, keeps its own counsel, and when it finally fights, it fights about trust.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), [Mars](/astrology/mars/) sits halfway between where it stood at each of your births, and describes the way the relationship goes after what it wants. [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/), fixed water, answers to two rulers: Mars in the tradition, which makes this a [domicile](/astrology/domicile/), and [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) in modern tropical astrology, a split [traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/) explains. In any chart I read both, and here they agree on intensity: the drive is concentrated, patient and hard to deflect. About 1 pair in 12 lands here, almost exactly an average sign's share.

Such a pair acts with focus, and as a fixed sign Scorpio keeps what it starts, so its projects tend to run for years rather than weeks. Once it chooses something, a house to restore, a cause, a shared discipline such as diving or climbing, it gives it everything and needs no audience. Competition is quiet and long: who holds out, who knows more, who keeps their nerve. Desire is private and wholehearted, with little interest in light flirtation and a great deal in being trusted completely. [Mars in Scorpio](/astrology/mars-in-scorpio/) in a birth chart concentrates one person's will; in the composite the concentration is shared, and it may surprise friends who know each of you as easy-going.

Arguments are rare, slow to surface, and serious when they do. Hurt goes underground, a grievance can be kept for months, and silence becomes the weapon of choice. Under most rows lies one question, *can I trust you completely?*, and power, who decides and who knows what, is the usual ground. Being intense isn't the same as being aggressive, and a private bond isn't a secretive one. What helps is saying the hard thing early, while it's small, and making openness a habit rather than a test.

If closeness ever turns into checking, control or fear, no chart can interpret that, and it isn't what this placement means: in the UK, dial 999 if you're in danger, and 116 123, the Samaritans' free line, to talk.

[Composite Mars in Libra](/astrology/composite-mars-in-libra/) negotiates; [composite Mars in Sagittarius](/astrology/composite-mars-in-sagittarius/) heads for the horizon. Scorpio digs.

Composites are a technique I explain, not one I draw. Its users would set this Mars beside the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), where [Mars conjunct Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-conjunct-pluto-synastry/) shows how the will of one meets the depth of the other, and beside the two birth charts, which stay primary. No composite predicts a break-up, or a lifetime together. Something this private should only be looked at with both people's knowledge ([Synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

One more thing about Scorpio's modern ruler. Pluto's stay in one sign runs from 12 to 31 years, so a couple of similar age usually finds its composite Pluto in the sign their generation shares, and a couple with years between them finds it halfway across. What tells pairs apart is Pluto's house and aspects, and that is where astrologers would look to see how much of this Mars's depth Pluto adds.
