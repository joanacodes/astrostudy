No single chart can hold a square between the Sun and Mercury, since the two are never more than about 28° apart in anyone's sky. Between two people it ranks among the most frequent contacts, and it lives in conversation: one person's way of thinking keeps meeting the other's sense of self at an angle.

## What flows {#what-flows}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) stands for who a person is becoming: identity, purpose, direction. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is how a mind works: it observes, reasons, questions and puts things into words. At a [square](/astrology/square/), two functions press on each other until both adapt, and in [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) that pressure runs between two people: one person's thinking, the other's identity.

The signs at either end of a square tend to share a modality. A Virgo Mercury and a Sagittarius Sun: precise questions put to someone who thinks in broad strokes. An Aquarius Mercury and a Taurus Sun: a mind that wants to rethink the system, talking to someone who would rather keep what works.

What the friction makes possible is sharper thinking on both sides. The Sun person's plans meet real questions and come out better argued; the Mercury person's ideas meet a real life and get tested against it. Neither lets the other get away with a vague answer, which makes this a good contact for editing, planning, debating and solving problems together.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

If it's your Mercury, whatever you say can seem to land as criticism. You meant to think aloud, to help, to ask; the other heard a judgement. Over time you may weigh your words so carefully that you stop offering them. As I read Mercury, analysis is not overthinking and a question is not an attack, but at a square, both need saying.

If it's your Sun, the other's comments can seem to miss you: right about the details, wrong about the point. You may feel questioned when you wanted backing, analysed when you wanted to be heard, and it's easy to hear a remark about your plan as a remark about you.

Depending on the angle, the same conversation takes another turn:

- **[Sun conjunct Mercury in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mercury-synastry/):** the mind sits on the identity it describes; less friction, and a different risk, the label.
- **[Sun sextile Mercury in synastry](/astrology/sun-sextile-mercury-synastry/):** a sign closer; the ideas serve the direction instead of testing it.
- **[Sun trine Mercury in synastry](/astrology/sun-trine-mercury-synastry/):** one sign wider and in one element; understanding arrives before the questions.
- **[Sun opposite Mercury in synastry](/astrology/sun-opposite-mercury-synastry/):** the far side; the ideas no longer cut across the direction but face it, as the other side of the case.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Ask which conversation you're having before you have it: "do you want me to think about this with you, or to back you?" The Sun person can say which, and the Mercury person can respect the answer. Keep critique for a second round, after a first response of plain curiosity. At work, separate review from launch; in a couple or a family, notice the times a disagreement about an idea has turned into a hurt about a person, and step back to the idea.

Within 6.8°, about 1 pair of people in 7 has this square, whichever of the two holds the Sun. Since Mercury keeps close to its own Sun, the square mostly links people whose birthdays fall about three months apart, between two and four, whatever the years: roughly a season.

The hours of birth hardly move either body, so they rarely decide whether the square is there; they decide which part of each life the questions are about, through the houses, and I won't start a synastry without both ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). A contact about how someone thinks is personal, too: I read it when both people have agreed ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
