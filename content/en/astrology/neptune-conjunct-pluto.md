Neptune conjunct Pluto is an aspect you won't find in the chart of anyone born between 1930 and 2009. The two planets move so slowly against each other that their angle changes very little in a lifetime, and in those eighty years they never came close. What the conjunction joins is still worth knowing, because you can meet it in a transit, or between two charts.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) is the part of a chart that imagines, feels with others and loosens the boundaries that don't need to be there: inspiration, compassion, a sense of belonging to something larger. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) is the part that transforms: it clears out what has outlived its use and renews what remains from the root. In a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/), two functions act as one while each keeps its own nature, so this meeting would join the vision and the renewal into a single movement.

In plain words, Neptune on its own dreams of something better, and Pluto on its own digs out what has to go. Joined, the dream would want to go all the way down, and the digging would want a vision to follow. I'd read it as a longing for change that is imaginative, even spiritual, at its core: never destruction, and never an escape.

Both planets are among the slowest a chart has, so a conjunction between them would be shared by everyone born over many years, a mood of its time more than a portrait of anyone. Its house would be what made it personal.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Nobody born between 1930 and 2009 has Neptune within 6° of a conjunction with Pluto: my count found no birth with it in those eighty years, which take in nearly every adult alive today. For most of that time the two were about 60° apart, in the [Neptune sextile Pluto](/astrology/neptune-sextile-pluto/) carried by about 7 people in 10 born then, with Neptune running ahead the whole way. So if you're looking for this pair in your chart, look at the angle you actually have: most likely the sextile, or no major aspect between them at all.

You can still meet the conjunction from outside your own chart. Between two people born a good many years apart, one person's Neptune can fall on the other's Pluto: [Neptune conjunct Pluto in synastry](/astrology/neptune-conjunct-pluto-synastry/) joins one person's ideals to the other's depth, an intense mix that works on both sides, and that I read only with both people's consent ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

The pair's other aspects, and who has them:

- **[Neptune sextile Pluto](/astrology/neptune-sextile-pluto/):** the angle of most births from 1943 to 1993; imagination and renewal cooperate when someone takes the opening.
- **[Neptune square Pluto](/astrology/neptune-square-pluto/):** nobody born 1930–2009; the dream and the clearing-out would rub against each other.
- **[Neptune trine Pluto](/astrology/neptune-trine-pluto/):** nobody in those years either; vision and renewal would flow together.
- **[Neptune opposite Pluto](/astrology/neptune-opposite-pluto/):** absent too; the two would face each other across the chart.

## In transits {#in-transits}

The conjunction can come into a life as a transit, when one of the two, moving in today's sky, reaches the other's place in your chart. For people born in these years only one direction is open. [Transiting Pluto conjunct natal Neptune](/astrology/transiting-pluto-conjunct-natal-neptune/) can happen, because Pluto trails Neptune in these charts and slowly closes the gap; when it arrives depends on your birth year and on Pluto's uneven pace, 12 to 31 years in a sign. Once there, it lingers for a couple of years, crossing back and forth as it turns retrograde.

[Transiting Neptune conjunct natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-neptune-conjunct-natal-pluto/) can't happen in these lives: Neptune was already past your Pluto at birth, moving away, and needs about 165 years to come round.

Weather, not a forecast: Pluto on your Neptune describes a long season in which what you dream of and what you're ready to let go of press on each other. It doesn't say what will change.
