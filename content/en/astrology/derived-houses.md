A birth chart belongs to one person, yet it's full of other people: partners in the 7th house, parents in the 4th and 10th, brothers and sisters in the 3rd. Derived houses are the old way of giving each of those people a chart of their own inside yours, by treating their house as a new starting point and counting on from it.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The houses of a chart are counted from the Ascendant: the 1st, the 2nd, and so on round to the 12th. Astrologers call those the radical houses. A derived house is counted from somewhere else. You choose a house, call it the 1st, and count the rest from there, a method known as turning the chart.

Three classic examples come from [horary astrology](/astrology/horary-astrology/), where the method is most at home and where [William Lilly’s Christian Astrology (1647)](/astrology/william-lillys-christian-astrology-1647/) is the classic English text:

- **The 8th is the 2nd from the 7th.** The [7th](/astrology/7th-house/) stands for a partner, the 2nd for resources, so the 8th carries a partner's money, which is why horary astrologers read it for other people's money.
- **The 10th is the 4th from the 7th.** The 4th stands for home and land, so the 10th carries what belongs to a partner or an opponent.
- **The 6th is the 3rd from the 4th.** The 4th stands for a parent, the 3rd for siblings, so the 6th can stand for a parent's brothers and sisters.

The derived meaning is added to a house, never swapped for its own. The 8th stays the house of intimacy and of what you share, and it carries the partner's resources as well ([the 8th house](/astrology/8th-house/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The counting is inclusive: the house you start from counts as the first. So the 2nd from the 7th is the 8th, not the 9th, and the 7th from the 7th is the 1st, a neat way of saying that your partner's partner is you. In figures, add the two numbers, take away one, and take away twelve more if you pass the 12th.

What gets counted is the order of the houses, and that order is the one the [natural zodiac](/astrology/natural-zodiac/) lays alongside the signs, the 1st with Aries, the 2nd with Taurus and so on. Derived houses reuse it from a new starting point.

The same move turns up elsewhere. Some astrologers count the houses again from the [Part of Fortune](/astrology/part-of-fortune/), as if it were a second Ascendant. And houses can be linked in another way altogether, when one sign opens two houses side by side and its ruler speaks for both ([duplicated signs](/astrology/duplicated-signs/)). Derived houses link houses by counting; duplication links them by sharing.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I use derived houses sparingly, and only for one thing: how the people in your life figure in it, seen from your side. The 8th as the 2nd from the 7th tells me about what you build and share with a partner. It tells me nothing about the partner's own finances, which live in their chart, not yours.

That's the line I keep. Horary turns the chart to answer questions about people who aren't there to ask: a parent, a sibling, a rival. Some of those questions, about someone's health or how long they'll live, are ones no chart can answer, and I don't ask it to ([Can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)). Another person's life belongs to their own chart, read with their agreement ([Synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)). I don't practise horary at all, and when derived houses come into a natal reading, they describe relationships, never outcomes ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Derived houses let you read someone without their chart.”** They describe how that person figures in your life. Their own life needs their chart, and their consent.
- **“The 2nd from the 7th is the 9th.”** The count includes the house you start from: the 2nd from the 7th is the 8th.
- **“A derived meaning replaces the house's own.”** It's added. The 6th still covers daily work and routine when it also stands for a parent's siblings.
