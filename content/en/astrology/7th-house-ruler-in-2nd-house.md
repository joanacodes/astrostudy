Here the planet of your partners lives among your money, your possessions and the things you value most. What passes between you and another person tends to show up in your accounts and in your sense of what you're worth, and what you own and prize shapes the people you choose.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

Income, possessions, the skills that could pay your way and how much you think you're worth make up the [2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/). The [7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) is the people you deal with as equals: partners in love and in business, clients across a desk, the rival you can name. With the 7th's ruler there, the business of partnership is done in the currency of the 2nd ([house rulers](/astrology/house-rulers/)).

Relationships, for you, have to make sense in practice. You want a bond where the exchange feels fair, where values match, and where generosity doesn't cost you your footing. Work often runs one to one: commissions, consultations, a business shared with someone, a contract that pays the bills. Love tends to be shown in concrete ways, a gift chosen with care, time given, the patience to build security together. Your worth can also be tested in a relationship, and some of this placement's best lessons are about asking for what you're owed without feeling you've spoiled something.

In the natural zodiac both houses belong to Venus: Taurus is the second sign and Libra the seventh ([the natural zodiac](/astrology/natural-zodiac/)). Whatever planet actually rules your 7th, this placement joins Venus's two sides in your chart, what you value and whom you choose. When Venus does rule it, with Libra on the Descendant and Aries rising, Venus in the 2nd can stand in Taurus, its own sign, and the pleasure of comfort built together comes easily.

There's a quieter detail in the geometry. Counted by sign, the 2nd and the 7th are five houses apart, a relation tradition calls [aversion](/astrology/aversion/), and a planet in one was said not to see the other. I read it as partnership handled at one remove: through budgets, agreements and shared priorities rather than talk about the relationship itself. The feelings are real; they just tend to arrive by way of the practicalities.

The planet sets the style. Saturn, ruling a Capricorn Descendant with Cancer rising, makes you slow to merge accounts and steady once you have; Jupiter, ruling a Sagittarius one with Gemini rising, gives freely and has to learn where giving should stop. Leo rising brings [Aquarius on the 7th house cusp](/astrology/aquarius-on-7th-house-cusp/) and a pair of rulers, Saturn and Uranus. I read both ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)): Saturn in the 2nd wants clear terms, while Uranus, from its own house, keeps the arrangements of your bonds unconventional.

No chart says what a partnership will earn or cost you, and I won't read one for that ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)). This one shows how you weigh what's exchanged.

In Placidus, the house system I use, the cusp between the 1st and the 2nd moves with a few minutes of the birth time. A later birth can lift the ruler into the 1st, where partners leave their mark on your manner ([Ruler of the 7th in the 1st house](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-1st-house/)); an earlier one can leave it in the 3rd, where bonds begin in conversation ([Ruler of the 7th in the 3rd house](/astrology/7th-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/)). Without the time there's no Descendant, and no ruler to follow ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

What this leaves you with is a practical kind of love: you show it with what you offer, and you trust it when the exchange runs both ways.
