Bellatrix means female warrior, and the name wasn't made for it. It first belonged to Capella, in the far north of the winter sky, and moved to Orion's left shoulder in the fifteenth century. In the zodiac the two stars have ended up neighbours, less than a degree apart in late Gemini.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Bellatrix is γ Orionis, the third-brightest star of Orion, on the shoulder opposite [Betelgeuse](/astrology/betelgeuse/). At about 245 light-years it is the nearest of Orion's bright stars. Its magnitude in the Yale catalogue, 1.64, is a defined value: Bellatrix was one of the bright stars used in 1963 to set up the UBV brightness scale, and only a survey of 1988 found that it varies slightly, between about 1.59 and 1.64.

[Capella](/astrology/capella/), the star the name came from, has ended up 55′ of longitude away: Bellatrix is at 21°18′ [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/) for 2026 (20°56′ for 2000, 19°57′ for 1930) and Capella at 22°13′, though 40° of sky separate them.

Bellatrix lies 16.8° south of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), level with Betelgeuse, 16° south. The Moon, which strays about 5° from the Sun's path, never reaches either of them, and their degrees are all a planet can share with them ([celestial latitude](/astrology/celestial-latitude/)).

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

The Latin name first appears in translations of Abu Ma'shar and in John of Seville, attached to Capella; astronomers of the Vienna school moved it to γ Orionis in the fifteenth century, and printings of the Alfonsine tables carried it on. Richard Hinckley Allen thought another name, the Amazon Star, was a loose rendering of the Arabic al-najīd, the conqueror. An Arabic globe of about 1275 calls it the lion, and a seventeenth-century Arabic catalogue the left shoulder of the giant, Humerus Sinister Gigantis in its Latin translation.

The Inuit of North Baffin Island and Melville Peninsula paired it with Betelgeuse as Akuttujuuk, "those two placed far apart". When both stood high in the south after sunset, in late February and early March, spring and the lengthening days had begun.

Beyond the warrior in its name, I haven't found an astrological reading of Bellatrix in a source I can check, so I give none here.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

By the tradition's measure, a planet or angle from 20°18′ to 22°18′ Gemini is conjunct Bellatrix in a 2026 chart, and from 18°57′ to 20°57′ in a 1930 one ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). The Sun passes on about 11 and 12 June, a day before it reaches Capella's degree.

[Rigel](/astrology/rigel/), Orion's foot, is 4° earlier in the zodiac, and [Orion's belt](/astrology/orions-belt/) begins 1°25′ later, at Mintaka. So a planet at about 22° Gemini, for a 2026 birth, is within a degree of three stars at once: Bellatrix, Capella and Mintaka. The Moon crosses that degree every month and always passes well north of the two stars of Orion and well south of Capella.

A name like female warrior tempts a reading straight from the label. I'd resist it: the planet on that degree keeps its own function and its sign, and in my readings the stars don't appear at all ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).
