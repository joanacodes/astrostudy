A quintile and a half makes the tredecile: two planets 108° apart, three-tenths of the circle, the angle at each corner of a regular pentagon. I take it as the aspect of reorganising things in an uncommon way, by adapting rather than breaking.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

One hundred and eight degrees is three signs and eighteen. Up to 12° into a sign, a planet's tredecile lands three signs on; beyond that, four, which is the more frequent case.

I read it as adaptive restructuring. Two planets keep reorganising each other's patterns, and through that they develop in an uncommon direction. The change comes as adaptation, not disruption: transformation here doesn't mean instability. Nor does it set anyone above others, or fix a destiny. Its harder form is resistance to change, or a reorganisation that never quite holds together; its easier one, a steady, integrated way of evolving. What it builds, at its best, is a new configuration that then settles: change that stabilises, rather than change for its own sake.

It's worth telling apart from the [sesquiquadrate](/astrology/sesquiquadrate/), which also restructures, but under compound tension. The tredecile belongs to the family of fives: craft and pattern, not pressure. The quintile's 72° comes from Johannes Kepler, who added it to Ptolemy's aspects in *Harmonices Mundi* (Linz, 1619); add half again and you have the tredecile, also called the sesquiquintile. Among [harmonic charts](/astrology/harmonic-charts/), the tenth-harmonic chart, built on twice five, makes it a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/).

Its orb is a degree or so (see [orbs](/astrology/orbs/)), which keeps it clear of the biseptile, about five degrees back at 102°51′. Between the [square](/astrology/square/) and the [trine](/astrology/trine/) it lies 18° from one and 12° from the other, and it's exactly a quintile short of the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/): 108° and 72° make 180°.

## Between which planets {#between-which-planets}

Tredeciles get no pages pair by pair here. I read them by how fast the two planets move, since that sets the pace of the reorganising.

For two personal planets, it reorganises the everyday: how you plan, how you respond, rearranged until an unusual arrangement works for you. With Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune or Pluto on one side, a personal planet adapts over years to a larger structure, and the uncommon path it takes is the point. A tredecile between two slow planets is shared by a generation, and only the houses make it yours.

Next to it, among the minor aspects, come the [decile](/astrology/decile/), at 36°, a third of it, where potential is refined, and the [undecile](/astrology/undecile/), at 32°44′, an eleventh of the circle, where difference itself is the theme.

## The tredecile in synastry {#in-synastry}

In synastry, a tredecile tends to show as two people reorganising each other's routines into something neither would have chosen alone, and both find workable. It's quieter than a [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) or a square between you, and easy to miss. When it's tight and touches a personal planet, it earns a mention in my [synastry readings](/synastry/), read from both sides, as a capacity for adapting together.

## The tredecile in transits {#in-transits}

A tredecile transit passes in hours from the Moon and in a couple of days from the Sun. From Saturn or the outer planets it can stay long enough to feel like a period of rearranging: routines that shift, a plan that takes a less obvious shape. Weather, not a forecast: a tredecile transit describes reorganisation in the air, not the form it will take.
