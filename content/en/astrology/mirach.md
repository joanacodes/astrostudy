Mirach is the star at Andromeda's hip, and the one in this corner of the sky that changed sign within living memory: it left Aries for Taurus in 1970. The Sun now reaches it right on the cusp between those two signs, on about 20 April.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Mirach is Beta Andromedae, a red giant some 86 times the Sun's size, whose colour shows even to the naked eye. Its magnitude drifts between 2.01 and 2.10, close enough to the 2.06 of [Alpheratz](/astrology/alpheratz/) that the two take turns as the brightest star of Andromeda. Stargazers use it to find the Andromeda Galaxy, and a faint galaxy nicknamed Mirach's Ghost sits just 7′ from it.

In the tropical zodiac it stood at 29°25′ [Aries](/astrology/aries/) for a birth in 1930, crossed into [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/) in 1970, and stands at 0°24′ Taurus for 2000 and 0°46′ for 2026. Anyone born before 1970 has it in the last degree of Aries ([the anaretic degree (29°)](/astrology/anaretic-degree-29/)); anyone born after, in the first degree of Taurus ([0° of a sign](/astrology/0-of-a-sign/)). It is the first of several stars from other constellations that sit in tropical Taurus, among them [Hamal](/astrology/hamal/) at 8°01′ and [Menkar](/astrology/menkar/) at 14°41′.

Like Alpheratz, it lies about 26° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), far beyond the Moon's reach, so a planet at its degree never stands beside it.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

The name is a chain of copies. A Latin translation of Ptolemy's Almagest used the Arabic word mīzar, girdle, for the star at Andromeda's left hip; the Alfonsine Tables of 1521 described it as super mizar, and Mirach, Mirac and Mirak grew from there. Arabic astronomers called it the side of the chained lady and counted it in a fish of their own, al-Ḥūt, much larger than the western Pisces. Mirach belonged to the 28th and last Arabic lunar mansion, the Belly of the Fish ([the 28 lunar mansions](/astrology/28-lunar-mansions/)).

Older still, the Babylonian star list MUL.APIN may name it the Deleter, though the title may belong to Alpha Cassiopeiae instead. In Chinese astronomy Mirach is the ninth star of the Legs lodge, and Chinese records used it as their standard black star, a deep red, set against [Antares](/astrology/antares/), the standard red.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Counted a degree either side, a conjunction to Mirach runs from 29°46′ Aries to 1°46′ Taurus for a birth in 2026, and from 28°25′ Aries to 0°25′ Taurus for one in 1930 ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). Either way it straddles the change of sign, so a Sun on Mirach is a cusp birthday, between 19 and 21 April depending on the year. The star doesn't blur the sign: at the moment of birth the Sun was in one or the other ([born on the cusp](/astrology/born-on-the-cusp/)).

Saturn crossed Mirach's degree for births in parts of 1939–40, spring 1969 and 1998–99. The Moon passes in a few hours each month and the angles in minutes, so for them everything rests on an exact birth time, which is why I don't read a chart without one ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

If Mirach turns up in someone else's reading of your chart, the first question is the year: before 1970 it is an Aries star, after 1970 a Taurus one. Stars stay outside my own readings ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).
