Scorpio's middle third is the only part of the sign where the Sun has any standing in the old tables. It is also where the sky turns the sign's name inside out: the stars sitting in these degrees once made the Scorpion's claws.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

Next after Mars in the [Chaldean order](/astrology/chaldean-order/) comes the Sun, so the middle face of [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/) is the Sun's ([faces](/astrology/faces/)), between Mars's face before it ([Scorpio, first decan](/astrology/scorpio-first-decan/)) and Venus's after it ([Scorpio, third decan](/astrology/scorpio-third-decan/)). It's the Sun's only dignity anywhere in Scorpio. With no home, exaltation or triplicity in the sign, and no terms of its own, a [Sun in Scorpio](/astrology/sun-in-scorpio/) counts as [peregrine](/astrology/peregrine-planets/) everywhere except these ten degrees. Here it is no longer a stranger in the sign, only a guest with a small room of its own.

The modern decans hand the middle of Scorpio to Pisces, the next water sign, and so to its two rulers: Jupiter in the tradition, Neptune in modern tropical astrology. Here too I read both, Pisces's two rulers side by side ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)). Jupiter widens what Scorpio deepens; Neptune loosens the grip Scorpio likes to keep.

## Its image {#its-image}

The Egyptian decan Wallis Budge matched with these degrees is Themes-en-khentet, the last of four neighbouring names built on khentet. Firmicus Maternus wrote Tepsisen or Asentatir. Cosmas of Maiuma gave the slice to Leto, the mother of the twins Apollo and Artemis in Greek myth. That a mother of twins falls on the Sun's face may be chance; the lists never give their reasons.

The sky offers a stranger image. The two brightest stars of the constellation Libra, [Zubenelgenubi](/astrology/zubenelgenubi/) and [Zubeneschamali](/astrology/zubeneschamali/), whose Arabic names mean the southern and the northern claw, sit in this decan of the sign Scorpio, at 15°26′ and 19°44′. They were the old Scorpion's claws before the Scales took them over, and precession has since slid them into Scorpio's degrees ([constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun passes through from about 3 to 12 November, a Scorpio of mid-season. Here the Sun has the one dignity Scorpio allows it, and I read the decan's shade on a Scorpio Sun as a self that wants to be known for what it has been through, not only for what it shows. I don't let that weigh more than the Sun sign it shades, and the Sun sign itself held about a third of the time in my notebook ([the invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

Planets here can share a degree with three bright stars: [Alphecca](/astrology/alphecca/), the gem of the Northern Crown, at 12°39′, which the Sun reaches about 4 or 5 November, and the two claws. Zubenelgenubi lies almost on the Sun's path, so a planet at 15° Scorpio can pass right beside it, or in front of it.

Generations share these degrees as well. Pluto, at home in Scorpio in the modern scheme, crossed them from late 1987 to 1991, [Neptune in Scorpio](/astrology/neptune-in-scorpio/) between late 1960 and 1966, and Uranus between late 1976 and 1979. The houses they fall in, which only a birth time can show, are what make them yours.
