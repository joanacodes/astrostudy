Most placements of Mars involve a translation: the planet that wants to act has to work in another sign's manner. In Aries there is nothing to translate. Mars rules the sign, so the urge and the style are one and the same, and I read Mars in Aries as the shortest path between wanting something and moving towards it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the part of a chart that starts things: initiative, effort, the courage to try, and the capacity to say *this far and no further*. [Aries](/astrology/aries/), cardinal fire, is the sign of beginnings, quick and self-starting. Because Mars rules Aries, a Mars there answers to no other planet: when I follow the chain of rulers from the sign it sits in, the chain stops at Mars itself. Tradition calls this its [domicile](/astrology/domicile/). I don't read it as more Mars than anyone else has, but as Mars without a filter, drive in its native form.

In a life, that shows as a short gap between deciding and doing. You tend to find out whether something works by trying it, and you're at your best at the starting line, before anyone else has moved. Competition feels like play, and a boundary gets said at the moment it's crossed.

Mars usually spends about 40 days in Aries, one of its quicker passages; when it turns retrograde there, a stay can stretch to 194 days. About 1 person in 14 born between 1930 and 2009 has it. Everyone born within the same few weeks shares the sign; the house it falls in says where in your life you go first, and that part is yours alone. Just before it, [Mars in Pisces](/astrology/mars-in-pisces/) acts from feeling and often for others; just after, [Mars in Taurus](/astrology/mars-in-taurus/) is slow to start and hard to stop. Aries is the clean start between them.

In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Mars was checked like every placement, its sign included, and it wasn't one of the three that held far more often; overall, about a third of what I checked held. So even at home, I treat this Mars as a strong style to look at, never as a verdict on who you are.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, Mars in Aries tends to make the first move and mean it. You'd rather hear a clear yes or no than live with a maybe, and you show interest by acting on it. Disagreements arrive fast and, for you, end fast: once it's said, it's done. The other person may need longer, and that gap is where most of the friction lives. Someone who enjoys a frank exchange suits this Mars, and so does someone with a life of their own, because you need room to act without consulting. The skill to learn is asking *what do you want?* before moving for two.

## At work {#at-work}

This Mars is built for first steps: launching something, answering an emergency, making the call nobody wants to make, going first so that others can follow. It does well with autonomy, short deadlines and visible results, and often prefers a hard challenge to an easy routine. Long approval chains wear it down. The middle of a project, when the novelty has gone and the end isn't yet in sight, is where you're most likely to drift; working with someone who loves finishing, or cutting the work into sprints, keeps your energy where it's strongest.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Speed can stand in for thought. You may commit before you've looked, start more than you can finish, or take someone's slower pace as an obstacle put there on purpose. Courage isn't recklessness, but from the inside the two can feel alike. When you're crossed, your reaction can be quick and sharp, then forgotten, by you, long before the other person has moved on.

## What helps {#what-helps}

When the stakes are high, put one breath, or one night, between the urge and the act. Give your energy a daily outlet: running, a martial art, any sport where you race your own last time. Choose your fights before they choose you. Finish one thing before starting the next, and let the finishing count as a win. And when a flare has passed, a quick, plain apology suits this Mars: it's as direct as the reaction was.
