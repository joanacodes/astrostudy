Astrology takes something anyone can check, where the Sun, the Moon and the planets stood in the sky at a given moment, and reads it as a language for a human life. The first half is astronomy. The second half is the part people argue about, and I arrived on the arguing side.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

An astrologer starts with a moment and a place. Seen from there, at that minute, the [Sun](/astrology/sun/), the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) and each planet stood somewhere along the same band of sky, and the horizon and the meridian crossed that band at points of their own. Write those positions down and you have a chart. Read them together, as a description of a person, a relationship or a stretch of time, and you have astrology.

What most people meet first is one branch of it: the reading of a birth chart, the sky at the minute someone was born. The other branches work from the same materials. [Synastry](/astrology/synastry/) lays two people's charts side by side. [Horary astrology](/astrology/horary-astrology/) casts a chart for the moment a question is asked, and [electional astrology](/astrology/electional-astrology/) looks for a good moment to begin something. [Mundane astrology](/astrology/mundane-astrology/) reads the sky for countries and the wider world. Other cultures built astrologies of their own on different foundations, [Chinese astrology](/astrology/chinese-astrology/) among them. The one I practise is [Western astrology](/astrology/western-astrology/): modern tropical astrology, with traditional techniques alongside.

It's old. The earliest birth charts we know of are cuneiform tablets written in Babylonia in the fifth century BCE ([Babylonian astrology](/astrology/babylonian-astrology/)). Some six centuries later, Ptolemy wrote the standard astronomy of the ancient world and then an astrology, the Tetrabiblos, and he opened the second by telling the two apart: one studies how the Sun, the Moon and the stars move; the other, what those movements bring about around us. The line he drew still holds. Astronomy measures, and astrology interprets.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A chart has four kinds of pieces, and every reading is about how they fit together.

- **The planets** are the functions. In the system I built for my readings, the Moon regulates needs and feelings, Mercury processes and exchanges information, Saturn builds structure and responsibility, and so on through all ten.
- **The signs** give each planet a style: the same Mercury thinks one way in Aries and another in Taurus. They're twelve equal stretches of the [zodiac](/astrology/zodiac/).
- **The houses** place each planet in an area of life, work, home, partnership, and they come from the horizon and the meridian at that place and minute. That's why I need [the birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/).
- **The aspects** are the angles between planets, and they relate one function to another: a [square](/astrology/square/) as friction, a [trine](/astrology/trine/) as ease.

That much is method, and it can be taught. How any of it would work, why the sky at your birth should describe you at all, is another matter. Nobody knows, me included, and I'd be wary of anyone who claims otherwise. What I can tell you is what happened when I checked. I went through my own chart like an invoice, then the charts of family, friends and strangers who agreed to it, with a tick or a cross beside every placement. About a third held: 30 to 40%. Saturn, Lilith and Chiron held 75 to 90% of the time ([my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). It's a notebook, not a study, and it proves nothing about astrology in general. The research that tries to answer the general question has its own pages, starting with [Does astrology work?](/astrology/does-astrology-work/)

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

So a reading, as I give it, is the whole chart read placement by placement, with the most time spent where the three that held best sit, and a plain word whenever a conclusion leans on something that held less often. It describes tendencies, needs and seasons: how you're inclined to move, never where you must end up. When the planets travel over your chart, I read that as weather, not as a forecast of events.

Some subjects I don't read at all: health, death, pregnancy, money and legal outcomes. A chart can't diagnose, plead or invest, and a reading that pretends it can does harm. I read for adults only, and never without a birth time. All of that is set out in [How I read](/how-i-read/). Here it's enough to say that astrology, as I practise it, is a conversation with yourself, and you stay in charge of it.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Astrology is your star sign.”** Your Sun sign is one placement among a dozen or more, and in my notebook it was one of those that held about a third of the time. A [birth chart](/astrology/birth-chart/) is the whole sky at your birth, not a twelfth of it.
- **“Astrologers think the planets push people around.”** I don't read a planet as a force acting on you. I read a chart as a description, and I'm honest that I don't know why it describes anything.
- **“Everyone born on your birthday should be you.”** People born on the same day share most planets' signs, but the minute and the place change the rising sign and the houses, and a person is far more than a chart.
- **“Astrology tells you what will happen.”** It describes seasons and tendencies. What you do with them is yours.
