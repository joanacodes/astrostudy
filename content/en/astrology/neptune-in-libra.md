Everyone born from October 1942 to August 1957 carries one of astrology's loftiest dreams of love: Neptune, the planet of ideals, in Libra, the sign of partnership. It's a beautiful inheritance, and a demanding one, because it asks you to love people who are only human.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Put the imagination and compassion of [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) in [Libra](/astrology/libra/), the cardinal air sign ruled by Venus that weighs, compares and looks for the balance between two sides, and the longing for something larger takes the shape of harmony: the right partner, a fair arrangement, an order in which everyone gets their due. And because Libra is cardinal, the ideal doesn't sit waiting to be found. It proposes, invites, takes the first step towards peace.

Libra is neither Neptune's home nor its detriment, and Neptune has no exaltation or fall, so there is no dignity to weigh here. There is your [Venus](/astrology/venus/), though. Venus rules Libra, so what you find beautiful and fair, and the house your Venus sits in, shows how this shared dream of harmony takes a personal form.

Neptune was in Libra for nearly fifteen years, and about 1 person in 6 born from 1930 to 2009 has it: a generation now aged roughly 69 to 84. Until August 1943 Neptune was still crossing back into Virgo, and from December 1955 it was already dipping into Scorpio, so for births in those months [Neptune in Virgo](/astrology/neptune-in-virgo/) or [Neptune in Scorpio](/astrology/neptune-in-scorpio/) may be yours instead; only the chart can say. Inside the generation, the house is what makes a Libra Neptune personal: the part of your life where you go looking for the perfect balance.

My [notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) didn't spare it the scales. Neptune's sign and house were checked like every placement, Neptune wasn't one of the three that held far more often, Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, and overall about a third held.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

This is where Neptune in Libra lives most visibly. Love is imagined as a meeting of equals, graceful and kind: two people who understand each other without needing to quarrel. That ideal gives a great deal, courtesy, tenderness, a real gift for seeing the other person's side. It also asks a great deal of anyone you love. A partner can feel admired for someone they aren't quite, and a first real disagreement can feel like a betrayal of the picture rather than an ordinary part of being two. The bond grows when the ideal describes how you treat each other, not who the other person has to be.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, this Neptune dreams of fairness. You may be drawn to mediating, negotiating, designing or counselling, any role where two sides need to meet in the middle, and you bring to it an instinct for what would feel just to everyone in the room. Beauty matters as well: a well-made agreement, a balanced design, a working relationship that feels like a partnership. The thing to watch is a workplace where keeping the peace quietly replaces saying what's true.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The hard part is the distance between the harmony you can imagine and the harmony you can have. Real partnerships include friction. Real fairness sometimes needs a difficult conversation, and real peace is negotiated, not dreamed. When that gap opens, a Libra Neptune may smooth things over instead of sorting them out, agree too quickly, or measure the people you love against an image they never agreed to.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Treat the ideal as a direction, not a test. Say the uncomfortable thing gently: a fairness that includes your own needs is the only kind that lasts. Notice the ordinary harmonies already there, the shared joke, the meal made without being asked. And keep some beauty in your days that asks nothing of anyone: music, a garden, a picture you love.
