Poseidon closes the list of Uranian astrology's eight hypothetical points. Friedrich Sieggrün proposed it in 1927 and gave it the Greek name of the sea god whose Roman name a planet already carried: Neptune.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

It is the last of Sieggrün's four, after [Vulkanus](/astrology/vulkanus/), and with it the Hamburg School had its full set: four points from Alfred Witte, four from Sieggrün, every one of them placed beyond Neptune ([The Hamburg School (Uranian astrology)](/astrology/hamburg-school-uranian-astrology/)).

Poseidon was one of the twelve Olympians: god of the sea, of storms, of earthquakes and of horses, protector of sailors, worshipped at Pylos and Thebes as the earth-shaker. When the sons of Cronus had overthrown their father, Homer says, they shared out the world by lot; Zeus drew the sky, Hades the underworld, Poseidon the sea. He was the tamer of horses, or their father, and in remote Arcadia he was worshipped in the form of a horse; a strike of his trident made springs rise from the ground, and in Greek the words for horses and springs are related. He lost Athens to Athena and flooded the plain in his anger. In the *Iliad* he takes the Greeks' side against Troy; in the *Odyssey* he keeps Odysseus from home for ten years for blinding his son, the Cyclops Polyphemus. His two brothers have Uranian points too, both of them Witte's: [Zeus](/astrology/zeus/) and [Hades](/astrology/hades/).

The Romans called him Neptune, and every chart already carries his emblem: the glyph for [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/), the planet found in 1846, is his trident.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Uranian astrologers read Poseidon for the spirit, and they say so in Neptune's terms: it is spiritual, like Neptune. At its best, in their reading, it is enlightened love, the selfless love that Greek calls *agape*, and they read it for psychic abilities and spiritual wisdom.

A Uranian chart holds both sea gods, then, with a shared theme and different footing. Neptune began as a calculation too: Urbain Le Verrier worked out where it must be from the way it pulled on Uranus, and in 1846 astronomers looked there and found it. It spends about fourteen years in each sign. Poseidon was proposed from charts, not from any pull on a planet, and in nearly a century since, nobody has seen anything where it is placed. The god of storms and earthquakes lends the point none of his violence, though: the meanings given for it are all calm water.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Poseidon isn't in my readings. The spirit, and the longing for something larger, I read in Neptune, by sign, house and aspect, and in [the 12th house](/astrology/12th-house/), which tradition called the Bad Spirit and which I read for what happens out of sight, the inner life included. For [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/), Neptune's sign, I read both rulers, Neptune and Jupiter. When I look ahead, Neptune's transits are among the ones I follow, and the one most people meet is its square to its own place, at about 41: a long stretch when what seemed solid can blur, read as weather, not a forecast.

Psychic abilities I don't read at all. A chart can describe how someone takes in the world around them; it can't prove a gift, or rule one out ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Uranian astrologers use Poseidon instead of Neptune.”** They use both: Neptune, the planet found in 1846, and Poseidon, the calculated point. The readings are close; the positions are not.
- **“Poseidon proves psychic powers.”** A reading of a point is no evidence of a gift, and no chart can show one.
- **“Every midpoint astrologer uses Poseidon.”** Reinhold Ebertin kept Witte's midpoints for [cosmobiology](/astrology/cosmobiology/) and rejected the hypothetical points, Poseidon with the rest.
