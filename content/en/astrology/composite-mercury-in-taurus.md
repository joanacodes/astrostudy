Some pairs take a month to choose a sofa and never argue about it again. Composite Mercury in Taurus is that pace in the relationship's own chart: a shared mind that would rather be sure than first.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), each planet stands halfway between its places in your two birth charts. Composite [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is the bond's way of thinking: what the two of you notice together, how you decide, how you explain yourselves to friends and family. In [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), fixed earth, that thinking is concrete and unhurried. About 1 pair in 13 has it, much the same share as people born with [Mercury in Taurus](/astrology/mercury-in-taurus/). That natal placement describes one person's mind. This one exists only between the two of you, and it can be slower than either of yours: two quick talkers may find their joint decisions settle at walking pace.

A relationship with this Mercury tends to talk best side by side rather than face to face: over a meal it has cooked, on a long walk, while repainting a room. It trusts what it has tried. News is digested before it's discussed, and a plan is tested on a small scale (one weekend away, one shelf, one recipe) before anyone commits to the large one. Its conversation returns to a handful of subjects, gladly, and a running joke can last as long as the friendship.

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) rules Taurus, and in a composite it's a midpoint like everything else, so its sign shows what the bond values enough to spend its thinking on. Venus also breaks the sky's rules more often than Mercury does: composite Venus sits more than 47° from the composite Sun in about 1 pair in 10, a gap no birth chart allows, while composite Mercury passes its own limit of about 28° in about 1 pair in 18. Most pairs with this Mercury find the composite Sun close by, in Aries, Taurus or Gemini; the few who don't are looking at an effect of the arithmetic.

Where it struggles is change that can't wait. A bond that likes to think things over can leave a needed conversation for later, and later again, because the current arrangement is comfortable; or it can hold to a view the two of you formed years ago after the facts have moved. Continuity isn't stubbornness, but it does need a door left open. A date in the diary helps (*twice a year, we look at how we share the week*), and so does starting small when one of you wants something new: this Mercury believes what it has tried more readily than what it has been told.

Beside it, [composite Mercury in Aries](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-aries/) decides in a sentence and [composite Mercury in Gemini](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-gemini/) keeps a dozen threads going at once. Taurus keeps one thread and makes it strong.

This page describes a technique, not a reading: composites aren't charts I draw. Astrologers who use them read them with both natal charts and the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) between them, which show how each of you talks to the other, a different question from how the bond itself thinks. Before any of it, both people agree to have their birth data used, at whatever pace the slower of you needs ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
