Mars opposite Neptune sets the part of you that acts across from the part that imagines and feels for others. One end says *this is what I want*; the other says *think of everyone else, and of what could be*. Living with it means learning to hear both.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is assertion and effort: the drive to start, to compete and to defend your ground. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) is the part that imagines and feels with others: compassion, transcendence, a sense of what lies beyond the self. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) makes them a polarity: each keeps its identity, and the tension between them depends on how you hold it.

In plain words, your energy may be pulled towards other people's needs, causes or visions, sometimes before you've worked out what you want yourself. Partners or groups can seem to carry the dream while you carry the effort, or the other way round. A plain no can feel unkind, and a cause more urgent than your own plans. None of that is weakness. The opposition teaches a balance that is genuinely hard and genuinely valuable: acting for others by choice, with your own wants still in view.

The mature form of this aspect knows that compassion is not self-erasure: generous effort, with a clear edge.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Opposite signs mirror each other ([polarity](/astrology/polarity/)), and the axis gives this polarity its subject. Across Virgo and Pisces, with Neptune in its modern sign, the pull runs between practical effort and an ideal; across Aries and Libra, with Mars in its own sign, between your initiative and a partner's picture of things. The houses name the arenas: Mars in the 6th opposite Neptune in the 12th, for instance, might set daily work against a quieter, inward calling.

Within 6° of exact, about 1 person in 33 has this opposition. Close to exact, the balance between will and ideal is central to how you relate; at the wide end, it shows when a cause or a person asks a lot of you.

Neptune lingers some fourteen years in a sign, and Mars crosses the opposite point once in each two-year cycle, so only a few weeks of births share the aspect each time. Because this aspect so often plays out with other people, I'll repeat what I say of any contact between two lives: a synastry is read with both people's consent, never behind anyone's back ([Synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

How the opposition differs from the pair's other aspects:

- **[Mars conjunct Neptune](/astrology/mars-conjunct-neptune/):** will and ideal in one place, so the dream drives you directly instead of arriving through others.
- **[Mars sextile Neptune](/astrology/mars-sextile-neptune/):** the two cooperate across a short gap, a link you take up when you choose.
- **[Mars square Neptune](/astrology/mars-square-neptune/):** friction at a corner, felt as inner hesitation more than as other people's pull.
- **[Mars trine Neptune](/astrology/mars-trine-neptune/):** effort and imagination flow together, a harmony the opposition has to build.

Between two charts, [Mars opposite Neptune in synastry](/astrology/mars-opposite-neptune-synastry/) often casts one person as the doer and the other as the dreamer. I read it in both directions, since either can lose sight of their own wishes in the other's.

## In transits {#in-transits}

Neptune reaches the point opposite your Mars at most once in a life and lingers for a year or two, in several passes: [Transiting Neptune opposite natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-neptune-opposite-natal-mars/), a season when other people's needs or visions press on your energy. Born with the opposition, you meet a square instead around 41, at the [Neptune square](/astrology/neptune-square/), when Neptune in the sky stands 90° from your Neptune and your Mars alike.

Mars in the sky opposes your natal Neptune about every two years, for a few days: a brief spell when someone else's wishes weigh on what you'd planned.

These transits ask for balance between giving and wanting. They're weather, not a forecast, and they predict nothing.
