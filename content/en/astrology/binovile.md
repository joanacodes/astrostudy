Two noviles end to end make the binovile: two planets 80° apart, two-ninths of the circle, between the quintile and the square. As I read it, it's ripening twice over: two developments that mature side by side and keep feeding each other.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

Eighty degrees is two signs and twenty. The first ten degrees of a sign decide it: a planet within them makes its binovile two signs on; a planet past them, three, which is the more common case.

Its theme, for me, is dual maturation. Both planets ripen at once, in parallel, and their development keeps folding back into a shared pattern, so that each round of growth on one side gives the other something to build on. What it reveals is complementary growth: what one planet learns turns out to be what the other needed, and over time the two settle into something coherent. It isn't perfection, destiny or a sudden awakening. In its less easy form, it synthesises too soon, or one side resists growing at the other's pace.

It needs a tight orb, about a degree (see [orbs](/astrology/orbs/)), because of where it sits: 8° past the quintile, which Kepler added to Ptolemy's aspects in *Harmonices Mundi* (Linz, 1619), 10° short of the [square](/astrology/square/) and 20° past the [sextile](/astrology/sextile/).

Three binoviles make 240°, a [trine](/astrology/trine/) measured the other way round, and two make the quadnovile, 20° short of the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/). Like every ninth, it belongs to the chart of nines among [harmonic charts](/astrology/harmonic-charts/): multiplied by nine, 80° becomes two full turns, a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/).

## Between which planets {#between-which-planets}

The binovile has no pair pages here. Since it describes two developments, the pace of each body decides how the two run together, and that's what I read first.

When both are personal planets, the Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus or Mars, the two developments are close to hand: a way of feeling and a way of acting, maturing together through ordinary experience. When one of the two is Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune or Pluto, the personal planet's ripening has to keep pace with a much slower one, and the synthesis takes years; that's the binovile I'd look at first. Two slow planets in binovile describe a generation's parallel development, read through the houses.

The [novile](/astrology/novile/), at 40°, is the single ripening this one doubles. The [quadnovile](/astrology/quadnovile/), at 160°, is two binoviles: the ninths' far reach towards the opposition.

## The binovile in synastry {#in-synastry}

Between two charts, a binovile describes two people who grow side by side: each matures in their own direction, and the relationship is where those two developments meet and combine. It rarely shows early, and it's easy to miss among the major aspects; often it's only in hindsight that two people see how much each one's growth leaned on the other's. I read it in both directions in [synastry readings](/synastry/), and only when it's tight; it describes growth that can be shared, not a perfect match.

## The binovile in transits {#in-transits}

When a moving planet makes a binovile to one of yours, it passes quickly: a few hours from the Moon, a day or two from the Sun, Mercury or Venus. From Jupiter outwards it can hold for weeks, and then it can feel like two parts of life ripening at the same time. Weather, not a forecast: a binovile transit describes growth on two fronts, never where that growth ends.
