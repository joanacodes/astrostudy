There's usually one person you call when a chapter closes. Here, one person's Pluto lies two signs on from the other's Sun, roughly 60° round the zodiac, and that person's capacity for renewal is within reach of the other's purpose.

## What flows {#what-flows}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) gives a person purpose and vitality: a self that keeps choosing a direction. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) brings depth, renewal and the clearing away of what no longer works, so that something stronger can grow. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) relates the two by opportunity: the resource is there, and using it is up to the pair. As [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) with Pluto go, this one is the least dramatic.

Holding the Sun, you have someone who doesn't flinch when things go wrong. When a project fails or a role ends, they help you see what's worth keeping and what can go, and their calm about endings makes your own direction feel more resilient. You borrow their ability to start again.

Holding Pluto, your intensity finally has a job: the other's purpose. Your insight is asked for rather than resisted, your depth is welcome without being too much, and you feel most useful at exactly the moments others avoid.

The elements set the style: between fire and air, renewal comes through bold restarts and new ideas; between earth and water, through practical clear-outs and honest talk about feelings.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

The depth on offer can sit unused. Pairs with this sextile often keep things light, and the Pluto person, who knows how much their insight can weigh, may hold it back until asked; the Sun person may not think to ask. The chance to go deeper waits, because a sextile's opportunity needs someone to take it up.

Timing matters too. An offer of depth that arrives in a busy week can feel like an intrusion, even when it's meant as help.

Depending on where it is in its long, uneven orbit, Pluto takes 12 to 31 years over a sign, so its side of the contact is common to a whole age group. The Sun person's birthday, which puts their Sun two signs on, is what makes it this pair's.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Ask at the turning points. The Sun person can bring the hard decision to the Pluto person on purpose, and say what kind of help they want; the Pluto person can make the offer a single time and trust the other to return to it. A couple might lean on this contact during a move or a change of career; friends, after a loss or an ending; family members, when an old pattern needs breaking; colleagues, when a project needs restructuring.

About 1 pair of people in 10 has this sextile, one way round or the other, within 4.8°. So narrow an orb means that near its edge the birth time can count, the Sun shifting about a degree in a day; it always counts for the houses, which show in which part of each life the renewal happens, and I don't read the contact without both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Other angles of the same pair:

- **[Sun conjunct Pluto in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-pluto-synastry/):** the depth goes straight to the core of the identity.
- **[Sun square Pluto in synastry](/astrology/sun-square-pluto-synastry/):** it presses from the side, and two wills have to adjust.
- **[Sun trine Pluto in synastry](/astrology/sun-trine-pluto-synastry/):** it runs steadily, with no need to be asked for.
- **[Sun opposite Pluto in synastry](/astrology/sun-opposite-pluto-synastry/):** it stands opposite, and the two have to balance their weight.

In one chart, [Sun sextile Pluto](/astrology/sun-sextile-pluto/) is a person's own resilience. Between two, the resilience is lent, and has to be asked for.
