Ask an Aries Sun who they are and you may get a shrug; watch them begin something and the answer is plain. The Sun was in the first sign of the zodiac when you were born, where tradition says it is exalted, and I read it as a self that is written in verbs.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is the part of a chart that organises who you are: your identity, your vitality, your sense of purpose, and how coherently you put yourself into the world. [Aries](/astrology/aries/), cardinal fire ruled by Mars, gives all of that a present-tense manner. With the Sun here, identity is something you feel in the act more than something you can describe, and your sense of direction tends to clear only once you're moving.

Tradition calls Aries the Sun's [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/). Ptolemy's reason was the sky itself: the Sun enters Aries at the March equinox, and from then on, in the northern hemisphere where he wrote, the days outlast the nights and the warmth builds. I read the exaltation as a self that needs very little permission to exist. It isn't a promise of being better, brighter or bolder than anyone else.

The Sun spends about 30 days in Aries, never more than 31, from about 21 March to 19 April, so about 1 person in 12 has it. Born within a day of either end, you need your [birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/) to be sure of the sign. A month earlier, the [Sun in Pisces](/astrology/sun-in-pisces/) builds a self by feeling its way into others; a month later, the [Sun in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-taurus/) builds one by keeping. The Aries Sun builds one by going.

[Mars](/astrology/mars/), the ruler of Aries, is the next thing I look at. Its sign and house show where this Sun spends its effort, and when that Mars has something real to do, the whole identity tends to feel lit.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, an Aries Sun wants to be met head on. You tend to show interest by acting on it, and you understand a partner best when they say plainly what they want. Being kept waiting, or steered with hints, can feel like being asked to disappear. What this Sun brings is a bond that keeps renewing itself: the new plan, the first apology, the suggestion that you start again after a bad week. What it has to learn is that someone else's slower timing isn't a verdict on you. A partner who needs a day to think is still on your side.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, the Sun in Aries is often at its brightest at the beginning: the launch, the first draft, the problem nobody has touched yet. Purpose sharpens when there's a clear target and dims when the job becomes pure maintenance. That doesn't mean you can't sustain anything. You sustain it best as a series of fresh starts, a long project cut into short campaigns, each with its own finish line. Recognition matters too, though not the loud kind: you want the first move to stay credited to you after the project has grown and other names have joined it.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Identity can come to depend on momentum. When something is moving, you know who you are; when it stalls, in a long wait or a job that has settled into routine, the sense of self can flicker with it, and restlessness moves in. You may start something new mainly to feel like yourself again, and leave a trail of good beginnings without middles. Other people may call that selfish. I'd call it a self that hasn't yet learned to recognise itself while standing still.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Treat your vitality as a rhythm rather than a constant: plan the sprint and the rest after it, and stop expecting yourself to burn evenly. Give a long project a new start every few weeks, a fresh target inside the old one. When your sense of self goes flat, move; for many Aries Suns that's the quickest way back. Keep a list of what you began and finished, and read it on the slow days: the thread through all those starts is the identity you're building.

In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the Sun sign held about a third of the time, alongside the rising sign, Venus and Mercury, while Saturn, Chiron and Lilith held far more often. So I take an Aries Sun as a chart's opening line, never its conclusion, which seems a fair place for a sign that likes to go first.
