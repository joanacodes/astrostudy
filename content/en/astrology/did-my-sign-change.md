Every few years, a headline tells you that your sign has changed, and usually that there's a thirteenth one now. It's an unsettling thing to read: a sign can feel like part of your name. Here's what has really moved in the sky, what hasn't, and how to check your own chart.

## The short answer {#the-short-answer}

No. In the [tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/), the zodiac of modern tropical astrology and the one I read, nobody's sign has changed. Its signs aren't the constellations: they're twelve equal stretches of the Sun's yearly path, counted from the March equinox, so they stay with the seasons rather than with the stars. What has moved, very slowly, is the backdrop, the constellations the signs were named after. That's real astronomy, and it's what the headlines keep mixing up.

If an app now shows you a different Sun sign, look at two things before you worry: whether it's set to a sidereal zodiac, and whether you were born within a day or so of a change of sign, where only the date, time and place can settle it ([Sign dates, and why they move](/astrology/sign-dates-and-why-they-move/)).

## What the research found {#what-the-research-found}

**The signs begin at the equinox.** The tropical zodiac takes its start from the seasons: Aries begins at the March equinox, and each sign is exactly a twelfth of the circle. Ptolemy set the rule down for astrologers in the second century: the beginnings of the signs, he wrote in the Tetrabiblos, "are to be taken from the equinoctial and tropical points". [Constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/) goes into the difference between the two.

**The stars drift.** In the second century BC, Hipparchus found that the star Spica had moved about 2° relative to the autumnal equinox since earlier astronomers measured it: the equinoxes themselves were moving against the stars. This is the [precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/), the slow turning of the Earth's axis. Ptolemy put its rate at a degree a century, which is too slow; the modern figure is about a degree every 72 years, a full circle in about 26,000. Over the centuries, the March equinox, where Aries begins, has moved out of the constellation Aries and into Pisces.

**The thirteenth sign is an old story.** The Sun's path crosses thirteen constellations, not twelve: Ophiuchus, the serpent-holder, lies between Scorpius and Sagittarius. The Sun stands in front of it from about 29 or 30 November, depending on the table, to 17 December, all within the sign of Sagittarius. More than once, the press has reported this as a change to the signs made by some astronomical body: in 1995, in 2011 and again in 2016, when the story was that NASA had done it. NASA hadn't changed anyone's sign. [Ophiuchus and the 13th sign](/astrology/ophiuchus-and-the-13th-sign/) has the whole story, and [Does Mercury retrograde break things?](/astrology/does-mercury-retrograde-break-things/) looks at another headline that outruns the sky.

**Another zodiac, not a correction.** A [sidereal zodiac](/astrology/sidereal-zodiac/), the kind Hindu astrology uses, keeps its signs fixed to the stars. Since the two zodiacs have drifted apart, a sidereal chart can put your Sun in another sign. That's a second system giving a second answer, not proof that the first one was wrong. Whether either makes astrology a science is a different question, taken up in [Is astrology a science?](/astrology/is-astrology-a-science/)

## What I found {#what-i-found}

I read the tropical zodiac, so the Sun sign in your chart is the one the season of your birth gives it, and no headline has moved it.

It's a notebook, not a study: it proves nothing about astrology in general, and that includes which zodiac is right. What it does show is how little weight I put on the Sun sign alone. The Sun sign was one of the placements everyone reads first, with the rising sign, Venus and Mercury, and it held about a third of the time; overall, **30 to 40%** of what I checked held. The three that held far more often, **Saturn, Lilith and Chiron, 75 to 90% of the time**, are where I spend most of a reading.

So if you've been told your sign has changed, take a breath. It hasn't, and a reading never rests on the Sun sign alone: a whole chart also needs your birth time, for the rising sign and the houses, and [here's where to find yours](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#finding-your-time).
