Secondary progressions let each planet move at its own pace. Solar arcs move them all together: the whole chart turns forward by the arc the progressed Sun has covered, and keeps its shape as it goes.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Take the distance your [progressed Sun](/astrology/progressed-sun/) has travelled since you were born, its arc, and add it to every point of the birth chart: each planet, the Ascendant, the Midheaven, the nodes. That is a solar arc direction. The arc grows by 57′ to 61′ a year, depending on the season of your birth, so it's often rounded to a degree a year, and some astrologers use the round figure instead. The difference adds up: at 30, the true arc lies between 28½° and 30½°, and a year of timing can depend on which one a reading uses.

Since every point moves by the same amount, the directed chart is the birth chart turned on itself, every natal aspect intact. What changes is the relation between the moving chart and the still one beneath it. A directed point reaches a natal one when the arc equals the distance between them, so the ages are written into the birth chart: if your Mars sits 25° further on in the zodiac than your Sun, your directed Sun reaches it at about 25.

Solar arcs belong to a family of symbolic clocks. [Tertiary and minor progressions](/astrology/tertiary-and-minor-progressions/) take a day after birth for a lunar month of life, or a lunar month for a year; [primary directions](/astrology/primary-directions/), already among Ptolemy's timing methods, are older still.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

A program does it in one step: choose solar arcs, give a date, and the directed positions appear around the birth chart. By hand, find your progressed Sun for the date, subtract your natal Sun, and add that arc to each natal position.

Then you look for contacts: a directed point within about a degree of a natal planet or angle, or of an aspect to it. At about a degree a year, a contact takes some two years to come into orb and pass, and the moving chart has as many points as the still one, so a single year can hold several.

The directed Midheaven is the natal Midheaven plus the arc, one of the ways a progressed Midheaven is worked out. The angles are where the birth time counts most: four minutes of clock time move the Midheaven by about a degree, and here a degree is a year. A time rounded to the quarter hour can put a direction to or from an angle three or four years out ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

Solar arcs are part of the timing I use. I read a direction as a stretch of a year or two when one part of the chart, moving, meets another that stayed put: directed Mars reaching your natal Midheaven joins your drive to the role you're seen in; the directed Moon reaching natal Venus joins what you need to what you value.

A direction on its own is a mark on the calendar. When a slow [transit](/astrology/transits/) or a progression reaches the same natal point in the same months, or an eclipse falls on it ([Eclipses on your natal planets](/astrology/eclipses-on-your-natal-planets/)), I read that theme as the season's main business. That agreement between methods is what I look for, much more than any single contact.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

The directed planets were never in those places: the method is symbolic from start to finish, and the slow unfolding it describes is a way of reading a chart, not a motion in the sky. It describes development and its timing, never a guaranteed event.

Its tidiness hides one oddity. In solar arcs the Moon moves at the Sun's pace, a degree a year, instead of the thirteen it covers in [secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/). The Moon's own rhythm, its phases and the chapters that run from one [progressed new moon](/astrology/progressed-new-moon/) to the next, belong to progressions, and the two methods are best read side by side.

And the narrow orb is a discipline, not a suggestion. Stretch it to three or four degrees and every year fills with contacts, until the method can explain anything and so explains nothing.
