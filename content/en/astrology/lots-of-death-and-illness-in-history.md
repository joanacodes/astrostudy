Some of the oldest lots were made for the questions people fear most: when a life will end, and what illness it will meet on the way. This page is about them as history, because that's the only way I treat them. I don't read death or illness in any chart, and nothing here is offered as a method.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The lists of lots grew from the dozen or so Paulus Alexandrinus worked with in the 4th century to the fifty-five Abu Ma'shar described in the 9th, and they followed the questions clients brought ([doctrine of the lots](/astrology/doctrine-of-the-lots/)). Next to lots for marriage, travel or trade, such as the [Part of Commerce](/astrology/part-of-commerce/), they included lots for death and for illness. Even the best known of all, the [Part of Fortune](/astrology/part-of-fortune/), was read in antiquity for the body and its health as well as for fortune.

The lots weren't alone. Ptolemy, who had no patience with lots, still put the length of life at the head of everything a chart could be asked after birth: it "takes the leading place among inquiries about events following birth" (*Tetrabiblos*, book III, chapter 10). His chapter on it is long and unusually detailed, and the French historian Auguste Bouché-Leclercq later called that calculation "the chief task of astrology, the operation judged most difficult by practitioners, most dangerous and damnable by its enemies" ([Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos](/astrology/ptolemys-tetrabiblos/)). Illness had a chapter of its own, on injuries and diseases (book III, chapter 12), and the manner of death another (book IV, chapter 9).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

What follows describes how these methods were meant to work, not how to use them.

The length-of-life method weighed five places: the Sun, the Moon, the Ascendant, the Lot of Fortune and the new or full moon before the birth. Later astrologers called what it drew from them the hyleg, from a Middle Persian word for nativity. So a lot sat at the heart even of Ptolemy's method. The years were then counted with [primary directions](/astrology/primary-directions/), one of the oldest timing techniques, which Ptolemy mentions in that same chapter. The method could find several "destructive" periods and then cancel some of them out with protective ones, leaving periods of danger or illness that weren't meant to end in death: a hint of how much was left to judgement.

Illness was read through the body. Ptolemy gave the planets organs, Saturn the spleen and Jupiter the lungs, and later medical astrology matched signs and planets to parts of the body and to remedies ([medical astrology, in history](/astrology/medical-astrology-in-history/)). By 1647, William Lilly's book on nativities still covered which diseases a person would suffer and whether they would die a violent death ([William Lilly's Christian Astrology (1647)](/astrology/william-lillys-christian-astrology-1647/)).

The lots of death and illness were read with all this, as one more pointer each. I don't print their formulas here, and I won't.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Death, yours or a loved one's, and health questions are on my list of what I won't read ([how I read](/how-i-read/)), and two pages say why in full: [can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/) and [can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/). I describe this history because people meet these lots in software and old books, and sometimes in a frightening reading built on them. If someone calculated a lot of death in your chart and told you what it meant, it meant nothing about your life.

What a chart can hold is gentler. The [8th house](/astrology/8th-house/), which tradition gave to death, I read as intimacy, trust and what you let go of. The [6th](/astrology/6th-house/), which it gave to illness, I read as daily work and daily care. And if the fear of dying or of falling ill keeps coming back, that fear deserves real help, from a doctor or a therapist ([astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A lot of death shows when someone will die.”** No method ever could. Even Ptolemy's needed judgement to decide which of its dangerous periods counted.
- **“A lot in the 8th house seals it.”** Tradition gave the 8th death; no placement there means one.
- **“Medical astrology was part of medicine, so it must have worked.”** It was taught beside medicine for centuries and declined after the 17th; it has no scientific basis.
- **“Knowing the old formulas protects you.”** A calculation can't protect anyone. A doctor, and the people who love you, can.
