Makemake was spotted just after Easter 2005, nicknamed Easterbunny, and named three years later after the creator god of Easter Island. Most names in the sky are chosen to suit a body; this one was chosen to suit a date.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Makemake is a dwarf planet in the Kuiper belt, the disc of icy bodies beyond [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/). It is about 1,430 km across, roughly three-fifths the width of [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/), and the fourth largest dwarf planet known, after Pluto, [Eris](/astrology/eris/) and [Haumea](/astrology/haumea/). Its surface is very bright, covered with frozen methane and stained reddish-brown by tholins, and in 2025 the James Webb Space Telescope detected methane gas around it. It has one known moon, perhaps 175 km across, seen in Hubble images of April 2015.

Its orbit takes about 307 years and runs from 38.2 to 52.8 times the Earth's distance from the Sun, tilted 29° to the Sun's path. Makemake is near its farthest point now, which it reaches in May 2033, and well above the plane of the planets: it won't cross the Sun's path again until 2103.

Michael Brown, Chad Trujillo and David Rabinowitz photographed it at Palomar Observatory on 31 March 2005, as part of a search for large bodies beyond Neptune, and Brown picked it out of the images on 3 April. They had meant to announce it later. Then, on 27 July, a Spanish team announced Haumea, which Brown's team had also been following, and Brown, afraid the same would happen to his other finds, announced Makemake and Eris two days later. The same team had found [Sedna](/astrology/sedna/) in 2003. Makemake was the first body named under the IAU's new procedure for likely dwarf planets, and the fourth announced as one, after Ceres, Pluto and Eris.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

In the religion of the Rapa Nui of Easter Island, Makemake was the creator of humanity, the god of fertility and the chief god of the bird-man cult, which followed the era of the great stone statues. He was said to drive the sea birds to nest on the islet of Motu Nui, and the bird-man ceremony turned on the first sooty-tern egg: chosen swimmers crossed to the islet to find it and carry it back to Orongo, on the main island. His face, with its large eyes, is a frequent subject of the island's rock carvings.

The name had nothing to do with the god's nature. The body's known features didn't suggest any myth, so Brown looked for a name that kept the Easter link and met the IAU's rule that the classical bodies of the Kuiper belt take the names of creator gods. He thought of the Anglo-Saxon goddess Ēostre and of Manabozho, a trickster rabbit of the Anishinaabe, and found neither usable. Makemake, god of the island that Europeans first reached on Easter Sunday 1722, met both conditions. Its symbol, designed by Denis Moskowitz and John T. Whelan, is the god's carved face, drawn to look like an M, and it is used mostly by astrologers.

In a chart, Makemake is among the slowest bodies anyone places. With 307 years to a circuit, and its farthest point close, it spends decades in a sign, so its sign marks a generation rather than a person ([Personal, social and generational planets](/astrology/personal-social-and-generational-planets/)). Only its house, which needs a birth time ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and a tight contact could ever make it personal.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Makemake's name was picked to fit a calendar, and that is the clearest reason I can give for leaving it out. A body named after a fertility god because it was found near Easter can't tell anyone about fertility: nothing in a chart predicts a pregnancy, and I never read one for that ([Can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/)).

It was also never tested. My notebook went through the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign, each planet, Chiron and Lilith, and the three that stood out, Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, held 75 to 90% of the time ([The three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)). A dwarf planet named in 2008 had no line beside them, and the system behind my readings doesn't compute it ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Makemake is named after a Greek god, like the planets.”** Its name comes from the religion of the Rapa Nui of Easter Island.
- **“It was found by chance at Easter.”** It turned up in a planned search for large bodies beyond Neptune; Easter gave it only its nickname, and in the end its name.
- **“Makemake was found late because it's faint.”** It is the second brightest body beyond Neptune in our sky, after Pluto. Its tilted orbit kept it outside the band most earlier surveys searched.
