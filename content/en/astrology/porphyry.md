Porphyry is what you get if you keep the four angles and share out the zodiac evenly between them: each quarter of the chart, cut into three equal slices. It's the simplest of the quadrant systems, and the oldest, older than the man it's named after.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Porphyry is a quadrant system, like Placidus and Koch: the four angles are fixed, and the other houses are found between them. It takes its name from Porphyry of Tyre (c. 234–c. 305), the Neoplatonic philosopher who edited the works of his teacher Plotinus and wrote an introduction to Ptolemy's *Tetrabiblos*. But the method is older than he is. The astrologer [Vettius Valens](/astrology/vettius-valens/) had already described it in the 2nd century, in the third book of his *Anthology*, which makes it the oldest quadrant method on record. Valens counted houses by whole sign too ([Whole Sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/)), and treated the count by degree as the more exact of the two.

Later systems kept the quadrants and divided them in other ways. [Alcabitius](/astrology/alcabitius/), which largely took Porphyry's place, divides time: the time the Ascendant's degree takes to climb to the meridian, cut into three. [Regiomontanus](/astrology/regiomontanus/), designed by the 15th-century German astronomer whose name it carries, divides the celestial equator into twelve equal parts and projects them onto the zodiac. It became one of the most popular systems in Europe until Placidus largely replaced it, and horary astrologers still commonly use it, following [William Lilly](/astrology/william-lilly/) ([horary astrology](/astrology/horary-astrology/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) opens the 1st house, the IC the 4th, the Descendant the 7th and the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) the 10th, so the horizon and the meridian cut the chart into four quarters. Porphyry measures each quarter along the zodiac and divides it into three equal parts.

The quarters are rarely 90° each, because the zodiac rises at a slant. Take a birth in London with about 28° Capricorn on the Midheaven: the Ascendant is then about 29° Taurus, and the quarter between them spans about 122°. Porphyry gives the 10th, 11th and 12th houses about 40° each, and the next quarter, only 58° wide, three houses of under 20°. Here Aquarius sits wholly inside the 10th house and Leo inside the 4th, so Porphyry intercepts signs too ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)).

Porphyry is to the quadrants what [equal houses](/astrology/equal-houses/) are to the whole circle. Both share out zodiac degrees evenly; equal houses do it from the Ascendant alone and let the Midheaven float, Porphyry does it between all four angles and keeps each on a cusp. Because it needs nothing but the angles, it can be drawn wherever an Ascendant and a Midheaven can be found, including the latitudes where Placidus and Koch give up.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I use [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/). Porphyry and Placidus agree on what matters most to me, the four angles on the four angular cusps, and part on the houses in between: Porphyry divides degrees, Placidus divides time. The gap can be wide. In the London chart above, Porphyry's 11th house begins at about 8° Pisces and Placidus's at about 21° Aquarius, so a planet at 2° Pisces sits in the 10th house in one system and the 11th in the other. That's why I always say which system a reading uses ([which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)).

Porphyry's strengths are its simplicity, its long history and the fact that it still works at high latitudes. Like every house system, it needs the birth time: the Midheaven moves about a degree every four minutes, and every cusp moves with it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Every house page on this site is written for Placidus ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Porphyry invented the Porphyry system.”** Vettius Valens had described it in the 2nd century, before Porphyry was born.
- **“Porphyry houses are equal houses.”** They're equal only within each quarter. From one quarter to the next they can differ a great deal: in London, 40° against under 20°.
- **“Like Placidus, it fails near the poles.”** It needs only the four angles, so it can be drawn wherever they exist.
