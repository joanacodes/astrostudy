Knowing which planet rules your chart is the first step. The next is asking how that planet is doing: whether it stands where it works in its own manner, where it has to translate, or where it is hard to see at all. Astrologers call that its condition.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Your chart ruler is the planet that rules your rising sign ([chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/)). Its condition is everything tradition looked at to judge how well placed a planet is, applied to the one planet that stands for you.

Tradition measured it two ways. Essential dignity comes from the sign and the degree: a planet in its own sign ([domicile](/astrology/domicile/)) or [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/), or in a sign of its own triplicity, term or face, has dignity; one opposite its home is in [detriment](/astrology/detriment/), one opposite its exaltation in [fall](/astrology/fall/), and one with none of these is peregrine ([peregrine planets](/astrology/peregrine-planets/)). Accidental dignity comes from the planet's place in this particular chart: how near it is to the angles, which planets it meets, which aspects it receives. William Lilly put the essential dignities in order, sign first and exaltation second; the Hellenistic astrologers before him, Ptolemy and Vettius Valens among them, gave sign, exaltation, triplicity and term about equal weight.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

I go through the chart ruler the way I go through any planet, with a short list.

- **Its sign.** Which dignity or debility it has there ([the table of dignities](/astrology/table-of-dignities/)). Venus, ruling a Libra-rising chart, is at home in Libra or Taurus, exalted in Pisces, in its fall in Virgo, in detriment in Aries or Scorpio.
- **Its house.** In an angular house, the 1st, 4th, 7th or 10th, a planet has its loudest voice; in a cadent one, the 3rd, 6th, 9th or 12th, a quieter one ([angularity](/astrology/angularity/)). The old books count a ruler in the 12th as poorly placed by accident, whatever its sign.
- **The Sun's light.** A planet close to the Sun is lost in its glare, which tradition calls combust ([combustion](/astrology/combustion/)). Within about 17′ of the Sun's centre, in its heart, the verdict reverses: that planet is [cazimi](/astrology/cazimi/), and counted strong.
- **Its motion.** Whether it moves direct or retrograde ([retrograde, explained](/astrology/retrograde-explained/)), quickly or slowly.
- **Its sect.** Whether it belongs to the chart's half of the day: the Sun, Jupiter and Saturn to a day chart, the Moon, Venus and Mars to a night chart, Mercury to either, depending on where it stands from the Sun ([sect condition](/astrology/sect-condition/)).
- **Its aspects and its host.** The planets that aspect it, a malefic's square counting against it in the old books, and the ruler of the sign it stands in, which lends it support or can't ([dispositors and the final dispositor](/astrology/dispositors-and-the-final-dispositor/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Condition tells me how easily the chart ruler's function works, not how well a life will go. I sort the list into three questions. How easily does it work? That's its sign. How visible is it? That's its house and the Sun's light. How much support does it have? That's its sect, its aspects and its host. A ruler can do badly on one and well on the others, and the combination is the reading: Mars in its fall in Cancer, ruling an Aries-rising chart, but angular, direct and well received, describes drive that takes effort to feel natural and still gets seen.

With Scorpio, Aquarius or Pisces rising, both rulers get the list, though the old tables say nothing about Pluto, Uranus or Neptune: they have no traditional exaltation or fall. Most of the dignity reading falls to Mars, Saturn or Jupiter, and the slow ruler speaks mainly through its house.

Two cautions. Everything here hangs on the rising sign, and the rising sign hangs on the birth time: a time rounded to the hour can hand you another ruler, in another condition ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). And in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the rising sign held about a third of the time, so I never build a reading on its ruler's condition alone.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A chart ruler in detriment or fall means a hard life.”** It describes a function working in a manner that isn't its own. Condition is ease and support, never a fate.
- **“A dignified chart ruler guarantees success.”** Comfort in a sign isn't fortune. A ruler at home can still sit in a quiet house or lie hidden in the Sun's light.
- **“Combust and cazimi mean the same thing.”** Tradition treated them as opposites: near the Sun, a planet is hidden; at its very heart, it is strengthened.
- **“Condition comes down to one score.”** Some astrologers add up points for each dignity and debility. A total hides which questions the planet answers well and which badly, so I read the factors one by one.
