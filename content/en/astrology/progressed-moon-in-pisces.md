Pisces is the last chapter of the progressed Moon’s round, the twelfth sign before Aries begins the next. For two years or so, what restores you tends to be quiet: time alone, music, water or a long walk, the company of people with whom you needn’t explain yourself.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[The progressed Moon](/astrology/progressed-moon/) colours each chapter of your [secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/), and in [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/) the colour softens. Its two rulers, both of which I read, are Jupiter for the tradition and Neptune for the moderns; the sign itself is mutable water. Needs in these years turn towards compassion, imagination and rest: feeling with others more easily, sensing what can’t be put into words, wanting time when nothing is asked of you. The edges of things can feel less fixed, and that is the chapter’s gift as well as its demand. Dreams may be more vivid, or simply better remembered, and intuition louder than argument; I take both as information to weigh, not as instructions.

Softness isn’t the same as having no edges. A Pisces chapter goes best when you can feel deeply without taking on everyone else’s weather; kindness to others includes kindness to yourself, and a boundary set gently is still a boundary. It may show as a creative or spiritual practice taken up, a habit let go of, a forgiveness you didn’t expect to find, or simply longer evenings and more dreams.

Because Pisces is the last sign, the chapter often carries a feeling of completion: things that have run their course can be let go, so that Aries finds the ground clear. Where all this happens depends on the house the progressed Moon crosses, and that comes from [your birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/): in Placidus, the system I use, I place the cusps from the exact time, and I don’t read a chart without it.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

In my count, for births from 1930 to 2009, the progressed Moon took between two and two and a half years to cross Pisces, usually about two years and four months, each year standing for a day of the real Moon’s journey after the birth. However timeless the chapter can feel, it ends on schedule, at the first degree of Aries. Because Pisces leads straight into Aries, its last months can carry two moods at once: the wish to rest, and the first stirrings of the next start.

It comes after the [progressed Moon in Aquarius](/astrology/progressed-moon-in-aquarius/) and closes the round, with the [progressed Moon in Aries](/astrology/progressed-moon-in-aries/) waiting beyond it. The whole round takes about 27 years, so the end of a Pisces chapter marks the end of a long cycle of feeling: a good moment to notice what the past twelve chapters taught you, before the next one begins. With a natal [Moon in Pisces](/astrology/moon-in-pisces/), this quiet is your own lifelong weather, met again for a while.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Protect some solitude. A Pisces chapter refills in quiet: an hour with music, a walk by water, a page of writing, a space where no one needs anything from you. If you look after others, let someone look after you as well. Water, music and images speak to Pisces more directly than plans do, so let some of your comfort be wordless.

Give the imagination something to hold, a creative practice, a spiritual one, or simply a notebook for dreams. Let endings be endings, gently, and don’t hurry to fill the space they leave; the new round will want room to begin. And if a low mood lingers, reach for a person rather than a chart: a friend, a doctor, someone trained to help.
