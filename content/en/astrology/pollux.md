Pollux is the brighter of the Twins, an orange giant 34 light-years away, the nearest giant star to the Sun. It gives its name to the immortal brother of the myth, and although it belongs to the constellation Gemini, in the tropical zodiac it stands in Cancer.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Pollux is β Geminorum, magnitude 1.14 in the Yale catalogue: the brightest star of the constellation, ahead of [Castor](/astrology/castor/), which Johann Bayer lettered alpha in 1603 without carefully telling the two apart. Pollux began as a hotter white star and, its core hydrogen spent, has swollen to almost nine times the Sun's radius. A planet announced around it in 2006, later named Thestias, is still disputed.

In the zodiac this star of Gemini belongs to the next sign, [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/): 23°34′ for 2026, 23°12′ for 2000 and 22°14′ for 1930. In Ptolemy's day it was still in the sign of Gemini, at about 27°; the signs are counted from the March equinox, and the equinox has drifted back against the stars since then ([constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/)).

Its latitude is 6.7° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), just beyond the Moon's present reach. No occultation of Pollux by the Moon has been visible from Earth since 30 September 117 BCE, seen then from high southern latitudes. Planets that reach its degree pass several degrees south of it ([celestial latitude](/astrology/celestial-latitude/)).

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

In Greek and Roman myth Pollux and Castor were twin brothers, sons of Leda. Pollux was the son of Zeus and immortal, Castor the son of a mortal king, Tyndareus. When Castor died, Pollux asked Zeus to give his brother immortality, and the god set the two side by side in the sky. Shelley's translation of the Homeric hymn to the twins (1818) calls the star "mild Pollux, void of blame".

A seventeenth-century Arabic catalogue names Pollux the end of the paw, Posterior Brachii in its Latin translation. In China it is the third star of the North River, an asterism it shares with Castor and ρ Geminorum.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

For a birth in 2026 the tradition counts a planet or angle as conjunct Pollux between 22°34′ and 24°34′ Cancer ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). Mid-July birthdays, about 15 and 16 July, put a [Sun in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-cancer/) there. The window moves with the year of birth: for 1930 it ran from 21°14′ to 23°14′ Cancer, so a planet at 23°30′ in a chart from that year missed the star that the same degree meets today.

Castor stands 2°58′ earlier, at 20°36′ Cancer: too far apart for one planet to be within a degree of both. [Orion's belt](/astrology/orions-belt/), a sign earlier, shows by contrast how near Pollux is to the planets' road: the belt's stars lie some 24° south of the ecliptic, Pollux less than 7° north.

The myth makes a tempting picture of a brother's loyalty, but it is a story about two stars, not a quality handed to whoever has a planet at 23° Cancer. My readings don't include fixed stars, so the immortal twin stays here, described rather than read ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).
