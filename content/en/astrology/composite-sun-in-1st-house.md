Some couples, friends or business partners arrive as a unit: people remember the pair before they remember either name. A composite Sun in the 1st house is the chart's way of describing that, and it says the relationship wears its purpose on the outside.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) of [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) begins at the composite [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), and there are two ways to find that point: as the midpoint of your two natal Ascendants, or worked out from the composite Midheaven for the latitude where the two of you live. Both need both birth times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and they don't always agree, so a [Sun](/astrology/sun/) close to the Ascendant can be in the 1st by one method and in the 12th by the other, which tells a very different story ([composite Sun in the 12th house](/astrology/composite-sun-in-12th-house/)).

Presence is what the 1st house governs, and with the relationship's Sun in it, that presence is the bond's: how it looks, moves and starts things. A pair with the composite Sun here tends to be known together, introduced together, remembered together. Its purpose is visible in its manner, in the way the two of you turn up at a door, divide a task or make a decision in public. Its vitality shows on the surface too, so when the relationship is tired, other people notice. The composite Sun's sign colours that presence: in Aries, the pair tends to arrive already moving ([composite Sun in Aries](/astrology/composite-sun-in-aries/)); in Libra, as two people visibly in step.

The 1st is an angular house, the most prominent kind in tradition ([angular, succedent and cadent houses](/astrology/angular-succedent-and-cadent-houses/)); its natural sign is Aries, where the Sun is exalted, and it was the joy of Mercury rather than the Sun. In one person's chart, the [Sun in the 1st house](/astrology/sun-in-1st-house/) describes someone whose sense of self is on display. Seen across two charts instead, [their Sun in your 1st house](/astrology/sun-in-1st-house-synastry/) tells how one person brightens the way the other comes across. In a composite, the presence belongs to neither of you, and two shy people can make a bond that walks into a room first.

A bond this visible can come to depend on how it appears. The relationship may feel it has to look well in company, and a private difficulty can feel like a public one. It helps to keep part of the bond out of view: something the two of you do that nobody sees or comments on, and an agreement between you about what is shared with others and what isn't. Next along, the [composite Sun in the 2nd house](/astrology/composite-sun-in-2nd-house/) gathers a bond around what it holds and values rather than how it looks.

You won't find a composite in my own readings; I set out the technique the way its practitioners use it, next to both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). Visibility isn't a forecast, and nothing in this placement says how long a relationship lasts or how it fares. A bond seen as a pair should be read as one, too, with both people aware and willing ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
