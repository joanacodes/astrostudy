Rasalhague marks the head of the Serpent-bearer, the brightest star of Ophiuchus, the constellation behind the "thirteenth sign" headlines. In the zodiac it sits at 22°48′ Sagittarius, but nearly 36° north of the Sun's path, where nothing in a birth chart can ever come close to it.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

At magnitude 2.08, α Ophiuchi really is the brightest star of its constellation, unlike [Sadalmelik](/astrology/sadalmelik/), the alpha of Aquarius, which its beta outshines. It is a pair of stars 48.6 light-years away, circling each other every 8.6 years, and the brighter of the two spins so fast, close to the speed that would tear it apart, that it bulges at the equator.

Its tropical degree has moved from 22°26′ [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/) in 2000 to 22°48′ in 2026, and here sign and constellation part company: Ophiuchus straddles the celestial equator, the Sun crosses its southern part every year from about 30 November to 18 December, and the zodiac goes on calling that stretch Sagittarius, because its signs follow the seasons ([Ophiuchus and the 13th sign](/astrology/ophiuchus-and-the-13th-sign/)).

Its latitude, 35.8° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), matters more than its degree. The Moon never strays more than about 5° from that line and even Pluto, the furthest wanderer, stays within about 17°, so nothing in a birth chart ever comes near Rasalhague in the sky ([celestial latitude](/astrology/celestial-latitude/)).

Five degrees to the west-north-west shines [Ras Algethi](/astrology/ras-algethi/), the head of the Kneeler, now called Hercules: two heads of two figures, almost touching.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

The name is Arabic, ra’s al-ḥawwā’, the head of the serpent collector, or charmer: Ophiuchus is a man grasping a great snake, the constellation Serpens. In Roman-era mythography the figure became Asclepius, the healer who learned to keep death at bay by watching one serpent bring another healing herbs. Jupiter struck him down so that the whole human race wouldn't become immortal, then set his image among the stars to honour his work.

The two heads are already neighbours in Aratus's *Phaenomena*: by the head of the kneeling figure, he writes, "mark near at hand the head of Ophiuchus". In Chinese astronomy Rasalhague is Hòu, a senior assistant to the emperor, sometimes said to be an astrologer, an unusual post for a star to hold.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun crosses Rasalhague's degree on about 13 and 14 December, when it is standing in front of the constellation Ophiuchus itself, but some 36° south of the Serpent-bearer's head: a [Sun in Sagittarius](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius/) birthday, whatever the constellation. Any planet early in the third decan of Sagittarius, from 21°48′ to 23°48′ in 2026, meets the star the same way, by longitude alone ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)).

The slowest planets turn the contact into a generation's. Neptune was on its degree for births in much of 1980 and 1981 ([Neptune in Sagittarius](/astrology/neptune-in-sagittarius/)), and Pluto in parts of 2004 and 2005 ([Pluto in Sagittarius](/astrology/pluto-in-sagittarius/)).

With a star this far north, some astrologers drop the degree and look at the sky instead: they ask whether Rasalhague rose, culminated or set at the same moment as a planet, at the place of birth ([parans](/astrology/parans/)). That follows the real sky, but it needs the birth time and place exactly.
