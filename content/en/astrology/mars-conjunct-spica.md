Mars conjunct Spica is the commonest contact this star has: about 1 person in 155 born between 1930 and 2009 has Mars within a degree of the ear of wheat, late in Libra. Mars owes that to a habit of slowing down nearby. Three times in those eighty years it stopped and turned within a couple of degrees of the star.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read [Mars](/astrology/mars/) as effort with a direction: what you go after, how hard you push, where you draw a line and keep it. [Spica](/astrology/spica/) gives Mars an unexpected word. An Arabic name for it is al-simāk al-aʽzal, the unarmed simāk, while its neighbour [Arcturus](/astrology/arcturus/), at almost the same zodiac degree but some thirty degrees further north, was the simāk of the lancer. At this point of the zodiac, the lance went to the other star.

So the planet named after the god of war sits, here, on the unarmed one. I like that better than any verdict the old books could hand down, because Mars, as I read it, was never about weapons. It is the force behind effort and initiative, and it can push very hard with empty hands.

The sign agrees. The star's degree, 22°51′ Libra in 1930 and 24°12′ in 2026, makes this a [Mars in Libra](/astrology/mars-in-libra/), which the tradition counts as Mars's [detriment](/astrology/detriment/), opposite Aries, its own sign. That describes effort that runs through relationships: you act best alongside someone, argue for what is fair, and may need time before putting your own wishes first.

The turns are what make the contact common ([Mars retrograde in Libra](/astrology/mars-retrograde-in-libra/), [natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)). In 1935 Mars crossed the degree on 11 February, stopped on 28 February less than two degrees beyond it, came back over it on 16 March and crossed for the last time on 17 July. In 1967 it crossed on 23 January, returned on 20 April, five days after standing opposite the Sun, and left on 5 July. In 1999 it crossed on 13 January and went on into Scorpio, then came back and stood still on 4 June about half a degree short of the star, so births from late May to mid-June 1999 have Mars nearly motionless on its degree. Each turn came 32 years after the last.

Spica sits so close to the Sun's path that Mars can come near it in the sky as well: in July 1967 the two were about a degree and a quarter apart, and in July 1935 hardly more. On those summer evenings the red planet and the blue-white star stood side by side in the south-western sky after dark.

Only one other body shares Mars's habit of turning near this star: [Saturn conjunct Spica](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-spica/), which stood still within three degrees of it in 1953 and 1982. The [Sun conjunct Spica](/astrology/sun-conjunct-spica/) and the [Moon conjunct Spica](/astrology/moon-conjunct-spica/) never go backwards at all, and [Venus conjunct Spica](/astrology/venus-conjunct-spica/) never did so on this degree between 1930 and 2009.

Spica, like every star, stays out of the charts I read. Mars in Libra, its house and its aspects carry the reading, and [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/) explains how astrologers who do use stars count a contact like this one.
