Somewhere between your 28th and 30th birthdays, Saturn comes back to the degree it held when you were born. Astrologers call it the Saturn return, and no other transit has such a loud reputation. I read it as something quieter and more useful: a review of what you've built, checked against what you actually want to carry.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) takes about 29.4 years to go round the Sun, and so about as long to go round the zodiac. When it reaches its birth position again, it closes one full cycle and starts the next. For everyone born between 1930 and 2009, the first exact pass came between the ages of 28.4 and 29.9: the date shifts with Saturn's speed in that part of its orbit, and with where its retrograde loops fall.

Those loops matter. Every year Saturn seems to back up for a few months, so it can cross your natal degree once, or three times: forward, back, then forward again. Born on 1 January 1990, you'd have met it three times, at about 29.1, 29.6 and 29.8; born on 1 January 1995, just once, at about 29.1. The second return follows one cycle later, around 58 to 60, and a long life may see a third.

The return isn't a single event in the cycle but its main milestone. On the way round, Saturn squares its birth place, opposes it, then squares it again in your early twenties ([Saturn squares and opposition](/astrology/saturn-squares-and-opposition/)), and each step reads as a checkpoint on the same long project.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A return adds nothing new to a chart. It brings Saturn's function, structure, responsibility, limits, long-term work, back to the exact place where your chart first set it, and asks what you've made of it. So the return is read through your natal Saturn: its sign (the manner of the lesson), its house (the area of life where it sits, which needs your birth time) and its aspects.

That last part is easy to miss. When transiting Saturn stands on its birth degree, it makes exactly the same angles to your other planets as it did when you were born. Born with Saturn square your Moon, you'll have transiting Saturn square your Moon at your return; born with Saturn trine your Sun, the trine comes back too. Every natal Saturn aspect gets its turn, which is one reason two returns at the same age can feel nothing alike.

With the orbs I use for timing, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, a Saturn transit to one point usually lasts about nine months, and sometimes only about three weeks when Saturn passes straight over. Some people count the whole time Saturn spends in its birth sign, about two and a half years, which is why you'll hear of Saturn returns that seem to last forever.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The Saturn return has a reputation as a crisis. In readings it looks more like an audit. Commitments you chose tend to deepen; ones you took on to please someone else, or because it was expected, tend to show their weight. Some people change course. Many simply settle into what they already have, with more conviction. None of that is predetermined, and nothing in the return guarantees a loss, a wedding or a breakthrough.

I give it particular care for one reason: natal Saturn was one of [the three placements that held best](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/) in my notebook, 75 to 90% of the time. So when I read a return, I'm reading the part of your chart I trust most, coming back for review. I read it gently, as a season, and alongside everything else that is moving at the same time: [transits](/astrology/transits/) from the other slow planets, progressions, and the return chart of the year. Weather, not a forecast.

Saturn isn't the only body that comes home. Chiron's orbit takes about 50 years, so its return falls around 50 ([Chiron return](/astrology/chiron-return/)), and Jupiter comes back about every twelve years ([Jupiter return](/astrology/jupiter-return/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“At 29, everything falls apart.”** It's a review, not a demolition. What you built on your own terms tends to hold.
- **“If you did the work, nothing will happen.”** Saturn's return still comes round; it just tends to feel like confirmation rather than correction.
- **“It means you'll marry, divorce or have a child.”** No transit decides those things. A return describes a theme of responsibility and structure, never a set event.
- **“Everyone your age goes through the same return.”** The age is shared; the house, aspects and life it lands in are yours.
