Continuity is the centre of a relationship whose composite Sun is in Taurus. Fixed earth, ruled by Venus, describes how the bond the two of you make tends to grow: steadily, by accumulation, around whatever you both decide is worth keeping.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[Taurus](/astrology/taurus/) brings one question to whatever it touches: how does this become durable? In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), where every planet stands at the [midpoint](/astrology/midpoints/) of its two natal places, that question is put to the relationship's [Sun](/astrology/sun/), and it becomes the bond's identity. It's held by about 1 pair in 12. Close to either edge of the sign, the birth times matter: a midpoint just short of 0° Taurus belongs to the [composite Sun in Aries](/astrology/composite-sun-in-aries/), one just past 0° Gemini to the [composite Sun in Gemini](/astrology/composite-sun-in-gemini/), and the hours of the two births can shift it by up to about a degree.

Its identity lives in what it has kept going: the walk you always take on Sundays, the kitchen where most of your conversations happen, the plan you add to a little each year. Repetition restores it: the familiar meal, the evening that goes the way those evenings go. And what it's for is plain enough: something the two of you can stand on, built at a pace both of you can keep. Set beside the two birth charts, the habits the bond is made of often turn out to belong to neither person's past: a third set, made of pieces of both.

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) rules Taurus, and here the composite does something no birth chart can. When one person is born with the [Sun in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-taurus/), Venus keeps close: within about 47°, so in one of the five signs from Pisces to Cancer. A composite keeps no such company. Venus's midpoint is found without reference to the Sun's, and about 1 pair in 10 ends up with the two more than 47° apart. If your composite Venus sits in Libra or Scorpio, that's arithmetic rather than a message; its sign and house still show what the bond values and where it finds pleasure.

Durability is a manner here, never a forecast: a Taurus composite Sun says how a relationship builds, and nothing about how long it will stand or how it will fare. Change is where it's tested. The bond's sense of itself lives in its routines, so a move or a new job asks it to rebuild them, and for a while the relationship can feel unfamiliar to you both. Carrying one ritual through the change untouched helps: a Sunday walk can survive a new city.

I describe the technique without drawing composites in my own work, and the astrologers who use it read it with both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), never alone. A composite is built from two people's birth data, and that data belongs to each of them like anything else of value: it's used only when both have offered it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
