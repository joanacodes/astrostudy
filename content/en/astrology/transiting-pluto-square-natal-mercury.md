Pluto square your Mercury puts pressure on the way you think. For two to three years, the reasoning you rely on, the explanations you give yourself and the way you put things meet a question they can't settle, and part of your thinking is rebuilt.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) pushes what has become unsustainable towards reorganisation. Your [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) processes what you meet, interprets it and puts it into words. A [square](/astrology/square/), 90°, relates them through friction that makes you adapt, and here what has to adapt is your reasoning: under this [transit](/astrology/transits/), thinking stops being a tool you use without noticing and becomes a problem in its own right. A belief no longer explains what you see. An argument you thought was finished comes back. A choice refuses to settle however many lists you make.

Changing your mind can feel like being caught out, especially in front of other people. It isn't humiliation. Being able to say "I was wrong about that" is one of the things this square builds, and a mind that can say it is easier to trust.

Analysis isn't overthinking, but under this square the two can blur. When the same thought has run round for days, more analysis rarely helps; sleep, a walk or saying it aloud to someone does more. If thoughts loop for weeks, keep you awake or begin to frighten you, a doctor is the person to tell, and no transit is a reason to put that off ([Astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/)).

Where the trine ([Transiting Pluto trine natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-pluto-trine-natal-mercury/)) lets a change of mind come easily and the sextile ([Transiting Pluto sextile natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-pluto-sextile-natal-mercury/)) offers one, the square insists. Unlike the opposition ([Transiting Pluto opposite natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-pluto-opposite-natal-mercury/)), it needn't come through another person: often it is a problem your usual reasoning can't crack. And unlike the conjunction ([Transiting Pluto conjunct natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-pluto-conjunct-natal-mercury/)), it leaves you enough distance to watch your thinking change. In a birth chart, [Mercury square Pluto](/astrology/mercury-square-pluto/) makes this friction a lifelong habit of mind; the transit lends it to everyone else for a few years.

With Mercury in the 10th and Pluto crossing the 1st ([Pluto transiting the 1st house](/astrology/pluto-transit-1st-house/)), the way you speak and decide at work rubs against a changing sense of who you are. Both houses come from your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

In my timing a transit counts from 1½° before exact to 1° after. On that measure the square lasts about two years and eight months. Three passes in five bring three exact hits, over about two years and seven months; the rest bring five, over about three years and seven months.

About seven people in eight born between 1930 and 2009 meet one of Mercury's two Pluto squares by 80, and about one in eight never does.

While Pluto is in Aquarius, until early 2044 ([Pluto in Aquarius, 2024–2044](/astrology/pluto-in-aquarius-2024-2044/)), it squares Mercurys in Taurus and Scorpio.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Argue the other side on paper. Writing the best case against your own view is the quickest way to find out which parts of it still stand.

Take hard conversations one at a time, and close each with what has been agreed, however small.

Sleep on any message written after midnight. Under this square, the morning version is usually the one you would rather have sent.
