Cancer is the Moon's own sign, and here it works without translation. Feelings run deep and remember a great deal, and the first question is always the same: is this a safe place, for me and for the people I look after?

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

A Moon in [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/) is in the one sign it rules, a cardinal water sign, and so in what tradition calls its domicile: the place where the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) is most itself. Everything the Moon does in a chart is turned up here rather than changed: taking in what happens, bonding, keeping memory, restoring safety. A room's mood reaches you before anyone has spoken, and your feelings come and go in tides rather than in flashes.

Domicile describes ease, not rank. The sign asks of the Moon exactly what the Moon already wants to give, so there's little friction between your needs and the way you go about meeting them. Cardinal signs start things, which is worth saying because Cancer is so often painted as shy: a Cancer Moon takes the initiative to protect. It moves first to make a home, to feed someone, to close ranks around the people it loves.

Because the Moon rules this sign, there's no other planet to pass the story to. With a Moon in Aries I'd turn next to Mars, with a Moon in Taurus to Venus; here the trail loops back, and the Moon's own house and aspects carry the whole weight. I read them closely.

Each time the Moon comes round, Cancer holds it for some two and a half days, enough for roughly 1 person in 12 to be born with it there. A little before, you might have the [Moon in Gemini](/astrology/moon-in-gemini/), which needs words before it can settle; a little after, the [Moon in Leo](/astrology/moon-in-leo/), which needs warmth and to be seen. If the change fell on your birthday, ask the clock ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You make a home of people. You remember how someone takes their tea, what frightened them as a child, which date is hard for them every year, and you use all of it to look after them. Closeness, for you, is shelter: a place to come back to where nobody has to perform. What you need in return is often harder to say than what you give. You may hope a partner will notice you're tired without being told, and feel hurt when they don't. Saying *I need looking after tonight* isn't a weakness in a Cancer Moon. It's the other half of the care you already give.

## At work {#at-work}

You work best where people feel like a team rather than a list of roles. You're often the colleague who notices who is struggling, keeps the birthday card going round, makes the new person feel at home. Work centred on care suits this Moon, as does any job where trust and continuity matter, and you can be fiercely protective of the people you manage. A cold, purely transactional workplace costs you more than you may admit: you'll do the job well and still feel homesick at your desk.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

A long memory works both ways. What keeps every kindness can keep every hurt, and an old slight can come back years later with its original sting. When you feel unsafe, you may close the door rather than say what's wrong, and hope someone knocks. And because looking after others comes so naturally, your own needs can end up last on a list you're always writing for everyone else.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Keep a real place of return, a room, a ritual, a person, for when the world is loud, and let it be a harbour rather than a fortress. Put yourself on the list of people you look after, by name. When an old hurt comes back, ask whether it belongs to now or to then. Say what you need in words, early, even when you hope it's obvious. And use your memory as a resource: recipes, photographs, letters, everything that reminds you of the care you've been given.

Being at home in its sign didn't buy the Moon a better result in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/). It went on the list like everything else; the ones that stood apart were Lilith, Chiron and Saturn, holding up far more often than anything else, and the list as a whole came out at about a third. So I read a Cancer Moon with care, alongside the rest of the chart, never instead of it.
