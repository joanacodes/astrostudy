Venus rules your Sun's sign and is exalted in your Moon's, so one planet is honoured on both sides of this pair. I read that thread as pleasure and beauty, felt first in the body and then in the imagination. The birth came late in the lunar month, the Moon thinning in the morning sky.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Earth and water, fixed and mutable, the Moon two signs behind the Sun: a sextile by sign, help that works when you take it up. The [Sun in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-taurus/) knows itself through the senses and through what lasts; the [Moon in Pisces](/astrology/moon-in-pisces/) absorbs the feeling of a room and settles through music, imagination, solitude and kindness. The Sun gives the Moon solid ground to come back to; the Moon gives the Sun a reason to look past what it already owns.

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) is close to a Taurus Sun by nature, and with the Sun early in Taurus it can even stand in Pisces, its exaltation, in your Moon's sign. Two planets speak for your Moon, and I read both: [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/), which modern astrologers gave to Pisces, for where you dream and lose yourself in what you love; and [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/), its ruler before Neptune was found in 1846, for where all that feeling finds a meaning. The lights themselves gain nothing from their signs here; the honour goes to Venus.

Between the last quarter and the next new moon, the Moon was 31° to 90° behind the Sun, some twenty-two to twenty-seven days into the month. In the eight-phase count, the [last quarter moon](/astrology/last-quarter-moon/) claims the great majority of these births, and the [balsamic moon](/astrology/balsamic-moon/) only the latest few. If the gap was about 60°, the sextile of signs is also an exact waning [Sun sextile Moon](/astrology/sun-sextile-moon/). The last quarter, as I read it, asks what to keep and what to release, the very question a Taurus Sun finds hard and a Pisces Moon finds easy. [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) sets out where each phase begins.

This combination belongs to roughly 1 person in 138. Pisces is the last sign the Moon crosses before Aries opens the round again, a stay of about two and a half days; a birth on the day it crossed in from Aquarius, or out into Aries, may instead belong to the [Sun in Taurus, Moon in Aquarius](/astrology/sun-in-taurus-moon-in-aquarius/) or the [Sun in Taurus, Moon in Aries](/astrology/sun-in-taurus-moon-in-aries/), and the time settles it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). That time also yields the rising sign: with it the two lights become an outline, and the houses and angles add the shading.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You love tenderly and steadily: the Taurus Sun with steadiness and a warm, physical kind of care, the Pisces Moon with intuition, forgiveness and a sense of what someone needs before they ask. The risk lies in loyalty and compassion working together. The Sun doesn't like to leave, the Moon doesn't like to hurt, and between them they can keep you giving to a bond that no longer gives back. A partner who says plainly what they feel, so you don't have to absorb it and guess, lets both lights rest.

## At work {#at-work}

Work that joins craft to imagination suits you: music, food, gardens, design, or caring work done with patience. The Sun wants something tangible to show; the Moon wants the work to mean something to someone. Noisy, aggressive workplaces drain the Pisces Moon, and a role without a visible result leaves the Taurus Sun unsure what it's for. A calm place, a steady rhythm and some solitude each day suit both.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The pair can take in a great deal and let very little go. The Pisces Moon absorbs other people's moods; the Taurus Sun is slow to release whatever it has taken in, so a sadness picked up on Monday may still be sitting with you on Friday. What helps is giving the Moon a way to release and the Sun a way to ground the release: a walk in the rain, an hour in the garden, music played rather than only heard, anything that lets the day's moods pass through the body and out.
