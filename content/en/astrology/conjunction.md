A conjunction is the easiest aspect to spot: two planets side by side, in the same part of the sky and so in the same part of your chart. The other aspects link planets across a distance. This one fuses them, and everything depends on who is meeting whom.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

A conjunction is an angle of 0°: two planets at the same degree of the zodiac, or close to it. They usually share a sign, and with it an element and a modality, and often a house as well. I read it as two voices that can no longer be heard apart. The Moon beside Saturn doesn't feel first and hold back afterwards: the holding back is part of the feeling, from the start. In the sky, the Sun and Moon make one every month, at the [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/).

How close is close? Astrologers allow a few degrees either side of exact, more when the Sun or the Moon is involved; the page on [orbs](/astrology/orbs/) goes into the detail. The tighter the meeting, the louder it speaks. It also matters whether the faster planet is still closing in or already moving away: many astrologers feel an [applying aspect](/astrology/applying-and-separating-aspects/) as building and a separating one as easing. And near the end of a sign, a conjunction can straddle two signs: a planet at 29° of one and another at 1° of the next are only two degrees apart. That's an [out-of-sign aspect](/astrology/out-of-sign-aspects/), still a meeting, but between two signs with different tempers.

Among the five major aspects, the conjunction is the odd one out. Ptolemy counted four: the [sextile](/astrology/sextile/), the [square](/astrology/square/), the [trine](/astrology/trine/) and the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/). The conjunction is a meeting rather than one of his four, though modern astrologers list it first. It also slips through the usual sorting into [hard and soft aspects](/astrology/hard-and-soft-aspects/): the trine and sextile are called soft, the square and opposition hard, and the conjunction is as easy or as demanding as the planets in it. Venus with Jupiter is rarely a struggle; Mars with Saturn is rarely a rest. When several planets crowd into one sign or house, astrologers speak of a [stellium](/astrology/stellium/): a part of life the chart keeps coming back to.

## Between which planets {#between-which-planets}

Any two planets can meet. These are the pairs people look up most:

- **[Sun conjunct Moon](/astrology/sun-conjunct-moon/):** born at a new moon; what you want and what you need pull the same way.
- **[Sun conjunct Mercury](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mercury/):** Mercury never strays more than 28° from the Sun, so the two often travel together; your thinking and your sense of self are hard to pull apart.
- **[Sun conjunct Venus](/astrology/sun-conjunct-venus/):** Venus never strays more than 47° from the Sun, so this is the only major aspect the two can make; warmth and taste sit close to who you are.
- **[Moon conjunct Saturn](/astrology/moon-conjunct-saturn/):** feelings handled with care, often from early on; a steadiness that learns, later, to soften.
- **[Venus conjunct Mars](/astrology/venus-conjunct-mars/):** affection and desire in the same gesture; what you love and what you go after are hard to tell apart.
- **[Sun conjunct Saturn](/astrology/sun-conjunct-saturn/):** a sense of self built slowly, under some weight; confidence that arrives late, and stays.
- **[Moon conjunct Pluto](/astrology/moon-conjunct-pluto/):** feelings that run deep and rarely by halves; trust given whole or not at all.
- **[Venus conjunct Jupiter](/astrology/venus-conjunct-jupiter/):** generosity in love and in pleasure; the only risk is too much of a good thing.
- **[Mercury conjunct Saturn](/astrology/mercury-conjunct-saturn/):** a careful, serious mind; words weighed before they're spoken.
- **[Moon conjunct Chiron](/astrology/moon-conjunct-chiron/):** a tender spot right where comfort should be; often, a gift for comforting others.

With the Sun, tradition has words of its own. A planet very close to it is called [combust](/astrology/combustion/), its light lost in the Sun's; one right at the Sun's heart is [cazimi](/astrology/cazimi/), and read instead as strengthened. Mercury and Venus never stray far from the Sun, so the question comes up often.

A word on my own tests. [My notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) counted placements, each planet in its sign and house, so it gives no figure for aspects. But Saturn and Chiron were two of the three placements that held 75 to 90% of the time, so when either one is half of a conjunction, I slow down.

## The conjunction in synastry {#in-synastry}

In [synastry](/synastry/), a conjunction means one person's planet sits on the other's. It's often the contact people feel first, because there's no distance in it: whatever that planet does, it does right on top of yours. [One person's Sun on the other's Moon](/astrology/sun-conjunct-moon-synastry/) is the classic — purpose meeting need — and it tends to feel like recognition. [Venus on Mars](/astrology/venus-conjunct-mars-synastry/) is attraction that's hard to miss. [Saturn on the Moon](/astrology/moon-conjunct-saturn-synastry/) can steady one person and weigh on the other, depending on how each takes structure. I always read a conjunction in both directions: the person whose Moon is touched doesn't live it the way the person whose Saturn is touching does. And I read two charts only when both people have agreed: [why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/).

## The conjunction in transits {#in-transits}

A [transit](/astrology/transits/) is a planet moving through today's sky and meeting a point of your birth chart. The conjunction is the most direct transit there is: the moving planet passes right over your natal one. The fast planets do it all the time — the Moon crosses every point of your chart every 27⅓ days, the Sun once a year — and those meetings last a few hours, or a day or two. The slow planets come rarely and stay: [transiting Saturn conjunct your natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-saturn-conjunct-natal-sun/) or [your natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-saturn-conjunct-natal-moon/), [Pluto on your natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-pluto-conjunct-natal-moon/), [Jupiter on your natal Venus](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-conjunct-natal-venus/). Because of retrograde motion, a slow planet can cross the same degree three times, forward, back and forward again, so one conjunction can stay with you for months.

Some conjunctions keep a timetable. Every year, around your birthday, the Sun comes back to its birth degree: your [solar return](/astrology/solar-return/). Jupiter comes back to its own place about every 12 years, the [Jupiter return](/astrology/jupiter-return/), and Saturn between about 27 and 30, the [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/). I read them all the same way: as weather, not a forecast. A transit describes the season you're in; it doesn't decide what happens in it.
