Walk into a party with this pair and the room seems to get bigger: more introductions, more invitations, more plans for next month. Composite Jupiter in the 1st house makes generosity the relationship's first impression.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

The 1st house begins at the Ascendant, and in [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) that angle is the hardest point to pin down. Drawn from the midpoints of the two charts' cusps, the composite Ascendant lies halfway between your two natal Ascendants. Drawn from the composite Midheaven, it is the Ascendant that goes with that Midheaven at the latitude where you live, in whichever house system the astrologer prefers; mine, for birth charts, is [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/). The two versions can differ by several degrees or more, so a [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) near the start of the composite [1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) may belong to the 12th by the other method. The Ascendant turns through the whole zodiac in a day, so a 1st-house Jupiter depends on both birth times more than on anything else, and it is never worked out from birth dates alone ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Where the house holds, it describes a relationship whose growth shows in the way it meets people. Identity, presence and the way a life presents itself belong to the 1st, an angular house whose natural sign is Aries; Mercury, not Jupiter, was said to rejoice there. With composite Jupiter inside it, the pair's shared identity is expansive: friends call them welcoming and encouraging, larger together than apart, and the relationship tends to answer yes before it has finished hearing the question. Two reserved people can find that, as a couple, they are the ones who invite the new neighbours round and make the introductions at a wedding.

That warmth isn't either person's alone. A [Jupiter in the 1st house](/astrology/jupiter-in-1st-house/) of one birth chart describes someone whose warmth people notice first; [their Jupiter in your 1st house](/astrology/jupiter-in-1st-house-synastry/) describes one person enlarging the other's sense of self. In the composite, the bigness belongs to the pair as a unit, and it can surprise you both, since neither of you may be the generous one on your own.

The difficulty is that a bond's image can outgrow its substance. A relationship that shows its best side readily can take on every invitation, every project and every friend's request, and find that the couple everyone sees has little time left to be a couple. Presence isn't the same as confidence, and openness doesn't oblige you to say yes to everything. What helps is keeping the private bond in step with the public one: an evening a week that nobody else gets, and a habit of checking, before agreeing to the next big thing, that each of you really wants it.

Composites aren't something I draw in readings; astrologers who draw them read this house beside both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and a Jupiter this close to the composite Ascendant, however visible, says nothing about how long a relationship will last. On one side of the Ascendant, [composite Jupiter in the 12th house](/astrology/composite-jupiter-in-12th-house/) grows out of sight; on the other, [composite Jupiter in the 2nd house](/astrology/composite-jupiter-in-2nd-house/) grows through what the pair values and keeps. The first thing a composite needs, before any Ascendant is drawn, is two people who have both agreed to it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
