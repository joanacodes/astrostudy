Some couples, friendships and partnerships stay close by leaving each other room: separate interests, separate friends, long conversations about the world rather than about themselves. A composite Moon in Aquarius describes a relationship that feels most secure when both people can stay entirely themselves inside it.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

The [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) puts its [Moon](/astrology/moon/) at the midpoint of your two natal Moons, and that Moon describes the relationship's needs: what settles it, what it misses, how it processes what it feels. [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/), fixed air, brings objectivity, originality and a feeling for the group. The bond tends to understand its feelings by stepping back a little: it talks a hurt through as an idea before admitting it as a pang, and finds comfort in shared principles and in friends who think along the same lines.

Friendship tends to sit at the heart of it, even in a love affair or a working partnership. Even two people who would happily merge can find that together they need air: a weekend each, separate projects, a household open to other people. The need for room doesn't have to come from a [Moon in Aquarius](/astrology/moon-in-aquarius/) in either chart; it belongs to what you make between you, and it tends to come with an unusual loyalty, steady and undramatic, the kind that shows over years rather than in grand declarations.

The difficulty is that distance can be mistaken for coolness, by the two of you as much as by anyone else. A relationship that steps back to understand can step back a little too far: discussing a feeling instead of offering comfort, treating a need as a principle to debate. Objectivity isn't detachment. It helps when the pair says the warm thing plainly, even when it isn't original.

Uranus and Saturn share the rulership of Aquarius, one modern, one traditional, and I read both. Composite [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) shows where the relationship needs to try things its own way; composite [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), where it gives itself a shape that lasts. This Moon usually needs the two together: freedom inside a structure you have chosen.

In the site's count, two million pairs of random birth dates between 1930 and 2009, each cast at noon, put the composite Moon in Aquarius for about 1 pair in 12. Real births don't all fall at noon, which is why your own composite Moon needs your two real birth times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). Near the start of the sign, the [composite Moon in Capricorn](/astrology/composite-moon-in-capricorn/) is close by, with its comfort in structure; near the end, the [composite Moon in Pisces](/astrology/composite-moon-in-pisces/), which needs quiet and tenderness.

Composites belong to other astrologers' practice rather than mine, so this page describes the technique as they use it, with the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) and both natal charts. Between friends as between partners, both people should know their charts are being compared ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)). An Aquarius composite Moon describes a need for room, never a sign that a relationship is drifting apart.
