Moon opposite Venus has a picture in the sky: on the evening of such a birth, Venus can be setting in the west as the full moon rises in the east, or the reverse at dawn. It describes your needs and your affections on opposite horizons, and a life of learning to hold both at once.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) shows what you need in order to feel secure, and how you care and are cared for. [Venus](/astrology/venus/) shows what you value: whom and what you're drawn to, and the reciprocity that keeps a bond fair. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) relates two functions as a polarity, two ends of one axis that ask for balance and complement each other.

In plain words, the things that make you feel safe and the things that delight you sit on opposite sides of the room. You might find that the people you love most don't always soothe you, or that comfort feels a little dull until someone you value shares it. Very often one end is met in other people first: a partner whose affection becomes your safe place, or someone who needs your care and teaches you what you value. Relationships act as the mirror. The work isn't to choose between looking after yourself and the give-and-take of love, but to keep both in your own hands.

Each end belongs to the other's axis. A Moon in Libra facing Venus in Aries pairs a need for company and harmony with a taste for directness; a Moon in Taurus facing Venus in Scorpio pairs a need for simple calm with a taste for depth.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Usually it puts relationships at the centre of life, along with a careful sense of what you give and what you need back. The houses show the axis: a Moon in the 4th with Venus in the 10th asks for comfort at home while your pleasure lies in public life, and the balance between the two is part of the story.

This opposition, within 8°, is found in about 1 person in 23. Because Venus wanders more widely than Mercury does, the window around the full moon is broader for this pair, a few days either side. Close to exact, the pull between the two ends is felt often; nearer 8°, it comes and goes like a tide. I never read it as a sign that you need someone else to be whole: the balance is yours to hold ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

The pair's other aspects describe other points of the lunar month, and other balances:

- **[Moon conjunct Venus](/astrology/moon-conjunct-venus/):** need and affection sharing one place, as they do when you're born near the new moon rather than the full.
- **[Moon sextile Venus](/astrology/moon-sextile-venus/):** a friendly link two signs apart, where comfort and warmth trade without a go-between.
- **[Moon square Venus](/astrology/moon-square-venus/):** the tension held inside, need and taste negotiating at right angles.
- **[Moon trine Venus](/astrology/moon-trine-venus/):** an unforced tenderness in one element, with no need for a mirror.

Between two charts, [Moon opposite Venus in synastry](/astrology/moon-opposite-venus-synastry/) often feels like attraction across a difference: one person's needs facing the other's affection, each supplying what the other leans towards. Each side feels that pull differently, so I always read it both ways.

## In transits {#in-transits}

Once a month, briefly, the Moon stands opposite your natal Venus: a moment when your needs and your pleasures ask to be balanced. About once a year Venus opposes your natal Moon, never far from the time the Sun does, for two or three days, and across several weeks whenever its retrograde falls near that point.

Weather, not a forecast: these days light up the give-and-take in your relationships, without telling you how anyone will answer.
