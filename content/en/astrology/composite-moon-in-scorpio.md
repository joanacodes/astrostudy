Some relationships only feel safe once nothing important is left unsaid. A composite Moon in Scorpio makes depth the bond's first need: trust built slowly, kept fiercely, and tested by honesty rather than by charm.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Halfway between your two natal Moons, the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) of a [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) describes how the relationship regulates its feelings and what reassures it. [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/), fixed water, works beneath the surface: depth, truth-seeking, resilience, the slow consolidation of what is shared. This Moon settles when the bond knows where it stands, and that usually takes a conversation most pairs would put off.

Neither of you need be an intense person for the relationship to be intense in this quiet way. Two people without a [Moon in Scorpio](/astrology/moon-in-scorpio/) can still find that, together, they talk about what they keep from everyone else, and that the bond has an inner room the outside world never sees. Loyalty runs deep in it, and small acts of trust count for a great deal: a confidence kept, a promise remembered, a hard truth told gently. So does memory: what has been forgiven isn't forgotten, it becomes part of what the two of you have come through.

Scorpio sits opposite Taurus, where the Moon is exalted, so tradition gives it the Moon's [fall](/astrology/fall/): the sign where it is least at ease. In a relationship's chart, that describes feelings that are guarded, not feelings that are lacking. The difficulty is that depth can slide into secrecy, and closeness into watching each other: a jealous question, a test, a silence held as leverage. Intensity isn't control, and privacy isn't hiding. This Moon does best when the pair makes honesty safe, so that nothing ever has to be found out.

Scorpio answers to two rulers, Pluto and Mars, and I read both. In a composite they behave differently. Composite [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) is the midpoint of two slow positions, so for two people of much the same age it sits near the sign their generation already shares, and an age gap moves it halfway between two generations. Composite [Mars](/astrology/mars/) belongs to the pair alone, and shows by sign and house where the bond spends its intensity.

The composite Moon sits in its fall for about 1 pair in 12, as many as have it in its own sign. The birth times behind it are worth checking against the written record: a time remembered as *around teatime* can be an hour or two out, and two hours move the composite Moon about half a degree, enough to matter beside the [composite Moon in Libra](/astrology/composite-moon-in-libra/) or the [composite Moon in Sagittarius](/astrology/composite-moon-in-sagittarius/) at either end of the sign ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

A composite reads two people's charts at their most private, and like any reading of two people it belongs only where both have agreed ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#a-chart-is-personal)). It isn't a chart I draw myself; this page describes the technique, which is read with the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) and with each person's own chart. A Scorpio composite Moon says how deep a relationship needs to go to feel safe, never what will become of it.
