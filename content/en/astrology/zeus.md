Every birth chart already carries Zeus's initial: the glyph for Jupiter began as a Greek Z with a stroke through it, short for Zeus. Uranian astrology adds a second Zeus, a point that Alfred Witte placed beyond Neptune and that no one has ever seen.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

On Witte's list of four points, Zeus comes between [Hades](/astrology/hades/) and [Kronos](/astrology/kronos/), names that keep the god among his family: Hades was his brother, Kronos his father. In 1935 Witte's publisher printed the positions of all four for any date, in a perpetual ephemeris computed from the orbits the school had given them ([The Hamburg School (Uranian astrology)](/astrology/hamburg-school-uranian-astrology/)).

In Greek religion Zeus was the god of the sky and of thunder, king of the gods on Mount Olympus, the cloud-gatherer whose emblems were the thunderbolt and the eagle. He was the son of Cronus and Rhea, the sixth of their children and the only one his father never swallowed: Rhea bore him in secret on Crete and handed Cronus a stone wrapped in swaddling clothes instead. Grown, he freed his brothers and sisters, and when they had overthrown the Titans together, the sky fell to him. In Rome he was Jupiter.

So a Uranian chart holds Zeus twice. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) is the planet, the largest in the Solar System, which you can see with your own eyes and which goes round the zodiac in about 12 years. Zeus is the point, which would need longer than Neptune's 165 years to make the same round, and which nobody has seen at all. The two names were one long before either glyph: Zeus and Jupiter both descend from the name of an ancient Indo-European god of the daytime sky, *Dyēus*, the Sky Father.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Creativity and ambitious achievement are what Uranian astrologers see in Zeus, and they treat it as a symbol of fertility too.

His myths offer a match for each. He fathered the Muses, among a great many divine and heroic children; he won his throne by overthrowing a whole generation of gods; and even the gods who weren't his children called him Father.

In practice a Uranian astrologer doesn't read Zeus on its own but in combination: Zeus standing at the [midpoint](/astrology/midpoints/) of two planets, or two planets set at equal distances either side of it, make one planetary picture. On [the Uranian 90° dial](/astrology/uranian-90-dial/), every point a conjunction, square or opposition away from Zeus lands on the same spot, which is how those pictures are found quickly.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Zeus isn't one of the points I read, and fertility is where I stop altogether. No chart can say whether or when a pregnancy will come, and I never read one for that ([Can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/)).

Creativity I read in [the 5th house](/astrology/5th-house/), what you do for joy, which tradition called Good Fortune. Drive and ambition I read in Mars and in Jupiter itself, each by sign, house and aspect. When I look ahead for someone, Jupiter's transits are among the ones I follow, its return to its own place every twelve years or so included, so the god's initial is already doing its work in the chart ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Zeus stands for power over others.”** Authority is the theme Uranian astrologers give Kronos; Zeus they read for creative and ambitious drive.
- **“Astronomers found Zeus while searching beyond Neptune.”** They found Pluto there, and later a great many smaller bodies; none of them sits where Zeus is placed.
- **“Zeus is the asteroid named after the god.”** An asteroid, 5731 Zeus, does carry his name; the Uranian point has nothing to do with it.
