Sun conjunct Algol is the birthday contact of 16 and 17 May: a Sun in the last degrees of Taurus, on the degree of the star that marks Medusa's head. No star in the old books carries a worse name, and the name is most of what this contact shares with it, since Algol lies 22° north of the Sun's path.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) as purpose and vitality: what you're steering by, and the energy you bring to it. [Algol](/astrology/algol/) arrives with the darkest reputation in the astrology of fixed stars. Ptolemy called it the Gorgon of Perseus, the head of Medusa that the hero holds up in the star maps, and connected it in book IV of the *Tetrabiblos* with beheading; later books counted it among the unluckiest stars in the sky. All of that is a tradition's language about a star, and I pass it on as such. It records what people once feared when they looked at Perseus, not what happens to anyone born in mid-May.

How far the label reaches depends on the orb, which is worth seeing in numbers. [William Lilly](/astrology/william-lilly/) treated any planet within five degrees of Algol as afflicted. Applied to the Sun, that rule covers every birthday from about 12 to 21 May, roughly 1 person in 36. The one-degree window used on these pages ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)) keeps it to 16 and 17 May, the same two dates in 1930, 1970 and 2009, and to about 1 person in 176 born in those eighty years. Nicholas DeVore's encyclopedia of 1947 pulled the other way, counting a star only when a planet came within 1° of it in latitude as well, and by that test nothing on the Sun's path ever meets Algol, 22° away. Same star, same Sun: the size of the crowd is set by a convention.

The sign is the steadier guide. Algol stood at 25°11′ Taurus in 1930 and stands at 26°31′ in 2026, so the Sun on it is always a [Sun in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-taurus/), in the sign's closing degrees, where purpose tends to be built slowly and kept: continuity, consolidation, a pace of your own that others can't hurry. The date gives you that much; only the hour adds the house, the part of life where the Sun does its work.

None of this is a forecast. A Sun on Algol says nothing about how long anyone will live or how a life will go, and no birth chart can tell anyone how or when they will die ([Can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)).

Now and then a slower body joins the Sun on the star. In May 1971 Saturn sat on Algol's degree during the Sun's own pass, so a birth on 16 or 17 May that year has [Saturn conjunct Algol](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-algol/) as well; on 17 May 1998 it was Mars's turn ([Mars conjunct Algol](/astrology/mars-conjunct-algol/)). The [Moon conjunct Algol](/astrology/moon-conjunct-algol/) and [Venus conjunct Algol](/astrology/venus-conjunct-algol/) come and go on rhythms of a month and of about a year.

Algol isn't part of the charts I read, and I would never turn its legend into a reading of someone's life ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). What I read is the Sun itself: its sign, its house, its aspects.
