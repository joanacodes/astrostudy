Three or four times a year, Mercury seems to stop, back up through part of a sign, stop again and carry on. It’s the best-known moment of the astrological year, and the most blamed: for lost parcels, frozen screens and messages sent to the wrong person.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) goes round the Sun in 88 days, so it laps Earth every few months. Each time it passes between us and the Sun, it seems, from here, to slide backwards against the stars for a while: that is Mercury retrograde. In the middle of it, Mercury is lined up with the Sun and lost in its glare. Before, it was an evening star, low in the west after sunset; afterwards, it rises ahead of the Sun as a morning star.

In the dates I work from, Mercury turns retrograde 129 times between 2000 and 2040: three times in most years, four in six of them, 2029 among them. Each lasts 20 to 24 days, and a new one begins every 108 to 125 days. There are sixteen between 2026 and 2030, and they show a pattern worth knowing. In 2026 all three fall in water signs: 26 February to 20 March in Pisces, 29 June to 23 July in Cancer, and 24 October to 13 November in Scorpio ([Mercury retrograde in Scorpio](/astrology/mercury-retrograde-in-scorpio/)). Each year after, the stations come 16° to 20° earlier in the zodiac, so in 2027 every retrograde backs into the sign before, and by 2028 all three are in air signs. The full list has its own page, [Mercury retrograde dates](/sky/mercury-retrograde-dates/).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Like every retrograde, Mercury’s has two turning points, the [stations](/astrology/stations/), where for a day or two it barely moves. Between them it backs over 8½° to 16° of the zodiac, and the season makes more difference than you’d think. Between 2026 and 2030, the retrogrades of late spring and early summer last longest, about 24 days, and cover the fewest degrees, 9 or 10. The winter ones are over in about 20 days and cover nearly 16°.

Astrologers also count the weeks around it, when Mercury is moving forward over the same degrees: [the shadow period](/astrology/shadow-period/), before and after. That’s how three weeks of retrograde become, in some calendars, two months of warnings. I count the retrograde from station to station, and the shadow as what it is, a stretch of the zodiac crossed three times.

Mercury turns back more often than any other planet. [Venus retrograde](/astrology/venus-retrograde/) comes every year and a half or a little more, and lasts about six weeks; Mars’s comes about every two years and lasts two months or more.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Mercury is retrograde about one day in five, so about one person in five is born with it. In a birth chart I read it as Mercury’s function, taking things in, sorting them and passing them on, turned inward first: the answer that comes in the shower, the letter rewritten three times before it goes, the conversation understood properly the next morning. I wrote about that rhythm in [Natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/). I’m also open about the evidence. In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Mercury was among the placements that held about a third of the time, so I read its retrograde as one strand of a chart, never as its key.

As a transit, Mercury retrograde backs through one area of your chart, the house it crosses, which needs your birth time to place. I read those weeks as a season for Mercury’s work turned towards what’s already there: the draft, the unanswered message, the plan that needs a second look. It’s weather, not a forecast. It describes a mood, and it never decides what becomes of a journey, a purchase or a signature.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Mercury retrograde makes your phone die.”** Devices fail in every month of the year. Whether they fail more often in these weeks is a question that deserves a count rather than an anecdote, and it has its own page: [Does Mercury retrograde break things?](/astrology/does-mercury-retrograde-break-things/)
- **“You mustn’t sign anything until it’s over.”** No transit decides whether a contract is a good one. Read it carefully, as you would in any month.
- **“It’s rare, so it must mean something big.”** It covers about one day in five. Something that common describes a rhythm, not an emergency.
- **“Born with Mercury retrograde, you’ll never express yourself well.”** It describes a mind that prepares before it speaks, often all the better for it. How you think isn’t how much you think.
