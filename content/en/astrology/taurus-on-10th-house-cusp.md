Taurus on the 10th house cusp puts the sign of continuity at the top of your chart, on the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/). What you'd like to be known for, the work you do in public, the standing you build: all of it, here, takes the long view.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The sign on the Midheaven is the style of the role you grow into ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)), and [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/) grows into it as a keeper. You tend to want work in which you're trusted with something worth keeping, a family firm, a collection, a garden, a craft tradition, the accounts of a small charity, and to leave it in better shape than you found it. Reputation, for you, accrues like interest: hardly visible for years, then suddenly substantial. Titles and quick promotions matter less than the sense that what you've built is still standing. A change of job, when it comes, is usually a move to tend something larger, not an escape. Capricorn, the 10th's natural sign, shares Taurus's element, and both measure a working life by what it leaves behind. None of this names a profession: on the [10th house](/astrology/10th-house/), a sign describes how you hold a role, whichever role you hold.

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) rules your 10th, and Libra, its other sign, holds your 3rd cusp in nearly every chart with this Midheaven, so your public work and your everyday words answer to one planet: tact, a good ear and the well-judged email are working tools for you. Your public manner tends to be courteous and unhurried, and people often trust your judgement of quality before they know much else about you. Scorpio holds the IC beneath, and the even temper people meet at work rests on private ground that is anything but shallow. The house of Venus is the part of life your career keeps returning to: the [1st](/astrology/10th-house-ruler-in-1st-house/), [2nd](/astrology/10th-house-ruler-in-2nd-house/), [3rd](/astrology/10th-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/), [4th](/astrology/10th-house-ruler-in-4th-house/), [5th](/astrology/10th-house-ruler-in-5th-house/), [6th](/astrology/10th-house-ruler-in-6th-house/), [7th](/astrology/10th-house-ruler-in-7th-house/), [8th](/astrology/10th-house-ruler-in-8th-house/), [9th](/astrology/10th-house-ruler-in-9th-house/), [10th](/astrology/10th-house-ruler-in-10th-house/), [11th](/astrology/10th-house-ruler-in-11th-house/) or [12th](/astrology/10th-house-ruler-in-12th-house/).

A Taurus Midheaven lasts almost exactly two hours each day, so it falls to about 1 birth in 12, at London as at New York: in Placidus, the house system I use, the Midheaven's sign follows the clock, not the latitude. Leo usually rises with it, so you tend to be noticed before you're known, and the Taurus Midheaven is what turns being noticed into standing. On either side, the cusps belong to other planets: Aries holds the 9th in nearly all of these charts and Gemini or Cancer the 11th, so neither [Taurus on the 9th house cusp](/astrology/taurus-on-9th-house-cusp/) nor [Taurus on the 11th house cusp](/astrology/taurus-on-11th-house-cusp/) is yours as well. In about 1 London birth in 7, Taurus is on no cusp at all ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)).

A clock time out by even ten minutes can move the Midheaven two or three degrees, which near the start or the end of Taurus decides whether this page is yours at all; the time comes before the reading ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). And a Midheaven, whatever its sign, describes a way of standing in public, not a forecast of how far you'll go.
