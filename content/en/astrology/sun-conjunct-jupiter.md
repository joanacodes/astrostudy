Once a year or a little more, the Sun catches up with Jupiter, and for a few weeks the two travel close together. Born in those weeks, you have Sun conjunct Jupiter: the part of you that is becoming someone and the part that wants to learn, grow and make sense of things, in one place.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is your organising centre: identity, vitality, purpose. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) widens: it expands understanding, draws meaning out of experience and supports growth over time. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) makes the two move together, each still doing its own work.

In plain words, you tend to become yourself by enlarging your world: a new subject, a new place, a bigger question, a principle worth living by. Meaning matters to you as much as achievement, and you're often generous with what you find, whether ideas, time or encouragement. People may come to you to feel that more is possible. That isn't luck, and it isn't wealth, two things Jupiter is often mistaken for: it's a capacity for growth, which still has to be used.

The lesson sits in Jupiter's own definition: expansion is not excess. With your sense of self and your appetite for growth fused, saying yes comes easily, and it's harder to notice when enough is enough: one more project, one more promise, one more horizon. Growth that knows its measure is the mature form of this conjunction, and well worth the learning.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Jupiter's sign, usually your Sun sign too, colours the growth. In Sagittarius, Jupiter's own sign, it travels and questions; in Cancer, where tradition says Jupiter is exalted, it nourishes; in Taurus it builds slowly and keeps what it builds. The house shows where you widen most: the 9th, study and travel; the 2nd, what you build up and value; the 10th, a public role that keeps growing.

Within 8°, about 1 person in 19 has the two together. Jupiter moves on about a sign a year, so the Sun catches it roughly a month later each year and stays near it for a few weeks: people born close together in one year often share the conjunction, while someone born on the same date a year later usually doesn't. Jupiter is never retrograde at this meeting, and the Sun, the faster, overtakes it slowly ([applying and separating aspects](/astrology/applying-and-separating-aspects/)).

Jupiter, in its sign and house, was checked in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) like every other placement, and it didn't stand out the way Saturn, Chiron and Lilith did. So when a reading leans on this conjunction, I say so.

Over the following months, the Sun makes the rest of the pair's aspects:

- **[Sun sextile Jupiter](/astrology/sun-sextile-jupiter/):** two signs apart; growth stops being built in and turns into openings you choose.
- **[Sun square Jupiter](/astrology/sun-square-jupiter/):** three signs apart; the appetite for more rubs against your direction, and measure has to be earned.
- **[Sun trine Jupiter](/astrology/sun-trine-jupiter/):** four signs apart, in one element; growth and purpose keep step without merging.
- **[Sun opposite Jupiter](/astrology/sun-opposite-jupiter/):** across the chart, with Jupiter retrograde; the wider view stands in front of you, often in a teacher or a mentor.

Between two charts, [Sun conjunct Jupiter in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-jupiter-synastry/) places one person's urge to grow right on the other's sense of self: encouragement, usually, and now and then too much of it.

## In transits {#in-transits}

Your Sun and Jupiter share a place, so every [Jupiter return](/astrology/jupiter-return/), roughly every twelve years, is also [transiting Jupiter conjunct natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-conjunct-natal-sun/): for you, one season rather than two. Jupiter takes about a year to cross a sign, which keeps it within a degree or two of your Sun for a few weeks, or over several months when its retrograde brings it back.

I read that season as a widening: study, travel, a larger role, a broader view. Weather, not a forecast: it describes room to grow, never success, money or luck.
