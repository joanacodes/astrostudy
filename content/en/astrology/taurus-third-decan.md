The third decan of Taurus is 20° to 30° of the sign, the last ten degrees of fixed earth before the Sun moves into Gemini. Saturn rules it in both lists, and Algol, the star the old books counted among the unluckiest, sits inside it.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

Lilly's [faces](/astrology/faces/) reach [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) at the end of Taurus, after Mercury and the Moon. The modern decans arrive at the same planet from the other direction: they give the last ten degrees of an earth sign to the ruler of the third earth sign, Capricorn, and Capricorn's ruler is Saturn. It is one of the few decans where the two lists name the same planet.

So two rulers speak here: Venus, for [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), and Saturn, for the decan. Venus says what a planet in these degrees values and wants to keep; Saturn adds structure, patience and the long view, the sense that what is kept has to be maintained. [Saturn in Taurus](/astrology/saturn-in-taurus/) between 20° and 30° stands in a decan it rules twice over, in a sign where it has neither domicile nor exaltation.

Saturn also matters to me for another reason. In my tests, Saturn's placements held 75 to 90% of the time, with Chiron's and Lilith's ([the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)). A decan that answers to Saturn sends me to your Saturn, which is where I spend the most time in a reading anyway: its sign, its house and what it touches.

## Its image {#its-image}

The Egyptian name brings Orion into the picture. Budge transliterates it Remen-ḥeru-an-Saḥ, and Saḥ was the god of a constellation that took in the stars of Orion and Lepus, the consort of Sopdet, goddess of Sirius. In Budge's list, four decans from here to the end of Gemini carry his name, and a fifth, Saḥ alone, follows them before Sothis opens the count again. Which stars each decan stood for is another matter: scholars of the Egyptian star tables say it is not known ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

The later lists go elsewhere. The Greco-Egyptian ones write Remen-hare, Firmicus Maternus Romanae or Arfa, and the *Testament of Solomon* Sphendonael. Cosmas of Maiuma put the Litai here, the Prayers, and Athanasius Kircher Apopis, the serpent of Egyptian myth.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun stands here for births from about 11 to 20 May ([Sun in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-taurus/)). In 2026 it crossed 20° Taurus on 10 May at 15:50 UT and left for Gemini on 21 May at 00:37 UT, so births on the 20th or 21st need their time ([Gemini, first decan](/astrology/gemini-first-decan/)).

On about 16–17 May the Sun passes [Algol](/astrology/algol/), at 26°31′ Taurus in 2026. Tradition's word for the star was unlucky; mine is that a star on a planet's degree is a detail of the sky, never a sentence. Further on, the Pleiades stood in the last degree of this decan for most of the twentieth century and crossed into Gemini around 2000 ([Alcyone and the Pleiades](/astrology/alcyone-and-the-pleiades/)).

Saturn has sat in its own decan for three groups of births: May 1941 to May 1942, on and off from July 1970 to February 1972, and May 2000 to April 2001, with a break that autumn. Uranus held these degrees from April 1940 to August 1941, with shorter stays on either side, and again from 2023 until 25 April 2026, when it left Taurus for good ([Uranus in Gemini, 2026–2033](/astrology/uranus-in-gemini-2026-2033/)). For anyone with a planet or angle here, those years were a long transit, read through the house it crossed.

The next ten degrees belong to Gemini, where Jupiter takes the face and Mercury the decan; behind lies the Moon's face ([Taurus, second decan](/astrology/taurus-second-decan/)).
