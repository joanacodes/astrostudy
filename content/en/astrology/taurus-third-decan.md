Saturn has the last word in Taurus: both lists of decan rulers give it the sign's final ten degrees, 20° to 30°. Algol, the star the old books counted among the unluckiest, sits inside them.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) closes Taurus in Lilly's table of [faces](/astrology/faces/), after Mercury and the Moon, and it closes it in the modern list as well, as ruler of Capricorn, the last of the three earth signs. Few decans get the same planet from both lists; this is one of them.

So two rulers speak here: Venus, for [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), and Saturn, for the decan. Venus says what a planet in these degrees values and wants to keep; Saturn adds structure, patience and the long view, the sense that what is kept has to be maintained. [Saturn in Taurus](/astrology/saturn-in-taurus/) between 20° and 30° stands in a decan it rules twice over, in a sign where it has neither domicile nor exaltation.

Saturn also matters to me for another reason. In my tests, Saturn's placements held 75 to 90% of the time, with Chiron's and Lilith's ([the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)). A decan that answers to Saturn sends me to your Saturn, which is where I spend the most time in a reading anyway: its sign, its house and what it touches.

## Its image {#its-image}

The Egyptian name brings Orion into the picture. Budge transliterates it Remen-ḥeru-an-Saḥ, and Saḥ was the god of a constellation that took in the stars of Orion and Lepus, the consort of Sopdet, goddess of Sirius. In Budge's list, four decans from here to the end of Gemini carry his name, and a fifth, Saḥ alone, follows them before Sothis opens the count again. Which stars each decan stood for is another matter: scholars of the Egyptian star tables say it is not known ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

The later lists go elsewhere. The Greco-Egyptian ones have Remen-hare, close to Budge's form. Firmicus Maternus offers two names, Romanae and Arfa, and the *Testament of Solomon* a third, Sphendonael. Cosmas of Maiuma put the Litai here, the Prayers, and Athanasius Kircher Apopis, the serpent of Egyptian myth.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun reaches these degrees around 11 May and stays until about the 20th, passing Algol on the way ([Sun in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-taurus/)). In 2026 it arrived at 15:50 UT on 10 May and moved on into Gemini just after midnight UT on 21 May, at 00:37, so births on the 20th or 21st need their time ([Gemini, first decan](/astrology/gemini-first-decan/)).

Its meeting with [Algol](/astrology/algol/), at 26°31′ Taurus in 2026, comes on about 16–17 May. Tradition's word for the star was unlucky; mine is that a star on a planet's degree is a detail of the sky, never a sentence. Further on, the Pleiades stood in the last degree of this decan for most of the twentieth century and crossed into Gemini around 2000 ([Alcyone and the Pleiades](/astrology/alcyone-and-the-pleiades/)).

Saturn has sat in its own decan for three groups of births: May 1941 to May 1942, on and off from July 1970 to February 1972, and May 2000 to April 2001, with a break that autumn. Uranus's last two visits came from April 1940 to August 1941, with shorter stays on either side, and from 2023 until 25 April 2026, when it left Taurus for good ([Uranus in Gemini, 2026–2033](/astrology/uranus-in-gemini-2026-2033/)). For anyone with a planet or angle here, those years were a long transit, read through the house it crossed.

The next ten degrees belong to Gemini, where Jupiter takes the face and Mercury the decan; behind lies the Moon's face ([Taurus, second decan](/astrology/taurus-second-decan/)).
