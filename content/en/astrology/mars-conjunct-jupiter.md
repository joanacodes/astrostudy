Mars conjunct Jupiter puts the part of you that acts and the part that looks for meaning in the same few degrees. When you believe in something you tend to move, and when you move you tend to find out what you believe.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the part of a chart that initiates: it starts things, asserts what you want, puts in the effort and holds a line. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) is the part that asks what things are for: it turns experience into understanding and supports your growth over time. In a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) they work as one unit, though each keeps its own job: the impulse to act and the wish to understand arrive in the same gesture.

In plain words, conviction and effort are hard to separate in you. A cause you care about gets your energy straight away; a task you take on soon acquires a larger purpose. People may notice enthusiasm, an appetite for challenge, or a gift for rallying others round an aim. The conjunction comes with two cautions, both about scale: expansion isn't excess, and courage isn't recklessness. It works best when the size of what you take on matches the reason for it, and when your principles stay open to being wrong.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Tradition gives this pair a neat asymmetry. Mars is exalted in Capricorn, where Jupiter is in its fall, and Jupiter is exalted in Cancer, where Mars is in its fall. So a conjunction in Capricorn puts disciplined effort in charge of a more cautious kind of faith, and one in Cancer lets protective, generous belief lead an effort that works by feel. In Aries, Mars's own sign, or Sagittarius, Jupiter's, one of the two is on home ground and sets the tone. The house shows where the energy goes: in the 9th, into study, travel or teaching; in the 6th, into daily work done for a reason you believe in.

About 1 person in 26 is born with Mars within 6° of Jupiter. Mars, by far the quicker, overtakes Jupiter a little over every two years, and the meeting stays in orb for about a month, so the conjunction belongs to a few weeks of births each time. The house it falls in needs a birth time, and that's where I start in a reading ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Mars and Jupiter at their other angles:

- **[Mars sextile Jupiter](/astrology/mars-sextile-jupiter/):** an opening rather than a fusion; purpose that helps effort along when you invite it.
- **[Mars square Jupiter](/astrology/mars-square-jupiter/):** action and meaning at an angle, learning proportion by rubbing against each other.
- **[Mars trine Jupiter](/astrology/mars-trine-jupiter/):** effort and belief in one element, easy partners without being one thing.
- **[Mars opposite Jupiter](/astrology/mars-opposite-jupiter/):** the two at opposite ends, so that doing and understanding are often split between you and someone else at first.

Between two charts, [Mars conjunct Jupiter in synastry](/astrology/mars-conjunct-jupiter-synastry/) tends to be energising: one person's drive and the other's sense of purpose land on the same spot. Plans multiply quickly, so I read it from both sides and ask who sets the pace.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Jupiter conjunct natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-conjunct-natal-mars/) comes about every twelve years and lasts a few weeks, or longer, in two or three passes, when Jupiter's yearly retrograde falls nearby. It's often a season of fresh energy for something you believe in. With the conjunction in your chart, each [Jupiter return](/astrology/jupiter-return/), at about 12, 24, 36 and so on, brings Jupiter back to its own place and so beside your Mars.

Mars crosses your natal Jupiter about once every two years, for a few days of eagerness.

Weather, not a forecast: momentum on offer, with the direction left to you.
