Equal houses do exactly what the name says: twelve houses of 30° each, all starting from the Ascendant. It's the tidiest of the systems on paper, and the one that never runs into trouble near the poles. The price is the Midheaven, which no longer opens the 10th house.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

In equal houses, the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/)'s degree is the cusp of the 1st house, and every other cusp follows at 30° intervals. With 17° Leo rising, the 2nd house begins at 17° Virgo, the 3rd at 17° Libra, the 4th at 17° Scorpio, and so on round: every cusp sits at the same degree of its sign. No house is ever larger than a sign, so no sign is ever intercepted ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)), and the system works at any latitude.

The cusp of the 10th is then the degree 90° before the Ascendant, a point astrologers call the nonagesimal, and the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) goes its own way. The horizon and the meridian don't cross at right angles in the zodiac: near the equator the angle between them stays within about 5° of a right angle; further north or south it strays more, and towards the polar circles it can be almost anything. So the Midheaven usually lands in the 10th or the 9th house; in Britain and northern France it can fall in the 8th or the 11th too.

Equal houses are easy to confuse with [Whole Sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/). Both use 30° houses, but Whole Sign starts at 0° of the rising sign, equal houses at the Ascendant's own degree. With 25° Gemini rising, the equal 1st house runs from 25° Gemini to 25° Cancer; the Whole Sign 1st house is the whole of Gemini.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Among the systems that divide by space rather than by time, equal houses are the simplest: they measure the zodiac itself and ignore the meridian. [Porphyry](/astrology/porphyry/) also measures the zodiac, but quadrant by quadrant: it keeps the Midheaven on the 10th cusp and cuts each quarter into equal thirds, so its houses vary in size. [Koch](/astrology/koch/) and Placidus divide by time instead, and stop at the polar circles; equal houses carry on wherever there's a rising degree.

The system owes its modern following to Margaret Hone (1892–1969), who revived it in her courses. Her *Modern Text Book of Astrology* was for many years the official textbook of the Faculty of Astrological Studies, which she helped to found. Its supporters argue that it is more accurate and less distorted than the quadrant systems at high latitudes, above about 60°, where Placidus houses become extremely unequal.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I use [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/), mainly because it keeps the Midheaven on the cusp of the 10th, and I want the top of the chart to be where the 10th house begins. That's the trade-off equal houses make in the other direction: they gain evenness and lose that alignment. Astrologers who use them still read the Midheaven as a sensitive point, through its sign and aspects, wherever it falls; it simply doesn't open a house.

Equal houses have real strengths. Every cusp at the same degree makes aspects to the cusps easy to see; no sign is ever squeezed out; a birth far north is no problem. They still need the birth time, because the Ascendant's degree sets every cusp ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). If your chart uses them, read about it in that system, and compare like with like ([which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)); on this site, every house is written for Placidus ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Equal houses and Whole Sign are the same thing.”** Equal houses begin at the Ascendant's degree, Whole Sign at the start of the rising sign.
- **“In equal houses the Midheaven is the 10th cusp.”** The 10th cusp is 90° from the Ascendant; the Midheaven floats, usually in the 9th or 10th.
- **“Equal houses are distorted far from the equator.”** Their houses stay 30° everywhere; what changes with latitude is where the Midheaven lands among them.
