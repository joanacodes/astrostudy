A relationship with composite Mercury in Libra asks *what do you think?* and waits for the answer. Its shared mind sits in the sign of the scales, and it reasons best with two voices in the room.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) gives every planet the halfway point of its two natal positions, and composite [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is the relationship's reasoning: how it weighs things up, decides and speaks for itself. [Libra](/astrology/libra/), cardinal air, gives it a mind built for two. Libra also opens the stretch of the zodiac where composite Mercury turns up most often: about 1 pair in 11 has it here, as in Scorpio, Sagittarius and Capricorn.

This bond decides by consulting. Neither of you simply announces a plan; it's proposed, turned over and adjusted until it suits you both. It enjoys a good argument for the pleasure of hearing the other case well made. Fairness matters to it more than speed, and it has a gift for the tactful sentence, the one that says something difficult without making anyone lose face. Others often notice: friends bring their quarrels to the pair, colleagues ask the two of you to settle a disagreement. Two blunt people can share this Mercury and find that together they become diplomats. If one of you was born with [Mercury in Libra](/astrology/mercury-in-libra/), that's how you reason alone; the composite is how you reason as a pair.

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) rules Libra, and the sign and house of composite Venus show what this relationship most wants to agree about: what it finds beautiful, fair or worth its time.

In a birth chart a Libra Mercury keeps the Sun in Virgo, Libra or Scorpio, and most composites do the same. About 1 pair in 26, though, finds the composite Sun around Aries, almost exactly opposite. It looks like a picture of the scales, two sides facing each other, and it would be easy to read meaning into it. It's the arithmetic of midpoints, worked out one planet at a time.

The difficulty is the sentence that never gets said. A relationship this keen on harmony can postpone the hard conversation to keep the peace, and a decision can circle (*you choose; no, you choose*) until the moment has gone. Harmony isn't the avoidance of conflict, and fairness sometimes means saying the unwelcome thing kindly, but saying it. Taking turns to decide helps, a week each; so does putting a date on the decisions that matter, and keeping to it.

[Composite Mercury in Virgo](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-virgo/), one sign back, checks the details before it speaks; [composite Mercury in Scorpio](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-scorpio/), one sign on, wants to know what lies beneath them. Libra wants to know what's fair.

I describe composites without using them in my readings. Astrologers who do use them read them with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and some weigh them against [the Davison chart](/astrology/davison-chart/), cast for a real moment between two births: a comparison this Mercury would enjoy. Fairness starts before any chart is drawn, with both people agreeing to it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
