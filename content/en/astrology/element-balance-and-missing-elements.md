Sort the planets of a chart by element and you get four small numbers: three in fire, say, four in water, two in air, one in earth. That tally is the element balance, and an element left with nothing is what people call a missing element.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Each sign belongs to one of [the four elements](/astrology/four-elements/), so each planet, standing in a sign, can be sorted by element as well. Element balance is the result: how the ten planets, from the Sun to Pluto, spread across fire, earth, air and water. When one element holds most of them, the chart has an emphasis there. When an element holds none, astrologers call it missing, or empty.

I don't like the word “missing” on its own. Every chart contains all twelve signs, each filling its 30 degrees of the wheel somewhere among the houses. What an empty element lacks is planets, not presence: its three signs are still drawn on your chart, and the planets that rule them are placed somewhere in it.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

There's no single rule for the count. Some astrologers tally only the ten planets; some add the Ascendant and the Midheaven; some give the Sun and the Moon more weight than the slow planets. Two programs can print two different tables for the same birth, so I always look at which planets make up a number before trusting it.

The slow planets are a good reason to look. Uranus spends about seven years in a sign, Neptune about fourteen, Pluto anywhere from 12 to 31, so everyone born in the same stretch of years shares their elements. Three planets in water may say more about a generation than about you, if two of them are Neptune and Pluto. The Sun, the Moon, Mercury, Venus and Mars change sign far more often, and they're the ones I weigh first.

The birth time enters the count in three places. The Ascendant and the Midheaven change sign through the day, the Ascendant about every two hours on average, so any count that includes them needs the time. The Moon moves into a new sign about every two and a half days, and on the day it does, the time decides whether it stands in the last degrees of a water sign or the first of the fire sign that follows. Above all, the houses, which show where in a life each element finds its room, are cast from the time and place. That's why there's no reading without a birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

The same tally can be made by modality, cardinal, fixed and mutable, and the two read best side by side ([Modality balance](/astrology/modality-balance/)): a chart whose fire is mostly fixed starts and keeps going differently from one whose fire is mostly mutable.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read an emphasis as the place where a chart's energy gathers: the style reached for first, the one that feels like home. A sign never acts on its own in my readings; it shapes how a planet does what it does. So four planets in earth don't make anyone practical by decree. They say that consolidating, refining and structuring, the ways Taurus, Virgo and Capricorn colour a planet, come back again and again, in different corners of a life.

An empty element I read through what remains of it. Take a chart with no planets in water. Cancer, Scorpio and Pisces are still on the wheel, and the houses they fall in show where that way of meeting life has room. Their rulers are placed somewhere too: the Moon for Cancer, Mars and Pluto for Scorpio, Jupiter and Neptune for Pisces, since those two signs have a traditional ruler and a modern one, and I read both. Missing water is not missing feeling. Feeling travels by another road, perhaps through a Moon in an air sign that talks things through, or one in an earth sign that shows care by doing.

Then I set the count beside everything else. A crowded element tells me less than the planets crowding it: the Sun, Moon and Venus together in one element weigh more than three outer planets passing through. And when an element holds exactly one planet, that planet carries the whole style alone, which deserves a look of its own ([Singletons](/astrology/singletons/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Missing water means you can't feel.”** It means no planet stands in Cancer, Scorpio or Pisces. Your Moon, wherever it is, still describes how you feel.
- **“A healthy chart is balanced.”** Ten planets can't split evenly four ways anyway. An uneven count describes a shape, not a fault.
- **“Your Sun sign sets your element.”** The Sun is one planet of ten. A Leo Sun can sit in a chart where water or earth holds most of the others.
- **“Every astrologer gets the same count.”** Which points go in, and how much each one weighs, varies. Check what a table counts before reading anything into it.
