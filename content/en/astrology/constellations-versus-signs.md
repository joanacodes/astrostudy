Look at the stars behind the Sun on your birthday and you'll probably find the "wrong" constellation there. Nothing has gone wrong: a constellation and a sign are two different things that happen to share a name.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A constellation is a patch of sky. Astronomers recognise 88 of them, and since 1930 every point of the sky has belonged to exactly one, inside borders that Eugène Delporte, a Belgian astronomer, drew up for the International Astronomical Union. Thirteen lie across the Sun's path, the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/): the twelve that gave the signs their names, plus [Ophiuchus](/astrology/ophiuchus-and-the-13th-sign/). They're star patterns of very different widths, so the Sun takes very different lengths of time to cross them.

A sign is a measurement. In the [tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/), the one I use, the circle the Sun travels in a year is divided into twelve equal parts of 30°, starting from the March equinox, when the Sun passes into the northern half of the sky. Aries is the first 30° after that moment, Taurus the next 30°, and so on round to Pisces. The signs are set by the seasons, not by any star.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The two once lined up, roughly: the signs took their names from the constellations behind them when the zodiac was set out. They've drifted apart since because of precession ([precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/)). The Earth's axis wobbles slowly, like a spinning top, and the point where the Sun stands at the March equinox creeps backwards against the stars: a full circle in about 26,000 years, so about a degree in a human lifetime. The Greek astronomer Hipparchus noticed the drift more than two thousand years ago.

Ptolemy, writing nearly three centuries after him, still counted the signs from the equinox: a circle has no natural beginning, he says, so the sign that begins at the spring equinox, Aries, is taken as the start of them all. The tropical signs have moved with the seasons ever since, and the constellations have stayed with the stars. Today, at the March equinox, the Sun enters the sign of Aries while standing in front of the constellation Pisces.

The [sidereal zodiac](/astrology/sidereal-zodiac/), used in Indian astrology, made the other choice: twelve equal signs anchored to the stars, which therefore drift against the seasons instead. Neither zodiac follows the constellations' real borders. Both divide the circle into twelve equal signs, which is the one thing the constellations never were.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I use the tropical zodiac. A sign, for me, is a season of the Sun's year, and its meaning comes from that place in the cycle: Aries is the start of the light half of the year in the north, Libra the start of the dark half. The star pattern behind the Sun isn't part of what I read, which is also why the "your sign has changed" headlines don't change anything in a chart ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

The stars still have a place, a smaller and more precise one. Some astrologers look at the bright stars a planet sits close to, measured by each star's own position, never by a constellation's borders ([fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/)). That's a separate layer of a chart, and a tight one: a degree or so either way.

So when your chart puts the Sun at 12° Aries, it gives you two facts. The degree is where the Sun was along its path; the sign is the season, and the season is what a chart is built on. Whatever constellation stood behind that degree is true too, and belongs to astronomy.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Astrologers don't know about precession.”** Hipparchus noticed it more than two thousand years ago, and Ptolemy still counted the signs from the equinox. The tropical zodiac follows the seasons on purpose.
- **“Your real sign is the constellation behind the Sun.”** That's a fact about the stars behind the Sun. Your sign is the season of your birth, and it's what a chart is built on.
- **“The sidereal zodiac uses the real constellations.”** It anchors twelve equal signs to the stars; it doesn't follow the constellations' unequal borders either.
- **“Precession is slowly changing everyone's sign.”** It changes which stars stand behind each sign. In the tropical zodiac, Aries begins at the March equinox every year, and always will.
