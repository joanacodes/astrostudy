With Venus in the 2nd house, what you value and what you own are two sides of one question: what is worth keeping? The planet of valuation sits in the house of belongings, talents and self-worth. I read it as a sure sense of quality, and a self-worth that does best when it isn't measured in things.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) is about having: what you earn and hold on to, the things you own, the skills that could pay your way, and underneath all of it, how much you think you're worth. It is succedent, the house that follows the 1st, and its natural sign is Taurus, one of Venus's own. [Venus](/astrology/venus/) is at ease here. Valuation is its first task, and in the 2nd it turns that judgement on things, skills and yourself: you tend to know quality when you hold it, and to choose fewer, better possessions.

Tradition put money in this house, and Venus here is often read as pleasure and earning walking together. I read it as a relationship with having, not a forecast. No chart says how much anyone will earn or own, and reading one for that isn't something I do ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)). What Venus shows is what you find worth paying for, in money, time and care, and whether you count yourself among those things.

When you were born, Venus sat below the eastern horizon, a few hours from rising, and because Venus keeps company with the Sun, the birth most often came in the dark hours before dawn. In [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/), the system I read, the cusps come from your exact time and place, and an hour's error can lift Venus into [Venus in the 1st house](/astrology/venus-in-1st-house/) or leave it in [Venus in the 3rd house](/astrology/venus-in-3rd-house/). That's why I won't read a house without the time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, a 2nd-house Venus shows care in tangible ways: a meal, a gift chosen with thought, comfort offered without fuss. You tend to value steadiness, and to feel loved when someone values what you value: your time, your things, your way of doing them. The house across is the 8th, where what is shared and owed lives, so questions of mine, yours and ours can matter more to you than you expect. Feeling secure in yourself, rather than in what a partner provides, is what lets this Venus relax in a couple.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, this Venus tends to make a talent of taste. You may earn your living, or simply your pleasure, through something you judge well: cooking, design, music, crafts, dealing in things of quality, appraising other people's work. You often know what a thing is worth and dislike pretending otherwise, which makes you a good judge of a fair price or a fair wage. Work you enjoy tends to feel worth doing in a way that has little to do with the payslip.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Worth can get tangled with having. On a bad day, buying something lovely may stand in for feeling lovely, and losing a possession can feel like losing part of yourself. Asking to be paid fairly may be hard, because naming your own value feels immodest; or it goes the other way, and you measure your value only by what you earn.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Write down what you value that money can't buy, and check your weeks against it now and then. Spend on what lasts, and on experiences you'll remember, rather than on comfort for the moment. Practise naming your fee, or your need, out loud, as plainly as you would price someone else's work. And keep at least one pleasure that costs nothing.

A note on weight: in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Venus held about a third of the time, and the 2nd house is where people most want certainty. I give a 2nd-house Venus its due as a picture of taste and self-worth, and I never let it stand in for advice about money.
