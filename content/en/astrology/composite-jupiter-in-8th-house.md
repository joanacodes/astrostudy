What two people hold in common runs deeper than a joint account: secrets kept, burdens shared, the version of each other no one else has seen. Composite Jupiter in the 8th house finds a relationship's growth in the trust all that takes, and in what it is willing to share.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The 8th house](/astrology/8th-house/) is the house of intimacy, trust, shared resources and obligations, and of the changes that renew a life. Succedent, it has Scorpio as its natural sign. With [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) placed here in [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), the bond's growth is a matter of depth: each time the two of you trust each other with something heavier, the relationship gets larger rather than more fragile. Its generosity is the kind few people see: a burden carried for a while by whichever of you has more strength that month.

Tradition gave the 8th death and inheritance, and I read neither into any chart. A composite Jupiter here predicts no death, no loss and no legacy ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)), and no gain from anyone else's money either. What it describes is a pair that handles what is shared with generosity: joint decisions made openly, help given in a crisis without keeping score, the strength to change together when life requires it.

Composite houses also inherit a house system. Whether the cusps come from the midpoints of the two charts' cusps or from the composite Midheaven, someone has chosen how to divide the sky: in [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/), the system I use for birth charts, houses come out unequal in size, while equal houses give each exactly 30°. A Jupiter near the edge of the 8th can change house with that choice alone, and either way both birth times are needed ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/); [which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)).

Intimacy and candour widen the life of someone with [Jupiter in the 8th house](/astrology/jupiter-in-8th-house/); in synastry, [their Jupiter in your 8th house](/astrology/jupiter-in-8th-house-synastry/) makes trust come more easily to the other. In the composite, that trust is a shared possession. Two guarded people can find that, together, they talk about money, fear and desire without flinching, and come out of a hard conversation closer than they went in.

The difficulty is merging until no one is quite separate. A bond this trusting can hold everything in common, money, friends, decisions, and lose track of what belongs to each of you; it can also make intensity the measure of closeness. Sharing isn't losing, and depth isn't obsession. What helps is keeping something that is simply each person's own, some time, some money, a friendship, and writing down what you do share, so that the generosity rests on clear agreements.

Composites have no place in my practice; the astrologers who use them read the house of shared resources with both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and never as news of a legacy or a loss. Either side of it, [composite Jupiter in the 7th house](/astrology/composite-jupiter-in-7th-house/) widens a bond through alliances and [composite Jupiter in the 9th house](/astrology/composite-jupiter-in-9th-house/) through belief, study and travel. A placement about trust should begin with it: both people choosing to share their birth data before anyone draws the chart ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
