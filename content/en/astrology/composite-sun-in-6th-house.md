Routines, service and the craft you get better at by doing make up the 6th house, the house of the working day. A composite Sun there makes that shared working life the relationship's centre, and it fits colleagues as naturally as couples.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Ask two astrologers to draw your composite houses and you may get two answers. One takes the midpoint of each pair of natal cusps; the other recalculates every cusp from the composite Midheaven at the latitude of your home. Both begin from your two birth times, so without them there's no [6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) in [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) to speak of ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and even with them, a [Sun](/astrology/sun/) near a cusp may sit in different houses by the two methods.

What the two of you do every day is the 6th house's territory. A relationship like this knows itself through its routines: who makes the coffee, how the week is divided up, the project you've both been refining for years. It's steadied by useful work, and its purpose often involves service: looking after something or someone together, doing a job well enough that others can rely on it. Its care is practical, and it includes the care two people take of each other's days. Even a holiday tends to come with a plan, a list and a division of labour, and both of you rather enjoy that.

Bad Fortune was tradition's name for the 6th, a tradition's word rather than a verdict; it's a cadent house with Virgo as its natural sign, and the joy of Mars ([planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/)). For one person, the [Sun in the 6th house](/astrology/sun-in-6th-house/) puts purpose into the working day; between two, [their Sun in your 6th house](/astrology/sun-in-6th-house-synastry/) marks someone who changes your routines. Put into a composite, the working day becomes shared property, and people who each work best alone can turn out to work very well side by side.

The 6th's traditional link with illness has no place in this reading or any other: a composite Sun in the 6th predicts no health problem for either of you ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)). The real difficulty is that the bond can become all work. A relationship organised around tasks can turn into a to-do list, and improvement can tip into always finding fault. Discipline isn't perfectionism, and one day a week when nothing useful happens can do this bond a great deal of good, as can thanks for the routine work, which is easy to stop noticing.

Coming after the 5th, where a bond plays ([composite Sun in the 5th house](/astrology/composite-sun-in-5th-house/)), and before the 7th, where it meets the world as a partnership ([composite Sun in the 7th house](/astrology/composite-sun-in-7th-house/)), the 6th is where it rolls up its sleeves. I don't work with composites myself; the astrologers who do read them beside both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and never as a forecast. The ordinary courtesy between colleagues applies here too: nobody's chart goes into a composite without their say-so ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
