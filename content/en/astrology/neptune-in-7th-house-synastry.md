This is the overlay people tend to ask about with a catch in their voice. Their Neptune in your 7th house sets another person's ideals at the very place where your chart turns to face someone, and partnership with them can feel like a dream you had half remembered.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) idealises and forgives, and longs for a union larger than everyday life. [The 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) is where you meet your counterpart: a partner in love or in work, an ally, even a rival who stands in plain view. Through this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their longing meets your idea of what two people can be.

With them, partnership can feel unusually tender. You may forgive easily, sense their moods across a room and want to give the bond your best; agreements, whether about a flat or a business, are entered in a spirit of trust. The word that comes up most often is soulmate.

Facing you, they may feel they've found the companion they had imagined for years, and their compassion finally has someone to rest on. Angular, the 7th shows its contents in the people you choose; its natural sign, [Libra](/astrology/libra/), seeks balance between two. On your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), the western angle, their Neptune joins the doorway every partnership comes through, and that conjunction speaks loudest.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Each of you may love the picture more than the person. Small doubts get explained away, uneven terms go unspoken, and a disappointment that's really an ordinary trait can arrive like a betrayal. Contracts signed in a haze of goodwill may say less than you think.

From their side, being idealised has its own weight: they may feel they have to stay the dream, or, if they're the one idealising, find it hard to see you when you need something unglamorous. With an open rival, the same overlay makes opposition hard to read: is this person against you, or simply misunderstood?

No overlay predicts a marriage, a separation or a lie. Neptune here describes ideals in partnership, and ideals become a strength when they're held with clear eyes.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Look at the person, and let them look at you. Name one ordinary flaw each, with affection, and check agreements in daylight, with a third reader if money is involved. In a couple, the dream lasts better when it includes chores, tiredness and differences; between business partners, a contract read twice protects the trust; between friends who feel like soulmates, saying what you need keeps the bond from floating; between rivals, a plain conversation clears a surprising amount of fog.

Your Neptune in their 7th house is the same bond seen from their seat, with your ideals facing them. [Their Neptune in your 6th house](/astrology/neptune-in-6th-house-synastry/) works in the daily round; [their Neptune in your 8th house](/astrology/neptune-in-8th-house-synastry/) goes further, into trust and what you share.

Every generation carries its own dream of the ideal partner, and Neptune's sign is part of that dream. Close in age, you share it, and if your own Neptune lies in your 7th ([Neptune in the 7th house](/astrology/neptune-in-7th-house/)), much of this soft light belongs to your era's hopes as much as to them; with a decade or more between you, two eras' pictures of partnership meet, and comparing them can be tender and illuminating. Their birth date pins Neptune down. Your Descendant, always opposite your Ascendant, moves with your birth time, and in Placidus, the house system I use, it is the cusp of the 7th ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Hopes about a partner are among the most private things a chart holds, so I read them only for two adults who have both agreed ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#what-i-ask)).
