The June solstice is the first degree of the first decan of Cancer: the Sun reaches it on the longest day of the northern year and spends the next ten days crossing the decan.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) holds the face: in Lilly's table of [faces](/astrology/faces/) the Chaldean sequence moves from the Sun, last in Gemini, to Venus, first in [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/). The modern list leaves the decan with the [Moon](/astrology/moon/), Cancer's ruler, which therefore holds these degrees by sign and by decan alike.

A planet in these degrees therefore takes its tone from the Moon first: Cancer's protectiveness and care, the need to feel held and to hold others. The face adds Venus's sense of what is worth keeping close. I look at your Moon's sign and house before anything else, then at your Venus.

The water triplicity complicates the picture, because the old authors disagree about it ([triplicity rulers](/astrology/triplicity-rulers/)). In Dorotheus's scheme Venus rules the water signs by day, so a daytime [Venus in Cancer](/astrology/venus-in-cancer/) between 0° and 10° has two dignities, triplicity and face; Ptolemy and Lilly gave water to Mars by day and by night, and in their scheme only the face is left.

## Its image {#its-image}

Here the Egyptian count began. In the table of decans as later writers set it out, the first place goes to Sepṭet, as Budge transliterates it: Sopdet, the goddess of Sirius, whom the Greeks called Sothis. The star's rising before dawn marked the Egyptian new year and the flooding of the Nile. Firmicus Maternus kept the name, Sothis or Socius, and so did Joseph Scaliger; the Greek Hermetic list writes Sotheir, and the *Testament of Solomon* Metathiax ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

Later lists added figures of their own. Cosmas of Maiuma set Nike, victory, over these degrees, and Athanasius Kircher Apollun. The star that gave the decan its name has moved on, meanwhile: in the tropical zodiac Sirius now stands at 14° Cancer, in the next decan ([Cancer, second decan](/astrology/cancer-second-decan/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun crosses these degrees for births from about 21 June to 1 July ([Sun in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-cancer/)), starting at the solstice itself: in 2026 that came on 21 June at 08:25 UT, and the Sun reached 10° on 1 July at 20:04 UT ([solstices and equinoxes](/astrology/solstices-and-equinoxes/)). Someone born on 20 or 21 June may still have a Gemini Sun ([Gemini, third decan](/astrology/gemini-third-decan/)).

From here on, seen from the northern hemisphere, the signs rise slowly. At the latitude of London these ten degrees take about three quarters of an hour to cross the horizon, more than twice as long as the first decan of Aries ([Cancer rising](/astrology/cancer-rising/)).

Precession carried the fixed star [Menkalinan](/astrology/menkalinan/), β Aurigae, over the Gemini–Cancer line in 2006, so in 2026 it sits at 0°16′ Cancer, at the decan's very start. For Uranus, the birth years run from June 1949 to July 1951, with a return due from 2032 to 2035; for Saturn, they are 1944–45, 1973–74 and 2003–04. A slow planet here, like any placement, describes how something tends to work in you, never what will happen to you ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).
