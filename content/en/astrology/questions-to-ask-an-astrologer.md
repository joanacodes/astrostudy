You can learn a great deal about an astrologer before they've read a line of your chart, simply from how they answer a few plain questions. Here are the ones I'd put to anyone, each with my own answer beside it, so that you can hold me to the same standard. Once you've chosen, [What to expect from a reading](/astrology/what-to-expect-from-a-reading/) goes through what comes next.

## Step by step {#step-by-step}

1. **"Do you need my birth time, and what happens without it?"** The minute you were born fixes the rising sign and the cusps of every house; on the day the Moon changes sign, it fixes the Moon's sign too. My answer: yes, and without it there is no reading ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). If someone offers to read without it, ask what they'll be leaving out. If yours is missing, [How to find your birth time](/astrology/how-to-find-your-birth-time/) is the place to start.
2. **"Which zodiac do you use?"** Ask, because there's more than one. The tropical zodiac ties the signs to the equinoxes and solstices, and so to the seasons; the sidereal zodiac, used in Vedic astrology, keeps them in line with the stars. Over the centuries, the [precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/) has moved the two apart, so a planet can stand in one sign in the first and in another sign in the second. My answer: the [tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/). The [sidereal zodiac](/astrology/sidereal-zodiac/) has its own page.
3. **"Which house system?"** Placidus, Koch, Equal and Whole Sign are a few of the ways of cutting a chart into houses. None of them meets every criterion astrologers have set for a house system, so each one is a compromise, and the debate goes on. Because their cusps differ, a planet near one can sit in one house in one system and in the next house in another. My answer: Placidus ([Which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)).
4. **"Which node, which Lilith, which rulers?"** A chart can show the mean lunar node or the true one, mean Lilith or true Lilith, and Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces each have a traditional ruler (Mars, Saturn, Jupiter) and a modern one (Pluto, Uranus, Neptune). My answer: I look at both nodes, I read mean Lilith, and for those three signs I read both rulers ([Mean node and true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/), [Which Lilith is mine?](/astrology/which-lilith-is-mine/), [Traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)).
5. **"What won't you read?"** A careful astrologer has the list ready before you ask. Mine: health diagnoses and medical questions, death, pregnancy, ongoing legal proceedings, investments and financial decisions, curses to lift, and anyone under 18, whether the reading would be for them or about them. These are the subjects where a chart, even well read, can do harm, and [How I read](/how-i-read/) says why.
6. **"Will you read someone else's chart?"** Ask on what terms. My answer: only an adult's, and never behind their back. For a synastry, the other person must know you're asking for it, and a colleague must agree as well. A chart shows things its owner may never have chosen to show you ([Why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
7. **"How sure are you, and how do you know?"** Listen for what didn't work as well as what did. My answer: I tested my own chart placement by placement, then other people's, with their permission. About a third of what I checked held overall, 30 to 40%, while Saturn, Lilith and Chiron held 75 to 90% of the time. It's a notebook, not a study, and it proves nothing about astrology in general, so I tell you which parts of a reading rest on placements that held less often ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).
8. **"Who prepares the reading, and what will I receive?"** Ask what it costs too, before you pay rather than after. My answer: each reading is prepared with a system I built myself, from my own method, and I read it before it's sent. It comes by email, in a private online space where you can make notes, and as a PDF.

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes}

- **Asking only about your future.** "Will I meet someone this year?" tells you nothing about how an astrologer works, and a careful one won't answer it anyway. Questions about method get you answers you can check.
- **Taking confidence for accuracy.** A smooth answer isn't a correct one. I'd trust "nobody knows exactly how it works, me included" over any guarantee.
- **Asking once you've paid.** Questions cost nothing beforehand. An astrologer who explains how they work only after payment has already shown you how they work.
- **Comparing readings made with different settings.** A different zodiac, house system or Lilith can move a sign or a house without anyone having made a mistake. Check the settings before deciding which reading to believe.
- **Asking about someone who doesn't know.** If the question is really about a partner, a parent or a colleague, the first answer you need is theirs: whether they're happy for their chart to be read.
- **Explaining away a warning sign.** If an answer comes with a curse to lift, a guaranteed result or a request to keep it quiet, stop asking questions and read [How to tell a careful astrologer from a scam](/astrology/how-to-tell-a-careful-astrologer-from-a-scam/).
