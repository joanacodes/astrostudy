Sun sextile Pluto links the two a sixth of the zodiac apart. It describes a strength that doesn't announce itself: the ability to let go of what's finished and begin again, there whenever you decide to use it.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is direction and vitality: the purpose that organises what you do, and the energy you put into it. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) is regeneration: releasing what no longer works, and the deeper strength that comes back afterwards. The [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) between them is an opportunity: the two cooperate, but they need you to open the door.

In plain words, when a job, a plan or a version of yourself comes to an end, you have access to something that can rebuild. You don't need a crisis to use it: you can choose to go further into a subject, a relationship or a piece of work, and come out changed for the better. It can look like a change of career made without fuss, or a home rebuilt after a move and made better than the last. Because a sextile waits rather than pushes, that capacity can go unnoticed for years, until a hard stretch shows you what you had. The depth is on offer, not automatic. As I read it, this is renewal that doesn't need to destroy anything first.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In a chart, it tends to show as resilience without drama: you can change course thoroughly, and quietly. The signs are usually two apart, with elements that suit each other. With Pluto in Libra, a Sun in Leo or Sagittarius gives fairness and partnership a warmer, more personal voice. The houses, which need a birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), show which two areas of life help each other: the Sun's house is where you want to shine, Pluto's where you go deep.

About 1 person in 11 has a Sun–Pluto sextile within 8°. Take Pluto in Virgo: the Sun sextiles it from Cancer about two months before their yearly meeting, and from Scorpio about two months after, so the same Pluto gives two quite different versions. From Cancer, feeling is brought to the work of fixing what's broken; from Scorpio, depth meets depth. The closer to 60°, the more readily the door opens.

The pair's other aspects put the same depth somewhere else:

- **[Sun conjunct Pluto](/astrology/sun-conjunct-pluto/):** renewal built into who you are rather than offered, and harder to set aside.
- **[Sun square Pluto](/astrology/sun-square-pluto/):** the depth pushes from the side, so change arrives as friction and strength has to be claimed.
- **[Sun trine Pluto](/astrology/sun-trine-pluto/):** depth flows in by itself; a quiet intensity that shows when things get hard.
- **[Sun opposite Pluto](/astrology/sun-opposite-pluto/):** the deep end is met in other people first, across the chart.

Between two charts, [Sun sextile Pluto in synastry](/astrology/sun-sextile-pluto-synastry/) makes an easy, steady alliance between one person's purpose and the other's depth: each can help the other change without pressure. I read it in both directions: the person with the Sun and the person with Pluto rarely feel it the same way.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Pluto sextile natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-pluto-sextile-natal-sun/) is a long, steady opening. Pluto moves between one and two and a half degrees a year, so it stays near the sextile for two years or more: a season when a deeper change of direction is within reach, if you take it up. The Sun in the sky sextiles your natal Pluto twice a year, for a day or two. Weather, not a forecast: a sextile describes a door, never what lies behind it.
