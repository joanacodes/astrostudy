The Topocentric house system came from Argentina with an unusual claim: that it was found by looking at lives first and geometry second. What it found was very nearly Placidus.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Topocentric houses, also called Polich-Page houses, are named after the two astrologers who put them forward, Wendel Polich and A. Page Nelson. Like [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/) and [Koch](/astrology/koch/), the system belongs with those that divide the sky by time, and its cusps always fall within a degree of the Placidus cusps for the same chart: the angles, and every cusp between them.

So close a match is no accident. The system can be described as a way of approximating Placidus, reaching nearly the same cusps by a calculation of its own. Where Placidus has very unequal houses, at the latitude of Stockholm for instance, so does Topocentric, and the same signs end up swallowed inside a house ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)).

The word itself is an astronomer's. A topocentric position is one seen from a particular place on the Earth's surface, rather than from the Earth's centre; astronomers need the distinction for precise work, such as tracing the path of an eclipse's shadow across the ground.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

What sets Topocentric apart is how its creators say they reached it. Rather than starting from a principle, a circle to divide or a time to measure, they report working empirically: observing events in people's lives and looking for the geometry of house division that would fit them. The houses they arrived at agree with Placidus to within a degree throughout. Whatever one makes of the method, the result is striking: one route from lives, one from geometry, and almost the same houses at the end of both.

In practice, the gap between them is smaller than the doubt in most birth times. A cusp moves about a degree for every four minutes of clock time, so a time rounded to the nearest quarter of an hour can be seven minutes out: nearly two degrees on every cusp, double the widest gap between Topocentric and Placidus. Before choosing between the two, it is worth knowing how the birth time was recorded ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

The house systems at the other end of the range ignore the horizon altogether. [Morinus](/astrology/morinus/) and the [Meridian (axial) houses](/astrology/meridian-axial-houses/) divide the celestial equator into equal steps of two sidereal hours, and keep their houses close to 30° at any latitude. Topocentric keeps everything that makes Placidus a system of the horizon and of time, its strengths and its unequal houses included.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I use Placidus, and between Placidus and Topocentric the choice rarely changes a reading. A planet more than a degree from a cusp sits in the same house in both. Only a planet within a degree of a cusp can change house, and for that planet I'd want the birth time checked before trusting either system ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)).

What I keep to is the frame. Every house on this site is written for Placidus, so a reader whose chart was drawn in Placidus reads about the same houses I read ([which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)). If your software is set to Topocentric, your signs, degrees and aspects are unchanged, and your houses almost certainly are too; a quick look at the planets nearest the cusps settles it.

The claim of an empirical origin is the creators' own account of how they built the system, and I take it as that. My notebook ticked placements one by one, in my own chart and in other people's, with their consent; comparing house systems was never part of it ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Topocentric is a different way of seeing the chart.”** Its cusps are never more than a degree from Placidus's.
- **“Topocentric was proven by events.”** Its creators report finding it empirically; that is their account of how they built it, not an independent test.
- **“The difference from Placidus matters for everyone.”** Only for a planet within a degree of a cusp, and only if the birth time is good to a few minutes.
