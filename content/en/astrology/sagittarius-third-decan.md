The year turns at the end of this decan: the Sun leaves Sagittarius at the December solstice. Before it gets there, it passes the Scorpion's sting and the direction of our galaxy's centre, all within the same ten degrees.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

After the Moon's face ([Sagittarius, second decan](/astrology/sagittarius-second-decan/)), the Chaldean order begins again with its slowest planet, so the last face of [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/) goes to Saturn ([faces](/astrology/faces/)), and for [Saturn in Sagittarius](/astrology/saturn-in-sagittarius/) these ten degrees are its face. In the modern scheme the last third goes to Leo, the fire sign not yet used, which hands the decan to the Sun.

Few pairs pull harder against each other. The Sun and Saturn rule opposite signs, Leo facing Aquarius, and they trade exaltation and fall: the Sun is exalted in Aries, where Saturn falls, and Saturn in Libra, where the Sun falls. Light and limit share the last ten degrees of the sign of the long view, and I read them as the moment a vision meets its conditions: what you believe, and what it will take.

## Its image {#its-image}

Cosmas of Maiuma names these degrees after Ananke, Necessity, the Greek personification of what cannot be avoided, usually shown holding a spindle. Wallis Budge's Egyptian list has Kenmu here, the Greek-Egyptian lists Konime, and [Firmicus Maternus](/astrology/firmicus-maternus/) Chenene or Chenem, which Joseph Scaliger copied as Chenen.

Beside Saturn's face, Necessity is almost too apt, and I'd rather leave it as a coincidence of the lists than build a reading on it. The sky makes a stronger picture: the Scorpion's sting, [Shaula](/astrology/shaula/) at 24°57′ and Lesath at 24°22′, with the head of the Serpent-bearer, [Rasalhague](/astrology/rasalhague/), at 22°48′.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun crosses these degrees from about 12 to 21 December and leaves them at the solstice, so a [Sun in Sagittarius](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius/) of the third decan is born in the shortest days of the northern year and the longest of the southern. For a birthday on 21 or 22 December, the time settles whether the Sun had reached Capricorn ([Capricorn, first decan](/astrology/capricorn-first-decan/)). For any planet here, I read Saturn's face as the patience to see a belief through, and the Sun's decan as the wish to stand behind it in person.

Two star clusters and a landmark of the galaxy sit here as well. [Aculeus](/astrology/aculeus/) and [Acumen](/astrology/acumen/), Messier 6 and 7, lie near 26° and 29° Sagittarius, and the [Galactic Centre](/astrology/galactic-centre/) at about 27°13′ in 2026; the Sun passes its degree around 18 or 19 December.

Sagittarius rising in its last decan takes about 46 minutes to rise at the latitude of Paris or London, so the Ascendant crosses it a good deal faster than it crossed Libra or Scorpio. A birth time that is only roughly known can't tell this decan from the first degrees of Capricorn ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Three generations carry these degrees: births from early 1979 to late 1984 have Neptune here, those from early 1986 to late 1988 [Uranus in Sagittarius](/astrology/uranus-in-sagittarius/), and those from the end of 2003 to 2008 Pluto.
