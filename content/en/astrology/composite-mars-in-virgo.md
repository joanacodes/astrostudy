The shelves went up level, the receipts are filed, and the two of you have had a long, friendly disagreement about the right way to stack a dishwasher. Composite Mars in Virgo is a relationship whose drive goes into making things work better, and whose quarrels are mostly about how.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[Virgo](/astrology/virgo/), a mutable earth sign that Mercury rules, offers Mars no dignity. In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), the sign colours the midpoint of your two natal Marses, the [Mars](/astrology/mars/) that shows where the relationship puts its effort, and Virgo aims that effort at precision and repair. It's the second most common composite Mars, about 1 pair in 10, just behind Leo: Mars lingers in these two signs longer than in any other.

Such a pair acts by improving. It restores the old bicycle, plans the allotment bed by bed, keeps a shared list that actually gets done, and is at its best with a practical problem in front of it. In the old order of the houses, Virgo corresponds to the 6th, the house of chores and craft where tradition says Mars rejoices ([planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/)); a joy belongs to a house rather than a sign, but this composite shares the same appetite for useful effort. Competition shows as standards: who did it properly, who found the better method. Desire is attentive, the remembered detail, the errand run without being asked, care you can see in what gets done. Where natal [Mars in Virgo](/astrology/mars-in-virgo/) describes a careful worker, the composite describes a careful partnership, with exactness as something you practise together.

Beside it, [composite Mars in Leo](/astrology/composite-mars-in-leo/) wants its effort admired and [composite Mars in Libra](/astrology/composite-mars-in-libra/) wants it shared fairly; Virgo wants it done well.

Disagreements are usually about method. The route, the budget spreadsheet, the order the kitchen gets cleaned in: each of you may have a better way, and the row is about whose. The risk is that correction starts to sound like criticism, and that the relationship tries to fix a person instead of a problem. Analysis isn't criticism, but it needs a warm frame: thanks for what's done, before any word on how to do it better.

I don't use composites in readings; what follows is the method's own view. Its practitioners would read this Mars with the composite [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), Virgo's ruler, which shows how the pair thinks about its tasks, and with the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), where a contact like [Mars conjunct Saturn in synastry](/astrology/mars-conjunct-saturn-synastry/) pits the drive of one against the other's sense of duty. None of it predicts whether you'll stay together or how you'll fare.

Nit-picking is friction. Being watched and corrected until you're afraid of getting something wrong is not, and no chart should be asked to interpret it: Samaritans answer on 116 123 in the UK and Ireland, and 999 is for an emergency. Because a composite looks this closely at two people, both should have agreed to it ([Synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

What helps most is asking before improving. *Do you want a hand, or an ear?* settles half the arguments before they start.
