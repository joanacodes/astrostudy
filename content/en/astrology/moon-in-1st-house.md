With the Moon in the 1st house, your feelings reach the front door before you do. People can often tell how you are from across a room, and you meet each new place by feeling it first.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) is the house of the self: your body, your manner, your first reaction, the impression you give before anyone has met you properly. It is an angular house, and what sits in one tends to show. Its natural sign is Aries. Put the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) here, the chart's keeper of feeling and need, and your emotional responses become part of how you appear. Your expression changes with what you feel; your posture tells people whether you feel safe. Others read you before you speak, and they're often right.

When you were born, the Moon was just below the eastern horizon, about to rise. In Placidus, the house system I use, the 1st house runs down from the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), so a Moon a little above that degree would sit in the 12th instead, and the horizon moves about a degree every four minutes. Whole sign houses would count a Moon anywhere in the rising sign as 1st house. That's why I don't place the Moon in a house without knowing when you were born ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)). Tradition gave this house's joy to Mercury, by the way; the Moon's is the 3rd.

The Moon's sign tells you what kind of responses people see. Its neighbours tell the rest: the [Moon in the 12th house](/astrology/moon-in-12th-house/) keeps its feelings out of sight, where this one wears them, and the [Moon in the 2nd house](/astrology/moon-in-2nd-house/) looks for safety in what it can count on, once it has arrived.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Partners know where they stand with you, because they can see it. Your care is immediate and visible: you light up when someone you love walks in, and it shows when you're hurt, even if you say nothing. That transparency builds trust quickly. It can also leave you feeling exposed, as if your inner weather were always on display. A partner who notices how you are without making a drama of it, and who lets you have an off day without asking you to explain it, helps this Moon relax.

## At work {#at-work}

You tend to bring your whole self to work, and people respond to it. Roles that meet the public, look after people or depend on reading a room can suit you, since you register how others feel almost at once. The difficulty is a job that asks for a neutral face all day: holding back what shows so naturally is tiring. You may also find that your state sets the tone for a team, which is a responsibility worth knowing you carry.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Being read before you're ready. People may respond to a feeling you haven't finished having yet, or decide what you need from your expression. You can take on the atmosphere of a place as if it were your own, simply by walking in. And because your feelings are so visible, you may feel you have to manage them in public, which can be more tiring than the feelings themselves.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Give yourself a pause at the door: a breath before you walk into a meeting, a few minutes alone after a crowded day. Learn to say *I'm fine, it's just my face* when that's true, and *actually, I'm not* when it isn't. Choose some places where you can be seen exactly as you are. And notice what your body tells you first, since with the Moon here it often knows how you feel before your mind does.

In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the Moon's house was checked just as its sign was, and neither stood out: that distinction went to Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, while across the board about one placement in three held. I read a 1st-house Moon, then, as a first impression of the chart, the way people take it as a first impression of you, and then I read on.
