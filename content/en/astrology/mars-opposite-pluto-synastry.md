Two strong currents, facing each other: one pushes outward, the other pulls things down to the root. In Mars opposite Pluto in synastry, the whole width of the zodiac lies between one person's Mars and the other's Pluto, and the question running between them is how strength gets shared.

## What flows {#what-flows}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the direct route: initiative, effort, competition, a line held. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) is the way down: transformation, regeneration, the end of what no longer works, the slow growth of real power. Through an [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) the two stand as poles, and of all the pair's [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/), it shows each person most plainly the strength they use least.

Facing your Mars, the other person's Pluto can teach you depth and patience: that some things change only when you stop pushing and look underneath. Facing your Pluto, their Mars can teach you directness and speed: that a decision you've weighed for months can simply be made. Together you cover both kinds of strength, quick and deep.

Opposite signs move in the same manner and want complementary things. Take Mars in Aquarius and Pluto in Leo, both fixed: an inventive, independent push meets an urge to rebuild pride and creativity from the root. Take Mars in Aries and Pluto in Libra, both cardinal: a go-it-alone push meets a deep wish to remake how partnership works.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

The seesaw tilts when the roles freeze. With the Mars, you may come to experience the other person as the immovable one, the will you keep running into, and push harder each time. With the Pluto, you may come to experience them as the force that never lets up, and answer by holding on to control, or by withdrawing to think. Each can feel that the other holds the power; both are usually wrong.

None of this makes the two of you enemies: you share one seesaw. Neither strength is a weapon either, since Pluto's is the power to renew and Mars's the right to assert, and the work is balance, not victory.

The same two strengths at other angles:

- **[Mars conjunct Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-conjunct-pluto-synastry/):** no distance between them; drive and depth become one concentrated force.
- **[Mars sextile Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-sextile-pluto-synastry/):** a working alliance, four signs nearer, taken up as needed.
- **[Mars square Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-square-pluto-synastry/):** pressure from the side, about pace and say, rather than face to face.
- **[Mars trine Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mars-trine-pluto-synastry/):** stamina flowing through one element, with no balance to strike.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Make agreements explicit and take turns leading. Decide which areas belong to whom, and swap now and then so neither of you is always the one pushing or always the one holding firm. The practice is to hold a position without having to come out on top, and to leave the other room to hold theirs. In a couple, alternate who decides the big things; at work, roles on paper; between a parent and an adult son or daughter, a renegotiation of who has a say in what; between friends, honesty with agreed edges.

This is the rarest contact of the pair: about 1 pair of people in 17 has it, one way round or the other, within 6°. The Pluto end of the seesaw is shared with the Pluto person's whole generation, since a sign holds Pluto for 12 to 31 years; the Mars end, set within a few weeks, seats this particular pair. The hour barely moves either body, but the houses show which parts of each life the balancing happens in, and those need both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Both of you feel it strongly, and neither should hear about it second-hand: a reading of it is for the two of you together ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

When a single chart holds [Mars opposite Pluto](/astrology/mars-opposite-pluto/), other people tend to carry its Pluto end for a while. Between two charts, each person really does carry one end, and the two can hand the weight back and forth.
