About nine years after you were born, the Moon's nodes have travelled halfway round the zodiac, and they cross your chart back to front: the North Node over the place of your natal South Node, the South Node over your natal North Node. Astrologers call this the nodal reversal, and it comes round again about every 18.6 years.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) and the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) mark where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's, and they always face each other across the chart: the [nodal axis](/astrology/nodal-axis/). In the sky they keep sliding backwards through the signs. Your chart keeps their positions at the minute you were born, so at some point the moving pair must lie exactly over the natal pair the wrong way round. That moment is the reversal.

Seen as a transit, it is an opposition: each transiting node stands opposite its own natal place, and both ends of the axis are crossed at once.

It is half of a rhythm. The [nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/), when each node comes back to its own place, falls at about 18½, 37, 56 and 74; the reversals fall in between, at about 9, 28, 46 and 65. Every nine years or so, then, the nodes line up with the axis you were born with, one way round or the other.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The ages hardly vary from one person to the next. Computed with the true node for 160 birth dates, two a year from 1930 to 2009, the first reversal fell between 9.2 and 9.4 years of age in every one. For someone born in 1965, the four came at 9.2, 27.8, 46.5 and 65.1. The nodes move at so steady a pace that the age is almost a constant; only the calendar dates follow your birthday.

How sharp the moment is depends on the node you follow ([mean node and true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/)). The mean node slides backwards evenly and reaches your natal South Node's degree once, on one date. The true node wobbles around it, so it can reach the degree, drift back, and reach it again. I look at both: the mean node dates the centre of the season, the true node's crossings show how long it lasts.

The eclipses come too. An eclipse needs a new or full moon close to the nodes ([why eclipses follow the nodes](/astrology/why-eclipses-follow-the-nodes/)), so during a reversal the eclipses fall along your natal nodal axis again, as they did around your birth and will at your next return. I count an eclipse only when it falls within about 3° of a natal point, at either end of its axis, so not every eclipse of the season touches your nodes.

The ages need only the birth date. Which houses the nodes cross, and so which areas of life the season concerns, needs the birth time; [without it](/blog/unknown-birth-time/), the reversal has nowhere to land, and I don't read a chart that way.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The true node is one of the bodies whose transits I follow in a forecast, so a reversal appears in the timetable I read.

I read it from the two poles of the axis. Your natal South Node describes capacities you consolidated early, the ones you reach for without thinking. When the transiting North Node, the pole of development, crosses them, those familiar strengths tend to be asked what they're for now: kept, but turned towards something new. At the same moment the transiting South Node, the pole of consolidation, crosses your natal North Node, and the less familiar direction you've been practising can start to feel like habit. Old and new trade places for a while.

What I look at:

- **The houses of your natal nodes:** the areas of life where this trade happens.
- **Planets on your natal nodes:** they are crossed as well, and lend the season their own colour.
- **The slower transits of the same months:** a reversal that coincides with one is read with it.

The first reversal, at about 9, falls in childhood, and I don't read [children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/); an adult may look back at it, nothing more. The second, at about 28, comes a little before the first [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/), which falls between about 28½ and 30, so the two seasons often follow closely and I read each in the light of the other. The third and fourth come in the mid-forties and the mid-sixties. Each describes a season in the life of the adult in front of me, which is how I read every transit ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The reversal undoes the progress you've made.”** Nothing is undone. The transiting nodes pass over your natal ones for a while and move on; what you learned stays yours.
- **“It's a karmic test.”** The nodes describe a direction of growth, not a debt. The reversal asks how old strengths and new skills fit together; it doesn't grade you.
- **“It only matters if you have planets on your nodes.”** Planets there add to it, but the reversal comes to everyone, at almost the same ages.
- **“Two people born nine years apart are bound by fate.”** Born about nine years apart, two people do have reversed nodes, one's North Node where the other's South Node is. Everyone born in the same months shares that, which is why I read it as a meeting of two generations more than a bond between two people ([the nodes in synastry](/astrology/nodes-in-synastry/)).
