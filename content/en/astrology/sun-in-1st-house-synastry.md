When someone's Sun falls in your 1st house, their sense of purpose lands on the front of your chart: the way you walk into a room, start things and come across before anyone knows you. People tend to notice this overlay early, from both sides, because it plays out in plain view.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) organises identity and purpose in a chart: what a person is trying to become, and the vitality they put into it. [The 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) is where you start from: your manner, your body, your first reactions. Set one person's Sun in the other's 1st and you have a [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/) in which their direction meets your way of showing up.

For you, it often feels like being lit from the side. You may move more decisively when they're around, speak up sooner, begin the thing you'd been circling for weeks. Their attention lands on you as a person rather than on your work or your plans, and being noticed for how you are, not for what you produce, is rarer than it should be.

For them, you can look strangely familiar. The way you arrive and begin seems to answer a question their own Sun keeps asking, so they notice you early and react to you first. The 1st is one of the four angular houses, where a planet is hard to miss, and [Aries](/astrology/aries/) is its natural sign: the first move, the start of the wheel. When their Sun lies just past your [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), it also meets the degree your whole chart begins from, and I read that conjunction alongside the house.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

A light that falls on your person can also feel like a spotlight. You may catch yourself adjusting your manner to whatever seems to please them, or feel watched on days you'd rather be ordinary. If they're quick to tell you how you come across, advice about your presentation can start to sound like a verdict on who you are.

From their side the difficulty is quieter. When you seem to embody what they're reaching for, it's tempting to treat you as proof that their direction is right, and disappointing when you go your own way. They can feel unexpectedly exposed as well: you react to their purpose in real time, on your face, before you've decided to.

Nobody is in the wrong here. The 1st house describes orientation, the place each of us starts from, and two people can share a starting point for a while without either of them owning it.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Notice whether you feel more like yourself with them, or more on show. If it's the first, tell them; people rarely hear that they bring someone to life. If it's the second, name it plainly and give it time. In a couple, that might be a talk about how you present yourselves together; between friends, permission to turn up unpolished; between colleagues, letting your way of starting a project stand beside theirs instead of copying it.

Seen from the other chair, this page describes your Sun in their 1st house, with the roles turned round. The houses on either side shift the picture: [their Sun in your 12th house](/astrology/sun-in-12th-house-synastry/) touches what you keep private, and [their Sun in your 2nd house](/astrology/sun-in-2nd-house-synastry/) what you value and count on.

Your 1st house begins at your Ascendant, which takes roughly two hours on average to cross a sign, so in Placidus, the house system I use, it can't be drawn without your birth time. Their Sun moves about a degree a day, so their time only decides the house when it sits within a degree or so of one of your cusps; I still start a synastry only with both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). And because this overlay is about how you appear to each other, it belongs in a reading you've both agreed to ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
