Jupiter, in the old scheme, ruled two signs, and your lights stand in both: the Sun in Sagittarius, the Moon in Pisces. You were born around the first quarter, the evening Moon somewhere between a crescent and a little past half. I read the pair as one search going two ways, for meaning out in the world and for a sense of belonging to all of it.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Pisces sits three signs on from Sagittarius, so the lights make a square by sign, both mutable, fire with water. The [Sun in Sagittarius](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius/) goes out looking: for the place, the teacher, the idea that would make sense of a life; the [Moon in Pisces](/astrology/moon-in-pisces/) steadies itself by letting the world's edges soften for a while: the swim, the long film in the dark, the hour spent listening to someone's trouble without trying to fix it. Fire wants to move outward and water to seep inward, and both adapt so readily that the friction can go unnoticed until you're tired.

Here the rulers nearly coincide. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) rules Sagittarius and, in the traditional scheme, Pisces as well; [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) is the modern ruler of Pisces, and I read both. So in the old books Jupiter speaks for both your lights, and its sign and house show where purpose and feeling meet; Neptune, which spends about fourteen years in a sign, adds a generation's colour by sign and a personal one by house. Neither light has a dignity in these signs.

You were born in the neighbourhood of the first quarter. The Moon had moved 61° to 119° ahead of the Sun, five to ten days after the new moon, and set late in the evening. These births divide almost evenly between the [waxing crescent](/astrology/waxing-crescent/) and the [first quarter moon](/astrology/first-quarter-moon/) as eight phases count them, and close to 90° the square is exact, a waxing [Sun square Moon](/astrology/sun-square-moon/). I read a first quarter birth as a time when a feeling has to become an action; for a Pisces Moon, that often means compassion that wants a job. [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) explains the turn at 90°.

About 1 person in 147 is born with this pair. On a day when the Moon moved from Aquarius into Pisces, or from Pisces into Aries, only the time can say whether this page is yours, or the [Sun in Sagittarius, Moon in Aquarius](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius-moon-in-aquarius/), or the [Sun in Sagittarius, Moon in Aries](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius-moon-in-aries/) ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)); near the quarter it settles your phase too. With the rising sign, from the same time, the lights give a first sketch, and nothing more.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You love generously and with imagination. The Pisces Moon senses what a partner feels before it's said and comforts without keeping count; the Sagittarius Sun brings honesty, humour and a belief in where the two of you could go. The square shows when candour meets tenderness: the Sun says the true thing a little too plainly, and the Moon takes it a little too deeply. A partner who is both kind and direct steadies both lights, and so does saying, now and then, *this is what I need*, instead of guessing.

## At work {#at-work}

Work with meaning and a human face suits you: teaching, care, music and the arts, travel with a purpose, charities, any job where understanding people is the skill. The Sun brings vision and momentum; the Moon brings empathy and imagination, the sense of what a room needs. You can inspire people and comfort them in the same week. Hard-edged, purely competitive workplaces drain the Moon, and tidy jobs with no larger point bore the Sun. Choose roles where your ideals have a schedule and your compassion has limits you set.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Two mutable signs give a great deal of give and few fixed edges. You may say yes to every cause, every friend in trouble, every promising horizon, and find the week has dissolved. The Sun can run from a feeling into a new plan; the Moon can soften a hard fact until it is easier to look at. What helps is shape: a routine that doesn't depend on mood, one commitment kept before a new one is made, and time alone on purpose, before tiredness insists on it.
