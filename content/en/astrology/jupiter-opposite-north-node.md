Jupiter opposite North Node puts Jupiter on the South Node: your generosity, faith and wider view belong to the familiar end of the nodal axis. The direction of growth sits across the chart, and the question this aspect asks is how to carry Jupiter's gifts over there.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) is how you grow in understanding and give it out: learning, principles, perspective, a generous reading of life. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) marks the less familiar capacity you build through practice; opposite it, the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) marks what came early and easily. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) relates Jupiter to the North Node through polarity, while its conjunction with the South Node ties it to your established resources.

In plain words, Jupiter's kind of growth comes naturally to you, so naturally that it can become a comfort zone. Teaching what you already know, believing what you've always believed, giving in the ways you always have: all good, and all at the familiar end. The North Node asks for something you've practised less. That doesn't mean giving Jupiter up. It means bringing it along, using a familiar strength to support an unfamiliar step.

An opposition is awareness, not a split. Often other people, or situations you didn't choose, show you the North Node's side first.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

About 1 person in 39 has Jupiter within 6° of the South Node, opposite the North Node: the same small share as the conjunction. In the houses, the axis shows what you're carrying across. Jupiter with the South Node in the 9th and the North Node in the 3rd might describe a broad, well-read philosophy that grows by being put into plain everyday words; with the South Node in the 2nd and the North Node in the 8th, a generous self-sufficiency that grows by learning to share and to receive. The birth time sets the axis ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Mean node or true node, the gap is a degree or two at most, and when the opposition is wide I check both before saying it's there.

Seen from Jupiter's place on the South Node, the other four:

- **[Jupiter conjunct North Node](/astrology/jupiter-conjunct-north-node/):** the mirror image, Jupiter on the growth point and leading towards the new.
- **[Jupiter sextile North Node](/astrology/jupiter-sextile-north-node/):** a light link forward and a trine back, help in both directions.
- **[Jupiter square North Node](/astrology/jupiter-square-north-node/):** Jupiter at right angles to both ends, a crossroads rather than a home.
- **[Jupiter trine North Node](/astrology/jupiter-trine-north-node/):** Jupiter supports the new with ease, keeping only a sextile to the familiar.

Between two charts, [Jupiter opposite North Node in synastry](/astrology/jupiter-opposite-north-node-synastry/) sets Jupiter in one chart on the South Node in the other: comfort and encouragement for what the other person already does well. Lovely, and sometimes a reason to stay put; each side lives it differently.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting North Node opposite natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-north-node-opposite-natal-jupiter/) means the South Node in the sky is crossing your Jupiter, once in each 18.6-year cycle, for a month or two. For a birth with this opposition, that transit returns at each [nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/); halfway between, at the [nodal reversal](/astrology/nodal-reversal/), the transiting North Node crosses Jupiter itself, a time many astrologers read as an invitation to take Jupiter's gifts towards the new. Jupiter, for its part, opposes your natal North Node at every Jupiter return, since it's back where it started.

Weather, here as always, and not a forecast: an invitation to carry something familiar a little further, never an instruction.
