Some pairs build a shelter almost without meaning to: a flat where friends end up on Sundays, a ritual for birthdays, a way of closing the door together on a hard week. A composite Moon in Cancer puts the relationship's needs in the Moon's own sign, and what it needs most is somewhere to come back to.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

A [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) is built from midpoints, and its [Moon](/astrology/moon/), halfway between your two natal Moons, describes how the relationship looks after itself: what it holds on to, what it needs to feel safe, how it responds when life shifts. [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), cardinal water, is the sign the Moon rules, its [domicile](/astrology/domicile/) in tradition. Here the bond's needs and the sign's manner are the same thing: protection, belonging, care that keeps going year after year. A relationship with this Moon tends to take the first step to shelter: it notices before anyone says so that one of you is worn out, and it changes the evening's plan.

That shelter is made by the two of you together, and it may look nothing like either of the homes you came from. Perhaps only one of you was born with the [Moon in Cancer](/astrology/moon-in-cancer/), and the other with a Moon that likes plenty of air; the composite still describes a bond that keeps memories, marks anniversaries and wants a place of its own, however small. It often becomes a home base for other people too: friends who drop in, relatives who are made welcome.

The difficulty is the other side of protection. A bond that guards itself well can also close its doors: a hurt kept indoors instead of said, an outside friendship treated as a threat, a long memory for slights as well as kindnesses. Protection isn't control, and belonging isn't merging. A Cancer composite Moon does best when each of you keeps a life outside the shelter and brings it home.

In its own sign, the composite Moon reports to no other planet. What a composite Moon in Aries would pass on to Mars, this one keeps: its house in the composite and its contacts with the other composite planets carry the whole story. Those depend on both birth times. Without them, a whole day's doubt in one chart lets that person's Moon move about 13°, which leaves the composite Moon about 6½° to wander in, nearly a quarter of the sign ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

In about 1 pair in 12 the composite Moon falls here, between two very different neighbours. The sign before, the [composite Moon in Gemini](/astrology/composite-moon-in-gemini/), settles a feeling by talking it through; the sign after, the [composite Moon in Leo](/astrology/composite-moon-in-leo/), wants its warmth to be seen. Cancer keeps it indoors, among the people it has chosen.

A family composite suits this Moon: a parent and a grown-up child, two adult siblings. Both people have to know, and no child's chart is read here ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#your-child)). Composites aren't charts I draw; I describe how astrologers use them, always beside the two birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). This Moon describes a need for home, not a household to come: it says nothing about moving in, marrying or having children.
