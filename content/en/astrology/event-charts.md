A birth chart is cast for the moment a person arrives. An event chart applies the same idea to anything else that starts: a marriage, a company, a voyage, a city. If the first minute of a life can be read, the reasoning goes, so can the first minute of a venture.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

An event chart is a chart cast for the date, time and place of a particular event and read as though the event were a person in its own right. The chart of a wedding is read as the chart of the marriage; the chart of a national event, as a description of what surrounds it. Its design and the way it is read are those of a birth chart.

The idea is ancient. The Greek word *katarche* means a beginning, an inception; it was first used of the moment a sacrifice was offered. In Hellenistic and Roman astrology it named the art of choosing a good moment to begin something, and also the reading of a chart drawn up for the start of something that had already begun, to judge how a venture would go and when it would develop. The oldest surviving treatise on the subject is the fifth book of the *Carmen Astrologicum* of Dorotheus of Sidon, from the first century ([Dorotheus’ Carmen Astrologicum](/astrology/dorotheus-carmen-astrologicum/)), and in late antiquity Hephaistio of Thebes gave a whole book of his *Apotelesmatics* to inceptions.

It has close relatives on this site. [Electional astrology](/astrology/electional-astrology/) chooses a moment before it comes; the event chart reads one that has come. [Horary astrology](/astrology/horary-astrology/) reads the moment a question is asked: in the late twentieth century, Geoffrey Cornelius argued in *The Moment of Astrology* that horary and inception charts belong together, both readings of a moment. And [mundane astrology](/astrology/mundane-astrology/) reads the founding charts of countries and cities, which Ptolemy already compared to births.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Cast a chart for the event exactly as you would for a birth: date, time, place. Then read it with the same tools, the Ascendant and the angles, the houses, the planets in their signs and aspects, the Moon's condition. In Hellenistic inception charts, the phase of the Moon was probably the single most influential factor; Paulus Alexandrinus, writing in 378, described eleven of them.

The difficulty is the time. A birth certificate may give a minute; an event rarely does. Which moment counts, the first word of the ceremony, the signature, the opening of the doors, is a choice, and each answer moves the angles and houses. An event without a recorded time has the same problem as a birth without one: the parts of the chart that depend on the clock can't be read ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Old medicine had its own inception charts. Nicholas Culpeper's *Astrological Judgement of Diseases from the Decumbiture of the Sick* (1655), one of the most detailed works on medical astrology of its time, took its chart from the moment a sick person lay down, the decumbiture of its title ([Medical astrology, in history](/astrology/medical-astrology-in-history/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

At most, a portrait of a beginning: the conditions a thing started in, described in the language of a birth chart. A company's chart with Saturn strong on an angle, read that way, describes a start under structure and responsibility; it says nothing about profits, and nothing about how long the company will last.

That's where I stop. The tradition read inception charts "to foretell the likely success of a venture", and that is precisely the kind of reading I don't do, whether the event is a wedding, a business or a court hearing ([how I read](/how-i-read/)). A shared beginning between two people is better described by their two birth charts, and by charts built from both, like the [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), read with both people's consent.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

An event isn't a person. It has no temperament of its own beyond the people who carry it, and a marriage, a company or a country is shaped far more by what they do than by the minute it started.

Event charts are also easiest to read after the fact. Once you know how a venture went, something in its chart will always seem to have said so, which is the oldest trap in astrology ([confirmation bias](/astrology/confirmation-bias/)).
