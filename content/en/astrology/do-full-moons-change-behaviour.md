The belief that the full moon brings out the strange in people is widespread, and old enough to be built into the language: *lunatic* comes from the Latin *lunaticus*, used for madness and epilepsy when both were thought to be caused by the Moon. It has also been tested many times.

## The short answer {#the-short-answer}

No, not in any way the research has been able to find. A meta-analysis of 37 studies found no significant link between the Moon's phases and human behaviour, and nearly half of the studies that had claimed one contained at least one statistical error. Sleep is the question still argued over, and there the results disagree with each other.

In a chart, I don't read a [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/) as something that changes how people act. It counts only when it falls within about a degree of a point in your own chart, and even then I read it as weather, never as a forecast.

## What the research found {#what-the-research-found}

**The meta-analysis.** In 1985 James Rotton and Ivan Kelly pooled 37 studies that had examined the Moon's four phases and human behaviour. Together they showed no significant correlation. Of the 23 studies that had claimed to find one, nearly half contained at least one statistical error. Earlier, a 1978 review by Campbell and Beets had also concluded that lunar phases and human behaviour are not related.

**Particular claims.** A review of twenty studies of the Moon's phase and suicides found that most showed no correlation, and that those reporting positive results were inconsistent with each other. A reported link between the Moon and the number of homicides in Miami-Dade County, in Florida, turned out on later analysis not to be supported by the data: it came from inappropriate and misleading statistical procedures. A 2009 study of over 23,000 aggravated assaults in Germany, between 1999 and 2005, found no correlation with lunar phases.

**Light, not lunacy.** One study points to a simpler kind of effect, from the light itself. In the District of Columbia, brighter moonlight had no effect on crime indoors but a positive effect on crime outdoors; its authors suggested that offenders could better size up victims and unsecured property, and that more people were out on lighter nights.

**Sleep, the open question.** In 2013 Christian Cajochen and colleagues at the University of Basel suggested a link between the full moon and the quality of human sleep. The criticism came a year later: their sample was small, 33 people, with inappropriate controls for age and sex, and a study with much larger samples and better controls found no effect of the lunar phase on sleep. Then, in 2021, researchers from the University of Washington, Yale University and the National University of Quilmes found that in the days before a full moon people went to bed later and slept less, even where they had electric light. So on sleep, honestly, the results disagree.

**Why the belief lasts.** It may come down to illusory correlation, the perception of an association that does not in fact exist. Another suggestion is that the belief is a cultural fossil from the time before outdoor lighting, when the bright light of a full moon might have kept people sleeping outside awake. A related habit of mind makes a vague description feel personal: [The Barnum (Forer) effect](/astrology/barnum-forer-effect/). For the same kind of question asked of Sun signs, see [Sun signs and personality: the studies](/astrology/sun-signs-and-personality-the-studies/).

## What I found {#what-i-found}

My notebook asked a different question. It checked charts, placement by placement: Sun, [Moon](/astrology/moon/), rising, each planet in its sign and house, a tick or a cross beside each, first in my own chart, then, with their consent, in the charts of family, friends and strangers.

The Moon was checked like every other placement, and it wasn't one of the three that held far more often: those were Saturn, Lilith and Chiron, at 75 to 90%. Overall, about a third of what I checked held, 30 to 40%.

It's a notebook, not a study, and it proves nothing about astrology in general. It's why, when something in a reading rests on a placement that held less often in my tests, I tell you. The count is in [my testing notes](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/).
