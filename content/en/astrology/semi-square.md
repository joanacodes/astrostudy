The semi-square is half a square: two planets 45° apart, close enough to bump into each other, too far apart to blend. The friction it describes is small and local, the kind you notice in a detail before you notice it anywhere else.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

Forty-five degrees is an eighth of the circle, a sign and a half. Because the angle stops in the middle of a sign, the two planets can be one sign apart or two. From 10° Aries, a semi-square reaches 25° Taurus; from 20° Aries, 5° Gemini. So it doesn't join signs of a fixed kind, and the angle itself carries the meaning.

What I see in a semi-square is localised, constructive tension. Two planets keep catching on each other in one particular place, and the aspect asks for incremental correction: a small change, made again and again, rather than one big decision. It's a rub, not a failure or a punishment, and far too small to be a crisis. Attended to, it sharpens a skill.

I give it a degree or two either side of exact, as most astrologers do for a minor aspect (see [orbs](/astrology/orbs/)), and I keep to the tight end: a semi-square I have to squint to see, I leave out of a reading.

It belongs to the family of eighths that runs from the [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) through 45° to the [square](/astrology/square/) at 90°, on to 135° and the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) at 180°, which is why many astrologers count it among the [hard aspects](/astrology/hard-and-soft-aspects/). It falls 15° short of the [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) and has nothing of the [trine](/astrology/trine/)'s ease, and neither the five major aspects nor Ptolemy's four include it. The difference from the square, as I read it: a square is a collision between two needs; a semi-square is the same rub scaled down to one corner of your life, where a small correction is enough.

## Between which planets {#between-which-planets}

You won't find a page here for Venus semi-square Saturn, or for any other pair: I read the semi-square in context, and the first thing I look at is speed.

Among the personal planets, the Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus and Mars, it's a friction of habit, felt in ordinary routines: how you speak at the end of a long day, how you act in a hurry. The Sun can make it with Venus only when Venus is near its greatest distance from the Sun, about 47°; with Mercury, never. When one of the two is slow, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune or Pluto, a personal need keeps catching on a larger pattern, and patient corrections do more there than any grand gesture. Two slow planets in semi-square belong to a generation; the houses show where that generation's rub reaches you.

On either side of it among the minor aspects sit the [semi-sextile](/astrology/semi-sextile/), at 30°, quieter still, adjustment rather than friction, and, three semi-squares round, the [sesquiquadrate](/astrology/sesquiquadrate/) at 135°: the same tension in two layers, which asks for a rebuild where the semi-square asks for a tweak. That one is among the aspects Johannes Kepler set beside Ptolemy's in *Harmonices Mundi* (Linz, 1619).

## The semi-square in synastry {#in-synastry}

In synastry, the semi-square is the aspect of small, repeated irritations rather than big arguments: one of you moves faster than the other likes in one particular area, or one person's way of keeping a shared space keeps undoing the other's. An adjusted habit, on either side, can ease it a great deal. It shows where a relationship asks for small corrections, never whether it will last, and I read it from both sides, as everything in my [synastry readings](/synastry/).

## The semi-square in transits {#in-transits}

When a moving planet comes within a degree of 45° from one of yours, the push is small. Any planet moving on from a conjunction with one of your planets passes the semi-square before the square, and on its way back round from the opposition it's the last of the hard angles before the next conjunction. The Moon is through it in a few hours, the Sun in a day or two; Jupiter and Saturn can take weeks, the outer planets months. Weather, not a forecast: a semi-square transit points to a small correction worth making, not to an event.
