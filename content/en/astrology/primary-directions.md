Primary directions use the oldest clock in the sky: its turning. In the hours after you were born, the Earth kept rotating, and every planet kept rising, culminating and setting. Primary directions count that motion, degree by degree, as the years of a life.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Astrologers call the sky's daily rotation primary motion, hence the name. The method takes two points of the birth chart: a significator, which stays where it was relative to the horizon, and a promittor, which the rotation carries towards it. The number of degrees of the equator that pass the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) while the promittor travels to the significator gives the year of life, about one degree for one year.

It is very old. Ptolemy mentions directions in the *Tetrabiblos*, in the chapter on the length of life, and again among the timing methods at the end of book IV ([Ptolemy’s Tetrabiblos](/astrology/ptolemys-tetrabiblos/)). The method became widely used in medieval Europe, and it was still being refined in the seventeenth century. Regiomontanus designed his *Tabulae directionum profectionumque*, printed in 1490 after his death, for astrology, house division included. Placidus de Titis, whose name the [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/) houses carry, published *De motibus directionum* in 1641 and his tables of the primary motion in 1657. Jean-Baptiste Morin described it thoroughly in book 22 of his *Astrologia Gallica* (1661), and William Lilly used directions among the tools for correcting an uncertain birth time ([rectification](/astrology/rectification/)).

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

The geometry is the hard part, and the reason the method is rarely used today. In Morin's account, the significator can be a planet, a house cusp or the [Part of Fortune](/astrology/part-of-fortune/); the promittor a planet, one of its aspects or antiscia, or a fixed star. The two meet in the sky's own space rather than along the zodiac. A line is drawn through the significator by the same method that divides the chart into houses (in the [Regiomontanus](/astrology/regiomontanus/) system, its circle of position), and when the turning sky carries the promittor across that line, the direction is complete.

Astrologers long argued over how to place an aspect for this purpose, in the plane of the zodiac or of the equator, which is why there are zodiacal and mundane directions and several other variants. The terms had a place in the method too ([Terms (bounds)](/astrology/terms-bounds/)). Let the significator turn with the sky instead, while everything else stays put, and you get converse directions ([converse progressions](/astrology/converse-progressions/)).

Everything depends on the birth time. The sky turns about a degree every four minutes, and here a degree is about a year, so four minutes of error move a direction by roughly a year ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

In the tradition, the significator named the area of life and the promittor what would come to it, and the completed arc dated the turn. Morin added a condition: a direction counts only when the planets involved are strong, for good or ill, in that area of life in the birth chart. The natal chart decides whether anything is dated at all.

Translated into the way I read, a direction would mark a year when two functions of the chart meet. I don't calculate them. The directions in my forecasts are [solar arcs](/astrology/solar-arc-directions/), which move every point along the zodiac at the pace of the progressed Sun, about a degree a year as well, with no spherical geometry; faster symbolic clocks, like [tertiary and minor progressions](/astrology/tertiary-and-minor-progressions/), aren't part of them either.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

Primary directions were built to predict events, and their defenders claimed a precision of a few months. No chart can date an event, and I won't read one for a date. Their oldest use is the hardest to hear: Ptolemy's directions measured how long a life would last, a question no chart can answer and I never touch ([Can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)).

The variants are a limit too. Zodiacal or mundane, one house system or another: each choice changes the dates, so it matters which ones a program has made. And because a degree is a year, a birth time known only roughly turns every date into a guess.
