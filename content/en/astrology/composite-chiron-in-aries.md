One couple argues with ease and still can't book a holiday; another launches a new plan every month and never says what it wants. With composite Chiron in Aries, the first move is the sore part of the relationship, and the nerve to make it anyway is what the bond slowly builds.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) is the part of a chart that adapts: it works around a limit that doesn't go away, and often turns that work into know-how. In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), built from the midpoints of two birth charts, it shows where the relationship itself keeps meeting such a limit. [Aries](/astrology/aries/), cardinal fire ruled by [Mars](/astrology/mars/), puts it in beginnings: who proposes, who says *I want*, whether a disagreement can be had out loud. A pair with this composite may find that its plans stall at the first step, or that every quarrel feels bigger than its subject, as though the bond weren't sure it had the right to push for anything.

Two people who are bold on their own can be oddly hesitant together, because the hesitation lives in what they make as a pair. So does the way round it: small, deliberate starts, an agreement that either of you may put a plan on the table, a habit of finishing an argument rather than winning it. Such pairs often become good at helping other people begin, a friend's first exhibition, a colleague's risky project, because they know what a first step costs.

In two million pairs of birth dates drawn from 1930 to 2009, about 1 pair in 7 had it: Aries is the commonest sign for composite Chiron, level with Pisces. Chiron crawls through Pisces, Aries and Taurus, so a great many birth charts have it in that stretch, and the midpoints between them keep landing in Aries. Two people both born between April 1968 and March 1977 share their generation's Chiron in Aries, and so does their composite. An age gap moves it to the midpoint. Born in June 1964, one person has Chiron at about 19° Pisces; born in June 1978, the other has it at about 8° Taurus; their composite Chiron sits at about 13° Aries, a sign neither was born with. Fourteen years apart, their two Chirons are only about 50° apart, so slowly was Chiron moving then.

In one birth chart, [Chiron in Aries](/astrology/chiron-in-aries/) describes a person learning nerve by using it. In a composite the nerve has to be found by two, and the impatient one and the reluctant one may swap roles from one plan to the next. The signs on either side tell the same kind of story in other keys: [composite Chiron in Pisces](/astrology/composite-chiron-in-pisces/) is tender about where one person ends and the other begins, [composite Chiron in Taurus](/astrology/composite-chiron-in-taurus/) about what the bond is worth and how safe it feels.

The hard part is haste. A bond that is sore about beginnings can rush to prove it isn't, take on the biggest project available, or treat a pause as a defeat. It helps to count a small start as a real one, to argue about one thing at a time, and to notice who usually goes first, so that the other gets a turn.

Composites aren't something I draw: this is the technique's reading, which the astrologers who use it weigh against both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). It says nothing about how long two people will last, whether they'll marry, or what they'll earn, and like any reading of two people it waits until both have said yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
