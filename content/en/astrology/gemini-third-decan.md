The third decan of Gemini is 20° to 30° of the sign, the last ten days before the June solstice. The Sun holds its face, and the element hands it to Aquarius, a sign with two rulers.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

Lilly's [faces](/astrology/faces/) give the end of [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/) to the [Sun](/astrology/sun/), after Jupiter and Mars. By element, the modern decans give the last ten degrees of an air sign to the ruler of the third air sign, Aquarius, and Aquarius has two: [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) in modern tropical astrology, [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) in the tradition. I read both ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)).

Here the choice of ruler changes what the decan says. Through Uranus, a planet in these degrees wants to try another arrangement, to change a pattern once it has understood it; through Saturn, it wants a structure that will last. I look at where both planets stand in your chart, and if they pull different ways I say so instead of choosing for you. Mercury, ruling the sign, still speaks first.

The Sun's face adds a centre to all that air. [Sun in Gemini](/astrology/sun-in-gemini/) in these degrees stands in its own face, the slightest of dignities, which I read as a curiosity that wants, by the end of the sign, to know where it is heading.

## Its image {#its-image}

Budge's list closes its Saḥ names here. This decan is A-Saḥ, and after it comes an entry that belongs to no sign at all, Saḥ alone, a thirty-seventh name, before Sothis opens the count again at the start of Cancer. Saḥ's constellation took in the stars of Orion ([Taurus, third decan](/astrology/taurus-third-decan/)).

The Greco-Egyptian lists write Phu-hor. Firmicus Maternus gives Tepis, or Atosoae, and Joseph Scaliger runs the two together as Tepisatosoa; the *Testament of Solomon* has Kourtael. Cosmas of Maiuma's figure is Praxidike, and Athanasius Kircher's a Titan ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun stands here for births from about 11 to 20 June. In 2026 it crossed 20° Gemini on 10 June at 21:14 UT and reached Cancer, the solstice, on 21 June at 08:25 UT; in 2027 it does both some five and a half hours later. Around the 21st, the time of birth decides between Gemini and the first decan of Cancer ([Cancer, first decan](/astrology/cancer-first-decan/), [what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Of the fixed stars this site describes, more stand in these ten degrees than in any other decan: Bellatrix at 21°18′ Gemini in 2026, Capella at 22°13′, the three stars of [Orion's belt](/astrology/orions-belt/) between 22°43′ and 25°02′, El Nath at 22°56′, Polaris at 28°55′ and [Betelgeuse](/astrology/betelgeuse/) at 29°07′, which crosses into Cancer around 2090. Many lie far from the Sun's path, so they share a planet's zodiac degree without ever passing near it.

Uranus held these degrees for births from May 1947 to August 1948, with shorter stays on either side, and returns from 2030 to 2033. Saturn passed in 1943–44, 1972–74 and 2002–03. The partial eclipse of the Sun on 12 June 2029 falls at 21°29′ Gemini, and the total eclipse of the Moon on 20 December 2029 at 29°18′: weather for any chart with something in these degrees, not a forecast.

The middle of the sign belongs to Mars and Venus ([Gemini, second decan](/astrology/gemini-second-decan/)); here the decan rulers are the Sun and Aquarius's pair.
