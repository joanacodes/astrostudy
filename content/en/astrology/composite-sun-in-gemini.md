Some relationships are built around a place or a project. One with its composite Sun in Gemini is built around an exchange that never quite finishes, and the two of you are its voices.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Every placement in [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) is a meeting point, halfway between where a planet stood in each of your two charts, and that suits a [Sun](/astrology/sun/) in [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/) well: the relationship's centre is itself an exchange between two positions. Mutable air makes that centre a matter of comparing notes, swapping news, asking the second question. About 1 pair in 12 has a composite Sun here, whatever signs their own Suns are in.

What passes between you is the bond itself: the running joke, the article one of you saves for the other, the evening that starts with a small question and ends three subjects later. Variety feeds it, and a week with nothing new to say can leave it flat. It often exists to connect, bringing together people, places and ideas that wouldn't otherwise meet, and it changes pace easily to suit whatever is going on around it. Exchange doesn't have to be loud, either: for two quiet people it can run through letters, shared playlists, notes left by the kettle.

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) rules Gemini, and here the composite breaks a rule of the sky. In a birth chart Mercury never strays more than about 28° from the Sun, so someone born with the [Sun in Gemini](/astrology/sun-in-gemini/) always has Mercury in Taurus, Gemini or Cancer. Composites ignore that limit: each planet's midpoint is found separately, and in about 1 pair in 18, composite Mercury ends up more than 28° from the composite Sun. A Gemini composite Sun whose ruler sits in Sagittarius is possible, then, and it tells you more about the arithmetic than about the two of you. How each of you actually talks to the other belongs to the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) between your charts, Mercury's above all; the composite only says that the bond is made of that talk.

The difficulty is coherence. With so many threads, the conversation the relationship most needs can be the one it keeps saving for later. Variety isn't inconsistency, and the bond's real work is knowing what all this talk adds up to, for the two of you. A habit of returning helps: a weekly walk on which the same question gets asked among all the others, *how are we?* Its neighbours hold a bond together with something solid, the [composite Sun in Taurus](/astrology/composite-sun-in-taurus/) with what it keeps and the [composite Sun in Cancer](/astrology/composite-sun-in-cancer/) with the shelter it builds; a Gemini bond holds together by curiosity alone, which weighs less and needs renewing more often.

None of this measures a relationship's future, neither its length nor its success. Composites aren't a technique I use in my readings, and the astrologers who do use them read them with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). Because the chart is drawn from two people's birth data, the first conversation it needs is whether both of you want it drawn at all ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
