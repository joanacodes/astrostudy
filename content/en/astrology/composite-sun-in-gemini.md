Some relationships are built around a place or a project. One with its composite Sun in Gemini is built around an exchange that never quite finishes, and the two of you are its voices.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) takes the midpoint of every pair of natal positions and reads the result as the relationship's own chart; its [Sun](/astrology/sun/) shows what the bond organises itself around. In [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/), mutable air, that centre is exchange: comparing notes, swapping news, asking the second question. About 1 pair in 12 has a composite Sun here, whatever their own Sun signs.

A bond like this tends to know itself by what passes between you: the running joke, the article one of you saves for the other, the evening that starts with a small question and ends three subjects later. Its vitality comes from variety, so a week with nothing new to talk about can leave it flat. Its purpose is often to connect, bringing together people, places and ideas that wouldn't otherwise meet, and it adapts quickly, changing pace to suit whatever is going on around it. Exchange doesn't have to be loud, either: for two quiet people it can run through letters, shared playlists, notes left by the kettle.

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) rules Gemini, and here the composite breaks a rule of the sky. In a birth chart Mercury never strays more than about 28° from the Sun, so someone born with the [Sun in Gemini](/astrology/sun-in-gemini/) always has Mercury in Taurus, Gemini or Cancer. Composite Mercury is a midpoint worked out by itself, and it lands more than 28° from the composite Sun in about 1 pair in 18. A Gemini composite Sun whose ruler sits in Sagittarius is possible, then, and it tells you more about the arithmetic than about the two of you. How each of you actually talks to the other belongs to the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) between your charts, Mercury's above all; the composite only says that the bond is made of that talk.

The difficulty is coherence. With so many threads, the conversation the relationship most needs can be the one it keeps saving for later. Variety isn't inconsistency, and the bond's real work is knowing what all this talk adds up to, for the two of you. A habit of returning helps: a weekly walk on which the same question gets asked among all the others, *how are we?* On one side, the [composite Sun in Taurus](/astrology/composite-sun-in-taurus/) keeps a bond going by keeping things; on the other, the [composite Sun in Cancer](/astrology/composite-sun-in-cancer/) by sheltering it. This one keeps it going by keeping you curious about each other.

None of this measures how long a relationship will last, or whether it will succeed. Composites aren't a technique I use in my readings, and the astrologers who do use them read them with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). Because the chart is drawn from two people's birth data, the first conversation it needs is whether both of you want it drawn at all ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
