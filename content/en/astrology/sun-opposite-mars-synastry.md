In a single birth chart, the Sun and Mars hardly ever face each other: about 1 person in 66 has it, always with Mars retrograde. Between two charts it's ordinary, and lively. One person's Mars sits opposite the other's Sun, and the drive of one stands facing the self of the other.

## What flows {#what-flows}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is the centre someone acts from: identity, purpose, vitality. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) is assertion and drive, the force a person brings to a task or a contest. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) faces two functions across the zodiac and asks them to balance; among the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) of this pair, none looks more like a contest, and none is less of one.

The signs face each other in the same mode. A Leo Mars facing an Aquarius Sun: warm, wholehearted drive, facing someone whose purpose is bigger than any one person. A Virgo Mars facing a Pisces Sun: precise, practical effort, facing someone who moves by intuition.

If it's your Sun, you meet in the other person a force you don't use yourself, or not like that: their push, their readiness to compete, their way of saying no. Being around it can strengthen you, the way a good opponent does, and sometimes it can feel like being pushed against.

If it's your Mars, the Sun person is someone your drive can meet head-on. You want to test yourself against them, to challenge, to provoke a little, and also to defend them when someone else tries the same. Here a contest can be a form of respect: you test the people you take seriously.

Sparring partners, negotiators on opposite sides who respect each other, siblings who wrestled as children and still argue for fun, business partners with opposite strengths, a couple who never run out of debates: the opposition can hold them all.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Doing and being sit at the two ends of this seesaw. The Mars person can end up pushing against the Sun person by habit, testing every plan, and the Sun person may spend more energy defending who they are than getting on with it. Contests can turn into the only way the two know how to talk. The work is to stop treating the other end of the seesaw as an opponent.

At closer angles, the drive meets the self differently:

- **[Sun conjunct Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mars-synastry/):** Mars right on the Sun, no distance at all; initiative becomes the question.
- **[Sun sextile Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-sextile-mars-synastry/):** two signs apart; effort offered on request, with no contest attached.
- **[Sun square Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-square-mars-synastry/):** a quarter turn; drive crossing direction, a contest of pace rather than a face-off.
- **[Sun trine Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-trine-mars-synastry/):** 120°, often in one element; the push runs alongside instead of facing you.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Put yourselves on the same side of something. A shared opponent, a shared deadline, a shared mountain: the energy of this opposition is best spent facing outward, together. Take turns leading, and keep contests in their place, with rules. When it gets heated, ask which end of the seesaw each of you is on today, and whether you'd like to swap.

About 1 pair of people in 12 has it, one way round or the other, within 8°. Inside a single chart, Mars faces the Sun only in the middle of its retrograde loop, when the two pull past each other in days; between two people, the Sun and Mars belong to two different birth dates, and the rarity disappears.

The hour of birth hardly alters either body's place, so it seldom decides the opposition; it decides the houses, the stage in each life where the contest is played out, and a synastry waits for both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). In one chart, [Sun opposite Mars](/astrology/sun-opposite-mars/) is a rare, inward version of this face-off. Between two adults, I read it with the respect I'd owe both if they were in the room, which starts with both of them asking ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
