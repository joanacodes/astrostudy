Asbolus means sooty, the black one. The centaur who carried the name was a seer who read omens in the flight of birds, and the small icy body named after him follows an orbit astronomers can't predict far ahead.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Asbolus is a centaur about 80 km across. Its orbit takes about 76 years and is strongly stretched: at its closest it comes 6.8 times the Earth's distance from the Sun, between the orbits of Jupiter and [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), as it last did in July 2002; at its farthest, 29.1 times, it almost reaches the orbit of [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/). It will next be closest in December 2078.

That path keeps it under the pull of three giant planets at once, and it is hard to follow far ahead. Astronomers class it among the centaurs whose closest point Saturn controls and whose farthest Neptune does, but Jupiter tugs on it too, and computed copies of its orbit suggest Jupiter may take over within about ten thousand years. An encounter with Jupiter in some 2,700 years makes its path unpredictable after that, and its orbit is much more chaotic than that of [Nessus](/astrology/nessus/).

James Scotti and Robert Jedicke of the Spacewatch survey found it at Kitt Peak, Arizona, on 5 April 1995. In 1998 the Hubble Space Telescope saw something unusual on its surface. Centaurs are dark, because sunlight and the solar wind darken their ice over long ages, but Asbolus showed a bright patch of fresh ice, which astronomers took for a crater less than 10 million years old, dug through the dark crust. Its name was published on 28 September 1999, the same day as that of [Chariklo](/astrology/chariklo/).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

In myth Asbolus was a centaur and a seer: Hesiod calls him an augur, one who read omens in the flight of birds. Ovid gives him his best scene. At the wedding of Pirithous, where the centaurs fought the Lapiths, Asbolus had warned his friends not to fight, and no one heard him. Seeing Nessus run, he called out that there was no need: Nessus would be safe until the day Heracles's arrow found his back. Nessus's own page tells how that ended.

He turns up again in [Pholus](/astrology/pholus/)'s story. In the version recorded by the Byzantine scholar Tzetzes, it was Asbolus who saw Pholus open the centaurs' wine for Heracles and brought the rest of the herd, and the fight that followed cost Pholus and Chiron their lives. His fellow centaur [Hylonome](/astrology/hylonome/) belongs to the same wedding battle.

For anyone placing Asbolus in a chart, the restless orbit matters. At 76 years a circuit it spends years in a sign, unevenly, since a stretched orbit hurries through some signs and lingers in others; the sign is shared across a span of births, the house depends on the birth time ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and it would take a contact within a degree or two of a personal planet or an angle to make it yours.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

A seer nobody heeded makes a tempting emblem for warnings, and I don't read Asbolus as one, or as anything else. The system I built for my readings computes the planets, Chiron, Lilith and the lunar nodes, and of the centaurs only Chiron is there. The augur read birds; my notebook read placements, a tick or a cross for the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign, each planet, Chiron and Lilith ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)), and Asbolus had no line in it, so there's no figure to quote.

If you meet it in someone's chart, it's worth remembering what kind of prophet Asbolus was: he named the danger in front of his friends and advised them; the choice was theirs. That's closer to what a reading should do than any forecast is ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Asbolus is a dark, dead rock.”** It is dark, like all centaurs, but Hubble found a patch of fresh ice on it, the trace of an impact less than 10 million years ago.
- **“Asbolus started the fight at Pholus's cave.”** In the version that gives him a part, he fetched the herd after Pholus had opened the wine they all shared; the arrows were Heracles's.
- **“Centaur orbits are fixed.”** They change. Asbolus's will become unpredictable after an encounter with Jupiter in some 2,700 years.
