Venus conjunct Regulus is the contact the Lion's heart receives most often: about 1 person in 146 born between 1930 and 2009 has Venus within a degree of the star. The reason lies in the eight-year rhythm Venus keeps with the Sun, which brings it back to the same stretch of sky in the same season.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read [Venus](/astrology/venus/) as the part of a chart that weighs and chooses: what you find beautiful, what you value enough to keep, how you like to give and receive affection. Ptolemy's description of [Regulus](/astrology/regulus/) has nothing of Venus in it; he compares the star to Mars and Jupiter, and its names call it a king. In the tradition's terms, then, Venus on Regulus is taste and affection given a regal bearing, with Mars's push and Jupiter's breadth in them. I pass that on as the old books' colour: Venus keeps its own work, and its sign, house and aspects describe it better than any star.

The frequency has a tidy explanation. Venus repeats its pattern with the Sun every eight years, and between 1967 and 2007 that pattern brought its retrograde loop onto Regulus every eighth summer: forward over the star in July, back over it in August or September, forward again around the start of October. Three passes in one season instead of one is why the star meets Venus more than any other body. A birth during the backward pass has Venus retrograde on the star ([Venus retrograde in Leo](/astrology/venus-retrograde-in-leo/), [natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)).

Regulus sits almost on the ecliptic, so Venus can pass right over it, and on 7 July 1959 it did: the last time any planet hid the star. When Venus turns back over it, near its closest approach to the Earth, it runs seven to nine degrees south of the star instead, and the two share a degree of the zodiac, not a place in the sky.

Venus never strays more than about 47° from the Sun, so Venus on Regulus belongs only to births between about 6 July and 8 October, with the Sun anywhere from Cancer to Libra. On a handful of days it shares the star with the Sun itself, as on 22–23 August in 1987, 1991 and 1995 ([Sun conjunct Regulus](/astrology/sun-conjunct-regulus/)). For births from 1930 to 2009 this is a [Venus in Leo](/astrology/venus-in-leo/), in the sign's last degrees. Since about 2012 the star has stood in Virgo, and a Venus on it there sits in the sign the tradition calls its [fall](/astrology/fall/), where Venus is said to judge more carefully, not to love less.

In my notebook Venus held about a third of the time, like the Sun sign, the rising sign and Mercury, and no star was ever on the list, so a reading of mine leans on Venus's sign and house and leaves Regulus out ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). Mars, the planet astrologers read beside Venus for desire, reaches the same star about every two years ([Mars conjunct Regulus](/astrology/mars-conjunct-regulus/)). The [Moon conjunct Regulus](/astrology/moon-conjunct-regulus/) can only be confirmed with a birth time, where Venus needs little more than the date, and [Saturn conjunct Regulus](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-regulus/) belongs to a few months each generation. All of them follow the one-degree rule set out under [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/), within the wider lore of [fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/).
