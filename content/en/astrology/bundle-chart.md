Most charts spread their planets round much of the wheel. A bundle chart doesn't: every planet, from the Sun to Pluto, sits within a third of the circle, and the rest of the chart is open space.

## What it looks like {#what-it-looks-like}

In a bundle, the ten planets fit within 120°, about four signs, and two-thirds of the wheel hold none of them. It's the tightest of the seven shapes [Marc Edmund Jones](/astrology/marc-edmund-jones/) described, and it's what a [bucket](/astrology/bucket-chart/) or a bowl becomes when everything is drawn in closer still. Inside it, the major aspects can only be short ones: conjunctions, sextiles, squares and, at the very most, a trine across its whole width. There's no room for an opposition between two of its planets.

A bundle needs the sky to cooperate. The slow planets have to stand within a third of the circle of each other, which happens only in certain stretches of years, and the faster ones have to gather on the same side at the same time. A bundle can hold a [stellium](/astrology/stellium/) or two inside it, but the shape describes something larger than any crowd in one sign: the whole chart working within one stretch of the zodiac.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The count behind this site found about 1 person in 56 born from 1930 to 2009 with a bundle, all ten planets within 120°: the rarest of the four shapes it checks. Shift that line by a few degrees either way and the share would move noticeably. The count also leaves out the Ascendant, the Midheaven, Chiron, Lilith and the nodes, which can fall in the empty space and give it a point of its own.

Astrologers who use the shapes read a bundle as concentration: someone who specialises, persists, goes deep into a few things. I'd say it more carefully. A bundle shows where attention gathers, and it tends to gather there for a long time, because every function in the chart is working within the same few signs and houses. The empty two-thirds aren't empty of life: they're where other people, places and experiences come in from outside, rather than where your own planets already stand. A cluster this tight still describes emphasis, not character, and I read its ten planets one by one before I read the bundle.

A bundle of four signs can fill three houses or five, and which ones decides a great deal: the same bundle across the 10th and 11th houses, or across the 4th and 5th, describes two different lives. The houses need the time of birth, and so does the Moon, which may sit just inside the bundle or just outside it depending on the hour. That's why I [wait for the birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/) before reading any shape.

## What helps {#what-helps}

- **Go deep on purpose.** Concentration is a strength when you choose what to concentrate on.
- **Let the empty space in.** The open part of the wheel is where you meet what you don't already contain. Leave room in your life for it.
- **Find the first and last planets.** The two at the edges of the bundle are where it meets the empty space; their functions often say how you go out to meet the world.
- **Use the aspects inside.** Squares within the bundle give it friction to work with; its sextiles and trines, easy ways to move between its parts.

Spread it a little wider, to two-thirds of the circle with a third left empty, and you have a [locomotive](/astrology/locomotive-chart/), the commonest shape of all in the count. A bundle is a chart with its weight in one place: steady to stand on, and best balanced by whatever the open part of the wheel brings in.
