The first decan of Leo is 0° to 10° of the sign, the start of fixed fire, which the Sun crosses in the last week of July. The Sun rules it twice, by sign and by element, and Saturn, the planet that tradition finds least at home in Leo, holds its face.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

Once the Moon has closed Cancer, the Chaldean order starts again from the top, with [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), so Lilly's [faces](/astrology/faces/) open [Leo](/astrology/leo/) with it. Saturn is the traditional ruler of Aquarius, the sign opposite (Uranus is its modern one), and tradition puts it in [detriment](/astrology/detriment/) in Leo. By element, the modern decans keep the first decan with the sign's own ruler, the [Sun](/astrology/sun/).

The two lists could hardly differ more. The Sun organises identity, vitality and the wish to be seen; Saturn brings structure, limits and the long view. Here the modern list gives the decan to the sign's own light, and the old one to the planet of the opposite sign. In a reading I let the Sun speak first, as ruler of both the sign and the decan, and take Saturn's face as a reminder that expression lasts when it is given a form.

[Saturn in Leo](/astrology/saturn-in-leo/) in these degrees carries both labels at once, detriment by sign and dignity by face. I read it as confidence learned slowly and creativity with a backbone, and the face as the small credit the tradition gave Saturn even here.

## Its image {#its-image}

Budge's list ends its run of Kenmut names here, with Kher-khept-Kenmut, after Ṭepā-Kenmut and Kenmut in Cancer. The Greco-Egyptian name, Char-Knum, answers the Knum of the decan before ([Cancer, third decan](/astrology/cancer-third-decan/)), and the Greek Hermetic list writes Chnoumos. Firmicus Maternus has Craumonis, or Afruicois, and Joseph Scaliger Aphruimis; the *Testament of Solomon* calls it Phobothel.

Cosmas of Maiuma gave these degrees to Hephaistos, the smith of the gods, and Athanasius Kircher to Typhon, the monster of Greek myth. A smith god over the first degrees of a fire sign is the kind of fit that tempts a reader; I note it and leave it there ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun is here for births from about 23 July to 1 August ([Sun in Leo](/astrology/sun-in-leo/)). In 2026 it entered Leo on 22 July at 19:13 UT and reached 10° on 2 August at 06:23 UT; in 2027, on 23 July at 01:04 UT and 2 August at 12:09 UT.

Leo rises slowly in the northern hemisphere: at the latitude of London each of its decans takes nearly an hour to cross the horizon, three times as long as one of Aries. That makes the decan of a [Leo rising](/astrology/leo-rising/) one of the easier details to fix, though it still depends on a birth time recorded to within half an hour or so ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Pluto held these degrees for births from June 1939 to October 1944, with visits from late 1937 and until mid-1946 ([Pluto in Leo](/astrology/pluto-in-leo/)). Uranus followed from mid-1956 to September 1957, after a first visit in late 1955 and before a last one in the first half of 1958. Saturn passed in 1946–47, 1975–77 and 2005–06, and comes back in 2034–35.

The total eclipse of the Sun on 2 August 2027 falls at 9°55′ Leo, in the decan's last degree ([Eclipse in Leo](/astrology/eclipse-in-leo/)). For a chart with a planet or an angle there, it is a sky event to notice, never a prediction about anyone.

Ten degrees on, Jupiter takes over both lists ([Leo, second decan](/astrology/leo-second-decan/)).
