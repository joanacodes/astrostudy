The Sun comes home in the first decan of Leo: these ten degrees open the sign it rules, and it crosses them in late July. Their face belongs to Saturn, the planet the tradition finds least at home in Leo.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

The Chaldean sequence ends with the Moon and begins with [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/); since the Moon has just closed Cancer, Lilly's [faces](/astrology/faces/) give the start of [Leo](/astrology/leo/) to Saturn. That is the traditional ruler of Aquarius, the opposite sign (Uranus is its modern one), and the tradition puts Saturn in [detriment](/astrology/detriment/) in Leo. The modern list, meanwhile, hands the decan to Leo's own ruler, the [Sun](/astrology/sun/).

The two lists could hardly differ more. The Sun organises identity, vitality and the wish to be seen; Saturn brings structure, limits and the long view. Here the modern list gives the decan to the sign's own light, and the old one to the planet of the opposite sign. In a reading I let the Sun speak first, as ruler of both the sign and the decan, and take Saturn's face as a reminder that expression lasts when it is given a form.

[Saturn in Leo](/astrology/saturn-in-leo/) in these degrees carries both labels at once, detriment by sign and dignity by face. I read it as confidence learned slowly and creativity with a backbone, and the face as the small credit the tradition gave Saturn even here.

## Its image {#its-image}

With Kher-khept-Kenmut, the third name built on Kenmut, Budge's list closes the run it began in Cancer with Ṭepā-Kenmut and Kenmut. The Greco-Egyptian Char-Knum likewise answers the Knum of the decan before ([Cancer, third decan](/astrology/cancer-third-decan/)), and the Greek Hermetic list turns it into Chnoumos. For Firmicus Maternus the decan is Craumonis, or Afruicois, for Joseph Scaliger Aphruimis, and the *Testament of Solomon* names it Phobothel.

The two later lists chose a craftsman and a monster: Hephaistos, the smith of the gods, in the list of Cosmas of Maiuma, and Typhon, from Greek myth, in that of Athanasius Kircher. A smith god over the first degrees of a fire sign is the kind of fit that tempts a reader; I note it and leave it there ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In its own sign at last, the Sun spends about 23 July to 1 August in this decan ([Sun in Leo](/astrology/sun-in-leo/)). Its 2026 entry into Leo came at 19:13 UT on 22 July, and it moved on to 10° at 06:23 UT on 2 August.

Leo rises slowly in the northern hemisphere: at the latitude of London each of its decans takes nearly an hour to cross the horizon, three times as long as one of Aries. That makes the decan of a [Leo rising](/astrology/leo-rising/) one of the easier details to fix, though it still depends on a birth time recorded to within half an hour or so ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Pluto's first years in Leo fall here: births from June 1939 to October 1944, with visits from late 1937 and until mid-1946 ([Pluto in Leo](/astrology/pluto-in-leo/)). Uranus followed from mid-1956 to September 1957, after a first visit in late 1955 and before a last one in the first half of 1958. Saturn's passages came in 1946–47, 1975–77 and 2005–06, and the next is due in 2034–35.

The total eclipse of the Sun on 2 August 2027 falls at 9°55′ Leo, in the decan's last degree ([Eclipse in Leo](/astrology/eclipse-in-leo/)). For a chart with a planet or an angle there, it is a sky event to notice, never a prediction about anyone.

Ten degrees on, Jupiter takes over both lists ([Leo, second decan](/astrology/leo-second-decan/)).
