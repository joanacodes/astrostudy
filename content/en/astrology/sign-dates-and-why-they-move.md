Every sign comes with a pair of dates, and every honest list puts "about" in front of them. The Sun doesn't change sign at midnight on a fixed day: it changes at a moment, and the moment moves a little every year.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Sign dates are simply the stretch of the year the Sun spends in each sign of the tropical zodiac. Here they are, as I give them across this site:

- [Aries](/astrology/aries/): about 21 March – 19 April
- [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/): about 20 April – 20 May
- [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/): about 21 May – 20 June
- [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/): about 21 June – 22 July
- [Leo](/astrology/leo/): about 23 July – 22 August
- [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/): about 23 August – 22 September
- [Libra](/astrology/libra/): about 23 September – 22 October
- [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/): about 23 October – 21 November
- [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/): about 22 November – 21 December
- [Capricorn](/astrology/capricorn/): about 22 December – 19 January
- [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/): about 20 January – 18 February
- [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/): about 19 February – 20 March

Each sign is exactly 30° of the Sun's path, counted from the March equinox, so the dates follow the seasons: Aries opens at the March equinox, Cancer at the June solstice, Libra at the September equinox and Capricorn at the December solstice ([solstices and equinoxes](/astrology/solstices-and-equinoxes/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Three things make the dates move.

- **The calendar.** The Sun takes about 365.2422 days to come back to the same point of the seasons, a little under 365 days and six hours. A calendar year has 365 days, so each year the Sun reaches the start of a sign nearly six hours later by the clock, until a leap day pulls it back by a day. Over the centuries, the leap-year rule keeps the calendar and the seasons in step, so the dates don't wander far.
- **The time zone.** The Sun enters a sign at one instant for everyone on Earth, but clocks don't agree on the date. If it changes sign at one in the morning in London, it's still the evening before in New York ([time zones and daylight saving in charts](/astrology/time-zones-and-daylight-saving-in-charts/)).
- **The Sun's speed.** The Earth moves fastest in its orbit in early January, when it's closest to the Sun, and slowest in early July. So the Sun crosses Capricorn in between 29 and 30 days and Cancer in between 31 and 32, and the signs never line up with equal months.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

For most people, the list is all they need: born on 5 May, you have the Sun in Taurus, whatever the year. Within a day or so of a change, though, the list can't settle it, and neither can I without the time. Then the date, the time and the place of birth decide, and the chart shows the Sun's exact degree ([born on the cusp](/astrology/born-on-the-cusp/)).

It's one more reason I don't read a chart without a birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). The time decides the rising sign and the houses, the Moon's sign on the day it changes, and the Sun's on the day the Sun changes. I write "about" before the dates on every page of this site for the same reason: they're the usual span, not a rule.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Sign dates are fixed.”** The boundaries move by up to a day from year to year, and with the time zone. Every list is a rounding.
- **“Two websites with different dates can't both be right.”** They can: one may be built from a different year, or a different time zone, from the other.
- **“The dates have shifted because of precession.”** Precession moves the stars behind the signs, not the tropical signs: Aries still begins at the March equinox.
- **“Born on the first day the list gives, you belong to the new sign.”** Only the moment of birth settles it, and the change can fall the day before or the day after.
