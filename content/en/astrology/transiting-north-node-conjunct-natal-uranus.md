Uranus takes about seven years to cross a sign, so the degree it held when you were born is shared with everyone born within months of you. When the North Node slides back over that degree, a whole age group meets the same transit in the same weeks, and each person meets it in a different house.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) is where a chart differentiates: autonomy, reform, the refusal to repeat what was handed down. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) is the less practised capacity your chart grows towards. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) fuses them, so under this [transit](/astrology/transits/) growing and doing things your own way become a single movement: you learn by trying the alternative.

It often shows as one experiment you have been putting off. A new way of working, a group you want to join or leave, a rule of your own you decide to keep. Uranus's blind spots come along too: mistaking novelty for progress, changing a system before finding out what still works in it. Across the chart the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) sits opposite your Uranus, on the familiar habits that keep things steady; they are worth keeping, as the ground the experiment stands on.

The generation shows it first. While the node crosses Aquarius, from July 2026 to March 2028, it reaches the Uranus of everyone born with [Uranus in Aquarius](/astrology/uranus-in-aquarius/), from April 1995 to December 2003, youngest first, from the autumn of 2026, and the eldest, born in 1995 and early 1996, last, in March 2028. Aquarius is Uranus's sign in modern tropical astrology and Saturn's in the tradition; I read both. The person shows it in the house: with Uranus in your 11th, the conjunction comes during the node's stay there ([North Node transiting the 11th house](/astrology/north-node-transit-11th-house/)), among friends, groups and causes. Your 11th needs your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

The window I use opens 1½° before exact and closes 1° after, and the true node usually needs about a month to cross it. In an eclipse season it can take three months or more, as the node hangs near your Uranus while the Sun passes the nodes, and about one pass in 25 brings three exact hits. Eclipses can reach your Uranus only in the year or so around this transit and around the opposition: the annular eclipse of 6 February 2027, at 17°37′ Aquarius, falls within 3° of the Uranus of many born in 1999 and 2000, a few months from the node's own crossing.

The first conjunction comes at an age set by your birth date: 2.0 for someone born on 1 January 1975, 8.2 for 1 January 1985. After that it returns every 18.6 years, four or five times by 80. With [Uranus conjunct North Node](/astrology/uranus-conjunct-north-node/) in your chart, it comes back at each nodal return.

About three years on, the node sextiles your Uranus ([Transiting North Node sextile natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-north-node-sextile-natal-uranus/)), then squares it ([Transiting North Node square natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-north-node-square-natal-uranus/)) and trines it ([Transiting North Node trine natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-north-node-trine-natal-uranus/)), until the South Node arrives about nine years later ([Transiting North Node opposite natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-north-node-opposite-natal-uranus/)).

## What helps {#what-helps}

Run one experiment on purpose, with a start date and an end date, rather than changing everything at once.

Before reforming something, list what still works in it. The change lands better on what you decided to keep.

Talk to people your own age about it. They are meeting the same transit, and their versions can show you options yours didn't.
