Nine years or so after the North Node crosses your Pluto, the South Node takes its turn. For a month or so, the strengths you built to get through hard times sit at the familiar end of the axis, and the less practised direction asks whether they still need to grip so hard.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) is where a chart survives and renews itself: it reorganises what has become unsustainable and gives back strength after loss. The [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) is capacity already consolidated, the reflex you reach for first; the [North Node](/astrology/north-node/), opposite, is capacity still in practice. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) relates two poles through awareness, so under this [transit](/astrology/transits/) your Pluto is seen from the end of long habit.

What tends to show is the survival skill that outlived its emergency. Keeping control of every detail. Expecting the worst so as never to be caught out. Holding a secret long after it needed keeping. These came from somewhere real, and the South Node doesn't ask you to drop them; it shows where the grip has become heavier than what it guards. Across the chart, the North Node often arrives through another person's lighter way of handling the same thing. Pluto's old links with death make some people uneasy here, so plainly: no transit foretells a death, an illness or a loss ([Can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)).

A large generation meets it in these months. From July 2026 to March 2028 the South Node crosses Leo, and with it the Pluto of everyone born with [Pluto in Leo](/astrology/pluto-in-leo/), from October 1937 to June 1958, about 1 person in 4 of all those born from 1930 to 2009. The total solar eclipse of 12 August 2026, at 20°02′ Leo, fell within 3° of the Pluto of many born from 1950 to 1953, about six months before the South Node itself arrives. Your own houses, drawn from your birth time, say where it lands ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

From 1½° before exact to 1° after, the South Node usually takes about a month over your Pluto. Caught by an eclipse season, the node lingers near the degree while the Sun is close, and the pass lasts three months or more; about one pass in 25 brings three exact hits. In the year or so around it, an eclipse at the South Node end may fall on your Pluto and count as a contact of its own ([eclipses on your natal planets](/astrology/eclipses-on-your-natal-planets/)).

It comes once a round, four or five times by 80. The first came at 8.3 for someone born on 1 January 1945 and at 15.9 for 1 January 1955. With [Pluto opposite North Node](/astrology/pluto-opposite-north-node/) in your chart, your Pluto sits on your natal South Node, and the nodal return brings this transit back each time.

Three years or so either side come [Transiting North Node trine natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-north-node-trine-natal-pluto/); beyond them, [Transiting North Node square natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-north-node-square-natal-pluto/) and [Transiting North Node sextile natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-north-node-sextile-natal-pluto/); and nine years away, [Transiting North Node conjunct natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-north-node-conjunct-natal-pluto/).

## What helps {#what-helps}

Name one way you learned to stay safe, and ask whether the danger it answered is still here.

Let someone else hold one thing you usually control, for a week, and see what happens.

Keep the strength and drop the armour. What got you through is still yours without the grip.
