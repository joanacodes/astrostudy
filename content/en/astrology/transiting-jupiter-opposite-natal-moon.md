Halfway between two crossings of your Moon, Jupiter stands directly across the zodiac from it. Home sits at one end of the line and the wider world at the other, and for a while both want you.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) reaches for a larger view; the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) keeps you feeling safe and tends to your needs. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) sets them facing each other along one line, each making the other easier to see. Under this [transit](/astrology/transits/), growth tends to arrive from outside the places that comfort you, and by contrast the comfort becomes clearer too.

It can be another person's large plan that needs your support, a course or a trip that would take you away for a while, an invitation into a wider circle. The pull runs both ways: some people feel homesick for a life they haven't left, others find they've outgrown a comfort they'd defended for years. The opposition doesn't decide between them. It shows you what each side is worth. Since the Moon is also the way you respond, you may see your own reactions in someone else for a while: a friend who says yes to everything, a partner who can't sit still.

Tradition drew the same line long ago. Jupiter's exaltation is in Cancer, which the Moon rules, and its [fall](/astrology/fall/) in Capricorn opposite, where the Moon is in detriment: the axis that runs from home to public life holds both Jupiter's best place and its hardest.

In Placidus, the house system I use, this opposition always comes while Jupiter crosses the house across from your Moon's, because opposite cusps sit exactly 180° apart. With your Moon in the 3rd, where tradition gave the Moon its joy, the opposition comes during [Jupiter transiting the 9th house](/astrology/jupiter-transit-9th-house/): the near and familiar set against study, belief and distance. If your chart has [Moon opposite Jupiter](/astrology/moon-opposite-jupiter/), every Jupiter return brings this transit, since Jupiter back at its birth degree faces your Moon.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Within my orbs, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the opposition holds for about eleven days in seven cases out of ten. In the other three, Jupiter's retrograde loop crosses the point three times and keeps it near for about eight months.

A Moon near a house cusp may sit in one house or the next depending on a few minutes of birth time, and the house Jupiter opposes it from moves with it. With no time, there's no house to read, and I leave the chart unread ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

By 80, people born between 1930 and 2009 have felt home and the wider world pull this way six or seven times, each about six years after [transiting Jupiter conjunct natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-conjunct-natal-moon/). On its way here, Jupiter has offered your Moon support ([transiting Jupiter sextile natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-sextile-natal-moon/)), pressed on its habits ([transiting Jupiter square natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-square-natal-moon/)) and eased its moods ([transiting Jupiter trine natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-trine-natal-moon/)); the road back holds the same three, ending at the next crossing.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Let other people's plans show you your own needs. When something far away tempts you, ask what it would give you that home doesn't, and whether home could make room for it.

Don't settle everything at once. The opposition tends to present home and the wider world as a choice; more often it is a balance you can adjust as you go.

Keep one anchor: a routine, a person or a place that stays put while the rest stretches.
