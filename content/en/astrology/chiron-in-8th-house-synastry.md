Some bonds reach the place where people keep what they've never said. With their Chiron in your 8th house, the other person's old hurt, and the steadiness it taught them, comes into your intimacy, your trust and what the two of you share.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) holds what someone has had to live with and the resilience that grew around it: the capacity to keep functioning, and to stay present, when something remains hard. [The 8th house](/astrology/8th-house/) covers what a bond builds and risks once it's real: closeness, sexual and otherwise, the price of trusting, joint money, loans, what's owed. As a [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their resilience meets your private depths.

With them, you may say what you've never said. They tend not to flinch at hard truths, and they can stay beside you in a difficult conversation without hurrying it to a tidy end. When shared matters get complicated, a joint account, a loan, a promise, they may be the one who insists, gently, on everything being out in the open, because they know what secrecy costs.

For them, closeness with you is where their guard can rise. Your openness, or simply the depth of what the two of you share, may press on the place where they once had to adapt, and they can want intimacy and brace against it in the same breath. The 8th is succedent, holding what a bond creates; [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/), its natural sign, has two rulers, Pluto in modern tropical astrology and Mars in tradition, and I read both.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Trust can become a test. They may hold something back long after you've opened up, and you may feel you have to keep proving you're safe. In shared money or joint plans, an old sensitivity of theirs can make an ordinary disagreement feel heavy. If intimacy is part of the bond, the pace matters more than usual: every step needs both people's yes, freely given.

From their side, being known this well is both what they want and what they fear. They may help you through your hardest moments while keeping their own out of sight, and confuse being useful in a crisis with being close.

Tradition gave the 8th death and inheritance. I read neither in any chart, alone or in a pair ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)): this overlay describes trust, depth and what two people hold in common.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Here trust is built at the speed of the more cautious of you, and "not yet" should be as easy to say as "yes". Put shared money in writing: who owns what, who owes what, and what happens if plans change. In a couple, closeness in all its forms goes best when it's talked about rather than guessed at; between friends, a secret shared is a trust held, so say which ones must not travel; between colleagues, sensitive information and joint budgets need clear limits; between adult relatives sharing a property or a responsibility, a written note of who does what spares a lot of hurt.

Their reading of the pair holds the reverse, your Chiron in their 8th house, where your old guard meets their intimacy. [Their Chiron in your 7th house](/astrology/chiron-in-7th-house-synastry/) stands across the table from you; [their Chiron in your 9th house](/astrology/chiron-in-9th-house-synastry/) goes looking for meaning.

Your birth time is what draws the 8th, in Placidus, the house system I use; their date already pins down their Chiron, slow as it is, and I begin only with both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Chiron is the placement I read most carefully, and in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) it was one of three, with Saturn and Lilith, that held 75 to 90% of the time. Those counts were made chart by chart; an overlay has no figure of its own, and in the 8th I go slowest of all. Intimacy is read only when both adults have asked for the reading ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#a-chart-is-personal)).
