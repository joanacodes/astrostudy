Every planet in your chart stands in a sign that some planet rules, so every planet answers to another. Follow that thread from planet to planet and it either stops at one standing in its own sign or goes round in a circle. The planet a thread stops at is a dispositor at the end of its chain.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A planet's dispositor is the ruler of the sign it occupies. Deborah Houlding's example is the Sun in Libra: Venus rules Libra, so Venus receives the Sun there, as a host receives a guest, and Venus is the Sun's dispositor. Every placement has one. Mars in Taurus has Venus, the Moon in Sagittarius has Jupiter, Saturn in Gemini has Mercury.

The idea comes straight from rulership ([domicile](/astrology/domicile/)): a planet in a sign it rules is its own dispositor, at home and answering to nobody. Every other planet hands on to the ruler of its sign, and that ruler, unless it too is at home, hands on to the ruler of its own sign. The chain is the same logic astrologers use for houses, where the ruler of the sign on a cusp carries that house's business to wherever it sits ([house rulers](/astrology/house-rulers/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Take a Moon in Sagittarius. Its dispositor is Jupiter. Say Jupiter stands in Virgo: Mercury rules Virgo, so the chain moves to Mercury. If Mercury is in Gemini, its own sign, the chain stops. Moon, Jupiter, Mercury: Mercury is where that thread ends.

Do the same from every planet and one of three things happens.

- **A single end.** Exactly one planet stands in its own sign, and every chain in the chart leads to it. That planet is the final dispositor.
- **Several ends.** Two or more planets stand in their own signs, and the chains split between them. There is no single final dispositor, only several places where threads stop.
- **A loop.** Two planets in each other's signs send the chain back and forth for ever: that is a [mutual reception](/astrology/mutual-reception/). Rings of three or more happen too: Mars in Capricorn hands on to Saturn, Saturn in Gemini to Mercury, Mercury in Aries back to Mars.

It follows that a chart with no planet in its own sign has no final dispositor at all: with nowhere to stop, every chain ends in a loop.

Rulers matter here. In the traditional scheme only the seven visible planets dispose of others. Modern tropical astrology gives Scorpio to Pluto, Aquarius to Uranus and Pisces to Neptune, so a planet in one of those signs hands on to a slow planet instead ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)). The two schemes can give two different sets of chains from the same chart.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read a dispositor as the planet a placement leans on. In the grammar my readings use, a planet is a function and its sign the manner in which it works, and the sign's ruler is where that manner comes from. A Moon in Sagittarius looks for room and meaning in Jupiter's way, so how Jupiter itself is placed, its sign, its house and its aspects, colours how easily that Moon finds them. A planet whose dispositor is comfortable has good backing; one whose dispositor struggles works with less. A planet with no dignity of its own leans on its dispositor most ([peregrine planets](/astrology/peregrine-planets/)). The same questions about condition apply to every planet in a chain, and to none more than the chart ruler, whose condition the whole chart leans on ([the chart ruler's condition](/astrology/chart-rulers-condition/)).

I follow both chains for Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces, as I read both rulers. They can tell different stories. Most people born between November 1983 and November 1995 have [Pluto in Scorpio](/astrology/pluto-in-scorpio/), its modern home, so in the modern scheme many of those charts end at Pluto. A final dispositor shared by twelve years of births says more about a generation than a person, so there I give the traditional chain more weight.

A final dispositor, when a chart has one, is a theme the chart keeps returning to. It isn't a boss. No single factor decides what a chart is about ([how I read](/how-i-read/)), and the houses each link in the chain occupies, which need the birth time, say where in your life the thread actually runs ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The final dispositor is the most important planet in your chart.”** It's where the chains end, which makes it a recurring theme, not the planet that runs your life.
- **“Every chart has a final dispositor.”** Many don't. With no planet in its own sign there is none, and with two or more at home the chains split.
- **“A dispositor has to aspect the planet it disposes of.”** Disposition comes from the sign alone. The two planets may make no aspect at all.
- **“Being disposed of by Saturn or Mars is bad.”** Tradition called them malefics ([benefics and malefics](/astrology/benefics-and-malefics/)). As dispositors they describe a manner of backing, careful or direct, never a verdict.
