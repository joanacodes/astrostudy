Draw the horizon and the meridian across a birth chart and you cut the wheel in half twice over. Hemisphere emphasis asks a simple question of those halves: where did the planets gather?

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The [horizon axis](/astrology/horizon-axis/), from the Ascendant to the Descendant, divides the chart into an upper half and a lower one. Planets in the upper half, houses 7 to 12, were above the horizon when you were born; planets in the lower half, houses 1 to 6, were below it. The [meridian axis](/astrology/meridian-axis/), from the Midheaven to the IC, makes the second cut: an eastern half on the left of the wheel, houses 10 to 3 around the Ascendant, and a western half on the right, houses 4 to 9 around the Descendant.

When most planets fall in one of these halves, the chart has a hemisphere emphasis. Some books call the upper half the southern hemisphere and the lower half the northern, because from northern latitudes beyond the tropics the Midheaven lies towards the south. I find above and below clearer, and I keep to them.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Everything here comes from the angles, and the angles come from the time and place of birth. The sky turns through all twelve houses in a day, so a planet rising at seven in the morning has climbed well into the upper half by noon. Two people born on the same day, a few hours apart, can have opposite emphases with the same planets in the same signs. For me the birth time isn't a detail but the condition of any reading ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

The Sun's half follows the hour. Born around sunrise, your Sun sits close to the Ascendant; around midday, near the Midheaven; around sunset, near the Descendant; around midnight, near the IC. A daytime birth puts the Sun above the horizon, and Mercury and Venus, which travel close to the Sun, often keep it company there. Before I call a chart an upper one, I check how much of the emphasis is simply the hour of birth carrying those three together.

In Placidus, the house system I use, the halves follow the house cusps exactly, because the four angles are the cusps of the 1st, 4th, 7th and 10th houses. In whole-sign houses, where each house is one sign, a planet can already be above the horizon and still count in the 1st house, so the same chart can show a slightly different balance there.

The counts by element and by modality sort the planets by sign, and they're nearly the same for everyone born on one day ([Modality balance](/astrology/modality-balance/)). Hemispheres sort them by house, which makes them personal to the hour and the place. Cross the two cuts and you get four quarters, which narrow the picture further ([Quadrants](/astrology/quadrants/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read an emphasis as where the chart's energy gathers. Above the horizon, it tends to reach outward: towards the world, other people, the things that can be seen and named. Below, it tends to work inward first: home, roots, the private groundwork that comes before anything shows.

East and west answer another question: who starts things. Planets gathered in the east, around the Ascendant, often describe a life that moves from its own impulses, choosing first and adjusting after. Gathered in the west, around the Descendant, they tend to describe a life that finds its direction by meeting others: partners, clients, rivals, collaborators. Neither half is freer than the other.

Then I look at the empty side, because it isn't really empty. Its houses are there, with signs on their cusps, and the rulers of those signs stand somewhere in the occupied half. That's the bridge. With nothing below the horizon, the ruler of the 4th house may sit in the 10th, and home gets reached through work rather than apart from it. A planet alone in an otherwise empty half stands out, and I give it a look of its own.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Planets below the horizon are weak.”** They were under the earth at your birth, not out of your reach. They often describe what you build quietly and keep.
- **“Nothing in the western half means no relationships.”** The 7th house is in every chart, with its sign and its ruler. An empty west describes where energy gathers, not whom you'll meet.
- **“An eastern emphasis makes you selfish.”** It describes initiative, choosing first, and initiative can just as well be spent on other people.
- **“Your hemisphere is in your birth date.”** The halves turn right round in a day; only the time and place can settle them.
