Some couples become known as a couple: the pair behind the bookshop, the two who run the choir, the partners whose names are always said together. A composite Neptune in the 10th house gives the relationship's public side that quality, and a dream to go with it.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

A couple can have a reputation quite apart from either partner's, and [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), built from the midpoints of two birth charts, gives the relationship a chart to match, read as a third thing with a place in the world. Composite [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) shows what that third thing looks up to. [The 10th house](/astrology/10th-house/), which opens at the composite [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/), is the top of the chart: vocation, public role, reputation, what the bond does in the world and is known for. With Neptune here, the pair's work together tends to feel like a calling. It may be art, care, teaching or a cause; it may simply be the way the two of you are seen, as a gentle, inspiring presence in your community. People who barely know you can feel they know what you stand for.

The difficulty is the gap between the public picture and the private one. A bond that others idealise can feel pressure to live up to its image, or hide its struggles to protect it. Ambitions here can also stay foggy: a shared mission everyone believes in and nobody has planned, roles at work that blur between the two of you, a vision that outruns the timetable. Neptune doesn't make the work unreal; it asks the pair to write down what the dream needs, and who does which part.

Alone in a birth chart, [Neptune in the 10th house](/astrology/neptune-in-10th-house/) describes one person's vocation led by imagination; in synastry, [their Neptune in your 10th house](/astrology/neptune-in-10th-house-synastry/) is one partner's ideals meeting the other's direction. The composite 10th is the couple's own vocation, the thing the relationship seems to be for in public.

Here, for once, the two ways of drawing composite houses agree on something: both put the composite Midheaven, and so the cusp of this house, at the midpoint of your two natal Midheavens. Where they part is the far edge, the 11th cusp. The birth times are another matter: a quarter of an hour's error in one time moves that chart's Midheaven by about 4° and the composite one by about 2°, so a Neptune near the top of the chart can slip back into the 9th ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

A composite promises no success and foretells no failure, and it says nothing about fame. What it describes is the relationship's sense of vocation, and with Neptune here, a vocation that only works while it stays honest about what it costs.

The houses before and after it carry the dream elsewhere: [composite Neptune in the 9th house](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-9th-house/) into belief and travel, [composite Neptune in the 11th house](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-11th-house/) into friends and shared hopes. I'm describing composites here, not offering them: those who draw them read this house beside both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). However public it is, a relationship is still made of two private people, and each should have agreed to be read ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
