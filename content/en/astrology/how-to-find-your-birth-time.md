Your birth time was probably written down somewhere; the work is finding out where. These are the places to look, in the order I'd try them, and what to do with whatever you find.

## Step by step {#step-by-step}

1. **Start with your birth certificate.** Whether it shows the time depends on the country, and sometimes on the region, the year of birth or the kind of copy you ask for. Where more than one kind of copy exists, ask for the fullest. Each country has its own page, with what its records hold and how to ask for them: [France](/astrology/birth-time-france/), [England and Wales](/astrology/birth-time-england-and-wales/), [Scotland](/astrology/birth-time-scotland/), [Ireland](/astrology/birth-time-ireland/), [the United States](/astrology/birth-time-united-states/), [Canada](/astrology/birth-time-canada/), [Australia](/astrology/birth-time-australia/), [Spain](/astrology/birth-time-spain/), [Germany](/astrology/birth-time-germany/), [Italy](/astrology/birth-time-italy/), [Portugal](/astrology/birth-time-portugal/), [Brazil](/astrology/birth-time-brazil/), [Belgium](/astrology/birth-time-belgium/), [Switzerland](/astrology/birth-time-switzerland/), [the Netherlands](/astrology/birth-time-netherlands/), [Mexico](/astrology/birth-time-mexico/) and [Argentina](/astrology/birth-time-argentina/).
2. **Ask the hospital.** If you were born in a hospital or a maternity unit, its records may note the time. Ask whether the file from your birth still exists, and how to request it.
3. **Ask your family.** A parent's or grandparent's memory is worth having, even a vague one: “just after lunch” is a start. Ask about what surrounded it as well as the hour: whether it was light or dark, a meal, a change of shift. Ask each person separately, and write down their own words rather than the version you agree on afterwards.
4. **Look for a baby book and family papers.** Some parents wrote the hour in a baby book; letters and other papers from the weeks after the birth can hold it too.
5. **Write down what you found, and where.** Copy each time exactly as given, without converting it, with its source and how sure it is: “15:42 on the certificate” and “around four, according to my aunt” aren't the same kind of time. When two sources disagree, keep both: the gap between them shows how far your chart could move.
6. **See how much the minutes matter.** Enter the time in a chart and look at the Ascendant, which moves about a degree every four minutes. If it sits near the edge of a sign, a few minutes either way change your rising sign ([How to find your rising sign](/astrology/how-to-find-your-rising-sign/)): try the earliest and the latest times your sources allow, and if the sign changes between them, an exact record matters all the more. Check the Moon too: on the day it changes sign, only the time says which one is yours ([the Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).
7. **If nothing turns up.** Without the time, the rising sign and the houses are missing, and I don't read a chart on the half that's left ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). I'm working on a project to find people's birth data accurately; for now I can only read with the time and place of birth. If you're unsure whether what you've found is enough, write to me before you order a [birth chart reading](/birth-chart/).

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes}

- **Stopping at the first document.** A certificate without a time doesn't mean no record has one: ask whether a fuller copy exists, then try the hospital and the family.
- **Treating a rounded time as exact.** “Around three” written as 15:00 is still around three. A time rounded to the hour can move the rising sign, and with it every house.
- **Mixing up 12 a.m. and 12 p.m.** By convention, 12 a.m. is midnight and 12 p.m. is noon, but the reverse has also been used, and many style guides avoid both for that reason. If your record says either, look for a second source before you trust it.
- **Filling the gap with a guess.** A chart drawn for noon, for sunrise or for a likely hour describes that moment, not your birth ([Noon and sunrise charts](/astrology/noon-and-sunrise-charts/)), and a program that shows a rising sign for a birth entered without a time has assumed one. It's a guess presented as a chart, and I'd rather wait for the time ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).
