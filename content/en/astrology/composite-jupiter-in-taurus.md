A vegetable patch in its tenth summer, a recipe improved a little every winter, a walk taken on the same evening of the week for years. Composite Jupiter in Taurus describes a relationship that grows by keeping what works and adding to it slowly.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) sets each planet at the midpoint of its two natal positions, and in it [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) stands for what the relationship widens: its understanding, its principles, the room it makes for each of you. [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), fixed earth, gives that widening a steady pace and a wish to make things last. It holds none of Jupiter's dignities. Its ruler is [Venus](/astrology/venus/), so composite Venus, what the pair values and enjoys together, shows what this Jupiter's slow growth is for.

About 1 pair in 14 has it, in two million random pairs born between 1930 and 2009. Two people born while Jupiter crossed Taurus share it: everyone born between May 1940 and May 1941, between late April 1952 and early May 1953, or between April 1964 and April 1965 has it with everyone else born in those months. Jupiter also comes back to Taurus about every twelve years, the length of its cycle and of a [Jupiter return](/astrology/jupiter-return/), so a pair born twelve years apart can share it as well. Someone born on 1 June 1952, with Jupiter at about 8° Taurus, and someone born on 1 June 1964, with Jupiter at about 12° Taurus, have their composite Jupiter at about 10° Taurus. With Jupiter, *the same age* can mean twelve years apart.

The relationship's growth, though, is its own. Two people who each like novelty can find that their bond, and only their bond, wants continuity: the same café on Saturdays, a skill practised together over years, shared principles that change only once something better has proved itself. [Jupiter in Taurus](/astrology/jupiter-in-taurus/) describes one person whose understanding settles slowly. In a composite, the steadiness belongs to the two of you, and its generosity looks like reliability: turning up when you said you would, keeping the promise, looking after what you've built.

Its difficulty is that growth this gradual is hard to see, and harder still to question. A pair that trusts what has lasted may keep an arrangement long after it stopped serving either of you, or treat any change as a threat to what you have. What helps is naming the growth now and then, out loud, and trying one new thing a year at the bond's own pace, so that steadiness stays a choice rather than a habit.

Taurus here is no sign of money, and Jupiter no promise of gain: what grows is trust and staying power, not a balance. Beside it, [composite Jupiter in Aries](/astrology/composite-jupiter-in-aries/) widens a bond through first times, and [composite Jupiter in Gemini](/astrology/composite-jupiter-in-gemini/) through questions and talk. Composites aren't part of my readings; I describe the technique here, which those who practise it read with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), never on its own. It needs two people's birth data, handed over knowingly by both ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
