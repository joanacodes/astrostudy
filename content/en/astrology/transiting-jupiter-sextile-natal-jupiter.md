Twice in every twelve-year cycle, Jupiter in the sky stands 60° from the degree it held when you were born. These are the softest moments of the cycle: no pressure, no turning point, just a door left ajar for the way you already like to grow.

## What changes {#what-changes}

Your natal [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) describes how you grow: its sign the manner, through action, comfort, ideas or care; its house the part of life where you most readily learn and trust. When transiting Jupiter makes a [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) to it, the same function is speaking to itself from two signs away, usually from a sign of the same polarity, fire with air or earth with water. Born with Jupiter in Pisces, you meet one sextile with Jupiter in Taurus and the other with Jupiter in Capricorn, the sign tradition calls its fall: two different climates, both friendly to the way you grow.

Each cycle has two sextiles, and they have different flavours. The first comes about two years after a [Jupiter return](/astrology/jupiter-return/), when the round that the return opened has had time to show what it wants: it's the first practical opening for it, an offer or a teacher that fits. The second comes about two years before the next return, and suits finishing and passing on what this round taught you.

A sextile is an opportunity that needs engagement. Nothing is pushed on you, which is why these weeks are so easy to sleep through, and why I mention them in a reading at all. Where the opening appears depends on your Jupiter's house, which only the birth time can give ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Eleven days or so is the usual length: the time Jupiter, moving straight on, takes to cross from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the orbs I use for timing. In about three cycles out of ten Jupiter is also turning retrograde near the point, and then you get three exact hits spread over about eight months ([Jupiter retrograde](/astrology/jupiter-retrograde/)). Thirteen of these [transits](/astrology/transits/) by the age of 80 is the count for every birth from 1930 to 2009, without exception.

The first came early, between 1.4 and 2.1 years of age, and your natal Jupiter's sign set the pace, since Jupiter, on its slightly oval orbit, covers some signs faster than others. Born at the start of 1975 or 1985, with Jupiter in Pisces or Capricorn, it came at about 1.4; at the start of 1935, 1945, 1995 or 2005, with Jupiter between Virgo and Sagittarius, at about 2.1. That one belongs to a toddler, and this site doesn't read [children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/); the ones I read come in adulthood.

The rest of the cycle continues from here: [transiting Jupiter square natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-square-natal-jupiter/) about a year after the first sextile, then [transiting Jupiter trine natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-trine-natal-jupiter/), then [transiting Jupiter opposite natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-opposite-natal-jupiter/) at the halfway mark, before the same angles return in reverse towards the next return.

## What helps {#what-helps}

At the first sextile of a round, take one concrete step towards what the return set going: enrol, apply, write the email you've been drafting in your head. At the second, finish something, or teach it.

Let your natal Jupiter's sign choose the form. Growth through ideas wants a course or a correspondence; growth through care wants a commitment to someone; growth through action wants a start date.

And give yourself a small deadline inside the window. Eleven days pass quickly when nothing is pushing, and the sextile never will.
