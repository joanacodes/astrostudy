The Flower moon is May's full moon in the almanacs' calendar, named for a month of flowers. The name belongs to folklore, not to astrology, and what it covers astrologically is usually a full moon in Scorpio, with the Sun opposite in Taurus.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

It comes after the [Pink moon](/astrology/pink-moon/) of April and before the [Strawberry moon](/astrology/strawberry-moon/) of June. Like any [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/), it's the night the Moon faces the Sun from the other side of the sky. The dates, in Universal Time:

- **2026:** 1 May, 17:23 UT, at 11°21′ Scorpio, and a second full moon on 31 May, 08:45 UT, at 9°56′ Sagittarius
- **2027:** 20 May, 10:59 UT, at 29°13′ Scorpio
- **2028:** 8 May, 19:49 UT, at 18°44′ Scorpio
- **2029:** 27 May, 18:38 UT, at 6°45′ Sagittarius
- **2030:** 17 May, 11:19 UT, at 26°37′ Scorpio

The Sun is in Taurus until about 20 May and in Gemini after, so May's full moon sits in Scorpio in four of these five years and slips into Sagittarius when it comes late, as in 2029. The 2027 Flower moon falls in the very last degree of Scorpio.

May 2026 held two full moons. The Moon's cycle of about 29½ days is shorter than May's 31, so a full moon on the 1st left room for another on the 31st. Both are May's; the second is the kind of extra full moon people call a [blue moon](/astrology/blue-moon/).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

This name was in print long before the almanacs took it up. Jonathan Carver's *Travels Through the Interior Parts of North-America* (1778) lists twelve moons, beginning in March, and the third is the moon of flowers. Carver presents his names as those of “the Indians” in general, without saying which people used them.

The Maine Farmer's Almanac kept the name when it began printing a name for every month's full moon in the 1930s, and NASA's full moon guide gives its reason in a phrase: May is when flowers are abundant. The same guide lists two other names for this moon, the Corn Planting Moon and the Milk Moon.

The rule is the calendar's: a full moon that falls in May is a Flower moon, whatever its sign.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The Flower moon is a folk and almanac name, and it isn't astrology. Flowers don't enter into it. What I read is the full moon, and May's is unusual because of the signs it nearly always falls in.

With the Moon in Scorpio and the Sun in Taurus, the [full moon in Scorpio](/astrology/full-moon-in-scorpio/) lies across the axis where tradition puts both the Moon's exaltation, in Taurus, and its fall, in Scorpio. A sign of fall isn't a bad omen. I read it as a less comfortable manner for the Moon's needs and feelings: more intense, more private, less at ease with the plain comfort Taurus offers across the way. In 2029, the Flower moon is instead a [full moon in Sagittarius](/astrology/full-moon-in-sagittarius/), with the Sun in Gemini, a very different pair.

In a birth chart, I let a full moon count only when it falls within a degree of a natal planet or angle, at the Moon's end or the Sun's. A Flower moon at 18°44′ Scorpio, as in 2028, touches a planet within a degree of 18°44′ Scorpio or Taurus, and only matters if a transit or progression is already working there. It brings that into focus; it doesn't announce anything new.

My [testing notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) checked birth placements, not the month's full moons, so it has nothing to say about the Flower moon either way. The house it falls in needs your birth time.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Flower moon makes things bloom.”** It's named after the season. South of the equator, May's full moon rises in autumn.
- **“A full moon in Scorpio, the Moon's fall, is a bad night.”** Fall describes a manner, more intense and less at ease, never a verdict on a night or on a person.
- **“May only has one full moon.”** Some years it has two, as in 2026, because May is longer than the Moon's cycle.
- **“The name gives the full moon its meaning.”** The meaning comes from its sign, Scorpio or Sagittarius, and the house it lights in your chart.
