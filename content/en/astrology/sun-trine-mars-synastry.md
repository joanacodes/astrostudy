Between two people, Sun trine Mars is a contact of momentum. One person's Mars stands a third of the zodiac from the other's Sun, mostly in a sign that shares its element, and effort runs in the direction the other is already going. It's also far commoner between two charts than inside one, and the reason is Mars's retrograde rhythm.

## What flows {#what-flows}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is purpose made personal: identity, vitality, a direction to act from. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) turns intention into action: effort, stamina, execution. A [trine](/astrology/trine/) puts two functions in step, and as [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) go, it's one of the most practical: one person knows where they're going, the other has the legs for it.

More often than not, a shared element joins them. A Sagittarius Mars with an Aries Sun: restless, generous energy for someone who likes to be first. A Cancer Mars with a Scorpio Sun: protective, tenacious effort for someone who commits completely.

If it's your Sun, your plans become easier to carry out. You decide, and the energy to follow through seems to arrive with them; projects that stall when you're on your own tend to move when they're around. If yours is the Mars, your effort finds an aim it agrees with. Working towards their goals doesn't feel like working for someone else, and you can push hard without feeling pushed.

Teammates, travel companions, colleagues racing a deadline, a couple who build things together: the trine shows in what gets done.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Momentum can hide its direction. When effort and aims agree this easily, a pair can go a long way without asking whether it's the right way, and neither notices the moment one of them would rather turn back. It can also hide who carries the load: the Mars person may do most of the lifting while the Sun person sets the course, and the arrangement feels so natural that nobody checks it. And two people who rarely clash may never learn how to, which matters the first time they must. A trine still needs someone at the wheel.

Alongside the trine, the pair's other contacts:

- **[Sun conjunct Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mars-synastry/):** the push lands on the self; closer than shared momentum, and harder to share out.
- **[Sun sextile Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-sextile-mars-synastry/):** two signs closer; the effort waits to be asked instead of running alongside.
- **[Sun square Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-square-mars-synastry/):** one sign less and no shared element; the push crosses the purpose, and pace becomes something to negotiate.
- **[Sun opposite Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-opposite-mars-synastry/):** six signs apart; the push stands opposite the self instead of alongside it.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Stop now and then to check the map: "is this still where we want to go?" takes a minute and can save months. Share out the heavy lifting on purpose, so momentum doesn't quietly become one person's job. Practise small disagreements while things are easy, and the big ones won't arrive as a shock. In sport, at work or on a shared renovation, the trine makes an excellent engine; just make sure both people have a hand on the wheel.

Between two charts, about 1 pair of people in 7 shows this trine, either way round, within 6.8°. Inside a single chart it's far rarer, about 1 person in 19 within 8°, because both trines fall near Mars's retrograde turns, when the Sun passes through them quickly. Between two people, the Sun comes from one birthday and Mars from another, so that rhythm no longer applies.

The birth time weighs almost nothing on the trine itself; it weighs on the houses, which show the part of each life the shared effort goes into, and I open no reading without both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). In one chart, [Sun trine Mars](/astrology/sun-trine-mars/) makes momentum an inner matter; between two people, it rests on both of them agreeing where to go.
