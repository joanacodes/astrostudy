On 12 March 1932, a Belgian astronomer photographed an asteroid passing within about 16 million kilometres of the Earth, closer than anyone had seen one come before. He called it Amor, the Latin for love. It's small, about a kilometre across, and it has given its name to a whole family of asteroids. It also shares its god with two other asteroids, which is where the confusion starts.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Eugène Delporte found Amor at the Royal Observatory of Belgium in Uccle, and the Heidelberg observatory caught it the same night. It goes round the Sun in about 2 years and 8 months, from just outside the Earth's orbit to well beyond Mars's, so it crosses the orbit of Mars but never the Earth's. Its closest pass since 1900 came in March 1940, at about 15.7 million kilometres; the next comparable one is due in March 2129.

Asteroids on orbits like that, approaching the Earth's from outside without crossing it, are called Amor asteroids after it. They're the second largest group of near-Earth objects after the Apollo asteroids, which do cross the Earth's orbit, and they number in the thousands. [Eros](/astrology/eros/), found in 1898, is one of them.

The name belongs to the god the Greeks called Eros and the Romans called Cupid. Latin gave him two names: *Cupido*, passionate desire, and *Amor*, love. Both became asteroids. [Cupido](/astrology/cupido-asteroid/), number 763, was found in 1913; Amor, number 1221, nineteen years later. In his most famous story, he falls in love with [Psyche](/astrology/psyche/), in Apuleius's tale of the 2nd century.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Nothing written before 1932 could mention it, so its name is all astrologers have to go on. That makes the choice of name matter more than usual, and here one god has three asteroids. Eros, Cupido and Amor have separate orbits, so in any chart they usually fall in three different places, and anyone who adds all three has three answers to one question. If you use one, decide which before you see where they land in your chart, so that the chart doesn't choose for you ([How to use asteroids without drowning the chart](/astrology/how-to-use-asteroids-without-drowning-the-chart/)). Nor is the god the only source of love's names among the asteroids: [Sappho](/astrology/sappho/), found in 1864, carries the name of the Greek poet best known in antiquity for her love poems.

Its motion follows from its orbit. Like other near-Earth asteroids, it moves through the zodiac unevenly, fast when it's near us and slowly when it's far, and its house in a chart depends on the birth time, like every house.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Asteroids aren't part of the system I built for my readings, and my notebook stopped at the planets and the points I read ([what I tested](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)), so I have nothing to tell you about how often Amor holds.

When love is the question, I turn to placements I did count. [Venus](/astrology/venus/) describes how you love and what you find beautiful; the [5th house](/astrology/5th-house/) is where pleasure and romance play out, and the [7th](/astrology/7th-house/) is partnership, the one person across the table. My notebook gave Venus a plain record, holding about one time in three, and I say as much when a reading leans on it.

Between two people, a chart comparison looks at how one person's planets meet the other's, both ways, and only with both people's agreement ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)). No asteroid can stand in for that, and none can say whether two people are meant for each other.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Amor shows your true love.”** An asteroid can't pick a partner out of a crowd, or promise you one.
- **“Amor, Eros and Cupido are one asteroid with three names.”** They're three bodies, numbers 1221, 433 and 763, on three orbits.
- **“Amor is the oldest love asteroid.”** It's the youngest of the three: Eros was found in 1898, Cupido in 1913, Amor in 1932.
- **“A love asteroid on your Venus means love is coming.”** A natal contact describes how you love, not when anyone will arrive.
