Give Mercury in the 6th house a task, a process that isn't working or a day that needs organising, and it settles; leave it idle and it starts reorganising the cupboard. This is a mind that thinks best with its sleeves rolled up.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) is the ordinary working day: tasks, routines, methods, colleagues, the care you give your body. Cadent and practical, it's a house of adjusting and maintaining. Its natural sign is Virgo, which Mercury rules and where it's exalted, and tradition called it Bad Fortune and gave Mars its joy there, as the house of toil. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) here thinks by doing. You learn a method by using it, improve it while you use it, and often write the instructions afterwards. The sign supplies the method: in Aries, fix it now; in Taurus, the slow routine that never fails; in Libra, a system that's fair to everyone who uses it; in Aquarius, a better process than the one everyone had accepted. Should Mercury also be in Virgo, the habit becomes a craft ([Mercury in Virgo](/astrology/mercury-in-virgo/)).

The old books also gave this house illness. A chart can't diagnose or predict anything about your health, and questions about it belong with a doctor ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)). With Mercury here, I read something more everyday: how you organise your days, and how much attention you pay to the body that carries them.

The house system matters with this placement. In Placidus, which I use, the 6th is drawn from the time of birth and can be wide or narrow; in whole sign houses it's simply the sixth sign from your rising sign. The same Mercury can sit in the 6th in one system and the 7th in the other, and in either it needs the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

In the house before, [Mercury in the 5th house](/astrology/mercury-in-5th-house/) plays; in the house after, [Mercury in the 7th house](/astrology/mercury-in-7th-house/) talks things through with a partner. The 6th is where the mind clocks in.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Love, for you, often sounds practical: *I've booked it*, *I fixed the tap*, *here's the plan for Saturday*. You show care by solving things and by keeping shared life running smoothly. A partner may want more words that aren't about logistics, and you may want more help with the logistics. Shared routines matter to you more than grand gestures; the walk you take together every evening can say more than an anniversary speech.

## At work {#at-work}

Process improvement, editing, administration, technical support, research assistance, quality control: any job where small things done right add up. You're the colleague who writes the manual, who notices the broken step and mends it before anyone else has seen it. You think better while working than while planning to work, and a clear, sensible routine frees your mind rather than boxing it in.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The to-do list that does your thinking for you. Work talk taking over every conversation, including the ones meant for rest. A habit of noticing what's wrong in a process, or a person, and saying so before saying what's right. And a body that gets attention only when it insists, because the day's tasks always seemed more urgent. Colleagues may lean on your lists more than they admit, and you may not say when the load has grown too heavy.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Question one routine a month: is it still the best way, or only the familiar one? Keep a list of what you finished, not just of what's left. Take questions about your health to a doctor, and give your body a routine of its own: sleep, food, a walk. Make some time with no task in it at all, and notice what your mind does when nothing needs fixing.
