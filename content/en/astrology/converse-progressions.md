Progressions read the days after your birth as the years of your life. Converse progressions turn round and read the days before it, so that the sky which led up to your birth becomes a second clock.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

In [secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/), the sky one day after your birth stands for your first year, ten days after it for your tenth. Converse secondary progressions do the same with the days before: the sky one day before your birth for your first year, thirty days before it for your thirtieth.

Every progression has a converse twin. Converse tertiary progressions take each day before birth for a lunar month of life, and converse minor progressions each lunar month before birth for a year, mirroring the forward versions ([tertiary and minor progressions](/astrology/tertiary-and-minor-progressions/)).

The same reversal exists in [primary directions](/astrology/primary-directions/), the oldest of these clocks. There, a direct direction carries one point of the chart, the promittor, towards another with the turning of the sky; a converse direction turns the other point, the significator, while the rest stays put. The tradition treated both kinds as equally significant.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

A program does it: choose the converse option, give a date, and it casts the chart for the matching moment before birth. The arithmetic follows from the reversal:

- **The converse Sun** moves back through the zodiac about a degree a year, as the forward [progressed Sun](/astrology/progressed-sun/) moves ahead. The degree of your Sun within its sign is roughly the age at which the converse Sun backs into the sign before: with the Sun at 10° Leo, it reaches Cancer at about 10.
- **The converse Moon** moves back about a degree a month, retracing in its first years the degrees the Moon crossed in the month before your birth, while the forward [progressed Moon](/astrology/progressed-moon/) moves on.
- **A planet that was direct at birth** moves backwards in the converse chart; one that was retrograde moves forward. A planet that turned shortly before the birth is met at its [station](/astrology/stations/) in the first years of the converse count.

One date stands out. The new or full moon that came before your birth fell no more than about fifteen days before it, so the converse Sun and Moon reach that same alignment by about the age of fifteen, at the age that matches the number of days between it and your birth. Ptolemy counted that new or full moon among the chart's chief places ([prenatal lunation](/astrology/prenatal-lunation/)).

The natal positions and angles they start from depend on the birth time, as in every progression ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

Astrologers who use converse progressions read them as they read forward ones: aspects to natal points within about a degree, changes of sign and house, read as development rather than events. What changes is the material. A forward progression draws on the sky after you arrived; a converse one, on the sky that came before.

I don't. My timing runs forward from birth: secondary progressions, [solar arcs](/astrology/solar-arc-directions/), the solar return, profections, zodiacal releasing and the transits of the slow planets ([how I read](/how-i-read/)). That's already several clocks, and I look for the places where they agree.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

Adding a converse chart to a forward one doubles the number of moving points, and with them the number of contacts that can be found after the fact. A method that can explain any year explains none in particular.

The vocabulary is a trap of its own. A converse planet moving backwards isn't retrograde, and a retrograde planet in a progressed chart isn't a converse one: the first is a direction of counting, the second a direction of motion in the sky ([Retrograde, explained](/astrology/retrograde-explained/)). And like every progression, the converse ones are symbolic: the sky before your birth didn't move again when you turned thirty.
