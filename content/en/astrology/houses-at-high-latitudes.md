House systems behave well in Lisbon, Paris or London. Go north of the Arctic Circle and the sky changes the rules: some degrees of the zodiac never rise, the Ascendant can run backwards, and the house system I use can no longer place every planet.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Every house system starts from the horizon, the meridian or both, and the horizon is where latitude bites. The further you are from the equator, the more steeply the zodiac rises at some times of day and the more gently at others. At the latitude of Paris, Pisces and Aries each take only about an hour to cross the eastern horizon, while Virgo and Libra take two hours and forty-five minutes; at the latitude of St Petersburg, Pisces and Aries take thirty-five minutes and Virgo and Libra three hours and twenty. Quadrant houses built on those rising times come out very unequal, and whole signs get swallowed inside a single house ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)).

At the polar circles, about 66°34′ north and south, something new happens: a degree of the zodiac stops rising and setting. On the Arctic Circle it is 0° Cancer: on the day the Sun stands there, the June solstice, the Sun doesn't set. Further north the stretch grows. At 70° north, the zodiac from about the end of Taurus to the start of Leo never sets, the opposite stretch, from the end of Scorpio to the start of Aquarius, never rises, and together they make about a third of the circle: no degree in either can ever be the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) there.

Inside the circles the Ascendant behaves strangely too. Twice a day it jumps, at the moments the zodiac lies flat along the horizon, and between the two jumps it moves backwards through the signs.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Systems that divide time need every degree to rise and set. [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/) cuts each degree's climb from the horizon to the meridian into thirds; a degree that never rises has no climb to cut, so beyond the polar circles some degrees, and the planets in them, can't be given a house without extending the system. [Koch](/astrology/koch/) times the Midheaven's degree and has the same limit: both are defined only between about 66° north and 66° south.

Other systems carry on, each for its own reason:

- **[Equal houses](/astrology/equal-houses/) and [Whole Sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/)** need only the Ascendant, and there is always one, even if fewer degrees can take the role. Supporters of equal houses argue that it suits latitudes above 60° better than quadrant systems.
- **[Porphyry](/astrology/porphyry/) and [Sripati](/astrology/sripati/)** need only the four angles, which can still be found.
- **[Morinus](/astrology/morinus/) and [meridian (axial) houses](/astrology/meridian-axial-houses/)** divide the celestial equator from the meridian and never use the latitude, so they come out the same way everywhere.

The limit is on the houses, not on the chart. Leaving these latitudes out of the tables doesn't mean there is no Ascendant there, only that fewer degrees can rise, and fewer still can form cusps in the time-based systems.

Who does this touch? The largest towns north of the Arctic Circle are in Russia, Norway and Sweden: Murmansk and Norilsk, Tromsø, Vorkuta, Bodø, Harstad and Kiruna. The circle also crosses Finland, Alaska, northern Canada and Greenland, and in Iceland only the small island of Grímsey.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I use Placidus, and I won't pretend it does what it can't. For a birth north of the Arctic Circle or south of the Antarctic, Placidus can't give every degree a house: a Placidus chart drawn there has either left part of the zodiac without a house or had the system extended, and the houses on this site, all written for Placidus, aren't a guide to it. If that's your chart, ask which system it was drawn in before reading anything into its houses ([which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)).

South of the circle, Placidus can always be drawn, however lopsided its houses get: a chart for Stockholm or Reykjavík is unequal, not broken. Near the circles the birth time matters even more than usual, because when a sign rises in half an hour a few minutes move the Ascendant a long way ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“People born in the Arctic have no Ascendant.”** They have one; fewer degrees can take the role, and at some moments it jumps.
- **“The problem is the software.”** It's the sky: a degree that never rises or sets can't be timed, whatever the program.
- **“Equal houses solve everything.”** They always give twelve houses, at the cost of the Midheaven, which near the polar circles can stray far from the 10th cusp.
