Sun square North Node sets the Sun a quarter of the zodiac from the North Node, and so a quarter from the South Node as well: right across the middle of the nodal axis. It describes a sense of self that stands between where you come from and where you're growing, and asks to come along.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is your identity and purpose: the self you organise your life around. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) points to the less familiar capacity your life grows towards, and the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/), opposite, to the familiar one you start from. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates the Sun to both through friction: it sits across the path, so the growth can't go round it.

In plain words, neither your old ways nor your new direction quite fits who you are on its own. When you lean on what's familiar, part of you feels unseen; when you push towards the new, the same part asks to be consulted first. That's the square: friction that keeps asking you to include yourself. The point isn't to choose between the two ends, or to set your sense of self aside for the sake of growth. It's to let who you are become the bridge, so that the direction you grow in is unmistakably yours.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In a chart, it tends to show as a personal style of growth: you develop, but on your own terms, and sometimes later or more slowly than a neat path would suggest. With the North Node in Cancer, a Sun in Aries or Libra keeps asking how home and feeling can fit with its own drive or with its partnerships; with the North Node in Leo, a Sun in Taurus or Scorpio asks how being seen fits with holding on and going deep. The Sun's house is the area of life that sits across both ends of the nodal axis.

About 1 person in 12 is born with the Sun within 8° of a square to the nodes. In the sky, it puts your birthday as far from the nodes as the Sun gets, halfway between two eclipse seasons: the quietest stretch of the eclipse calendar. The Sun reaches it twice in each round of about 346 days, roughly three months either side of its meeting with the North Node. My notebook doesn't name the nodes, so I have no figure for them; I read this square as a question worth asking, never a verdict ([the invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

The pair's other aspects put the Sun elsewhere along the axis:

- **[Sun conjunct North Node](/astrology/sun-conjunct-north-node/):** on the path itself, at the growing end.
- **[Sun sextile North Node](/astrology/sun-sextile-north-node/):** at a friendly angle, with a trine to the familiar end.
- **[Sun trine North Node](/astrology/sun-trine-north-node/):** in easy step with the growing end, and sextile to the familiar one.
- **[Sun opposite North Node](/astrology/sun-opposite-north-node/):** on the familiar end, with the new direction across from it.

Between two charts, [Sun square North Node in synastry](/astrology/sun-square-north-node-synastry/) sets one person's Sun across the other's path: it can feel like a distraction from their direction, or like the very thing that makes it their own, depending on whose chart you start from.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting North Node square natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-north-node-square-natal-sun/) comes twice in each 18.6-year round of the nodes and lasts a month or two. The eclipses of those months fall about 90° from your Sun rather than on it. Twice a year, too, the Sun in the sky squares your natal nodes for a day or so. Weather, not a forecast: a time when the question of how to bring yourself along comes up again.
