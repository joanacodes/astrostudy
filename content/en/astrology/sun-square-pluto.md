Sun square Pluto sets the two a quarter of the zodiac apart, so the part of you that wants to be yourself meets, from the side, the part that wants everything renewed at the root. It often describes strength that was claimed rather than given, and a self that knows where its power comes from because it had to find it.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is identity and agency: the self you organise your life around, and your capacity to act from it. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) is transformation and empowerment: clearing what's obsolete, regenerating, growing into a deeper kind of authority. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates them through friction that drives development. Each presses on the other, and neither gives way for long.

In plain words, whenever you settle into a version of yourself, something asks you to dig under it. A role that fitted starts to pinch; an arrangement that worked shows what it was costing. Sometimes the pressure seems to come from outside, from a person with strong views about who you should be, and part of living with this square is learning that authority over your own life is yours to take. That isn't a fight against the world. Empowerment isn't domination, and transformation isn't destruction: what the square builds is a self that has been rebuilt more than once, and is sturdier for it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In a chart, it tends to show as intensity under pressure, and as a refusal to stay in a shape that no longer fits. Both signs share a modality, so they move alike and want different things: with Pluto in Leo, a Sun in Taurus or Scorpio wants to hold on to what it has, and keeps meeting a need to create and be seen that won't stay small. In the houses, the rub becomes concrete. With the Sun in the 1st and Pluto in the 4th, the way you meet the world is shaped by deep roots and family stories with weight.

About 1 person in 11 has the Sun within 8° of a square to Pluto. Pluto moves so little in a year that the square comes round on nearly the same dates for years on end, so brothers and sisters born a year or two apart, at the same time of year, can share it. The closer to exact, the more insistent the pressure.

The pair's other aspects place the same depth differently:

- **[Sun conjunct Pluto](/astrology/sun-conjunct-pluto/):** depth at the centre rather than at an angle; intensity is part of who you are, not a pressure on it.
- **[Sun sextile Pluto](/astrology/sun-sextile-pluto/):** renewal as an offer you can take up, with nothing to push against.
- **[Sun trine Pluto](/astrology/sun-trine-pluto/):** strength that comes without being fought for, and shows when things get hard.
- **[Sun opposite Pluto](/astrology/sun-opposite-pluto/):** the pressure faces you across the chart, usually through other people's power.

Between two charts, [Sun square Pluto in synastry](/astrology/sun-square-pluto-synastry/) rubs one person's sense of self against the other's power to transform it: compelling, and a contact in which both need room to stay themselves. I read it in both directions, and only with both people's agreement ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Pluto square natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-pluto-square-natal-sun/) is one of the long transits astrologers watch most closely. Pluto stays within range for two years or more, and the season is usually read as one in which something about who you are asks to be rebuilt from the ground up. Twice a year, the Sun in the sky squares your natal Pluto for a day or two, a brief echo of the same friction. Weather, not a forecast: a Pluto transit describes a deep season, and what changes in it is yours to decide.
