A sign has one ruler, or two in the modern scheme. Its element has others. The tradition gave each of the four elements its own planets, and those change with the hour of birth, which makes triplicity the only one of the five dignities that a birth date can't settle by itself.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A triplicity is the set of three signs of one element: fire, earth, air or water ([Triplicities](/astrology/triplicities/)). Its rulers are planets put in charge of all three signs at once. In the scheme handed down from [Dorotheus of Sidon](/astrology/dorotheus-of-sidon/), each element has a ruler for charts cast by day, one for charts cast by night, and a third that takes part in both. Fire, for example, has the Sun by day, Jupiter by night and Saturn alongside; water has Venus by day, Mars by night and the Moon alongside.

A planet standing in a sign of an element it rules has dignity by triplicity, the third of the five essential dignities after domicile and [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/). [Jupiter in Leo](/astrology/jupiter-in-leo/) is neither at home nor exalted, but Leo is a fire sign and Jupiter rules fire by night: in a night chart, it stands among its own.

Day and night are a matter of [sect](/astrology/sect-day-and-night-charts/). A chart is a day chart when the Sun was above the horizon at birth, and a night chart when it was below. Two people born on the same date, one at noon and one at midnight, have nearly the same planets in the same signs, and different triplicity rulers in charge.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Not every author gives the same table, and the differences are worth knowing before you trust one.

- **Dorotheus**, in the first century, gave each element three rulers, and he held triplicity to be the most powerful of the dignities, the one that showed most plainly in a life.
- **[Ptolemy](/astrology/ptolemy/)**, a century later, changed the water signs: Mars rules them by day and by night (*Tetrabiblos*, book I). [William Lilly](/astrology/william-lilly/) kept that change in *Christian Astrology* (1647).
- **The sharing ruler**, the third planet in each element, went out of use after the period of [Hellenistic astrology](/astrology/hellenistic-astrology/) (Joseph Crane, *A Practical Guide to Traditional Astrology*, 1997).

In the medieval habit of scoring, triplicity is worth 3 points, after domicile's 5 and exaltation's 4. Dorotheus, who thought it the strongest, would have put it higher: the ranking belongs to the later centuries, not to the dignity itself.

The rulers were also used for timing. One old method divided a life into three parts: the triplicity ruler that belongs to the chart's sect was read for the early years, the one out of sect for the middle years, and the sharing ruler for the last. It reads chapters, not outcomes, and that's how I treat it: as a piece of the tradition's history. No planet's condition tells anyone how their later years will turn out.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

If a sign is the manner a function works in, as I read it, triplicity widens the frame from the sign to its element. A planet with dignity by triplicity isn't in its own house, but it's in its own neighbourhood: the element's way of working supports the function even where the sign belongs to another planet. Mars in a water sign, by Ptolemy's table, acts with the backing of water's way of feeling things through, wherever in Cancer, Scorpio or Pisces it stands.

It's also the dignity that most clearly needs the hour. Whether the Sun was above or below the horizon decides which ruler leads, and for a birth near sunrise or sunset, a few minutes decide that. That is something a birth time settles and a birth date never can ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Below triplicity, the tables go down to the degree. The [terms](/astrology/terms-bounds/) cut each sign into five unequal parts, each with its own planet, and the faces into three equal ones.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A planet needs its own sign to have any dignity.”** Triplicity gives dignity across a whole element: Jupiter has it by night in all three fire signs, though it rules only Sagittarius.
- **“Day and night charts read the same.”** For the triplicity rulers they don't: the planet in charge changes at sunrise and sunset.
- **“Triplicity is a minor dignity.”** For Dorotheus it was the strongest. Its third place in the scoring came later.
- **“The third ruler is a modern addition.”** It's the oldest layer. Later authors dropped it, not added it.
