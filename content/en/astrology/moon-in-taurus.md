Taurus is the sign where tradition says the Moon is exalted. I read it as feeling at a walking pace: what you feel arrives slowly, settles through the body and through routine, and stays a long time once it has arrived.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

With the Moon in [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), the work the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) does in every chart, absorbing what happens, attaching to people, restoring a sense of safety, takes on the weight and continuity of fixed earth. A feeling goes through the senses before it reaches words. Food, warmth, touch, a familiar walk, a garden, a song you've played for twenty years: for a Taurus Moon these aren't distractions from feeling. They are how the feeling gets processed.

Exaltation is tradition's word for a placement where a body is honoured and at ease. Ptolemy gives Taurus as the Moon's, with its fall in Scorpio, the opposite sign. I read it as a Moon whose need for safety and the sign's love of steadiness point the same way, which tends to make your feelings hard to knock over. That isn't a better heart than anyone else's: the [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/) describes ease, not virtue.

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) rules Taurus, so I look at it next. Venus shows what you value and take pleasure in, and with the Moon here, that is very often what soothes you. Its sign and house say which comforts work best for you, and where you go to find them.

Two and a half days or so is all the Moon spends in Taurus on each trip round the zodiac, and about 1 person in 12 is born in that window. The day before, it may still have been in Aries, the [Moon in Aries](/astrology/moon-in-aries/), quick to flare and quick to finish; the day after, it may have reached Gemini, where the [Moon in Gemini](/astrology/moon-in-gemini/) talks a feeling through. If the Moon changed sign on your birthday, the time you were born decides ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You love through presence. A meal made, an arm round a shoulder, someone's favourite chair kept free every evening: your care is physical and dependable, and you notice when it isn't returned in kind. Trust builds slowly and then holds. Once you've let someone in, you don't let go easily, even after a hard year. You're often the calm one when a partner is in a storm, which is a real gift, as long as your own feelings get their turn. Change in a relationship needs time to become familiar before you can welcome it, so a partner who announces one suddenly may meet a wall that is really only a pause.

## At work {#at-work}

You do your best work at a pace you set, in a place you can arrange. Constant restructures, hot-desking and last-minute changes cost you more than they cost most people, not because you can't adapt but because you adapt thoroughly, and thoroughly takes time. Work with tangible results suits this Moon: something made, grown, mended or counted by the end of the day. Colleagues tend to lean on your steadiness, and you're usually the one who remembers how it was done last time.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Comfort can become a lid. When something hurts, the familiar pleasures that usually settle you can be used to keep the feeling from surfacing at all: another treat, another early night, another evening exactly like the last. And because you don't flare, irritation can build quietly for months, then come out all at once, to everyone's surprise, yours included. Being asked to change before you're ready can feel like having the ground moved under your feet.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Take change in small doses: visit the new place before you move, try the new routine one day a week. Keep your comforts, and let them carry a feeling instead of covering it: a walk where you say what's wrong, a meal over which you tell someone the truth. Say early when something bothers you, while it's still small enough to move. And keep one pleasure purely for yourself, since this Moon tends to share its comfort before it takes any.

The exaltation didn't earn the Moon a better score in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/). It was ticked or crossed like every other placement, the three that came out ticked far more often were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, and across everything I checked, roughly one placement in three held. So I treat a Taurus Moon as good solid ground, and build the reading on the rest of the chart as well.
