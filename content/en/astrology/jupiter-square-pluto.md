Jupiter square Pluto is the argument between *more* and *deeper*. One part of you wants to widen, add, believe and go further; another wants to strip things back to what's true underneath. The square doesn't settle the argument for you. It keeps it alive, and that is where its strength comes from.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) is how you grow in meaning: you learn, form principles and reach for what's possible. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) is how you renew: you let go of what's obsolete, go to the depth of things and take back power over your own life. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates them through constructive friction, so each keeps bumping into the other until something adapts.

In plain words, big plans meet the demand to be real all the way through, and deep changes meet the demand to lead somewhere. It can show as impatience with half-measures, as convictions that come under pressure and come out stronger, or as an appetite for growth that keeps asking to be renovated rather than extended. It's intense. It isn't a power struggle by nature: growing stronger isn't overpowering anyone, and friction isn't failure.

Over time, the square tends to give a particular kind of authority: beliefs that have been tested, and the ability to rebuild a project when its foundations give way. That ability is practised rather than handed over, which is what squares are for.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Within 6°, about 1 person in 17 of those born from 1930 to 2009 carries this square. Jupiter squares Pluto twice between each pair of meetings, a few years after one and a few before the next, so the births fell in windows during 1933–34, 1940–41, 1946–47, 1952–53, 1959, 1965–66, 1972, 1978, 1985, 1990–91, 1998 and 2003–04. Those born inside a window share it as a year-group.

The signs of a square usually share a modality. In cardinal signs, Jupiter and Pluto push to start things; in fixed signs, to hold and deepen; in mutable signs, to adapt and rethink. The houses show where the argument takes place. Jupiter in the 7th square Pluto in the 10th, for instance, can rub a partnership's wish to grow against a career that keeps transforming; Jupiter in the 1st square Pluto in the 4th, a generous way of meeting the world against a home life that renews itself from below. None of that can be placed without a birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Set at other angles, the same two urges work differently:

- **[Jupiter conjunct Pluto](/astrology/jupiter-conjunct-pluto/):** the argument becomes an alliance, and *more* and *deeper* arrive as one push.
- **[Jupiter sextile Pluto](/astrology/jupiter-sextile-pluto/):** a friendly opening where this square has friction; the change waits for your step.
- **[Jupiter trine Pluto](/astrology/jupiter-trine-pluto/):** the two urges agree, so renewal feeds growth with little resistance.
- **[Jupiter opposite Pluto](/astrology/jupiter-opposite-pluto/):** they face each other rather than cross, so other people often hold one end.

Between two charts, [Jupiter square Pluto in synastry](/astrology/jupiter-square-pluto-synastry/) catches one person's urge to expand on the other's urge to go deep. It can feel like being pushed to grow in a direction you didn't choose, or like being asked to stop skimming. I read it in both directions.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Jupiter square natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-square-natal-pluto/) comes about every six years, twice per Jupiter cycle, and lasts a few weeks, or a few months in three passes when Jupiter turns retrograde close by. [Transiting Pluto square natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-pluto-square-natal-jupiter/) is a long one: close to exact for a year or two, crossing back and forth as Pluto turns retrograde, and when it comes depends on your birth year and on how fast Pluto happens to be moving then, about 12 years a sign at its quickest and 31 at its slowest.

Weather, not a forecast, as always: a stretch when what you want to grow and what needs to change rub against each other. The friction can be useful; it doesn't decide how things turn out.
