Some colleagues make you wonder why you ever agreed to do it that way. In your 6th house, their Lilith puts the other person's refusal to be managed in the middle of your working week.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Black Moon Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/) holds autonomy: the line a person won't let others redraw, and the instinct that bristles at being managed. [The 6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) is your ordinary week at close range: the tasks rather than the title, the habits that keep you going, the colleagues beside you, how you look after yourself. As a [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their autonomy meets your routines.

With them, the rules you never chose may start to look optional. The task that was never yours, the extra unpaid hour, the timetable someone else wrote: around them, you may question these out loud, and keep only the habits you'd pick for yourself. Your routines can become yours again.

For them, your way of working can be a frame or a cage. Your discipline may reassure their restlessness, because it's chosen rather than imposed; or your lists and rotas may stir their oldest refusal. The 6th, cadent, keeps the machinery of daily life turning; [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/) is its natural sign, and its traditional name, Bad Fortune, sits oddly with the joy Mars was said to take in it. Near the end of the house, close to your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), the person who won't be managed beside you starts to stand opposite you as an equal.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Freedom at work can land on someone else's desk. If they ignore a rule you'd rather keep, skip a chore, or treat your care for detail as fussiness, you may end up carrying what they refused. You may also feel your diligence quietly mocked.

From their side, being asked to fit a routine can feel like the old taming all over again. They may resist even sensible arrangements, simply because someone else set them.

The old books looked for illness in the 6th. Nothing in a chart sees into a body, and I never read one for that ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)); this overlay concerns how two people share the work of ordinary days.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Agree which rules are shared and which are personal, and write the shared ones down. Let each person keep a method of their own. A couple can split the chores by preference and leave each other's way of doing them alone; friends can run errands together without one directing the other; colleagues can turn a refusal into a better process instead of a stand-off; adult relatives looking after a household can take turns deciding.

When the roles are reversed, your Lilith in their 6th house, your refusal meets their working day. [Their Lilith in your 5th house](/astrology/lilith-in-5th-house-synastry/) meets your play; [their Lilith in your 7th house](/astrology/lilith-in-7th-house-synastry/), the person facing you.

Your birth time fixes your Descendant, which in Placidus, the house system I use, ends the 6th, so the minutes count when their Lilith sits near that line; their date places mean Lilith, the one I read in both charts ([which Lilith is mine?](/astrology/which-lilith-is-mine/)), and I wait for both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), single charts checked placement by placement, Lilith held 75 to 90% of the time, as did Saturn and Chiron; nothing in it measured an overlay, so I offer this one without a figure. A colleague is asked before their chart is read, like anyone else ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#what-i-ask)).
