The last ten degrees of Libra are crowded. Saturn's exaltation degree, two of the brightest stars in the sky and the border with Scorpio all fall within them.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

After Saturn ([Libra, second decan](/astrology/libra-second-decan/)), the [Chaldean order](/astrology/chaldean-order/) moves on to Jupiter, so the third face of [Libra](/astrology/libra/) is Jupiter's ([faces](/astrology/faces/)), and [Jupiter in Libra](/astrology/jupiter-in-libra/) between 20° and 30° stands in its own face. The modern decans send these degrees to the one air sign left, Gemini, and so to Mercury.

The pairing is an odd one. Jupiter and Mercury rule opposite signs, Sagittarius facing Gemini and Pisces facing Virgo, so each is in detriment where the other is at home. Here the two systems lay the planet of the wide view and the planet of detail over the same ten degrees, and I let them talk: a planet in this decan tends to want both the principle and the small print.

The decan also holds the peak of Saturn's exaltation. Tradition places it in the 21st degree of Libra ([exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/)), which is this decan's first degree: Saturn between 20° and 21° Libra stands on the very degree of its exaltation.

## Its image {#its-image}

Wallis Budge's list of the Egyptian decans gives these degrees Khentet-ḥert. Later lists turned it into other names: Phou in a Greek Hermetic text, Senta or Atepiten in [Firmicus Maternus](/astrology/firmicus-maternus/), Ophionius for the Jesuit scholar Athanasius Kircher in 1652. None of them comes down with a picture I can vouch for.

The clearest image in these degrees now belongs to a star rather than to the decan: [Spica](/astrology/spica/), the ear of wheat in the Virgin's hand, at 24°12′ Libra, with [Arcturus](/astrology/arcturus/), the Herdsman's star, at 24°36′. Both stars belong to other constellations, Virgo and Boötes, which is what [precession](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/) has done to the old match between signs and stars.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun crosses this decan from about 13 to 22 October, and on about 16 and 17 October it passes Spica. A [Sun in Libra](/astrology/sun-in-libra/) this late is often told it is nearly Scorpio. It isn't: at the minute of birth the Sun is in one sign, and when a birthday falls on 22 or 23 October, the time settles which ([born on the cusp](/astrology/born-on-the-cusp/)). Even the modern decans, which borrow a ruler from another sign, borrow it from Gemini here, not from the sign next door ([Scorpio, first decan](/astrology/scorpio-first-decan/)).

For any planet here, Libra's manner stays in front: weighing, pairing, keeping things even. Jupiter's face widens the question from this one exchange to the principle behind it, and Mercury's decan wants the terms written down.

Spica and Arcturus I describe on their own pages. Like every fixed star, they stay out of my readings, which are built on the planets by sign, house and aspect ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

A generation shares these degrees through the slow planets: [Neptune in Libra](/astrology/neptune-in-libra/) crossed them from late 1951 to 1957, and Pluto from late 1979 to 1984. Which house they fall in is what makes them yours, and that takes a birth time.
