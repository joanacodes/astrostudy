A composite Sun in Pisces gives a relationship a centre that's easier to feel than to describe. The bond the two of you make tends to live in a shared inner world, made of images, music, compassion or faith, more than in anything you could list.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is the midpoint of your two natal Suns, and every pair of positions actually has two midpoints, exactly opposite each other. The composite keeps the nearer one. Behind every composite Sun in [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/) stands a second, unused point in Virgo, set aside by convention and never read. About 1 pair in 12 ends up with its Sun in Pisces this way, in mutable water, the last sign of the zodiac.

Read as the bond's own Sun, this puts its identity in what the two of you imagine and feel together. Such a relationship often has a world only its members can enter: the songs that mean something to you both, a private language of images, the care for someone else that you took on together. It's revived by beauty and by quiet, and its purpose tends to be compassion, for each other and for whoever needs it. Being mutable water, it adapts: a move, a new job, a change in the family can be absorbed without the bond losing its sense of itself, as long as the inner world comes too. Two thoroughly practical people can find, in a bond like this, the one place where they're allowed to dream.

Modern tropical astrology gives Pisces to [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/); the tradition gives it to [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/). In a birth chart I read both, and the composite has a version of each: composite Neptune, by sign and house, shows where the bond opens to imagination, and composite Jupiter where it grows. In one person's chart, the [Sun in Pisces](/astrology/sun-in-pisces/) describes an imagination at work in a single life; here the imagination is shared, and it often belongs to neither of you alone.

In a bond this porous, it can be hard to tell whose feeling is whose. Things go unsaid because they seem understood, and being understood isn't the same as agreeing. Empathy isn't the absence of edges, though, and the bond is safer for having some: saying the plain thing plainly, about money, time or plans, keeps the shared world from swallowing the two people in it. Read after the [composite Sun in Aquarius](/astrology/composite-sun-in-aquarius/), with its eyes on the future, and before the [composite Sun in Aries](/astrology/composite-sun-in-aries/), which starts everything afresh, a Pisces composite Sun sounds like the pause in between: a place to rest and imagine.

A bond this given to imagining should know where imagination stops. I describe composites rather than draw them, and the astrologers who use them read them with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), never as a forecast of how long a relationship lasts or what becomes of it. Compassion includes the other person's say, too: their birth data is theirs to offer, and a composite waits for both people's yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
