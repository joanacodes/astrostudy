Halfway between two crossings of the top of your chart, Jupiter reaches the bottom: the IC, the degree exactly opposite your Midheaven. From there it faces your public direction across the whole chart, and the growth it brings works out of sight for a while, in the foundations your public life stands on.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) widens and gives things meaning; the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) is how your capacities become visible in the world. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) relates them through polarity, so when Jupiter stands across from your Midheaven it lights the other end of the axis: the base, the continuity and the belonging that make public life possible. Like all [transits](/astrology/transits/), it works with what your chart already holds, and here that's the part of your chart people rarely see.

The point opposite your Midheaven is your [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), and in Placidus, the house system I use, it begins the 4th house: this crossing is the first day of [Jupiter transiting the 4th house](/astrology/jupiter-transit-4th-house/). The IC is a foundation rather than an address: it covers home and family, and also whatever keeps you steady, rested and rooted. Belonging isn't only a place, either: a community, a language or a practice you keep coming back to can be where this transit does its work.

Seen from the Midheaven, this is the low point of the twelve-year round. About six years later Jupiter reaches the top again, and what grows at the root this year often becomes what you stand on then. That makes it a good season for building quietly rather than being seen to build.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Down at the root of the chart, Jupiter usually spends about eleven days within my orbs of 1½° before exact and 1° after, and seven crossings in ten go that way. Three in ten are slower: [Jupiter retrograde](/astrology/jupiter-retrograde/) sets in nearby, and Jupiter goes back and forth over your IC for about eight months.

The dates depend on your birth time more than people expect. Twins born twenty minutes apart can have their IC about five degrees apart, enough for one to meet this crossing weeks or months before the other. So I read from the recorded time, never from a guess ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Six or seven times by 80, people born between 1930 and 2009 have Jupiter at the root of their chart, always halfway between two passes of [transiting Jupiter conjunct natal Midheaven](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-conjunct-natal-midheaven/). Jupiter gets here after [transiting Jupiter sextile natal Midheaven](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-sextile-natal-midheaven/), [transiting Jupiter square natal Midheaven](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-square-natal-midheaven/) and [transiting Jupiter trine natal Midheaven](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-trine-natal-midheaven/) have each come once, and makes all three again on its way back up.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Look after the base your work stands on: sleep, the people who steady you, the place you come back to at night. Jupiter at the root rewards upkeep as much as expansion.

Step back from visibility on purpose, for a while. A season out of the spotlight can feel like losing ground; with Jupiter on your IC, it's often where the next direction takes shape.

Write down what you'd like your public life to rest on in six years, when Jupiter tops your chart again. Then start laying it now, where nobody needs to see it.
