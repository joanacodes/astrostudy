Keep it or let it go? Saturn opposite Pluto in synastry gives that question two owners: one person's Saturn, about 180° from the other's Pluto. What the first maintains, protects and keeps going sits across from what the second needs to end, clear out or rebuild. Each can look to the other like the risk to what matters most.

## What flows {#what-flows}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) guards: structures, duties, the things worth keeping because they took so long to build. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) clears the ground: it ends what is finished, uncovers what was buried, and gives it back as strength. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) sets two functions face to face, so that each is seen more clearly, and among the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) this one puts the keeping in one chart and the ending in the other.

The fears behind each end are worth knowing. As I read them, Saturn fears chaos and wasted effort, while Pluto needs the truth about what has stopped working, so that things can be reorganised. One person is afraid of losing what was built, the other of living inside something already dead.

If the Saturn is yours, the other person can seem ready to tear down what you've spent years maintaining, and you may hold on harder the more they push.

If the Pluto is yours, the other person can seem to cling to forms that no longer hold any life, and you may push harder the more they hold on.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

An opposition is a seesaw, never enmity, but this seesaw is about endings, which makes it heavy. Each end can harden into a position: the guardian who won't let anything go, the reformer who wants everything gone. What gets lost is the middle ground where most real decisions live: what to keep, what to change, what to end, and when.

The pair's other angles tell a different story. [Saturn conjunct Pluto in synastry](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-pluto-synastry/) folds keeping and ending into one point; [Saturn sextile Pluto in synastry](/astrology/saturn-sextile-pluto-synastry/) breaks the work into stages; [Saturn square Pluto in synastry](/astrology/saturn-square-pluto-synastry/) presses the two together at right angles; [Saturn trine Pluto in synastry](/astrology/saturn-trine-pluto-synastry/) lets them carry change together.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Say what you fear losing and what you fear keeping. Then sort, item by item, what continues, what changes and what ends, with a date for each. Siblings deciding what to do with a family home, partners in a business that has outgrown its first form, an adult child and a parent dividing up the family's responsibilities: each can use the opposition to make an ending deliberate instead of forced.

It can span a parent's generation: a person born in mid-1995, Saturn in Pisces, has it with anyone born between August 1967 and September 1971, Pluto in Virgo. Or just eight years: a person born in mid-1955, Pluto in Leo, has it with anyone born from April to August 1963, Saturn in Aquarius. With every birth of those spans sharing it, the opposition can't tell me anything about two people in particular, so it stays out of my count, which only takes contacts with the Sun, the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, the Ascendant or the Midheaven. In the houses, it reads as one era's guardianship facing another era's need for renewal.

Without both birth times there are no houses to read; the opposition itself takes no notice of the hour ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). If their Pluto falls in your 11th house, for instance, the question of what to end reaches your friendships and the groups you belong to ([Their Pluto in your 11th house](/astrology/pluto-in-11th-house-synastry/)).

[Saturn opposite Pluto](/astrology/saturn-opposite-pluto/), inside one chart, asks one person to balance holding on and letting go. Between two, the balance is negotiated, and I read it only when both people have agreed ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
