Some pairs clear the air within the hour: a sharp word, an apology, a laugh, and the evening carries on. A composite Moon in Aries describes that emotional tempo inside a relationship, as something the two of you make together rather than something either of you brings.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

A [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) places each body at the [midpoint](/astrology/midpoints/) of its two natal positions, and its [Moon](/astrology/moon/) describes what the relationship needs in order to feel safe: how it takes in what happens, which habits it falls into, what it reaches for after a hard day. [Aries](/astrology/aries/), cardinal fire, gives those needs a direct and impatient manner. A bond with this Moon tends to feel steadiest when something is moving: a plan begun on the spot, a problem dealt with the day it comes up, a hurt named before it has time to harden.

That tempo belongs to the relationship. Apart, you may each take days to say what upset you, and still find that together you say it at once; one of you may have the [Moon in Aries](/astrology/moon-in-aries/), or neither. Living inside it often feels like a shared reflex: the bond is ready to move on from a disagreement before the slower of you has finished feeling it. Saying so out loud, *I'm not there yet*, gives the quieter feeling its turn.

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) rules Aries, so composite Mars, another midpoint, shows where this restless energy can go to work: a renovation, a sport you share, a cause. Anger isn't written into the placement. Aries supplies initiative and directness, and what this Moon finds harder are the feelings that don't answer to speed: disappointment, grief, the fear that takes weeks to admit. Those need a pause the relationship doesn't take by reflex, and a pair who learns to build one in loses none of its spark.

The Moon is the fastest body in a chart, about 13° a day, so this placement depends on both birth times. An hour's error in either one moves the composite Moon by about a quarter of a degree, enough near 0° Aries to hand it back to the [composite Moon in Pisces](/astrology/composite-moon-in-pisces/), a softer and more porous mood. There's a stranger case too. When your two Moons sit almost opposite each other, one in Cancer and one in Capricorn, say, a degree or two decides whether the nearer midpoint lands in Aries or in Libra, across the circle. It's one of the reasons I won't read any chart without its time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

About 1 pair in 12 has the composite Moon in Aries, as many as in any other sign: two Moons that move this fast give every sign its share. Next door, the [composite Moon in Taurus](/astrology/composite-moon-in-taurus/) takes its comfort slowly and keeps it a long time; beside it, an Aries composite Moon lives in the present tense.

This chart isn't one I draw in a reading: what you have here is the technique, set out so you can weigh what you're told about your own. The astrologers who use it read the composite Moon with both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), never alone, and none of them can tell from it whether a relationship will last. A quick Moon is no sign of a short bond.
