Give the planet of rules a pair of scales and it starts weighing everything: promises, favours, who did the washing-up, who owes whom an apology. Tradition thought this was Saturn at its best. I'd add that it's Saturn at its most conscientious, and conscience can be heavy to carry.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) gives structure, asks for responsibility and keeps the long view. [Libra](/astrology/libra/), cardinal air ruled by Venus, is the sign of balance, partnership, diplomacy and agreements. Here Saturn's lesson is fairness: commitments that are kept, rules that apply to everyone, and relationships built slowly enough to last.

Ptolemy lists Libra as Saturn's [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/), opposite its fall in Aries. I read it this way: Saturn's rules work best when both sides agreed to them. And because [Venus](/astrology/venus/) rules Libra, your Venus shows what this Saturn is trying to keep fair, and with whom.

Saturn was in Libra from November 1950 to October 1953, September 1980 to August 1983 and October 2009 to October 2012. Between 1930 and 2009 it crossed the whole sign only twice, and its third stay had barely begun when that span ends, which is why this Saturn sign is rarer than most: about 1 person in 16. The edges blur, too. Late 1950 to summer 1951 and late 2009 to summer 2010 were shared with [Saturn in Virgo](/astrology/saturn-in-virgo/), late 1982 to summer 1983 with [Saturn in Scorpio](/astrology/saturn-in-scorpio/), as Saturn crossed back and forth. Everyone born in these years shares the sign, so the house decides where the scales hang: in the 10th it weighs fairness at work, in the 4th fairness at home.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Commitment is serious business for you: a promise is a contract, even when nobody wrote it down. You want a partnership of equals, with the load shared and the rules clear, and you can stay a long time in one that isn't, out of duty or a hope that patience will even things out. The lesson is that a fair relationship isn't a scoreboard. When two people ask me to compare their charts, I read Saturn with particular care, because it held 75 to 90% of the time in my notebook, and only ever once both of them have said yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

## At work {#at-work}

At work you're drawn to the parts of a job where fairness is the point: negotiating, mediating, drafting the agreement, designing something that has to balance. Colleagues trust you to be even-handed, and an exalted Saturn tends to hand authority to whoever is seen to be fair, so responsibility often finds you as a request to judge, chair or arbitrate. The cost is slowness to decide when every option has a fair case, and difficulty saying no to anyone who asks nicely.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Keeping score, quietly. Agreeing to things to keep the peace, then resenting the bargain. A fear of being unfair that makes it hard to choose, or to want something just for yourself. And sometimes the opposite: holding others to rules so strictly that the relationship starts to feel like a court. Cardinal air starts conversations easily; ending an unfair arrangement is harder, because it means admitting that the balance you worked for didn't hold.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Say agreements out loud, and revisit them; a deal that was fair five years ago may not be now. Tell *fair* from *equal*: sometimes the fair thing is uneven. Practise a kind, clear no. When Saturn comes back to Libra from September 2039, people born with it in 2009–12 will meet their first [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/), a chance to look at which commitments they chose and which they simply inherited.
