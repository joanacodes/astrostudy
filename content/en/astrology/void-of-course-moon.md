Some astrological calendars mark a few hours every couple of days with a small *v/c* beside the Moon. That's the void-of-course Moon: the last stretch of the Moon's passage through a sign, after it has met every planet it was going to meet there. The idea is simple. How astrologers count it, and what they do with it, is not.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) crosses a sign in about two and a half days, and on the way it makes aspects to the other planets, one after another. Once it has made the last one it will make in that sign, it is said to be void of course until it enters the next sign. Then it starts meeting planets again, and the void is over.

So it happens every time the Moon changes sign, and it can last from a few minutes to many hours. If the last aspect falls in the sign's final degrees, the void is short; if it falls early, the Moon may travel most of a day without touching anything.

The idea is old, and it has changed on the way. The Greek texts spoke of the Moon *running in the void*, *kenodromia*: the Moon making no exact aspect within the next 30°, whether or not a new sign began in between (Chris Brennan, *Hellenistic Astrology*, 2017). By William Lilly's time the sign had become the limit: a planet is void of course, he wrote, when it has separated from one planet and applies to no other while it stays in its sign (*Christian Astrology*, 1647).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Astrologers still don't count it the same way, which is why two calendars can disagree by hours.

- **The usual rule today**, followed by most calendars: from the Moon's last exact major aspect (conjunction, sextile, square, trine, opposition) to any planet, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto included, until it enters the next sign.
- **The traditional rule**, Lilly's: the same, but with the seven planets visible to the eye, from the Sun to Saturn. Without the outer planets the Moon runs out of aspects sooner, so its void often starts earlier.
- **The oldest rule**: no exact aspect within the next 30°, so a Moon about to meet a planet just over the sign's edge isn't void at all, and one with nothing ahead may be void for a long way.

Some astrologers add minor aspects, which shortens the void again. None of these rules is wrong; each belongs to a way of working, and a calendar should tell you which one it follows.

The void-of-course Moon is one of several old tests of a planet's condition, like [besieging](/astrology/besieging/), a planet hemmed in between Mars and Saturn, or [mutual reception](/astrology/mutual-reception/), two planets each in the other's sign. In Lilly's book it belongs to [horary astrology](/astrology/horary-astrology/), where a chart is cast for the moment a question is asked: when the Moon is void, he wrote, matters "go hardly on", though he allowed that a void Moon in Taurus, Cancer, Sagittarius or Pisces "performs somewhat" (*Christian Astrology*, 1647). Its other home is [electional astrology](/astrology/electional-astrology/), the choosing of dates, where many astrologers avoid starting anything important while the Moon is void.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I take the void-of-course Moon as weather, and never as a rule. It describes a few hours when the Moon, the part of a chart that responds and adjusts, has nothing left to respond to in that sign: a mood some people notice as drifting, unfocused time, and others never notice at all. That's worth knowing about. It isn't worth rearranging a life around. A job interview, a hospital appointment or a signature belongs on the day it's needed, and what decides how it goes is what you bring to it.

In a birth chart, a Moon born void of course is read differently by different astrologers. I read a natal Moon by its sign, its house and its aspects, whether void or not, and a void Moon at birth still has the aspects it made before, which are part of the chart like any other. Which house it sits in depends on the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and that tells me more than the void does.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Nothing started during a void Moon will work.”** It's a convention of horary and electional astrology, read inside those methods, and even Lilly made exceptions.
- **“Everyone agrees on when it starts.”** There are at least three rules in use, and they can disagree by hours.
- **“The void Moon is rare and ominous.”** It comes round every couple of days, every month of every year.
- **“A void Moon at birth is a missing Moon.”** The natal Moon keeps its sign, house and aspects, and is read like any other.
