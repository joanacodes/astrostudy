Neptune was discovered in 1846, while it was crossing Aquarius, and in January 1998 it came back to that sign for the first time since. Everyone born from then until February 2012 shares its Aquarian dream: a world where people are connected, equal, and kind to strangers.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/), fixed air, thinks about the many: the group, the network, the future people might share. With [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) here, compassion widens to include people you'll never meet, and imagination is drawn to whatever could connect everyone: communities without borders, fairness on a large scale, a sense of belonging to one shared story. The sign is fixed, so it keeps faith with its ideals and doesn't trade them in when they become inconvenient.

Aquarius has two rulers, and I read both. [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), its modern ruler, shows where this dream of a connected world wants to try something new; [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), the traditional ruler, shows where it has to build patiently. Read together, they describe the push and pull inside the placement: the wish to reinvent the world, and the patience it takes to change any part of it.

In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), placements weren't marked as a group: each got its own tick or cross, Neptune's sign and house included. Three of them held far more often than the rest: Saturn, Chiron and Lilith. Neptune wasn't among those three, and overall about a third held.

Neptune was in Aquarius from January 1998 to February 2012, and about 1 person in 7 born from 1930 to 2009 has it. Until November 1998 Neptune was still moving back and forth with Capricorn, and from April 2011 with Pisces, so a birth in those months could belong to [Neptune in Capricorn](/astrology/neptune-in-capricorn/) or [Neptune in Pisces](/astrology/neptune-in-pisces/). The eldest of this generation are in their late twenties. The youngest are still teenagers, and I don't read [children’s charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/), so this page is for those of you already grown. What's yours within the generation is the house: the part of your life where you look for your people.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, an Aquarian Neptune often starts from friendship. The ideal is a partnership of equals who are also allies, two people who believe in the same future and give each other room. You may find it easier to be warm towards a crowd than to show it to one person, which can puzzle a partner who wants to be singled out. The bond grows when your wide compassion lands on someone in particular: their odd habits, their bad days, the details that make them unlike anyone else.

## At work {#at-work}

The common good is this Neptune's dream at work. Collective projects, networks, research, campaigning, community work, science or technology turned to shared use: anything that connects people suits it. You can picture a better system long before others see the need for one. The risk is giving years to a cause or a group without noticing that it no longer gives anything back, or that the people running it have stopped living up to its ideals.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

It can be easier to love humanity than the person in front of you. A beautiful ideal of the group can make every real group a disappointment, and a dream of fairness can grow impatient with the imperfect people it was meant to serve. Distance can feel like freedom when it's really a way of not being reached. And when the shared future looks dark, this Neptune can take the whole world's trouble as its own.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Choose one cause and give it hands: a local group, a shift a week, a project with people you can name. Let friends be particular rather than representative. Step away from the wider world now and then, and come back to it rested, because a dream of community needs at least one community you actually belong to.
