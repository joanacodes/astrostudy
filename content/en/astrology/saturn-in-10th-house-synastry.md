Your work gains a demanding witness when the other person's Saturn sits in your 10th house. They look at what you do in the world the way a good examiner reads a thesis: seriously, critically, and with an eye on where it could lead in ten years.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) is the planet of long climbs: authority earned rather than given, ambition on a timetable, the patience to build a reputation. [The 10th house](/astrology/10th-house/) covers what you're known for: work and title, the responsibilities people see you carry, the reputation you'd like to leave. With their Saturn in it, this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/) ties their sense of duty to your standing.

For you, it can be the mentor you needed: someone who believes your ambitions deserve a plan, reads your application twice and tells you which year to aim for. Under their eye, you may take on responsibility sooner and build a career more deliberately.

For them, your public life can feel like something they're partly accountable for. They may want to guide your choices, worry over your risks, and take your setbacks to heart. No house is more public than the 10th, an angular house whose natural sign, [Capricorn](/astrology/capricorn/), is Saturn's own: here Saturn stands close to its natural ground. Where it opens the house, their Saturn conjoins your [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/), and I read that contact as closely as the overlay.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Guidance can become supervision. Even when they aren't your boss, they may behave like one, and you may start working for their approval rather than your own direction. Praise from them can be rare, and their criticism weigh more than anyone else's.

For them, your success can become their proof of having done right by you, and your failure theirs: responsibility and self-worth blur. The authority they hold over you, at work or in feeling, can make honest feedback hard for them to hear.

No career can be read in advance from this overlay, yours or theirs; it describes how ambition and authority meet between you.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Separate their advice from your decision, and say so. Ask for their standards when they help, and set them aside when they don't. In a couple, two careers need a shared plan for whose turn it is; between colleagues, a real hierarchy works best with clear roles and regular reviews that run both ways; between friends, this is the one who asks about your five-year plan, and can be told when to stop; between adult relatives, notice when their hopes for your work are really their own unfinished ambitions.

In their chart the card turns over: your Saturn lands in their 10th house, you examine their ambitions, and it falls to you to remember they're not your project. [Their Saturn in your 9th house](/astrology/saturn-in-9th-house-synastry/) tests your beliefs; [their Saturn in your 11th house](/astrology/saturn-in-11th-house-synastry/), your friendships and hopes.

Every minute of your birth time shows in your Midheaven, the cusp of the 10th, and in Placidus, the house system I use, in every cusp after it. Everything their Saturn needs is in their date of birth, though I ask for their time too, before any synastry ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). A mentor about thirty years older can carry Saturn in your own Saturn's sign, one full cycle earlier, and so can a colleague a year or two apart; with your own Saturn in your 10th ([Saturn in the 10th house](/astrology/saturn-in-10th-house/)), the long climb is familiar on both sides. In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Saturn, Chiron and Lilith held 75 to 90% of the time, counted sign and house in single charts; that gives no figure for an overlay, and I don't offer one. Work is shared ground, but reading two charts still needs both people's consent ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
