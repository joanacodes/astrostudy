Ask a Western astrologer what sign you are and the answer comes from the tropical zodiac, even if nobody says the word. It's closer to a calendar than to a star map: its signs are fixed to the seasons, and that single choice explains most of what people find puzzling about it.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The [zodiac](/astrology/zodiac/) needs a starting point, and the tropical zodiac takes the March equinox: the moment, around 20 March each year, when the Sun, moving along the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), crosses the celestial equator heading north. Wherever the Sun stands at that instant is 0° Aries, the [Aries Point](/astrology/aries-point/), and the other signs follow in steps of 30°.

Count on and the year falls into place. The Sun reaches 0° Cancer at the June solstice, 0° Libra at the September equinox and 0° Capricorn at the December solstice ([solstices and equinoxes](/astrology/solstices-and-equinoxes/)). That's why those four signs, the cardinal ones, are said to open the seasons, and why your Sun sign follows the calendar so closely: Aries from about 21 March to 19 April, Taurus from about 20 April to 20 May, and so on round the year, shifting by a day from one year to the next.

The name comes from the tropics, the two circles that mark how far north and south the Sun goes: *tropē* is Greek for a turning, and the solstices are where the Sun turns back. Astronomers still call the March equinox point the First Point of Aries, and mark it with the sign's glyph, ♈.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The tropical zodiac is tied to the Earth's year, not to the stars, and the stars behind it slowly shift. The Earth's axis wobbles like a spinning top, so the equinox slides back along the ecliptic by about 50 seconds of arc a year, a degree in about 72 years ([precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/)). The sign of Aries, still starting at the equinox, has drifted away from the constellation it was named after, and today it begins in front of the stars of Pisces.

None of this took the tradition by surprise. Ptolemy, writing in the second century, knew of precession from Hipparchus and used it in his astronomy, and in the Tetrabiblos he still counted the signs from the equinoxes and solstices, on the grounds that the signs take their natures from those turning points and from nowhere else. The tropical zodiac was chosen with open eyes.

Astrologers who prefer to count from the stars use the [sidereal zodiac](/astrology/sidereal-zodiac/), and the two now sit about 24° apart. A planet at 10° Taurus in one is at about 16° Aries in the other. Neither position is a mistake: they're the same point of the sky, measured from two different zeros.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Every chart I read is drawn in the tropical zodiac. It's the frame of modern tropical astrology, the tradition I work in, and the meanings I give the signs belong to that frame: Aries starting things, Cancer looking after someone, Libra weighing one thing against another, Capricorn taking responsibility.

Because the signs follow the calendar, birthdays near a change of sign need care. The Sun changes sign at a different hour each year, so a birth on 20 April or 21 March can fall on either side, and only the time settles it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). The Moon needs that care more often still, since it changes sign every two and a half days or so.

The tropical frame is also why many astrologers watch the four cardinal degrees closely: a planet at 0° Aries, Cancer, Libra or Capricorn sits exactly on one of the year's turning points ([0° of a sign](/astrology/0-of-a-sign/)). And it's why I'm careful with software settings: a program set to the sidereal zodiac moves most planets back by a sign, and the reading with them. When I say a planet is in Leo, I mean tropical Leo, and [How I read](/how-i-read/) sets out the rest of my choices.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The tropical zodiac is wrong because the constellations have moved.”** It was never meant to follow them. It measures the year from the equinox, and the drift has been known since antiquity.
- **“Tropical astrologers have never heard of precession.”** Ptolemy knew of it and still set the signs by the equinoxes. Choosing the seasons over the stars is a decision, not an oversight.
- **“Tropical means the zodiac of hot countries.”** It refers to the tropics, the lines where the Sun turns back at the solstices.
- **“My sign has changed.”** Not in the tropical zodiac: the signs are still counted from the equinox, as they always were. Stories that say otherwise are about the constellations or the sidereal zodiac ([Did my sign change?](/astrology/did-my-sign-change/)).
