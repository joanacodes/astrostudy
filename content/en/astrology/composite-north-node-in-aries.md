Some couples are the best hosts in town and still can't choose where to spend their own holiday. Composite North Node in Aries points a relationship away from that practised courtesy, towards a question it rarely puts to itself: what do the two of us actually want?

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) places every body at the nearer midpoint of its two natal positions, and the [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) is no exception: halfway between yours and the other person's, it marks the direction the relationship grows in, while the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/), exactly opposite, marks its familiar ground. With the South Node in [Libra](/astrology/libra/), the pair is fluent in tact, compromise and the art of not making a fuss. The North Node in [Aries](/astrology/aries/), cardinal fire ruled by [Mars](/astrology/mars/), asks it for what it practises less: a preference said first, a plan begun before everyone has signed up to it.

That habit belongs to the relationship, not necessarily to either of you. Two people who are quick to decide at work can become a committee of two at home, each waiting for the other's yes, because between them harmony has always come first. The Aries end doesn't ask anyone to stop caring what the other wants. It asks the bond to have wants of its own and to act on them: book the trip, start the project, turn down the third dinner invitation of the month. As in a birth chart, the sign's ruler is where astrologers look to see where that practice happens and how fast, here composite Mars.

For one person, [North Node in Aries](/astrology/north-node-in-aries/) describes learning to choose for yourself. Here the choosing is joint, and that's harder than it sounds: whoever goes first can feel pushy, and whoever follows can feel overruled. Many pairs with this node do best when they take turns being the one who decides, and count a small decision made quickly as progress rather than as a lapse in consideration.

In two million random pairs born between 1930 and 2009, about 1 pair in 11 had it, slightly more than an even spread across the twelve signs would give. Two people of the same age carry it when both were born while the node itself stood in Aries: between January 1949 and July 1950, August 1967 and April 1969, March 1986 and November 1987, or December 2004 and June 2006. An age gap can produce it as well. Born in April 1965 and August 1971, with natal North Nodes near 15° Gemini and 15° Aquarius, two people get a composite North Node near 15° Aries, in a sign neither of them was born with.

Close to the sign's edges, the method itself gets a say. The mean node and the true node are never more than a degree or two apart, but that's enough to send a composite node at 0° Aries back into [composite North Node in Pisces](/astrology/composite-north-node-in-pisces/), or one at 29° on into [composite North Node in Taurus](/astrology/composite-north-node-in-taurus/). I look at both, and when they split, both pages apply.

The hard part is overcorrection. A bond that has spent years agreeing can lurch into deciding everything at once, or treat each quarrel as proof that it has found its nerve. The Libra end isn't the obstacle: its tact is what lets two people act together without trampling each other, and it stays useful long after the first bold plan.

Composites aren't part of my readings, so this is the technique's own reading, which its practitioners weigh against both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). It describes a direction, never a verdict: nothing in it says whether two people will stay together, marry or prosper. And a chart of two people needs two yeses before anyone draws it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
