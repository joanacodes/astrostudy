Some relationships are the centre of a wider circle: the friends who gather at yours, the group you joined together, the shared hopes you talk about late at night. A composite Moon in the 11th house finds the bond's comfort in friendship and in a future the two of you are working towards.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In the [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), halfway between your two natal Moons, the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) finds itself in the setting of [the 11th house](/astrology/11th-house/): friends, groups, networks and hopes. The house is succedent, naturally Aquarius's; tradition knew it as the Good Spirit, where Jupiter rejoices. A relationship with its Moon here feels safest when it belongs to something bigger than the two of you: a circle of friends, a community, a cause, a plan for the years ahead.

That sense of belonging is the relationship's own. Someone born with a [Moon in the 11th house](/astrology/moon-in-11th-house/) finds belonging in a chosen circle, and someone whose Moon falls in yours becomes part of your hopes ([their Moon in your 11th house](/astrology/moon-in-11th-house-synastry/)). The composite 11th is the circle the pair gathers around itself and the future it imagines together: a plan to start something with friends, a community garden, a long-term dream the two of you keep coming back to. At heart it is often a friendship, even when the bond is something more as well, and two solitary people can find that together they host, join, organise and keep in touch with half a town.

Its difficulty is that the circle can crowd out the couple. A bond that settles among friends can find one-to-one time strangely hard, use the group to avoid a private conversation, or measure itself by other people's approval. Community isn't conformity, and friendship isn't popularity, however full the calendar. The relationship needs evenings for two as well as evenings for twelve, and hopes of its own as well as the group's.

There's no frequency to give for this placement. This site's count, two million pairs of random birth dates, covers composite signs only, but houses depend on two birth times and on the method used to draw them, from the midpoints of the two charts' cusps or from a composite Midheaven cast for where you live, so no single figure would mean much. For your own pair, only the two real times can say whether this Moon is in the 11th at all ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)).

Friends curious about their composite need each other's agreement before their birth data is put side by side, as with any two people ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)). Beside this house sit the [composite Moon in the 10th house](/astrology/composite-moon-in-10th-house/), which needs a public role, and the [composite Moon in the 12th house](/astrology/composite-moon-in-12th-house/), which needs a private retreat. I describe composites without drawing them; for the astrologers who do, this Moon is read beside the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) and both birth charts, and it says how a relationship belongs, not what its friends or its future will bring.
