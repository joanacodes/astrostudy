What you want to change and what you're bound to keep pull at right angles whenever Saturn squares your Uranus, twice in each round of about 29½ years. For some months neither the rebel nor the guardian in you gets an easy win.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) differentiates: it's where you want autonomy, new arrangements, the freedom to do things your own way. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) maintains: rules, continuity, commitments other people rely on. A [square](/astrology/square/), 90°, relates them through constructive friction, and friction isn't failure: it's how each learns what the other protects.

It often feels like restlessness inside limits. A routine starts to chafe; a rule you'd accepted looks arbitrary; a plan for change runs into a contract, a lack of time or someone's need for stability. The temptation is to break out, or to clamp down. Uranus tends to underestimate the value of continuity, and Saturn to confuse control with safety, so the square asks for change that respects what still works, and order that leaves room to breathe. A transit describes a pressure, not an event, and I don't read this one for sudden losses or upheavals.

Uranus moves so slowly that people born a few years apart share its sign; the house is what makes this square yours. With Uranus in the 4th, Saturn squares it from your 1st ([Saturn transiting the 1st house](/astrology/saturn-transit-1st-house/)) or your 7th, and the wish to live differently at home meets your own limits of time and energy, or a partner's need for things to stay as they are. Uranus in your 4th is a statement about your birth time as much as your birth year; without the time, I don't read the square ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Under my orbs, which run from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the square tends to press for about nine months, which is why a quick escape rarely settles it. In a little over half of its [transits](/astrology/transits/) Saturn's yearly loop catches the square, so the exact angle comes three times over about nine and a half months; otherwise the crossing can be done in three weeks.

The first square follows the angle between Saturn and Uranus at your birth. It came at 0.7 for someone born on 1 January 1975, at 3.3 for 1995 and 4.6 for 1945, at 7.5 for 1965, at about 10 for 1985 and 2005, and at 11.5 and 13.5 for 1935 and 1955. For all births from 1930 to 2009 the first square fell between the first weeks of life and about 15½, a median of about 8, and five or six follow by 80.

For the generations born with [Saturn square Uranus](/astrology/saturn-square-uranus/), in the early 1930s, the early 1950s, the mid-1970s and around 2000, keeping and changing have argued since birth, and this transit is a season in which the argument can be settled a little better. In each round, the first square comes about seven years after [transiting Saturn conjunct natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-saturn-conjunct-natal-uranus/) and two or three after [transiting Saturn sextile natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-saturn-sextile-natal-uranus/); the second follows [transiting Saturn opposite natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-saturn-opposite-natal-uranus/), with [transiting Saturn trine natal Uranus](/astrology/transiting-saturn-trine-natal-uranus/) in between.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Name what you're keeping, and why. Change goes better when you know which parts are load-bearing.

Change the rule, not the whole system. Under this square, a targeted reform beats a revolution.

Give restlessness an outlet that doesn't burn anything down: a side project, a trial period, a new skill.
