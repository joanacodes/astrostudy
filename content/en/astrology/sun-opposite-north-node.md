Sun opposite North Node puts the Sun at the far end of the nodal line: when you were born, it stood beside the South Node, facing the North across the chart. It describes a self rooted in what you already know how to be, and growth that means reaching right across.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) organises identity in a chart: purpose, vitality, a self that holds together and shows. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) is a point, not a planet, and I read it as a capacity built by practice, unfamiliar at first and widening once it takes. Exactly opposite lies the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/), what came easily or early. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) relates two ends through polarity; here the Sun has settled on the familiar one.

In plain words, your sense of who you are grows out of what you already do well. That's a strength: people recognise you quickly, and you recognise yourself. The catch is that the North Node's capacity can feel like not being you at all, so first attempts feel like wearing someone else's coat. The opposition doesn't ask you to give up your Sun to grow; it asks the two ends to talk, the self you know lending its confidence to the skill you don't have yet.

It's often read as an ego blocking the path. I don't read it that way: developing the North Node adds to what's there, and the familiar end stays yours.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Lived with, it usually shows as an identity that formed early and firmly, with a growth edge that feels foreign. The Sun usually shares the South Node's sign, with the North Node in the opposite one: a Sun in Virgo with the North Node in Pisces is a self built on precision and usefulness, growing towards trust in what can't be planned. The houses say where: the Sun in the 10th with the North Node in the 4th is a self made at work and asked to grow at home. Houses need a birth time.

About 1 person in 21 is born with the Sun within 8° of this opposition, roughly a week either side of the Sun's yearly crossing of the South Node, which falls a little earlier each year as the nodes drift backwards. Those weeks are an eclipse season: with the Sun near a node, a new or full moon can become an eclipse ([why eclipses follow the nodes](/astrology/why-eclipses-follow-the-nodes/)). Within a degree or two, familiar ground pulls hard; near 8°, the mean and true node can even disagree about whether you have it ([mean node and true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/)); I look at both. My notebook doesn't name the nodes ([the invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)), so I treat this as a direction to try, never a verdict.

Each of the pair's other aspects places your sense of self differently on the line:

- **[Sun conjunct North Node](/astrology/sun-conjunct-north-node/):** the mirror image, the Sun on the growing end, so becoming yourself and stretching are one movement.
- **[Sun sextile North Node](/astrology/sun-sextile-north-node/):** an opening towards growth, with a trine back to the South Node: new ground within reach of old strengths.
- **[Sun square North Node](/astrology/sun-square-north-node/):** the Sun halfway between the ends, a self asking to be part of the path rather than pick a side.
- **[Sun trine North Node](/astrology/sun-trine-north-node/):** easy flow towards growth and a sextile to the familiar: change that doesn't feel like betraying who you were.

Between two charts, [Sun opposite North Node in synastry](/astrology/sun-opposite-north-node-synastry/) puts one person's Sun on the other's South Node: the feeling of having known each other for ages, comfortable and sometimes too comfortable. I read it in both directions, because the two rarely feel it the same way.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting North Node opposite natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-north-node-opposite-natal-sun/) is the South Node in today's sky passing over your Sun. The nodes take about 18 months to cross a sign, so it lingers for months, with that stretch's eclipses falling near your Sun; astrologers read it as a time for finishing and clearing out. Born with this opposition, you meet it at each [nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/), at about 18½, 37 and 56, when the eclipse seasons fall around your birthday again. At the [nodal reversal](/astrology/nodal-reversal/), around 28 and 46, the North Node crosses your Sun instead ([transiting North Node conjunct natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-north-node-conjunct-natal-sun/)).

Weather, not a forecast: seasons when the ground you know and the next step are lit at once. They don't say what you'll put down, or what you'll pick up instead.
