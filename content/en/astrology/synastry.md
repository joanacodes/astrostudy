Two people, two birth charts, laid side by side: synastry asks how they speak to each other. One person's Venus meets the other's Mars, one person's Saturn lands in the other's 7th house, and between those contacts a picture forms of how the two tend to meet. Not whether they should.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Synastry is the comparison of two natal charts, and it has two parts. The first is the aspects between them: the angle one person's planet makes to the other's, read with the same aspects as a single chart ([synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/)). The second is the houses: where each person's planets fall in the other person's chart, the area of life one lights up in the other ([house overlays](/astrology/house-overlays/)).

The comparison is old. Ptolemy already looked at two nativities side by side: marriages, he wrote, tend to last when the Sun and Moon of the two charts are in trine or sextile with each other, and for friendships he compared the places of the Sun, the Moon, the Ascendant and the Lot of Fortune in both (*Tetrabiblos*, book IV). The word *synastry* is more recent; the habit of comparing is not.

It sits beside two other ways of reading a pair, which work differently: [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), built from the midpoints of the two charts, and [the Davison chart](/astrology/davison-chart/), cast for a moment halfway between the two births.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Both charts come first, each read on its own. Then come the contacts between them, and those need choices.

- **Orbs.** The ones I use take the wider of the two bodies: 8° for the Sun and Moon, 6° for Mercury, Venus and Mars, 5° for Jupiter and Saturn, 4° for Uranus, Neptune and Pluto, 3° for Chiron, Lilith and the North Node; the full orb for a conjunction or an opposition, less for a trine or square, less again for a sextile. With those orbs, about 1 pair of people in 7 has one person's Sun square the other's Moon, one way round or the other.
- **Which contacts count.** Everyone born within a few years shares the slow planets' positions, so a contact between two slow bodies says more about two generations than about two people. I count only contacts with at least one personal body: the Sun, the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, the Ascendant or the Midheaven. Two slow bodies I read through the houses they fall in.
- **Both directions.** Every contact is read from each side. The person whose Moon is touched by the other's Saturn doesn't live it the way the person with the Saturn does ([Moon conjunct Saturn in synastry](/astrology/moon-conjunct-saturn-synastry/)).
- **Both birth times.** Without them, the houses are unknown, and the Moon, moving about 13° a day, can make or unmake a contact ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

Synastry describes interaction: what flows between two people, where they rub, and what each tends to bring out in the other. A [Venus square Mars](/astrology/venus-square-mars-synastry/) contact can be the spark of a bond and its sore point at once. A Sun–Moon trine can make time together easy, and so easy that nothing is ever discussed. Most pairs have both kinds, and the mix is what I read.

I give particular care to contacts with Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, the three placements that held best in my notebook: they show where two people can hurt each other without meaning to, and where, once they know it, they can be gentle about it. That's also why I read a synastry only when the other person knows it's happening ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)), and only between adults. My own readings for couples, friends, co-workers and family are on the [synastry readings](/synastry/) page.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

Synastry describes patterns, not outcomes. It can't tell you whether a relationship will last, whether someone is your soulmate, or whether you should stay or go, and I won't answer those questions with it. Easy contacts don't guarantee anything, hard ones don't doom anything, and context, maturity and choice weigh more than any aspect.

Each person's own chart stays primary. A contact can only bring out what is already in the two charts, so a synastry read without both natal charts is a list of angles, not a reading. And Sun signs alone, the shortcut many people start with, leave out almost everything a full comparison looks at ([sun-sign compatibility, and why it isn't enough](/astrology/sun-sign-compatibility-and-why-it-isnt-enough/)).
