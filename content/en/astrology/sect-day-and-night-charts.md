Hellenistic astrologers gave more weight to one question than to almost any other: was this person born by day or by night? The answer, called sect, sorts the planets into two teams and decides which of them leads.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A chart is a day chart when the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) was above the horizon at birth, anywhere in houses 7 to 12, and a night chart when it was below, in houses 1 to 6 ([horizon axis](/astrology/horizon-axis/)). Each sect has a leader, its luminary: the Sun by day, the Moon by night ([The sect light](/astrology/sect-light/)).

The planets divide along the same line. The Sun, Jupiter and Saturn are day planets; the Moon, Venus and Mars are night planets. Mercury has no fixed side and takes one from its phase: it joins the day when it rises before the Sun, as a morning star, and the night when it sets after the Sun, as an evening star ([Oriental and occidental](/astrology/oriental-and-occidental/)). A planet of the chart's own sect was thought more comfortable there, and more effective, than a planet of the other.

The doctrine mattered most to the Hellenistic astrologers. Paulus Alexandrinus, writing in Alexandria in 378, treats it at length in his *Introductory Matters*. Medieval astrologers kept it but gave it less weight, and built finer conditions on it, such as [hayz](/astrology/hayz/), for a planet whose sect, sign and side of the horizon all agree.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The line is the horizon of the chart, drawn by geometry rather than by daylight. Sunrise tables give the moment the top edge of the Sun appears, lifted into view by the atmosphere, while the centre of its disc is still about 50′ below the true horizon. A birth at the printed sunrise can therefore be a night birth for a few minutes more, and twilight never counts as day. Near the poles the clock stops deciding: north of the Arctic Circle at the June solstice the Sun never sets, and every birth that day is a day birth.

Once the sect is known, several things follow:

- **The light in charge.** By day the Sun leads its team, Jupiter and Saturn; by night the Moon leads Venus and Mars.
- **The benefic and the malefic of the sect.** Each chart has one benefic and one malefic of its own sect, and one of each from the other ([Benefic and malefic of the sect](/astrology/benefic-and-malefic-of-the-sect/)).
- **The lots.** Many astrologers reverse the formula of the [Part of Fortune](/astrology/part-of-fortune/), and of the lots built on it, for a night birth.
- **The triplicity rulers.** Each element has a ruler by day and another by night: for the fire signs, Dorotheus gives the Sun by day and Jupiter by night ([Triplicity rulers](/astrology/triplicity-rulers/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Sect needs the birth time more than most things in a chart. The Sun's side of the horizon depends on the hour, and near sunrise or sunset on the minute: without the time, there's no knowing which sect a chart belongs to, and I don't read it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

When I look at sect, I'm looking at the light a chart was born into, not at its luck. A day chart isn't better than a night chart. What changes is which planets work with the grain of the chart and which across it, and I let that colour how I describe them rather than rank them. None of it is a verdict on a life, and where tradition is speaking rather than anything I've tested, I tell you so ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A night chart is a darker, harder chart.”** Sect divides the planets into two teams; neither is the lucky one.
- **“Born at midday means a day chart.”** Almost always. But north of the Arctic Circle at the December solstice the Sun stays below the horizon all day, and a birth at noon there is a night birth.
- **“Mercury has no sect.”** It has no fixed one, but it takes a side in every chart from its phase: a morning Mercury is diurnal, an evening Mercury nocturnal.
- **“Uranus, Neptune and Pluto belong to a sect.”** The doctrine was drawn for the seven bodies the eye can see; the outer planets have no place in it.
