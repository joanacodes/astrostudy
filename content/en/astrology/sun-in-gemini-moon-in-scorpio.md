Curiosity is your Sun's way into the world, and your Moon lets only a few people into its own. With the Sun in Gemini and the Moon in Scorpio, you can talk easily with almost anyone and still keep your deepest feelings for a very small circle. A gibbous Moon, swelling towards full, was in the sky at your birth.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Mutable air and fixed water, with the Moon five signs ahead: a [quincunx](/astrology/quincunx/) by sign, a pair of signs without a shared quality, which keep adjusting to each other's presence. The [Sun in Gemini](/astrology/sun-in-gemini/) wants to know, to connect, to keep moving; the [Moon in Scorpio](/astrology/moon-in-scorpio/) needs to trust before it opens, and rests only when it knows what lies under the surface. One light skims and the other dives. Together they make an unusually good investigator: quick to gather, and unwilling to stop at the obvious answer.

Scorpio is the Moon's [fall](/astrology/fall/) in the old scheme, which I take as a description of effort: feelings here have to go all the way down before they rest. Your Sun answers to [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/). Scorpio has an old ruler and a new one, and I read both together: [Mars](/astrology/mars/), from the older tradition, for what this Moon guards, and [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/), from modern tropical astrology, for what it is willing to transform. When Mercury aspects either of them, thinking and feeling become hard to separate, and the mind digs.

By the time of your birth the Moon had pulled 120° to 179° ahead of the Sun, somewhere between the tenth day of the lunar month and the fifteenth, with the full moon at most five days off. Almost every one of these births is a [waxing gibbous moon](/astrology/waxing-gibbous-moon/) birth in the eight-phase count, the earliest few [first quarter moon](/astrology/first-quarter-moon/) ones, and if the Moon was about 150° ahead, the angle between the lights is itself a quincunx, as exact as the one between their signs. I read the gibbous phase as refining what is nearly ready, and this Moon refines by going deeper. [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) shows the whole set.

One person in 141 or so has this pair. The Moon's stay in Scorpio, between Libra and Sagittarius, lasts about two and a half days, and a birth on either crossing day may belong to the [Sun in Gemini, Moon in Libra](/astrology/sun-in-gemini-moon-in-libra/) or the [Sun in Gemini, Moon in Sagittarius](/astrology/sun-in-gemini-moon-in-sagittarius/) instead: the time settles it ([the Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). The rising sign and the houses come from it too, and for a Moon this private, the house it occupies says a great deal; I won't guess at it without the time.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In company you're easy; in intimacy, demanding. The Gemini Sun chats, teases and keeps things light; the Scorpio Moon is looking for the person it can trust completely, and it checks quietly before it gives. Partners may enjoy the conversation for months before they meet the depth. What helps the bond is letting the two lights meet sooner: use the Gemini gift for words to say one true, heavy thing, early, and see what the other person does with it.

## At work {#at-work}

Journalism, research, detective work of any kind, data analysis, psychology: anything that rewards both curiosity and persistence suits this pair. The Sun brings range and quick connections; the Moon brings focus and a refusal to accept the surface story. You may be the colleague who asks the question nobody wanted asked, and is right to. Small talk for its own sake wears the Moon down, while being sworn to silence by others can chafe at the Sun's need to share what it knows.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Wit can become a way of not being reached. The Gemini Sun deflects with a joke or a change of subject; the Scorpio Moon keeps the real feeling locked away, and between them nobody gets close enough to hurt you, or to help. Turning a hurt over and over can also make it bigger: the mind keeps examining it, the Moon keeps holding it, and an old slight grows in the retelling. What helps is choosing one or two people with whom you stop performing, and telling them, in plain words, what's actually going on.
