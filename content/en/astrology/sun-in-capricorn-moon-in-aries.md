A Capricorn Sun starts things by strategy; an Aries Moon starts them by impulse. Both are cardinal signs, both begin, and your birth fell around the first quarter, when the month's first half moon hangs in the evening sky. I read the pair as a long plan with a short fuse, learning to use both.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Aries is three signs on from Capricorn: a square by sign between two cardinal signs, earth and fire. The [Sun in Capricorn](/astrology/sun-in-capricorn/) organises a life in stages, each one load-bearing; the [Moon in Aries](/astrology/moon-in-aries/) steadies itself by moving at once: the reply sent tonight, the run before breakfast, the problem tackled before it has finished arriving. The Sun is always thinking about next year and the Moon about the next ten minutes, and both are sure they're the one getting things done.

The rulers have opposite fortunes in each other's signs. [Mars](/astrology/mars/), your Moon's ruler, has its [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/) in Capricorn, so the Moon's push is put to good use on the Sun's ground; [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), your Sun's ruler, has its [fall](/astrology/fall/) in Aries, so the Sun's caution gets short shrift from the Moon. Both lights stand in signs that give them no dignity. When the Sun's long plans give the Moon's energy a target, the pair is formidable; when the Moon is kept waiting too long, the energy turns into frustration.

You were born around the first quarter. The Moon was 61° to 120° ahead of the Sun, five to ten days after the new moon, in the evening sky and close to half lit. Roughly half of these births belong to the [waxing crescent](/astrology/waxing-crescent/) and half to the [first quarter moon](/astrology/first-quarter-moon/) of the eight phases, and at 90° the square by sign becomes exact, a waxing [Sun square Moon](/astrology/sun-square-moon/). A first quarter birth, to me, is friction that wants acting on rather than mulling over, which an Aries Moon will gladly do. [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) explains the turn.

About 1 person in 145 has this pair. If the Moon crossed from Pisces into Aries, or from Aries into Taurus, on your birthday, the hour settles which of three pages is yours: this one, the [Sun in Capricorn, Moon in Pisces](/astrology/sun-in-capricorn-moon-in-pisces/) and the [Sun in Capricorn, Moon in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-capricorn-moon-in-taurus/) ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)), and close to the quarter it picks your phase too. The rising sign and the houses of Saturn and Mars come from the same hour.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love you're loyal, direct and protective. The Aries Moon says what it feels as soon as it feels it; the Capricorn Sun shows love through commitments kept and problems handled. Rows tend to be short and honest, followed by a practical repair. The friction appears when the Moon's quick heat meets the Sun's reserve: you may flare, then feel you've let yourself down by flaring. A partner who mistakes neither the heat nor the reserve for coldness gives this pair room to be both.

## At work {#at-work}

You're well made for leadership that has to act and plan at once: starting a business, running a department through a crisis, building something from nothing on a deadline. The Moon brings nerve and speed, the Sun strategy and stamina, and you can be the person who moves first and still has a plan for year five. Bureaucracy that slows every decision frustrates the Moon; chaos with no structure offends the Sun. Short sprints inside a long plan suit both.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Two cardinal signs both want to lead, and they lead at different speeds. The Moon wants the answer now; the Sun wants it to be the right answer in ten years. Pressure can build into impatience with yourself: frustrated by the delay, then by your own frustration. What helps is giving the Moon somewhere to spend its heat that doesn't put the plan at risk, a sport, a quick project, a hard walk, so the Sun's long work isn't hijacked by a bad Tuesday.
