Their Moon in your 7th house brings another person's emotional needs to the place in your chart where you meet someone as an equal. Whatever the bond, it tends to take on the feel of partnership: two sets of needs facing each other across the table, and learning to take turns.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[The 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) is about the people you face one to one: lovers and business partners, close collaborators, and the rivals who oppose you openly. The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) is how a person needs, attaches and seeks comfort. In this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their attachment lands in your house of partnership.

For you, they can feel like someone whose needs you naturally take into account, the way you would with a partner. Their moods register as part of "us", and their comfort matters in the plans you make. You may also recognise in what they need something you need yourself, and find it easier to see in them than in your own life.

For them, being one to one with you can feel safe. Their feelings find a counterpart, someone who answers rather than only listens, and they may relax into the relationship sooner than they usually would. The 7th is angular and visible; [Libra](/astrology/libra/), the sign of balance and fair exchange, is the one nature pairs with it. Placed right on your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), the point where your chart turns to face others, their Moon touches the very frame through which you meet other people, and I read the two together.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Partnership asks for balance, and needs don't always arrive in equal measure. If theirs fill the space, you can end up carrying their moods as if they were the weather of the relationship, adjusting your plans, your tone, even your own needs to keep things even. Or you may expect them to meet needs you haven't named, because they feel so much like a partner.

From their side, the risk is leaning. Having found someone who answers, they may bring you every feeling, and then worry that they take up too much room. If you meet them as a partner when they wanted a friend, or the other way round, the mismatch can hurt even though nobody meant it.

An overlay doesn't decide what you become to each other; it shows how naturally the two of you slip into the feel of partnership.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Put both sets of needs on the table, and take turns. "What do you need this week?" asked in both directions keeps the scales level. In a couple, that means checking that care flows both ways rather than settling into a carer and a cared-for; between business partners, paying attention to the emotional climate of the partnership as well as the contract; between friends, letting the friendship stay a friendship if that's what it is; between rivals, remembering that the person opposite has feelings too.

Reverse the charts and your Moon sits in their 7th house instead: your needs arrive in their idea of partnership, with the same care for balance. [Their Moon in your 6th house](/astrology/moon-in-6th-house-synastry/) shows care in daily tasks; [their Moon in your 8th house](/astrology/moon-in-8th-house-synastry/) takes it into trust and intimacy.

Of all their planets, the Moon is the one whose house in your chart their birth time can change most easily: it moves about 13° a day. Your Descendant, opposite your Ascendant, is set by your own time, and in Placidus, the house system I use, so is every cusp, and the reading waits until both times are in hand ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). If you've only just met, it may be too early for this reading anyway ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#we-just-met)).
