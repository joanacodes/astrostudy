Halfway between your Sun and your Moon there is a degree that belongs to neither and to both. Astrologers who work with midpoints often start with it, because it is where the chart's two lights meet, and whatever stands there is read as part of that meeting.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) organises identity and purpose, the sense of where you are heading; the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) regulates feeling and need, what you require to feel safe on the way. The Sun/Moon midpoint is the degree halfway between the two, written with a slash like every midpoint ([midpoints](/astrology/midpoints/)), and it stands for the two functions taken together: what you aim for and what you need, in one place.

Where it falls is set by the lunar phase you were born in, the distance between the two lights ([your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/)):

- **Born at a [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/):** the Sun and Moon are together, and the midpoint sits right on them.
- **Born at a [first quarter](/astrology/first-quarter-moon/) or a last quarter:** the lights are 90° apart, so the nearer midpoint lies 45° from each, a semi-square to both.
- **Born at a [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/):** the lights face each other, and the two midpoints fall 90° from each, square to the Sun and to the Moon.

Between those moments the midpoint slides from one arrangement to the next, which makes it a kind of summary of the phase.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

With the Moon covering about 13° a day and the Sun about 1°, the point midway between them keeps pace with neither: it travels about 7° a day, a quarter of a sign. Two people born on the same day can have their Sun/Moon midpoints in different signs, and the Moon's exact degree depends on the birth time ([the Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). So does the house the midpoint falls in.

Midpoint astrologers read the Sun/Moon midpoint and the degree opposite it as one axis, and look for anything in a hard aspect to it: a conjunction, a semi-square, a square, a sesquiquadrate or an opposition. A planet there is read as a third function joining purpose and feeling. Saturn brings structure and responsibility into that meeting; Venus, valuing and attraction; Mars, initiative; Mercury, the words for it. The Ascendant or the Midheaven there is read as the meeting showing in how you meet people or in your public direction.

It is easy to confuse with another point built from the same two lights. The [Part of Fortune](/astrology/part-of-fortune/) takes the distance from the Sun to the Moon and counts it on from the Ascendant, so it moves with the hour; the Sun/Moon midpoint sits between the lights themselves and doesn't depend on the Ascendant at all.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I don't use midpoints in my readings, and this one is no exception. What I read instead is everything the midpoint summarises: the aspect between your Sun and Moon, if there is one ([Sun square Moon](/astrology/sun-square-moon/), for example), the two signs they're in and how those signs get along, and the phase you were born in. Those carry the same story with fewer steps between the sky and the sentence.

If you work with midpoints yourself, read this one as a link between two functions you already have, keep the orb tight, and check the birth time: this midpoint shifts a degree every three and a half hours or so. The other pair astrologers single out is the [Venus/Mars midpoint](/astrology/venus-mars-midpoint/), where valuing meets pursuing.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Sun/Moon midpoint shows your soulmate.”** It describes how two parts of you work together. No point in a chart can show who you will love.
- **“It's the same as the Part of Fortune.”** Both use the Sun and Moon; only Fortune depends on the Ascendant.
- **“Everyone born on the same day shares it.”** It moves about 7° a day, so the birth time changes it, sometimes into another sign.
- **“An empty Sun/Moon midpoint means something is missing.”** Most midpoints have nothing on them. The Sun and Moon are still there, doing their work.
