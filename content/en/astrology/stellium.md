Some charts have a crowd in them: three, four or five planets packed into one sign while whole stretches of the wheel stand empty. Astrologers call the crowd a stellium. It's the easiest pattern to spot, and the one most often read as a label rather than a leaning.

## What it looks like {#what-it-looks-like}

A stellium is several planets close together, and authors draw the line in two ways. Some count by sign: three or more planets in the same sign, whatever their degrees. Others count by degree: planets linked in a chain of [conjunctions](/astrology/conjunction/), each one close enough to the next, so that the group works as one even when its two ends are too far apart to touch. The two methods usually agree, but not always. Planets at 2°, 15° and 28° of one sign are a stellium by sign and no chain at all; a planet at 29° of one sign with two more at 1° and 3° of the next make a tight chain spread across two signs. The edges of the signs decide the first kind, and [orbs](/astrology/orbs/) the second.

Some planets are made for crowds. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) never strays more than about 28° from the [Sun](/astrology/sun/), and [Venus](/astrology/venus/) never more than about 47°, so the three often share a sign, and one more planet beside them makes a stellium of four. The slow planets crowd in another way. Uranus spends about 7 years in a sign, Neptune about 14 and Pluto 12 to 31, so when two of them share a sign, everyone born in those years has the pair. That part of a stellium belongs to a generation more than to a person ([personal, social and generational planets](/astrology/personal-social-and-generational-planets/)), and I read it mostly through the house it falls in.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The count behind this site looked at one chart a day for everyone born from 1930 to 2009, the ten planets from the Sun to Pluto, by sign. About 7 people in 10 have at least three planets in one sign; about 1 in 5 have four or more, and about 1 in 27 five or more. So three in a sign is the ordinary state of a chart, not a distinction. The figures leave out the Ascendant and Midheaven, Chiron, Lilith and the nodes, and they use no orbs at all: count chains of conjunctions instead of signs, or add those other points, and the numbers move a great deal.

I read a stellium as emphasis. A sign the chart keeps returning to becomes a manner you reach for again and again, in whatever each planet does: Virgo's precision in how you think, how you love and how you work, say, if Mercury, Venus and Mars all sit there. It doesn't make you a type. Patterns come from repetition, and no single factor runs the chart: one crowd is a loud voice, not the whole choir. So I read each planet in the group for its own function first, and then for the colour the group lends it.

Two things decide where that emphasis lands, and both need the time of birth. The first is the house: the same four planets in the 10th house and in the 4th describe very different lives, and only the time says which. The second is the Moon, which changes sign about every two and a half days; on the day it moves, the hour decides whether it joins the crowd. That's why I [don't read a chart without the birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/): without it, a stellium has a sign but no house to live in.

The ruler of the crowded sign matters as well. Wherever that planet sits, it carries the whole group's business into another part of the chart. Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces have two rulers each, a modern one and a traditional one, and I read both.

## What helps {#what-helps}

- **Give the crowd one task at a time.** Several functions sharing a sign can feel like a meeting where everyone talks at once. Notice which planet is speaking, and let the others wait their turn.
- **Look at the empty signs and houses.** A stellium means other parts of the chart hold less. They're quieter, not missing ([empty houses](/astrology/empty-houses/)).
- **Find the doors.** A planet in the crowd that aspects something elsewhere is a way out: a trine to another sign, or a corner of a [grand trine](/astrology/grand-trine/), gives the group somewhere to go.
- **Keep the count in proportion.** Three planets together is common; five is rarer and louder. Neither is a verdict on who you are.

A crowded sign is a skill you get to practise from several sides at once, until its manner feels like your own choice rather than your only setting.
