Some placements take years to show; this one is visible before you've said a word. The Sun was just below the eastern horizon when you were born, about to rise, and I read it as an identity worn on the outside: the self you're becoming is also the self people meet first.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) holds the self you meet the world with: your body, your manner, the way you start things. It's angular, beginning at the Ascendant, and its natural sign is Aries, where the Sun is exalted. With the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) here, the function that organises your identity, vitality and purpose works in the most personal setting a chart has. Who you're trying to become isn't kept for later or for private life; it shows in how you walk into a room.

In Placidus, the house system I use, a 1st-house Sun was below the eastern horizon at your birth, in the last hours of the night before sunrise. Born a little later, after sunrise, you'd have the [Sun in the 12th house](/astrology/sun-in-12th-house/); a little earlier, in the small hours, the [Sun in the 2nd house](/astrology/sun-in-2nd-house/). So this page means nothing without your [birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/): near the Ascendant, a few minutes can move the Sun across the line. [Whole sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/) and other systems draw the 1st differently, and I describe them rather than dismiss them, but Placidus is the one I read.

Because the 1st house runs on from the Ascendant's degree, a Sun here usually sits in your rising sign or the sign after it, so your Sun sign and rising sign often speak with one voice.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, people meet your Sun before they meet anything else about you, and they may fall for the energy before they know the person. That can be wonderful and a little lonely: you may wonder whether you're loved for your presence or for yourself. Partners belong to the 7th house, directly opposite, so a 1st-house Sun often needs someone who can stand across from it without competing or shrinking. The lesson is to let a partner see you tired, unsure and off duty, the self behind the one that arrives.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, your person tends to be part of the job. Roles where you're the face of something, where you start things, or where your own way of doing them is the point suit this Sun: your own practice, presenting, teaching, coaching, any work that can't be handed to someone interchangeable. You tend to be noticed early, which helps. The harder part is being judged on how you come across rather than on what you deliver, so it's worth building places where your work can speak without you in the room.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

When identity and presence are this close, a bad day can feel like a verdict on who you are. You may feel responsible for the mood of every room you enter, or find it hard to be ordinary and unnoticed in a crowd. Vitality shows too: when you're tired, everyone can tell, and you may push on rather than let it be seen. Being seen this easily is tiring: this is a self with nowhere to hide, still learning that it doesn't have to be switched on all the time.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Keep some time when you're nobody in particular: a walk, a class where you sit at the back, a holiday where no one knows you. Let your body set the pace, since in the house of the body your energy is easy to read and easy to overspend. Read your Sun together with your rising sign and any planet touching your Ascendant, which colour what people meet. And when you start something new, notice how much of yourself goes into the first impression, and save some for what comes after.

In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the Sun sign and the rising sign were both among the placements that held about a third of the time, and a 1st-house Sun often joins the two. I checked the Sun's house like every other placement; the three that held far more often were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith. So I read this Sun warmly, and never as the whole of you.
