Moon conjunct Antares is the contact the Scorpion's heart receives most often: about 1 person in 171 born between 1930 and 2009 has the Moon within a degree of it, a little more than chance would give. Two old calendars of the Moon gave the star a mansion of its own.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) as the part of you that responds and regulates: what you need, how you settle, how you look after yourself and others. [Antares](/astrology/antares/) brings it the colour of its name, the rival of Ares, a star red enough to be mistaken for Mars. Laid over the Moon, the tradition reads that colour as heat in your responses, feelings that turn quickly into action. I'd soften it before passing it on: Mars-like doesn't mean angry, and the Moon's sign and house say far more about how your feelings move than any star. Astrology books also count Antares among the four royal stars; on the Moon, the most private placement of a chart, that crown sits oddly, and I leave it with the books.

The Moon's own calendars knew this star. In India, Antares and the two fainter stars beside it make Jyeshthā, "the eldest" or "the biggest", probably for the star's size, one of the lunar mansions the Moon passes through each month. In Sogdiana, in Central Asia, a lunar mansion carried the name of Sadwēsh, the Zoroastrian commander of the southern sky, whom W. B. Henning thought most likely to be Antares ([the 28 lunar mansions](/astrology/28-lunar-mansions/)).

In the sky, the Moon covers Antares only in some years, when its path dips far enough south: the star lies 4.6° below the ecliptic, and those years come in runs that follow the 18.6-year circuit of the Moon's nodes. When the Moon does cover it, a small telescope can sometimes catch what the red supergiant normally drowns out, a fainter blue-white companion, visible for a few seconds while Antares itself is behind the Moon. In the other years the Moon passes north of the star, sharing its degree but not its place.

On paper the window lasts about four hours a month. The Moon moves about half a degree an hour, so a time noted as "about ten o'clock" may not be enough to say which side of the window you fell; only the recorded hour can put your Moon inside it, and the same hour sets the house it falls in ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). For births from 1930 to 2009 that Moon sits at about 8° to 11° Sagittarius ([Moon in Sagittarius](/astrology/moon-in-sagittarius/)). Its sign, house and aspects are what I read; the star I only describe here, since my readings run on a system that has no stars in it.

Its neighbours on this star are the [Sun conjunct Antares](/astrology/sun-conjunct-antares/) of 1 and 2 December; [Venus conjunct Antares](/astrology/venus-conjunct-antares/) and [Mars conjunct Antares](/astrology/mars-conjunct-antares/), the two rarest of the five at about 1 in 212 and 1 in 210; and [Saturn conjunct Antares](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-antares/), which belongs to 1956–57 and 1986. The one-degree rule they all follow is set out under [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/), and its history under [fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/).
