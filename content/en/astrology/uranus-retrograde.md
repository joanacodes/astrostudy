Uranus sits at the edge of what the eye can see. On a dark night it is just bright enough, yet no ancient culture appears to have noticed it, and it was William Herschel who identified it as a planet in 1781. Like every planet beyond Earth, it spends part of each year backing up: in its case, about five months.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

With a lap of about 84 years, roughly seven years a sign, [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) is overtaken by Earth every 369 to 370 days, so each loop starts four or five days later in the calendar than the one before, and lasts 149 to 154 days. Halfway through, Uranus stands opposite the Sun, at its nearest and brightest for the year, which is still faint.

Each September a new loop begins, about four degrees further on than the last:

- **6 September 2025 to 4 February 2026**, from 1°27′ Gemini back to 27°27′ Taurus;
- **10 September 2026 to 8 February 2027**, from 5°41′ to 1°40′ Gemini;
- **15 September 2027 to 12 February 2028**, from 9°57′ to 5°55′ Gemini;
- **19 September 2028 to 16 February 2029**, from 14°14′ to 10°12′ Gemini.

Uranus first crossed into Gemini in 2025, slipped back into Taurus with that autumn's loop, and is back in Gemini from 2026 ([Uranus in Gemini, 2026–2033](/astrology/uranus-in-gemini-2026-2033/)). Its loops stay wholly in Gemini until 2031; the loop of 2032–33 turns back from 1°39′ Cancer into Gemini.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Each loop covers only about four degrees. The loops have also been getting a little shorter: 154 days in the first decade of the century, 151 in the mid-2020s, 149 from 2034.

Four degrees crossed three times, over the better part of two years, is enough to catch almost any point in a chart that lies inside them. With the orbs my forecast uses, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, more than 9 in 10 of Uranus's transits to a natal point make three exact passes, and a typical one keeps Uranus within reach for about twenty months. The [Uranus opposition](/astrology/uranus-opposition/), around the age of 42, usually arrives that way too: in three passes, not one.

Its neighbours bracket it. [Saturn retrograde](/astrology/saturn-retrograde/) is a little shorter, about four and a half months, over some seven degrees; [Neptune retrograde](/astrology/neptune-retrograde/), the next planet out, lasts a little longer and covers less than three.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Uranus, in the system I built for my readings, is the function that innovates, sets you apart and releases what no longer fits, never chaos or rebellion. Roughly two people in five have it retrograde at birth, and with them I look for change rehearsed before it's made: a need for freedom examined privately, differences from the people around you thought through before they are voiced. A change that waits until it is ready keeps a rhythm; it isn't late ([It isn’t delay](/blog/retrograde-planets/#it-isnt-delay)).

Uranus's sign belongs to a generation, and its retrograde to everyone born across five months of a year. The personal part is its house and its contacts with faster planets, and the house needs the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). My notebook has no separate figure for Uranus: it was checked with everything else, and the placements that stood out were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith.

As a [transit](/astrology/transits/), Uranus retrograde works its four degrees of one house for five months. I read it as a season for testing whether the change you want is the change you need, and for finding what still works before you replace it. That's weather, not a forecast: the loop doesn't bring upheaval, and it decides nothing about how a change turns out.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Uranus retrograde brings upheaval.”** Change gets thought through in those months rather than imposed, and upheavals belong to lives, not to loops.
- **“It's a rare event.”** It happens every year and covers about two days in five.
- **“Born with Uranus retrograde, you resist change.”** It describes change prepared inside, which often lasts better for it.
- **“You can feel the day it turns.”** Uranus barely moves for weeks around each [station](/astrology/stations/). There's no single day to feel.
