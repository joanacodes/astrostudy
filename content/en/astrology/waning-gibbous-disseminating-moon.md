After the full moon, the light starts to go. Night by night the Moon rises later and a little thinner on one side, and at dawn it is still up, pale over the west. That shrinking, still bright Moon is the waning gibbous.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

In the sky, the waning gibbous runs from the [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/) to the [last quarter moon](/astrology/last-quarter-moon/): the Moon 180° to 270° ahead of the Sun, which is the same as 180° to 90° behind it. More than half its face is lit, and less each night. Seen from the northern hemisphere the lit side is now the left; from the southern, the right. It rises later each evening, until by the end of the stretch it comes up around midnight, and it stays in the sky well after sunrise.

In Dane Rudhyar’s eight phases, from *The Lunation Cycle* (1967), the first 45° after the full moon still belong to his full moon phase, and the next 45°, from 225° to 270°, carry a name of their own: disseminating. To disseminate is to scatter widely, as seed is sown, and the word comes from the Latin for seed. For an astrologer who follows Rudhyar, the sky’s waning gibbous is therefore partly full moon and partly disseminating.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

After the full moon, the Moon closes in on the Sun again, from the other side. The disseminating phase opens at the waning sesquiquadrate, 135° behind the Sun, and takes in the waning trine, 120° behind. The system I built for my readings reads a trine as flow, two functions reinforcing each other, and that is a fair picture of the stretch: after the face-off of the full moon, purpose and need can work together again, and what was seen can be put to use.

It is as much a morning Moon as an evening one. The day after the full moon it rises soon after sunset; a week later it rises around midnight and lingers into daylight, low in the west, while people are on their way to work. Its sign moves the other way from the waxing gibbous’s: with the Sun in Aries, the disseminating Moon is usually in Sagittarius or Capricorn.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Born under a disseminating moon, your Moon was between about 135° and 90° behind your Sun, and if it was near 120° behind, your chart has a waning [Sun trine Moon](/astrology/sun-trine-moon/). I read this birth as understanding that wants to be passed on. What you have worked out tends not to stay private for long: it becomes advice, explanation, something taught or simply shared. That isn’t a job description. It describes a rhythm, and the signs and houses of your Sun and Moon say what you pass on, and how.

The boundaries of the phase are where the birth time matters: near 225° or 270° ahead of the Sun, a few hours decide ([the Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) explains how to find yours, and how the eight phases follow one another.

As for transits, the waning gibbous days get no separate reading from me. The full moon before them is the marker, and only when it falls on a point that is already active.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Disseminating is an astronomers’ word.”** Astronomy says waning gibbous. Disseminating is the name astrologers who count eight phases give to its second half.
- **“The waning Moon is a bad time.”** Waning is the half of the month after the full moon. Nothing in it is unlucky; the light simply grows less, and comes back.
- **“Born under a waning Moon, you’re past your best.”** No phase describes a decline in a person. A disseminating birth describes a way of sharing what you know, at any age.
- **“A disseminating birth makes you a teacher.”** No phase hands out a profession. It describes a habit of passing things on, which each life fills in its own way.
