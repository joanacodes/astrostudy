Urania was the muse of astronomy and of astrology, and the asteroid named after her goes round the Sun between Mars and Jupiter. An astrologer could take that as a compliment from the sky. I take it as a reason for care.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Asteroid 30 Urania is a stony body about 90 km across, in the inner part of the main belt, between 2.1 and 2.7 times the Earth's distance from the Sun. It goes round once every 3.6 years and turns on its axis in about 13.7 hours; measured from the Canary Islands in 2000, its outline came out as an oval about 111 km long and 89 km wide.

John Russell Hind found it on 22 July 1854. It was the last asteroid he discovered; two years earlier he had found [Fortuna](/astrology/fortuna/). Its first orbital elements were published by Wilhelm Günther, an assistant at the Breslau Observatory. It took the number 30, four places after [Proserpina (26)](/astrology/proserpina-26/). Astrologers who use it draw it as a pair of compasses.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Urania was one of the nine Muses, the daughters of Zeus and Mnemosyne, Memory. Ancient art gives her a globe and a pointer, and her province was the sky: astronomy and astrology both. Her name means heavenly, and the historian Diodorus explained it this way: she raises those she has taught up to the heavens, because imagination and the power of thought lift people's souls to heavenly heights. In myth she was also a great-granddaughter of the sky god Uranus, whose name the seventh planet carries ([Uranus](/astrology/uranus/)).

Poets kept calling on her for centuries. When Milton reached the creation of the world in *Paradise Lost*, he invoked Urania, adding that it was "the meaning, not the name" he called on. Observatories took her name too, in Berlin, Vienna, Zürich and elsewhere, and the Royal Astronomical Society of Canada carries the motto *Quo ducit Urania*, where Urania leads.

Anyone who places the asteroid in a chart places it as they would any other: a sign shared with many people born around the same time, since it takes 3.6 years to go round; a house that only a birth time can give ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)); and contacts that count only when they're tight ([Orbs](/astrology/orbs/)). Its return to the place it held at your birth comes roughly every three and a half years. None of that comes with a meaning attached. The meaning would come from the muse, and for an astrologer that's the trap: a body named after the muse of one's own craft invites you to read talent into it, and nothing but the name supports that.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Urania isn't in my readings, because a coincidence of names is all it offers, and coincidences are what I've learned to check before trusting ([Personal-name asteroids](/astrology/personal-name-asteroids/)). A name chosen in 1854 tells you about the people naming asteroids that year, not yet about the person whose chart the asteroid lands in.

My notebook is where I did that checking. I went through charts line by line, the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign, each planet, Chiron and Lilith, and gave each placement a tick or a cross ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). Urania had no line, so I have nothing to report on it, for or against.

If the muse stands for anything in my work, it's that habit of checking: a placement earns its place in a reading by holding up when it's tested, not by having a beautiful name ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). If you use Urania yourself, choose it before you look at where it falls, and keep it to tight contacts ([How to use asteroids without drowning the chart](/astrology/how-to-use-asteroids-without-drowning-the-chart/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Urania is Aphrodite.”** The muse is sometimes confused with Aphrodite Urania, heavenly Aphrodite, because of the shared epithet. They are different figures.
- **“The asteroid is named after Uranus.”** It's named after the muse. Uranus, the sky, was her great-grandfather in myth, and the planet is a separate body with its own name.
- **“Astronomy and astrology had separate muses.”** Urania served both.
