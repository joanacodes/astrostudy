Imagine you had been born at the same moment, but on the equator. The degree rising in the east there is your East Point, whatever latitude you were actually born at.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The East Point is also called the equatorial ascendant, and the second name says what it is: the [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) you would have if your birthplace sat on the Earth's equator, at the same moment and the same longitude. On the sky, it is where the ecliptic meets the great circle that runs through the ecliptic's poles and the east point of the horizon.

It is a calculated point, like the [Co-Ascendant](/astrology/co-ascendant/) and the [Anti-Vertex](/astrology/anti-vertex/), and like them it is easy to mix up with its neighbours. Each is built from a different circle of the sky. The Anti-Vertex comes from the prime vertical, the circle through the east point, the west point and the point overhead; the East Point comes from the circle through the ecliptic's poles. Different circles give different points.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The East Point is fixed by local sidereal time, the time of day measured against the stars, which comes from the birth time and the longitude of the birthplace. Latitude plays no part. Move a birth north or south along the same line of longitude, at the same moment, and the Ascendant changes while the East Point stays exactly where it was.

Two things follow:

- **At the equator, it is the Ascendant.** The further a birthplace lies from the equator, the more the Ascendant depends on latitude, and the more the two can part.
- **It keeps step with the Midheaven.** Because both come from sidereal time alone, the East Point sits roughly 90° on from the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) in every chart, whatever the latitude.

Picture two births at the same minute on the same line of longitude, one far to the north and one near the tropics. Their Midheavens match, and so do their East Points; their Ascendants may not even share a sign, because the Ascendant shifts with latitude. The East Point is what the two charts still have in common in the east.

It turns with the sky as fast as the angles, so the minute of birth matters. [Without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/), there's no East Point, any more than there is an Ascendant, which is why I don't read a chart without the time.

House systems agree on its degree and differ only on its house; in Placidus, the one I use, the cusps decide which, as they would for any point.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Because it is built like an Ascendant, astrologers who use the East Point read it in the Ascendant's terms: the way a person meets what is in front of them, with the sign and house of the point, and the planets close to it, adding detail. Read that way, it sits beside the Ascendant rather than replacing it, and for a birth far from the equator, the gap between the two is itself something to look at.

Not every astrologer uses it; [How I read](/how-i-read/) sets out what goes into my own readings. If you meet it in a chart, it's worth knowing what it is: a second view of the eastern horizon, the one the chart would have at the equator, and nothing stranger than that.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The East Point is just another name for the Ascendant.”** Only for births on the equator. Elsewhere the Ascendant depends on latitude and the East Point doesn't.
- **“It's the same as the Anti-Vertex.”** Different circle, different point.
- **“Your birthplace doesn't matter for it.”** Latitude doesn't, but longitude does, through local sidereal time.
- **“It shows a hidden personality.”** It's a calculated degree. What it adds, for those who use it, sits beside the Ascendant, not underneath it.
