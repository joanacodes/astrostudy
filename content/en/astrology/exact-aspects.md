Most aspects in a chart are a few degrees off their angle. A few are right on it, or within a hair: those are the exact aspects, and they tend to be the loudest voices in the chart.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

An aspect is exact when the distance between two planets is precisely its angle: 90°00′ for a [square](/astrology/square/), 120°00′ for a [trine](/astrology/trine/), 0° for a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/). The orb, the gap between the actual distance and the angle, is then zero ([orbs](/astrology/orbs/)). In chart talk the word stretches, and an aspect within a degree is often called exact as well, while "exact to the minute" keeps the strict sense ([degrees, minutes and seconds](/astrology/degrees-minutes-and-seconds/)).

There's a quick way to see one. At the exact angle, both planets stand at the same degree and minute of their signs: Venus at 14°22′ Taurus and Mars at 14°22′ Leo are exactly square. The closer the two degree numbers, the closer the aspect, provided the signs themselves are in aspect.

That's also why an exact aspect can never be out of sign. Taurus and Leo are a square apart, so planets at the same degree in them are too. Only the orb lets an aspect reach across a sign boundary, as when 28° Gemini squares 1° Libra, two air signs that would normally make a trine ([out-of-sign aspects](/astrology/out-of-sign-aspects/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Every aspect passes through exact on its way from applying to separating ([applying and separating aspects](/astrology/applying-and-separating-aspects/)). How long it stays close depends on the faster body. The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) gains about half a degree an hour on the slower planets, so a Moon aspect stays within a degree of exact for three or four hours in all, and within a minute of arc for three or four minutes. Between the Sun and Mercury, a degree either side lasts a day or two; between two slow planets, it can last for months.

In births from 1930 to 2009, by The Astro Study's count, the average chart has about ten major aspects involving the Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus or Mars within the orbs used on this site, and one or two of them within a degree of exact. About four charts in five have at least one such aspect within a degree, a little over one in five within ten minutes of arc, and about one in forty within a single minute.

One exact aspect has a name of its own. A planet conjunct the Sun within 17 minutes of arc is [cazimi](/astrology/cazimi/), "in the heart of the Sun", the limit William Lilly gave in 1647.

To find a chart's exact aspects, the [aspect grid](/astrology/aspect-grid/) is the fastest route. Most programs print each aspect's orb in degrees and minutes, and the smallest numbers are the closest aspects.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read a chart's tightest aspects first, because they tend to colour everything around them. Exactness raises the volume and never changes the meaning: an exact trine is still ease, an exact square still friction, and the planets, signs and houses still decide what each one is about. A wide aspect involving the same two planets would say the same thing, more quietly.

Exactness is also where the birth time shows. With the Moon's aspects lasting only hours, and its exact ones only minutes, I call a Moon aspect exact only when the time is reliable to the minute, as on a full birth record. A time remembered as "just after lunch" is a start for the chart, not for this ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). The same caution applies to aspects to the Ascendant and Midheaven, which move about a degree every four minutes.

In transits, the exact day is the centre of a transit, not its event. A slow planet's transit builds before it and fades after it, and what it describes is weather, not a forecast ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Exact aspects are rare.”** Close ones aren't: about four charts in five have an aspect with the Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus or Mars within a degree. Exact to the minute is rarer, about one chart in forty.
- **“An exact square is worse than a wide one.”** It's louder, not worse. Exactness turns up the volume; it doesn't change what the aspect says.
- **“An exact aspect can be out of sign.”** At the exact angle, both planets sit at the same degree of signs that are themselves in aspect.
- **“A transit does its work on the exact day.”** The exact day is the middle of the transit, not a deadline.
