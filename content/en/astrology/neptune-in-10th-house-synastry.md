A job that starts to feel like a vocation, a reputation that takes on a slight glow: with their Neptune in your 10th house, another person's ideals climb to the top of your chart, where your public life and your direction are on view.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) dreams of work that means something and of a role that serves more than oneself. [The 10th house](/astrology/10th-house/) sits at the summit of your chart: your career and standing, the authority you earn, and what you would like to be known for. In this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their dream meets your direction in the world.

With them, ambition can turn into vocation. You may find the nerve to work in an art, a caring profession or a cause, or simply come to see your present job as service rather than a ladder. They tend to talk about your work as if it mattered beyond the payslip, and sometimes that is what lets you believe it.

Your public role can look to them like an ideal made real. They may admire your standing, picture you doing even greater things, or speak of you to others with a warmth that adds a little glow to your name. The 10th is angular and the most visible house of all, and [Capricorn](/astrology/capricorn/), the patient climber, is its natural sign. Should their Neptune sit right on your [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/), it conjoins the summit itself, and the overlay is then felt most in how the world sees you.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Fog at the top of a chart can blur direction. Career plans may drift towards a dream without a route, or you take on unpaid or loosely defined roles because the cause seems worth it. Your reputation can become a story others tell, a little larger and softer than you are.

From their side, the person behind the role may disappoint the image: the boss who doubts, the artist who has bills to pay. If one of you works for the other, expectations can stay unspoken until they bruise.

None of this predicts success, failure or anyone's career; no chart can. It describes how ideals and public life meet between two people.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Give the dream a route. Write down what a calling would involve, week by week, and let them see the work behind the image. In a couple, support each other's vocations with practical help as well as faith; between friends, admiration is best when it doesn't need you to stay impressive; between a manager and a team member, roles and pay deserve to be clear precisely because the mission feels inspiring; between adult relatives, a family story about who you were meant to become can be gently retired.

The other half of the picture is your Neptune in their 10th house, your ideals reaching their public life. [Their Neptune in your 9th house](/astrology/neptune-in-9th-house-synastry/) widens your beliefs; [their Neptune in your 11th house](/astrology/neptune-in-11th-house-synastry/) moves among your friends and causes.

What a good career looks like changes with the times, and two people a generation apart often carry different dreams of work. Close in age, this overlay may mirror your own Neptune, and the vocation it hints at belongs to your shared era; with a wider gap, their generation's ideals of work meet your direction, and comparing them honestly can help you choose. Neptune moves so slowly that its place comes from their birth date alone; their birth time matters for everything on their side of the reading, so I wait for it. Your Midheaven runs through all twelve signs between one sunrise and the next, which is why your birth time is needed to place it, and in Placidus, the house system I use, the 10th begins there ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Between colleagues above all, a chart is read only with its owner's yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#what-i-ask)).
