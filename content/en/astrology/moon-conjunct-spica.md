Moon conjunct Spica can be more than a shared degree. Spica lies so close to the Moon's path that in some years the Moon passes right in front of it, month after month; in every year, the Moon crosses its degree, late in Libra, in three or four hours, and the birth time decides who has it.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) as the part of a chart that tends to you: what you need in order to settle, what you return to, whom you attach to and how. [Spica](/astrology/spica/) belongs to the Moon's own calendars more than to any other body's. Chinese astronomers made it the first star of Horn, the first of the twenty-eight lunar mansions that divide the Moon's month into stations of about a day each; in India it marks the lunar station Chitrā ([the 28 lunar mansions](/astrology/28-lunar-mansions/)). In that Chinese count, the Moon on Spica stands where the month's long list of stations begins.

Spica lies 2.1° south of the ecliptic, well inside the band the Moon wanders through, about five degrees either side. When the Moon's path is tilted the right way, it crosses the star itself, and those occultations come in runs: month after month from the spring of 1987 to the summer of 1988, from early 1994 into the spring of 1995, and most recently from July 2024 to July 2025, each one visible from some part of the Earth. The runs follow the Moon's nodes, the two points where its path crosses the Sun's, which take about 18.6 years to go round ([nodal axis](/astrology/nodal-axis/)): two runs to each circuit, alternately about seven and twelve years apart. Between the runs, the Moon slides by up to seven degrees north or south, and the meeting is one of degree and near neighbourhood.

For births from 1930 to 2009, the window ran from about 22° to 25° Libra, so this is always a [Moon in Libra](/astrology/moon-in-libra/): a Moon that finds its footing through harmony with others and a sense that things are even. Spica's grain names and the medieval list that gave it to Venus and Mercury sit easily on top of that, as a tradition's colour.

Ask your birth certificate before you ask the star. The contact lasts three or four hours a month, and only a recorded time can tell whether you were born inside them. The same time sets the Moon's house, and with it the part of life where those needs are met ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

About 1 person in 187 born between 1930 and 2009 has the Moon on Spica's degree, near the average for a one-degree window. Of the star's five contacts, the Moon's is the only one that the birth time has to confirm: the [Sun conjunct Spica](/astrology/sun-conjunct-spica/), [Venus conjunct Spica](/astrology/venus-conjunct-spica/), [Mars conjunct Spica](/astrology/mars-conjunct-spica/) and [Saturn conjunct Spica](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-spica/) can usually be settled from the date alone, except at the very edges of their windows.

No star appears in the charts I read, Spica included. What I'd read for a Moon here is the Moon in Libra, its house and its aspects; how a star contact is counted, and why only the conjunction matters, is in [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/).
