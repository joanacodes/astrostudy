Markab is the corner of the Great Square of Pegasus that comes first in the zodiac, at 23°50′ Pisces. Its name may come from a scribe's slip over a neighbour's, and three old lunar calendars paired it with that neighbour, Scheat.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Markab is Alpha Pegasi, though Bayer's first letter flatters it: at magnitude 2.49 it is only the third brightest star of Pegasus, after Enif and Scheat. With [Scheat](/astrology/scheat/), [Algenib](/astrology/algenib/) and [Alpheratz](/astrology/alpheratz/) it marks the Great Square, traditionally the body of the winged horse, high in the sky on autumn evenings in the northern hemisphere.

In the tropical zodiac it stood at 22°30′ [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/) for a birth in 1930, at 23°28′ in 2000 and at 23°50′ in 2026, creeping forward about a degree every 72 years. The Square's four corners spread over some 21° of the zodiac, and the Sun reaches them in this order between mid-March and early April: Markab, Scheat at 29°44′ Pisces, Algenib at 9°31′ Aries, Alpheratz at 14°40′ Aries. Earlier in Pisces, at 5°41′, lies [Deneb Adige](/astrology/deneb-adige/).

Markab is 19.4° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/). Even the Moon, which can stray about 5° from that line, passes at least 14° south of it, so a planet on Markab's degree shares a number with the star, not a place in the sky ([celestial latitude](/astrology/celestial-latitude/)).

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

The name is usually read as Arabic for the saddle of the horse. It may instead be a copying slip for mankib, the shoulder: Mankib al-Faras, the shoulder of the horse, was a name for Scheat. Either way the two stars belong together. They form the western side of the Square, and three calendars that divide the Moon's monthly path into stations made that side a station of its own ([the 28 lunar mansions](/astrology/28-lunar-mansions/)):

- **In the Arabic manāzil**, the pair is al-Fargh al-Awal, the First Spout; the eastern side, Alpheratz with Algenib, is the Second Spout.
- **In India**, the two stars lead Purva Bhadrapada, the first of the blessed feet, the 25th nakshatra, whose lord is Jupiter; the eastern side makes the 26th, Uttara Bhadrapada ([Vedic astrology](/astrology/vedic-astrology-jyotish/)).
- **In China**, they are the Encampment, one of the 28 lodges, with Markab as its first star; the eastern side is the Wall.

Three calendars drew a line through the same two stars. I find that more telling than any single meaning laid on Markab, and it is the part of its tradition I could check.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

For a birth in 2026, a planet or angle between about 22°50′ and 24°50′ Pisces would count as conjunct Markab; for a birth in 1930 the window sat a degree earlier, from 21°30′ to 23°30′. No other aspect counts ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). The Sun is there on about 13 and 14 March each year. The Moon's visit lasts a few hours once a month, so it hangs on the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Saturn, which comes round in about 29½ years, stood on it for births in parts of 1936–37, 1966 and 1995–96.

Markab and Scheat are about 6° apart in longitude, so a planet in late Pisces may meet one and miss the other; I'd check which, and how closely, before anything else.

The system I built for my readings leaves stars out, so on this site Markab gets a description and no interpretation ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). In a chart, the planet on its degree is still the thing to understand first, by sign, house and aspects; the star is old lore laid over it.
