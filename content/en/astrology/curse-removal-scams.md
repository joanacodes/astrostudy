It often starts with something true: a run of bad luck, a break-up that makes no sense, illness in the family, the feeling that something is working against you. Then someone offers an explanation, a curse, the evil eye, a rival's jealousy, along with a price for taking it away.

## The short answer {#the-short-answer}

Nobody can sell you the removal of a curse. When the person who discovers the curse is also the one selling the cure, that is the scam, every time, however kind or gifted they seem. ‘Curses to lift’ are among [the subjects I refuse](/how-i-read/), together with any paid ‘work’ to free you from one, because there is no honest version of that offer to make.

A birth chart doesn't show curses either: no planet, house or aspect means one. Someone who knows a little astrology may point at your [12th house](/astrology/12th-house/), which tradition named the Bad Spirit and gave hidden enemies, or at a hard [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/), and call it proof. I read the 12th as the part of life that happens out of sight: rest, retreat, what you carry without saying. And I read a Saturn return as a season of review and responsibility, never a punishment.

## Why it matters {#why-it-matters}

These scams tend to move through the same steps. Seeing them laid out makes them easier to recognise, for yourself or for someone you love.

1. **The opening.** A free or cheap reading, a message from someone who says they ‘saw something’ about you, a card through the door. It tends to find people at a hard moment, which is exactly when an explanation feels like relief.
2. **The diagnosis.** A curse, a blockage, someone ‘working against you’. It explains everything that has gone wrong, and nothing you say can disprove it.
3. **The fee, then the next one.** Candles, materials, a ritual; then a stronger ritual, because the curse ‘resisted’. Each payment makes the next one easier to ask for: stopping would mean admitting that the money so far is gone.
4. **Fear and secrecy.** Warnings that the curse will come back, or pass to your children, if you stop. Requests to tell no one, because ‘your family is part of it’ or ‘talking breaks the work’. Secrecy protects the seller, never you.

None of this measures anyone's intelligence. It works on grief, fear and exhaustion, and those come to everyone sooner or later. If it happened to you, or to someone close to you, the shame belongs to the person who took the money.

## What to do instead {#what-to-do-instead}

If someone is asking you to pay, or you already have:

- **Stop paying.** Whatever you're told will happen, the threats are part of the method, and the one sure effect of stopping is that the payments stop.
- **Keep everything.** Messages, voice notes, receipts, bank statements, and the names, numbers and accounts they used. Shame makes people delete things; keep them anyway.
- **Tell someone you trust.** The secrecy you were asked for is what keeps this going, and saying it aloud to one person often loosens its hold.
- **Call your bank** if money has left your account, and ask what can be done.
- **Report it** to the police: [How to report a scam](/astrology/how-to-report-a-scam/) goes through where and how.

If someone you love is caught in one, go gently. Mockery tends to push people back towards whoever is offering them an explanation. Questions work better than verdicts: what exactly were you told, how much have you paid so far, what did they say would happen if you stopped?
