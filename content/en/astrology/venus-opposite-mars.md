Venus opposite Mars puts liking and pursuing at opposite ends of the chart. It's also the rarest way these two planets meet, and the reason is written in the sky.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) values: it spots what draws you, what's worth keeping and what makes an exchange fair. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) acts: it asserts, starts, makes the effort and holds its ground. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) works by polarity: liking and pursuing become the poles of one axis, each showing up the other, and they ask for balance, not a choice between them.

In plain words, liking and pursuing sit across a table from each other. You may lean towards one end for a while, all grace and accommodation, then swing to the other, all push, and find each swing teaches you something about the end you left. The other end often turns up in someone else first: you're drawn to people who go after things the way you don't, or who value what you're busy chasing. I read that as a tendency to notice, never a script for whom you'll meet ([how I read](/how-i-read/)). Over time, the seesaw tends to settle into something rarer than either end: the ability to want and to appreciate in the same breath.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The two signs are opposite, so they share a modality and join complementary elements. Two axes of the zodiac belong to this pair, since one of the two planets rules each end: Aries and Libra, Taurus and Scorpio, counting Mars's traditional rule of Scorpio. Across Aries and Libra, or Taurus and Scorpio, the opposition puts both planets in their own signs, or both in the signs opposite their own, so each end speaks in its purest or its least familiar accent. In the houses, Mars in the 1st with Venus in the 7th might have you act on your own account and look for grace in partners; Venus in the 4th with Mars in the 10th, cherish home and push at work.

About 1 person in 87 is born with Venus and Mars within 6° of an opposition, against 1 in 11 to 1 in 19 for the pair's other aspects. The reason is the Sun. Venus keeps within about 47° of it, so for Mars to face Venus, Mars has to be far round the sky from the Sun, which happens only in the months around Mars's own opposition to the Sun, about every two years. At a degree or two, the seesaw is a central theme of the chart; near 6°, it's a quieter pull.

The rest of the pair's aspects, from this end:

- **[Venus conjunct Mars](/astrology/venus-conjunct-mars/):** the geometry turned inside out; the two side by side, so close that liking and acting move as one.
- **[Venus sextile Mars](/astrology/venus-sextile-mars/):** the commonest of the five; a friendly angle where a welcome first step comes easily.
- **[Venus square Mars](/astrology/venus-square-mars/):** a right angle rather than a straight line; the two rub in timing instead of mirroring each other.
- **[Venus trine Mars](/astrology/venus-trine-mars/):** 120° apart, in one element; support that needs no go-between.

Between two charts, [Venus opposite Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-opposite-mars-synastry/) is one person's taste facing the other's drive across the table: a strong pull that tends to need a careful give and take, and that each person may describe quite differently.

## In transits {#in-transits}

In [transits](/astrology/transits/), a planet that reaches one end of your opposition faces the other at the same moment. When Venus in the sky reaches your natal Mars, about once a year, it also opposes your natal Venus; when Mars crosses your natal Venus, about every two years, it opposes your natal Mars. Each lasts a few days, longer when Mars is retrograde near the spot, and lights up the whole seesaw at once.

Weather, not a forecast: a few days when both ends ask for attention, with no word on how anything turns out.
