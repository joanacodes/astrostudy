Pluto is so far away that, from here, its yearly wobble is mostly our own motion reflected back. For close to half of each year it creeps backwards a couple of degrees, and with every decade the creeping lasts a little longer.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A single lap of the zodiac takes [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) about 248 years, and its stay in a sign runs anywhere from 12 to 31 years. Earth passes it every 367 days or so, and each time Pluto appears to back up for 158 to 164 days ([Retrograde, explained](/astrology/retrograde-explained/)), its loop beginning a day or two later in the calendar than the year before. Halfway through it stands opposite the Sun, but only a sizeable telescope will show it: discovered in 1930 and classed a dwarf planet in 2006, it is far too faint for the eye.

From 2024 to 2027, every loop begins in early May:

- **2 May to 12 October 2024**, from 2°06′ Aquarius back to 29°38′ Capricorn, the last to reach Capricorn;
- **4 May to 14 October 2025**, from 3°49′ to 1°21′ Aquarius;
- **6 May to 16 October 2026**, from 5°30′ to 3°04′ Aquarius;
- **8 May to 18 October 2027**, from 7°10′ to 4°44′ Aquarius.

From 2025 every loop stays in Aquarius, to the end of the dates I hold, in 2040 ([Pluto in Aquarius, 2024–2044](/astrology/pluto-in-aquarius-2024-2044/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Pluto's loops are changing shape. In 2000 a loop lasted 158 days and covered about 2¾ degrees; by the 2020s it lasts 162 days, and from 2035 it will last 164 days while covering only about 2¼. Pluto came closest to the Sun in September 1989 and has been drawing away ever since. The farther it gets, the more slowly it moves along its orbit, so Earth's yearly swing takes up more of the year, and the arc it seems to trace, seen from this distance, grows smaller.

Each year's loop also moves on only about a degree and two-thirds, less than the ground it covers, so consecutive loops overlap. With the orbs my forecast uses, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, three in five of Pluto's transits to a natal point make three exact passes and two in five make five. A typical one lasts about two years and eight months, and one in ten goes on for about three years and nine months. The [Pluto square](/astrology/pluto-square/) unfolds at that pace, at an age that depends on your birth year.

Its neighbour, [Neptune retrograde](/astrology/neptune-retrograde/), lasts a few days less each year and traces a slightly wider loop, just under three degrees, that hardly changes from decade to decade.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Pluto is, in my readings, the function of transformation and regeneration: letting go of structures that no longer work, and the strength that comes back afterwards. I never read destruction, violence or death into it. About four people in nine are born with it retrograde, and in their charts I see that transformation working underground first: change that gathers out of sight, power reclaimed quietly, a sense of what has stopped working long before anything is let go. It's the inward rhythm of [Natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/) at its slowest.

Pluto's sign is shared by everyone born over a decade or more, and its retrograde by nearly half of them, so it is a generational placement twice over. I read it through the house it falls in, which needs the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and through its aspects to the personal planets.

As a [transit](/astrology/transits/), Pluto retrograde spends five months on two degrees of one house, often over a point it has already crossed. I read it as a slow review of what has become unsustainable there. It is weather, not a forecast: no Pluto transit predicts a loss, a death or a disaster ([Can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Pluto retrograde brings destruction.”** Pluto's function is transformation and renewal. Nothing in its loop destroys anything.
- **“It's when buried things come to the surface.”** The months are quiet ones, given to review; what comes to light, if anything, depends on people, not on the loop.
- **“Pluto isn't a planet any more, so its retrograde doesn't count.”** Astronomers reclassified it in 2006; its apparent motion works exactly as before, and astrologers read it for its function.
- **“Born with Pluto retrograde, you're secretive.”** It describes depth, which isn't the same as secrecy.
