Leo's heart wants to be seen. In a composite chart, Neptune there would turn the relationship's dream into a love story told in public, and only the very oldest couples alive could have it: at least one partner would need to be born before July 1929.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) takes two birth charts and gives the relationship a third, built from midpoints, which astrologers read as the bond's own character. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) in it is the bond's ideal: the picture of love the two people hold together, the compassion and imagination they share. [Leo](/astrology/leo/), fixed fire ruled by the [Sun](/astrology/sun/), would make that ideal want an audience. A pair with this Neptune would dream of romance as an art: a love that writes its own story and wants to share it, a home full of music or theatre, generosity offered with warmth, a relationship the two of them live as a work in progress, the way a painter lives with a canvas.

Leo sits at the edge of what living couples can have. Neptune was in Leo from September 1914 to July 1929, so the oldest generation alive was born with it. Because a composite Neptune lies halfway between two natal Neptunes, a composite in Leo needs at least one partner born before July 1929, ninety-seven or older today, and a second born not long after. Two people born in the mid-1920s would share it, and so would a pair born in June 1925 and June 1932, whose composite Neptune falls at about 28° Leo. No pair in a random sample of two million, all born between 1930 and 2009, had it; the next pairs to share it will come from Neptune's return to Leo, between September 2078 and July 2093.

The Sun, which rules Leo, would be the planet to read beside it: in a composite, the Sun shows what a relationship organises itself around, and here the bond's centre and its dream would both ask for light. In one birth chart, [Neptune in Leo](/astrology/neptune-in-leo/) describes that oldest generation's own dream of grand romance and art; a composite can hold it even when only one of the two partners was born with it. Before it, [composite Neptune in Cancer](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-cancer/) has in practice no living couple; after it, [composite Neptune in Virgo](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-virgo/) belongs to the oldest couples born after 1930.

On paper, the shadow of this ideal is a love story that needs its audience more than its two people: a bond that idealises its own romance until ordinary days feel like a letdown, or that measures itself by how it looks from outside. The gift on the other side is just as real: a relationship that gives its warmth away freely and makes something beautiful out of the time two people share.

Composites belong to the techniques I describe, not to the ones I practise, and for Leo the honest account is short: an ideal, the dates that allow it, and the rare pairs who could carry it. Any of them wanting it read would need both natal charts, the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and a yes from each partner ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
