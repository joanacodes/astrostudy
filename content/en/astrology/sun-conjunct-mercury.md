Mercury never wanders far from the Sun, and on the day you were born the two stood together. Sun conjunct Mercury puts your thinking at the centre of who you are: the way you take things in and put them into words is part of your identity, not a tool you pick up now and then.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is the organising centre of a chart: identity, purpose, the direction your vitality takes. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) processes: it takes in what reaches you, sorts it into sense and passes it on, in words, questions and explanations. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) binds the two into one active unit, and neither disappears into the other.

In plain words, you tend to know yourself by thinking out loud. You understand what you want by explaining it, and an idea that excites you can feel like a statement of who you are. Your purpose often takes the shape of a question you keep working on, a subject you return to, a way of describing the world that's recognisably yours. That doesn't make you talkative, or clever, or anxious: Mercury describes how a mind works, not how much it says or how it scores.

The cost, when there is one, is closeness. A challenge to your idea can land as a challenge to you, and an argument you lose can sting more than it should. The learning I see with this conjunction is gentle: noticing that an idea is something you have, and can change, without changing who you are.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Mercury, held within about 28° of the Sun, keeps coming back to it, so about 1 person in 4 has the two within 8°. That makes this a common theme rather than a rare signature, so I look at what changes it from one chart to the next. The sign is usually shared, which gives your thinking the same temperament as your sense of self; near the edge of a sign, Mercury can sit in the neighbouring one and add another tone. The house shows where mind and purpose work hardest: the 3rd, say, for learning and close conversation, or the 10th, for work that asks you to explain things in public.

Mercury meets the Sun twice in each of its cycles: once on the far side of the Sun, moving forward faster than it, and once between the Sun and the Earth, in the middle of a [Mercury retrograde](/astrology/mercury-retrograde/). So some people with this conjunction have Mercury retrograde in their chart, and I read that as a mind that works inward first, then speaks ([natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)). Mercury is the faster of the two except when it's retrograde, so it's usually Mercury that makes and breaks the meeting ([applying and separating aspects](/astrology/applying-and-separating-aspects/)).

Tradition has its own words for a planet this close to the Sun, [combustion](/astrology/combustion/) and [cazimi](/astrology/cazimi/), and each has its page. I don't read a combust Mercury as a weak mind: the closer the meeting, the more your thinking and your identity simply work as one.

The two can make no other major aspect: a sextile needs 60°, more than twice as far as Mercury ever strays. Between two people's charts any angle is possible, and [Sun conjunct Mercury in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mercury-synastry/) is one person's sense of self meeting the other's way of thinking: often an easy conversation, in which one of them seems to find words for who the other is.

## In transits {#in-transits}

Mercury always passes your natal Sun within about four weeks of your birthday, since it can't stray further than that from the Sun, and in a year when its retrograde loop covers that degree, it passes three times. Each pass lasts a day or two, longer when Mercury slows down to change direction. The Sun crosses your natal Mercury once a year, and with this conjunction that crossing falls near your birthday too ([transits](/astrology/transits/)).

So the weeks around your birthday are when the sky keeps touching this pair. I read them as a season for thinking things through and saying them clearly. Weather, not a forecast: these transits describe where attention goes, never what happens.
