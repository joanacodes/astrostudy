From the fair-minded years of Libra, the progressed Moon goes down into Scorpio, the sign tradition calls the Moon’s fall. For two years or so, feelings tend to run deeper and quieter, and what comforts you is trust: one or two people who can hold the truth, and the truth itself.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[The progressed Moon](/astrology/progressed-moon/) is the part of your [secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/) that sets a chapter’s emotional key. [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/) is fixed water with two rulers, Mars in the tradition and Pluto for modern astrologers, and I read both. In this sign the Moon’s needs turn towards depth: wanting what is real under what is said, wanting closeness that has been tested, and preferring a few bonds to many light ones. Feelings are held longer and felt more fully; trust, once given, is given whole. It may show as a conversation you finally have, a loyalty you choose to keep, or a habit of comfort you outgrow. Coming out of Libra, the first months can feel like a door closing on small talk; by the last, the same intensity is often ready to look outward again.

Tradition calls Scorpio the Moon’s fall, opposite its exaltation in Taurus, and says comfort is harder to come by there. I read that as feelings that go underground before they come up again, never as a dark chapter: what Scorpio does with a feeling is transform it, and a feeling worked through to the bottom tends to come back up changed.

If these years bring feelings heavier than you can carry alone, that is a matter for someone who can be with you in person, a friend, a doctor or a therapist, not for a chart ([astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/)). Which part of life the chapter’s depth gathers in depends on the house the progressed Moon crosses, and the houses need [your birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/); in Placidus, the system I use, I can’t draw them without it, and I don’t read a chart that way.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

A fixed sign doesn’t slow the progressed Moon down, whatever the chapter feels like from inside. For births from 1930 to 2009, its crossings of Scorpio lasted between two and two and a half years, usually about two years and four months, as many years as the real Moon took days to cross the sign after each birth.

It follows the [progressed Moon in Libra](/astrology/progressed-moon-in-libra/) and comes before the [progressed Moon in Sagittarius](/astrology/progressed-moon-in-sagittarius/), which is where the chapter comes up for air. Like every sign, Scorpio comes round again about every 27 years, and with a natal [Moon in Scorpio](/astrology/moon-in-scorpio/), the depth of this chapter is familiar ground rather than a surprise. When a slow transit touches the same part of your chart during these years, I give the theme more weight, though still as weather, never as an event on its way.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Choose your confidants. A Scorpio chapter is not the time to tell everyone everything, nor to tell no one anything: one or two people who can hear the whole of it are worth more than a crowd. Writing helps too, in a Scorpio chapter more than most: a private page nobody else reads is a safe place for feelings that aren’t ready to be spoken.

Look at both rulers: the houses of your natal Mars and Pluto show where the chapter’s intensity wants an outlet, and where it asks for change. Let what is ending, end; Scorpio is good at letting something finish so that something truer can grow. And keep an eye on the horizon, because Sagittarius, next, brings the open air back.
