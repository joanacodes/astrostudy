Lay the twelve signs round the wheel with Aries on the 1st house and the rest in order, and you have the natural zodiac: a model in which every house has a sign of its own. It's one of the most useful templates in astrology, as long as nobody mistakes it for a birth chart.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The natural zodiac pairs the signs and the houses by number: [Aries](/astrology/aries/) with the 1st house, Taurus with the 2nd, Gemini with the 3rd, and so on round to Pisces with the 12th. Each house gets a natural sign, and through it a natural ruler, the planet of that sign: Mars for the 1st, Venus for the 2nd and the 7th, Saturn for the 10th. The house pages of this site list each house's natural sign, because it's the quickest key to what a house is about: the 7th, [Libra](/astrology/libra/)'s house, is partnership and balance; the 10th, Capricorn's, is the public role and the long climb.

Some patterns line up neatly. The four angular houses, the 1st, 4th, 7th and 10th, get the four cardinal signs; the succedent houses get the fixed signs; the cadent houses the mutable ones ([angular, succedent and cadent houses](/astrology/angular-succedent-and-cadent-houses/)). The houses of each element fall a third of the circle apart, as the signs do: the 1st, 5th and 9th for fire, the 4th, 8th and 12th for water ([triplicities](/astrology/triplicities/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

As a doctrine, the natural zodiac is younger than most people assume. Alan Leo taught early in the 20th century that a planet, its sign and the house of the same number share one nature, an equation sometimes written as planet = sign = house. He called it natural rulership, as opposed to the actual ruler of a house in a given chart, and many astrologers since have read each house through its natural sign.

Older astrology built its houses differently. Each house had its own meanings, and the planets had houses they rejoiced in, the [planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/), which don't follow the order of the signs at all: Mercury rejoices in the 1st, which the natural zodiac gives to Mars; Venus in the 5th, the Sun's house there; Mars in the 6th, Mercury's; Saturn in the 12th, the house of Pisces. The two systems answer different questions, and both still have a place.

The natural zodiac also shares something with [derived houses](/astrology/derived-houses/): both lean on the fixed order of the houses. Derived houses count that order again from a new starting house; the natural zodiac lays the signs along it from Aries.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I use the natural zodiac as a key, not as a ruler. When I read your 7th house in [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/), the house system I use, I start from the sign on its cusp, which is always the sign opposite your rising sign, and from that sign's ruler. If Capricorn opens your 7th, Saturn governs it, and your partnerships take Capricorn's style, whatever Libra and Venus mean for the house in general. The natural sign tells me what kind of territory the 7th is; your own cusp tells me how you live there ([chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/) explains the same logic for the 1st).

And a wheel with 0° Aries on the Ascendant and every sign on its own house deserves a second look. Unless you were born at the moment 0° Aries rose, it's the natural zodiac, a template, not your chart. Your houses start from your rising sign, and the rising sign needs the time and place of birth ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Mars rules everyone's 1st house.”** It's the 1st house's natural ruler. In your chart, the 1st is ruled by the planet of your rising sign.
- **“The houses got their meanings from the signs.”** Houses had meanings of their own long before the 20th-century equation, and the planets' joys follow a different pattern.
- **“The natural zodiac is the same as Whole Sign houses.”** [Whole Sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/) start from your rising sign; the natural zodiac always starts from Aries.
