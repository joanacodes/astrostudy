Zubenelgenubi is the southern claw of the Scorpion, a name from before the stars of Libra became scales, and it sits almost exactly on the Sun's path. Of all the stars this site describes, it is the one the Sun, Moon and planets come closest to; in the zodiac it reads 15°26′ Scorpio.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Zubenelgenubi is Alpha Librae, though at magnitude 2.75 it is the constellation's second star, after [Zubeneschamali](/astrology/zubeneschamali/). Binoculars split it in two: a blue-white star of magnitude 2.7 and a fainter white one of 5.2, a true pair travelling together about 77 light-years away, and each of them double again.

It stood at 14°06′ [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/) for a birth in 1930, at 15°04′ in 2000 and at 15°26′ in 2026. The [precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/) has moved Libra's stars about a sign along the zodiac, so the Scorpion's old claw now sits in the Scorpion's sign.

Its latitude is only 0.3° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), less than that of any other star on this site, Regulus included, and a world away from a star like [Algenib](/astrology/algenib/), 12.6° north. So the bodies of the chart really can meet it ([celestial latitude](/astrology/celestial-latitude/)). The Moon passes in front of it in some years, and the planets very rarely: Venus covered it on 25 October 1947, and the next will be Mercury, on 10 November 2052. Each year the Sun's centre passes about 20′ south of it, its edge almost touching the star, which is why Zubenelgenubi vanishes into the Sun's light in early November and shines all night in early May.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

Al-zubānā al-janūbiyy, the southern claw, is a name older than the Scales. Greek skies saw these stars as the Claws of the Scorpion, and Aratus, whose poem spread the name through the Hellenistic world, was unimpressed: "few conspicuous stars the Claws can boast, and their dim light mid brighter gems is lost". Once the claws had become a balance, the star became Kiffa Australis, the southern pan.

The two claws together made an Arabic lunar mansion, az-Zubānā, the Two Claws of the Scorpion, and in India they mark Vishakha, the forked one, the 16th nakshatra, whose lord is Jupiter ([the 28 lunar mansions](/astrology/28-lunar-mansions/)). Chinese astronomy counts Zubenelgenubi as the first star of the Root, a lodge it shares with Zubeneschamali and two fainter stars of Libra.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Any planet or angle from 14°26′ to 16°26′ Scorpio, for a birth in 2026, meets the tradition's test of a conjunction; for 1930 the span ran from 13°06′ to 15°06′ ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). With this star the word can mean what it says in the sky as well as on paper. The Sun is there on about 7 and 8 November, a [Sun in Scorpio](/astrology/sun-in-scorpio/) at mid-sign, almost in front of the star. The slow planets mark years instead of days: Neptune held the degree in parts of 1962–64, and [Pluto in Scorpio](/astrology/pluto-in-scorpio/) at intervals from December 1988 to September 1990, a contact shared by everyone born inside those months. Whether the Moon was there, and whether it was hiding the star, depends on the hour of birth ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

I don't fold stars into my readings ([How I read](/how-i-read/)), but Zubenelgenubi is worth knowing all the same: it is the rare star where a chart's conjunction and the sky's agree.
