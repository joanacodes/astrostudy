Under or beside most chart wheels sits a staircase of small boxes, some empty, some holding a symbol and a number. That's the aspect grid. It looks like a timetable, and it works like one: it tells you, pair by pair, which planets in the chart are talking to each other, and how closely.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The grid lists the planets and points of a chart along two edges. Each one gets a row and a column, and the box where two of them cross holds the aspect they make: ☌ for a conjunction, ⚹ for a sextile, □ for a square, △ for a trine, ☍ for an opposition, and, if the program shows them, the minor aspects, such as ∠ for the semi-square or ⚻ for the quincunx. [Glyphs: the cheat sheet](/astrology/glyphs-the-cheat-sheet/) has the full set.

It's a triangle because each pair needs only one box. The Sun's row meets the Moon's column once, and there is no point in showing the Moon meeting the Sun again. Ten planets make 45 pairs; add the Ascendant, the Midheaven, the North Node, Chiron and Lilith, and fifteen points make 105. Most of those boxes stay empty, and that's normal.

Most programs print the orb inside the box as well: how far the aspect is from exact, in degrees and minutes. Some add a letter for whether the faster planet is closing in or pulling away ([applying and separating aspects](/astrology/applying-and-separating-aspects/)). An orb close to zero marks one of the chart's [exact aspects](/astrology/exact-aspects/), usually among the loudest.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

To read it, find one planet's row, follow it to the other planet's column, and look at the box. "□ 1°40′" in the box where Venus meets Saturn means Venus square Saturn, 1 degree and 40 minutes from exact.

What lands in the grid depends on the program's settings, so two grids for the same birth can disagree:

- **Orbs.** One program allows 8° for a square, another 6°. An aspect 7° wide appears in the first grid and not the second. [Orbs](/astrology/orbs/) explains why astrologers set them differently.
- **Which aspects.** Some grids show only the five major aspects; others add half a dozen minor ones, which can fill the triangle quickly.
- **Which points.** The angles, the nodes (mean or true), Chiron and Lilith (mean or true) are switched on or off by setting, and each changes the count.
- **The birth time.** The Moon moves about 13° a day, so its aspects can appear or vanish with the hour of birth, and the Ascendant and Midheaven rows depend on the time altogether. That's one reason I [don't read a chart without one](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon).

Between two charts, in [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), the grid becomes a full square: one person's planets along the top, the other's down the side. Nothing is repeated, because one person's Sun meeting the other's Moon is not the same contact as the reverse, so the ten planets make 100 boxes. [Synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) is about reading that square.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I use the grid as a checklist, never as the reading. It tells me what is there; the planets, signs and houses tell me what it means. I start with the tightest orbs, because those aspects colour the whole chart, then I look at the balance between [hard and soft aspects](/astrology/hard-and-soft-aspects/): a grid full of squares and oppositions describes a lot of friction to work with, one full of trines a lot of ease that may never be tested.

I also look for shapes. Three boxes can describe a triangle: two planets in opposition that both square a third make a [T-square](/astrology/t-square/), three planets in mutual trine a [grand trine](/astrology/grand-trine/). And I look at the rows of [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), Chiron and Lilith with particular care, because in my notebook those three placements held far more often than the rest ([the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)).

An empty row matters too. A planet with no aspect at all, within the orbs used, is an [unaspected planet](/astrology/unaspected-planets/), and I read it as a function that works on its own terms.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“An empty box means the two planets have nothing to do with each other.”** They still meet through their signs, their houses and their rulers. The grid only records aspects.
- **“The more aspects in the grid, the stronger the chart.”** A full grid often just means wide orbs or many minor aspects switched on.
- **“Every program draws the same grid.”** Orbs, aspects and points are settings, and they change the result.
- **“A minor aspect in the grid weighs as much as a major one.”** Most astrologers keep minor aspects to a degree or two and read them as nuance, not as the backbone of a chart.
