A contra-parallel is the parallel seen in a mirror: the same distance from the celestial equator, but on opposite sides of it. Where the parallel is read like a conjunction, the contra-parallel is read like an opposition.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Two planets are contra-parallel when their [declination](/astrology/declination/) numbers match but their directions don't: one 15° north of the [celestial equator](/astrology/celestial-equator/), the other 15° south. The equator sits between them like a mirror. Astrologers also call it the antiparallel, or a split parallel, and read it like an [opposition](/astrology/opposition/): two functions facing each other, each making the other clearer, asking to be balanced rather than chosen between.

With the [parallel](/astrology/parallel/), it makes up the pair of aspects measured north and south of the equator rather than along the zodiac. Neither is widely used.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

For bodies sitting exactly on the Sun's path, a contra-parallel turns up in two places. The first is the opposite degree. A planet at 10° Taurus stands about 14°49′ north of the equator, and the degree facing it, 10° Scorpio, about 14°49′ south: on the ecliptic, an exact opposition is always a contra-parallel too, which makes the comparison with the opposition a natural one. The second is the degree mirrored across the Aries–Libra axis. 10° Taurus and 20° Aquarius lie equally far from the March equinox point and share the same 14°49′, one north and one south. Tradition calls that pair contra-antiscia ([antiscia and contra-antiscia](/astrology/antiscia-and-contra-antiscia/)).

So a contra-parallel can link planets whose signs are not opposite at all: Taurus and Aquarius are square to each other. And because the planets wander a little above and below the ecliptic, an opposition in the zodiac isn't always a contra-parallel in the sky.

The Sun can't help a planet [out of bounds](/astrology/out-of-bounds-planets/). Its declination never passes about 23°26′, so a planet beyond that line finds no mirror in the Sun, only in another body as far out on the other side of the equator.

To find one, read the declination column: two equal values with opposite signs, one + and one −. The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) is the body most likely to need the birth time here. In the years of its widest swings it travels from about 28° on one side of the equator to 28° on the other in a fortnight, so a contra-parallel to it comes and goes within a day ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read a contra-parallel the way I read an opposition, as polarity: two functions that need each other to be seen clearly, pulling in different directions and asking for a balance between them. It describes a tension that can be worked with, not a conflict to be won, and never a verdict. The Moon contra-parallel Mars, for example, would set the need for emotional security against the drive to act and assert, both real, both asking for room.

It stays a second layer for me. The aspects on the wheel come first; a contra-parallel adds to them, or names a polarity the wheel misses. I hold it as lightly as its partner, the parallel, and for the same reason: none of my own testing measured declination ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A contra-parallel means conflict.”** Like the opposition it's compared to, it describes polarity: two needs to balance, not a fight with a winner.
- **“It needs planets in opposite signs.”** On the ecliptic, 10° Taurus and 20° Aquarius are contra-parallel, and those signs are square.
- **“An out-of-bounds planet can be contra-parallel the Sun.”** The Sun never goes that far north or south, so the declinations can't match.
- **“Contra-parallel and contra-antiscion are the same thing.”** They coincide only for bodies on the ecliptic; a planet off it can have one without the other.
