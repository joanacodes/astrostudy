Most Suns meet Neptune once or not at all: its round takes some 165 years. When it does arrive, for about two and a half years the sharp outline of who you are and what you're aiming for gives way to something wider, and harder to define.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) dissolves boundaries that are no longer needed and opens the way to imagination, compassion and a meaning larger than one life. Your [Sun](/astrology/sun/) organises identity, purpose and vitality around a centre. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) makes the two work as one, so this [transit](/astrology/transits/) works from the inside: the drive that organised your life may lose some of its grip, ambitions that defined you can feel less urgent, and something gentler, more creative or more spiritual asks for a place at the centre.

Neptune's blind spot here is confusing surrender with helplessness. Letting a goal soften is a choice you can make with open eyes, and the centre doesn't disappear: it widens to take in more than it used to. Vitality isn't constant energy either, and some people find they run on less for a while. Tiredness that lasts is a question for a doctor, never for a chart ([Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)).

The house of your Sun shows where the softening is felt. With your Sun in the 10th, the work you're known for may start to matter for different reasons ([Neptune transiting the 10th house](/astrology/neptune-transit-10th-house/)); in the 4th, it's home and belonging that change their meaning. Houses come from the birth time, and I don't read a chart without it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Within my orbs, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, Neptune stays on your Sun for about two and a half years, a little under two at the shortest. It always crosses the exact degree more than once: three times in about three passes out of four, five times in the rest, when one year's retrograde loop overlaps the next ([Neptune retrograde](/astrology/neptune-retrograde/)).

By 80, a little over half of people born between 1930 and 2009 have never had it, and no one has had it twice. On its way to your Sun, Neptune passes the trine first ([transiting Neptune trine natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-neptune-trine-natal-sun/)), then the square ([transiting Neptune square natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-neptune-square-natal-sun/)), then the sextile ([transiting Neptune sextile natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-neptune-sextile-natal-sun/)); past the Sun the same three come in reverse order, and the opposition ([transiting Neptune opposite natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-neptune-opposite-natal-sun/)) lies halfway round. In 80 years Neptune covers a little less than half the zodiac, so no life holds them all. Born with [Sun conjunct Neptune](/astrology/sun-conjunct-neptune/), you began life under this transit, and it doesn't come round again.

Neptune is in Aries until March 2039, so it's the turn of Aries Suns, birthdays from about 21 March to 19 April, the earliest first. A Sun in the first degree or so of Aries had Saturn there as well, in 2026 ([the Saturn–Neptune conjunction of 2026](/astrology/saturn-neptune-conjunction-of-2026/)).

## What helps {#what-helps}

Keep one aim concrete. A softer centre still needs something to point at: a project with a date, a practice you keep.

Check the picture other people hand you. Under Neptune it's easy to take on someone else's idea of who you are, admiring or not; ask what you'd choose for yourself.

Give the wider sense somewhere to go: music, art, service, a spiritual practice. Neptune on the Sun asks less for answers than for a form.
