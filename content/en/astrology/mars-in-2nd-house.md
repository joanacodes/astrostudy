Mars in the 2nd house works for what it can hold: a skill, a wage, a well-kept toolbox, a sense of its own worth that nobody can talk it out of. Effort, here, likes to end in something real.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is effort and drive: what you go after, and how hard you push. [The 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) is what you have and what you value: the money you earn, the things you own, the talents you could live from, and the sense of worth underneath them. Here Mars puts its effort into resources. You tend to want to earn your own way, to build skills you can use, and to defend what's yours, including your price. The 2nd is succedent: it follows an angle and consolidates what the angle began ([angular, succedent and cadent houses](/astrology/angular-succedent-and-cadent-houses/)), so this Mars works to make gains last rather than to start something new every week.

No chart can say what you'll earn, lose or spend, and I don't read one for that. What I read here is your relationship with effort and worth: what you'll work for, and how you stand up for your value.

The 2nd's natural sign is Taurus, the sign where Mars is in detriment, and the echo is worth noticing: in both, a planet that wants to act meets a part of life that wants to keep. Across the chart sits the 8th, what you share with others; with Mars in the 2nd, the weight falls firmly on what is your own.

I work in Placidus, where the cusps are calculated from the time and place of your birth, so a time an hour out can carry a planet near a cusp into the next house ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Next door, [Mars in the 1st house](/astrology/mars-in-1st-house/) leads with its presence and [Mars in the 3rd house](/astrology/mars-in-3rd-house/) with its words.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In close relationships, this Mars needs a measure of independence: your own money, your own things, your own say in how shared resources are used. You'd rather pay your share than be looked after, and you notice at once when a split feels unfair. Love often comes in practical form: you provide, fix, buy the thing that was needed before anyone asked. Arguments, when they come, may be about spending and value more than about feelings. Partners who respect what you've built, and who talk about money plainly and early, make the ground firm under you.

## At work {#at-work}

Work is where you turn effort into something you can count. You can work very hard for a tangible reward, negotiate your pay without apologising, and keep honing a skill until it's worth something. Trades, crafts, sales, a business of your own, anything where effort and result are visibly linked, tend to motivate this Mars. Unpaid or invisible effort drains it faster than long hours ever do.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Measuring your worth by what you earn or own. Spending fast for the satisfaction of having, then working harder to make up for it. Defending a possession, or a position about money, more fiercely than the matter deserves. And competing over who has more, which rarely leaves anyone feeling richer.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Decide what you value before you go after it, so the effort serves something you actually want. Put a pause between wanting and buying: a day, for anything that isn't urgent. Name your price, and practise saying it out loud. Build skills as deliberately as savings, since they're the resources nobody can take from you. And remember that your worth isn't a number; this Mars works best once it knows that in its bones.
