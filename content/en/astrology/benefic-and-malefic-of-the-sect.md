Tradition had two benefics and two malefics, and sect splits each pair. One benefic and one malefic belong to the day, the other two to the night, so in every chart one of each is on home ground.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

By day, [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) is the benefic of the sect and [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) the malefic of the sect, while [Venus](/astrology/venus/) and [Mars](/astrology/mars/) are contrary to it. By night it turns round: Venus and Mars belong, Jupiter and Saturn don't. The pairs come straight from the two teams [sect](/astrology/sect-day-and-night-charts/) draws up, the Sun with Jupiter and Saturn, the Moon with Venus and Mars, each led by its light ([The sect light](/astrology/sect-light/)). Where the labels benefic and malefic come from has its own page ([Benefics and malefics](/astrology/benefics-and-malefics/)).

Ptolemy gives the ground for the split. He explained the planets through heat, cold, moisture and dryness, the benefics moderate, the malefics excessive, Saturn in cold and Mars in dryness, and he matched the day planets to the nature of the Sun and the night planets to the nature of the Moon. So Saturn, the cold one, goes to the day, and Mars, the dry one, to the night. I read the pairing as balance: the coldest planet set beside the Sun's warmth, the driest kept for the night.

What follows from it is simple. A planet of the chart's own sect was read as more comfortable there, and one of the other sect as less so. In every chart, then, one malefic is more at ease and the other less, and the same goes for the benefics.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

That gives four roles in any chart:

- **The benefic of the sect:** Jupiter by day, Venus by night, the benefic on home ground.
- **The benefic contrary to the sect:** Venus by day, Jupiter by night, still a benefic, less at ease.
- **The malefic of the sect:** Saturn by day, Mars by night, the malefic on home ground.
- **The malefic contrary to the sect:** Mars by day, Saturn by night, the malefic least at ease in that chart.

The houses echo the split. Each of the four has a joy, a house where tradition said it was glad to be, and the day pair rejoice above the horizon, Jupiter in the 11th and Saturn in the 12th, while the night pair rejoice below it, Venus in the 5th and Mars in the 6th ([Planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/)).

Medieval astrologers went further, weighing whether a planet's sign and its side of the horizon also suited its sect ([Sect condition](/astrology/sect-condition/)). None of it replaces the rest of a planet's condition: its sign, its house and its aspects still count.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I keep the old labels as history and read the planets by what they do ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). Sect adds something to that: a sense of which functions in a chart run with its grain and which across it. Saturn in a day chart reads to me as structure that comes more naturally, built steadily; in a night chart, as structure that asks more patience to build. Mars in a night chart reads as drive that finds its outlet; in a day chart, as drive that needs a clearer aim. Growth and valuing work the same way, coming more readily from whichever of Jupiter and Venus belongs to the sect.

None of that is a forecast of hardship. Saturn, the planet tradition feared most, is one of the three placements that held 75 to 90% of the time in my notebook ([The three that held: Saturn, Chiron and Lilith](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/#saturn)). Being accurate about Saturn has never meant expecting the worst from it.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The malefic contrary to the sect means a hard life.”** It is the malefic least at ease in that chart: a description of where effort costs more, not a sentence.
- **“A day birth makes Saturn harmless.”** Saturn of the sect is Saturn at ease. It still asks for time and structure, as Saturn does.
- **“Venus is the lesser benefic, so night charts get less help.”** In a night chart Venus is the benefic on home ground, and Jupiter the one away.
- **“Benefic of the sect means good luck.”** It means the benefic most at ease in the chart; no planet hands out luck.
