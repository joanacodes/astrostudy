They're the pair who end up running the quiz, hosting the street party and painting a mural on the garden wall. Composite Mars in Leo gives the relationship's chart a warm, visible drive: the bond acts with heart, wants to be admired, and argues about respect.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), [Mars](/astrology/mars/) stands at the midpoint of your two natal Marses: the relationship's way of putting effort and courage behind what it wants. [Leo](/astrology/leo/) is fixed fire, ruled by the Sun, and Mars has no dignity there; the sign gives the drive warmth, staying power and a wish to be seen. This is the most common composite Mars of all, about 1 pair in 10, a shade ahead of Virgo. Mars lingers longest in Leo and Virgo, about 49 days a visit, and averaging two positions pulls the midpoints a little further towards where Mars spends most time, so composites land here slightly more often than birth charts do.

Such a pair acts generously and in full view. It throws the party, leads the fundraiser, builds the treehouse with a turret, and it works best when its effort has an audience, even an audience of two. Competition between you can be lively: who tells the better story, who wins the game, who is praised more at dinner. Aimed outwards, the same pride makes a formidable team. Desire is demonstrative and playful, and this bond likes being chosen out loud. [Mars in Leo](/astrology/mars-in-leo/) in one birth chart is one person's bold heart; in the composite, the boldness is something you hold together.

Arguments turn on respect. The question under most of them is *do you see me, and do you value what I did?* Pride can make them theatrical, and it can make the ending hard, because neither of you wants to be first to give way. The warmth usually wins, and a row here tends to finish in a large, sincere reconciliation. What helps is generosity in both directions: praising the effort before correcting it, and treating an apology as a gift rather than a defeat. Visibility isn't attention-seeking, but the relationship should check now and then that both of you are getting the light.

Drama is friction; intimidation is not. If a quarrel ever leaves one of you feeling unsafe, set the chart aside, because no placement explains or excuses that: in the UK, 999 is for an emergency, and Samaritans take calls on 116 123 at any hour.

[Composite Mars in Cancer](/astrology/composite-mars-in-cancer/) defends its people quietly, from home; [composite Mars in Virgo](/astrology/composite-mars-in-virgo/) puts its effort into getting the details right. Leo wants the effort to shine.

Composites aren't drawn in my readings; I set out the method so that you know what it claims. Astrologers who use it look at the composite [Sun](/astrology/sun/), Leo's ruler, for what all this pride serves, and at the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), where one person's Mars on the other's Sun ([Sun conjunct Mars in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mars-synastry/)) can feel like encouragement or like rivalry. No composite tells you whether to stay, marry or part. The sign needs both birth dates, and the times only within about half a degree of Cancer or Virgo. A composite is personal too: it's drawn only with both people's agreement ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
