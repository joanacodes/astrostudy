With the Moon in Pisces, the line between your feelings and other people's is drawn in pencil. You walk into a room and take on its weather; the work, and the gift, is finding your own again.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Pisces](/astrology/pisces/) is mutable water, the last sign of the zodiac, and with the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) here your emotional responsiveness has very few walls. You pick up what others are feeling, sometimes before they know it themselves, and you respond with a compassion that doesn't wait to be asked. Imagination is part of how you process: music, stories, images and dreams carry feelings you couldn't put into a sentence. The need underneath is for gentleness, and for regular time on your own, where what you absorbed can drain away and what's yours can come back.

Pisces gives the Moon no traditional dignity or debility, so what shapes it here is the element and the rulers rather than any old ranking.

Pisces answers to two rulers, and I read both: [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) for modern astrologers, [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) in the older books. Neptune shows where your edges soften most and where imagination and compassion run strongest; Jupiter shows where all that feeling finds a meaning and somewhere to grow. When the two point the same way, the softness has a direction. Compassion doesn't require you to disappear, and this pair, read together, often shows how a Pisces Moon can give and still stay itself.

Pisces closes the zodiac. The Moon stays roughly two and a half days, long enough for about 1 person in 12 to arrive under it, and then it crosses into Aries and the round begins again. Just before came the [Moon in Aquarius](/astrology/moon-in-aquarius/), which needs distance to understand a feeling; just after comes the [Moon in Aries](/astrology/moon-in-aries/), which needs to act on one. A birth on the day of that crossing can only be placed with the time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You love with your whole heart and your whole imagination. You often sense what a partner needs before they say it, and you give comfort freely, without keeping score. That openness is a great gift, and it needs looking after. You may take on a partner's moods as your own, lose track of where their sadness ends and yours begins, or stay out of compassion long after a relationship has stopped being kind to you. A partner who is gentle, steady and willing to say plainly what they feel takes a weight off this Moon: you no longer have to guess.

## At work {#at-work}

Work with meaning and some quiet in it suits this Moon: creative work, caring work, anything that uses imagination or compassion, ideally somewhere you aren't flooded by noise and other people's urgency. You're the colleague who notices when someone is struggling and knows what to say. Harsh or relentlessly competitive workplaces can drain you faster than the work itself. Protect some time each day when nobody needs anything from you.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Not knowing whose feeling it is. You can come home from a day among people carrying a sadness that started in someone else, and spend the evening trying to solve it. Saying yes to keep the mood around you steady can leave your own needs unspoken. Boundaries can feel unkind, so you may put them off until you're worn out. And when the world is too loud, withdrawing helps; withdrawing for too long can leave you more alone than you want to be.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Ask one question when a feeling arrives: *is this mine?* The answer often frees you. Build a daily return to yourself, music, a bath or a swim, a walk, a page in a notebook, anything that lets the day's moods wash off. Give your imagination somewhere to go, since what you make can hold feelings you can't carry all day. And treat a boundary as a kindness to both people: it lets you keep giving without vanishing.

I try to be gentle with every Moon, and honest too. In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) this one was checked like any other placement; the three that stood far above the rest were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, not the Moon, and overall a third or so held. So I offer a reading of a Pisces Moon as something to try on, not something to believe.
