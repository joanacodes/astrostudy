Sun square Jupiter belongs to people who want a great deal from life and keep having to choose among it. Between your direction and your appetite for more, set at right angles, the friction does the teaching.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) holds your direction: the self you're shaping and the life it's aiming at. [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) reaches beyond it, for more understanding, more meaning, more room to grow. The [square](/astrology/square/) between them is constructive friction: two functions, neither replacing the other, each pressing on the other's plans.

Put simply, your appetite for growth tends to run ahead of your purpose. You say yes to the course, the trip, the second project and the favour, then find your direction stretched thin; you promise generously and spend weeks keeping the promise. Beliefs can do the same: a big idea about how things ought to be can crowd out the patient work of becoming who you are. None of that is excess by nature. As I read Jupiter, expansion is not excess; the square simply makes it easy to overshoot.

What the square teaches here is measure. Each time the appetite overshoots, you learn a little more about what's worth growing into, and over the years that becomes a practical generosity, one that knows its size, and a sense of proportion other people come to trust.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Its two signs are usually of one modality, and that sets the tone. Cardinal, a Sun in Aries with Jupiter in Cancer sets the urge to start against the urge to nurture; fixed, a Sun in Scorpio with Jupiter in Aquarius sets depth against breadth; mutable, a Sun in Virgo with Jupiter in Sagittarius sets the detail against the big picture.

The houses show which two areas of life compete for your time: the Sun's, where you're becoming yourself, and Jupiter's, where you keep wanting more. Those houses come from the birth time; without it, I can say that the two bargain, but not over what ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Roughly 1 person in 11 is born within 8° of this square. The Sun and Jupiter make two squares in each cycle of about thirteen months, so most years hold two stretches of a few weeks when everyone born shares it, and the Sun, always the faster, forms and dissolves each one ([applying and separating aspects](/astrology/applying-and-separating-aspects/)).

Its siblings show the same two functions at other angles:

- **[Sun conjunct Jupiter](/astrology/sun-conjunct-jupiter/):** no angle at all; growth is part of your identity, and measure comes as a gentler lesson.
- **[Sun sextile Jupiter](/astrology/sun-sextile-jupiter/):** a sign closer; the same appetite arrives as openings you can pick from.
- **[Sun trine Jupiter](/astrology/sun-trine-jupiter/):** a sign further, in one element; purpose and growth agree on the pace.
- **[Sun opposite Jupiter](/astrology/sun-opposite-jupiter/):** the far end, Jupiter retrograde; the pull towards more stands across from you, often in someone else.

Between two charts, [Sun square Jupiter in synastry](/astrology/sun-square-jupiter-synastry/) is one person's expansiveness pressing on the other's sense of direction: inspiring one week, too much the next, and where that line falls is for each of them to say.

## In transits {#in-transits}

The square between Jupiter in the sky and your natal Sun comes round about every six years, from one side and then the other: [transiting Jupiter square natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-square-natal-sun/). Crossing a sign takes Jupiter about a year, so each square lingers for a few weeks, and comes back in up to three passes when Jupiter turns retrograde near it.

To me, the Jupiter square is a season when it's easy to take on too much: good for ambition, less good for keeping track of what you've already promised. Weather, not a forecast: it describes an appetite, not what you'll get.
