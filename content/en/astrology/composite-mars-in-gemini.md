Composite Mars in Gemini belongs to the relationship whose energy runs on talk. Saturday has three plans by Thursday, the two of you have argued cheerfully about each, and somehow two of them happen.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Take the place Mars held when each of you was born, find the point halfway between them, and you have the composite [Mars](/astrology/mars/): the bond's capacity to act, assert itself and go after what it wants ([the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/)). In [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/), the mutable air sign Mercury rules, that capacity works through exchange and variety. Mars has no dignity here, neither home nor weakness, so the sign simply lends its manner. In the count of random pairs, about 1 in 11 lands here.

A pair like this gets moving by talking. Decisions are argued into being; the evening's plan changes twice on the way out of the door; projects multiply, a language course, a podcast, a spare room to clear, and some of them get finished. Gemini is mutable, so the pair adapts fast too: a plan dropped at lunch is replaced by dinner, without much regret. Competition is verbal and mostly fun: quizzes, a crossword raced against the clock, the running debate about whose turn it is. Desire travels by conversation as much as by touch, and a well-timed text can do what flowers do elsewhere. Natal [Mars in Gemini](/astrology/mars-in-gemini/) belongs to one quick-tongued person; this composite belongs to a conversation, and it can make two quiet people lively with each other.

Arguments follow suit. They're fast and many-sided, and the subject can change three times before anything is settled: the dishes lead to the holiday, which leads to the in-laws. Point-scoring is the risk, winning the exchange instead of solving the problem, and so is the row conducted by text, where tone gets lost. What helps is one subject per disagreement, said aloud and face to face once things heat up, with a last sentence that states what was agreed.

Sharpness is one thing, menace another. If words between you ever turn into threats, or one of you grows afraid to speak, put the charts away: call 999 if you're in danger, and if you'd rather type than talk, Shout answers texts sent to 85258, at any hour, in the UK.

Composites stay outside my readings; I explain the technique because so many people meet it online. Those who draw them add the composite [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), Gemini's ruler, for the words this drive reaches for, and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/): a contact like [Mercury square Mars in synastry](/astrology/mercury-square-mars-synastry/) shows whose words sting whom, which no composite can tell apart. None of it forecasts whether you'll stay together. Two birth dates are enough to place this Mars unless it falls under half a degree from Taurus or Cancer, and both people's consent comes before anyone looks ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

[Composite Mars in Taurus](/astrology/composite-mars-in-taurus/), before it, says little and holds on; [composite Mars in Cancer](/astrology/composite-mars-in-cancer/), after it, acts to shelter its own. Gemini's best tool is curiosity: asking *what do you mean?* before answering defuses more than any clever reply.
