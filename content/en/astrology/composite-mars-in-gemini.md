Saturday has three plans by Thursday, and the two of you have argued cheerfully about each of them. Composite Mars in Gemini gives the relationship's chart a drive that runs on talk: the bond acts by discussing, competes with words and keeps several things on the go at once.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) in [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) is the midpoint of your two natal Marses, read as the bond's capacity to act, assert itself and go after what it wants. In [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/), mutable air ruled by Mercury, that capacity works through exchange and variety. Mars has no dignity here, neither home nor weakness, so the sign simply lends its manner. About 1 pair in 11 has it.

A pair like this gets moving by talking. Decisions are argued into being; the evening's plan changes twice on the way out of the door; projects multiply, a language course, a podcast, a spare room to clear, and some of them get finished. Competition is verbal and mostly fun: quizzes, a crossword raced against the clock, the running debate about whose turn it is. Desire travels by conversation as much as by touch, and a message sent at the right moment can do what flowers do elsewhere. Natal [Mars in Gemini](/astrology/mars-in-gemini/) belongs to one quick-tongued person; this composite belongs to a conversation, and it can make two quiet people lively with each other.

Arguments follow suit. They're fast and many-sided, and the subject can change three times before anything is settled: the dishes lead to the holiday, which leads to the in-laws. Point-scoring is the risk, winning the exchange instead of solving the problem, and so is the row conducted by text, where tone gets lost. What helps is one subject per disagreement, said aloud and face to face once things heat up, with a last sentence that states what was agreed. Curiosity is this Mars's best tool: asking *what do you mean?* before answering defuses more than a clever reply.

Sharpness is one thing, menace another. If words between you ever turn into threats, or one of you grows afraid to speak, put the charts away: in the UK, call 999 in danger, or Samaritans on 116 123, free, day and night.

[Composite Mars in Taurus](/astrology/composite-mars-in-taurus/), before it, says little and holds on; [composite Mars in Cancer](/astrology/composite-mars-in-cancer/), after it, acts to shelter its own. Gemini keeps the conversation moving between them.

This is a technique I explain rather than practise. Astrologers who draw composites would also look at the composite [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), Gemini's ruler, for the words this drive uses, and at the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), where one person's Mercury meeting the other's Mars, as in [Mercury square Mars in synastry](/astrology/mercury-square-mars-synastry/), shows whose words sting whom. None of it forecasts whether you'll stay together. Both birth dates place this Mars; the times matter only when the midpoint lies within about half a degree of Taurus or Cancer. And because a composite reads two people at once, it needs both of them to agree ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
