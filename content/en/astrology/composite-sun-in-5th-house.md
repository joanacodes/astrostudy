Tradition put pleasure, play and the things we make for the joy of it in the 5th house. A composite Sun there gives the relationship a centre made of enjoyment, and the bond knows itself by what it creates and celebrates.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) gets its houses from no sky at all, only from arithmetic on two charts: either each cusp is the midpoint of your two natal cusps, or all of them are rebuilt from the composite Midheaven for the place where you live. Both need both birth times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and near the edges of [the 5th house](/astrology/5th-house/) the two can put the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) on different sides of a cusp. The house is only as reliable as the two times behind it.

As the setting for the bond's Sun, the 5th is about delight. A relationship like this tends to be organised around what the two of you enjoy: the band, the allotment, the dinner parties, the long tradition of a particular film on a particular night. It's revived by play, and its purpose often involves making something purely because you both like making it. For a couple it can mean romance kept alive; for friends, a shared pleasure that has outlasted several jobs and addresses. Its creations needn't be grand: a recipe book written in two hands, a running joke turned into a little magazine, a garden photographed every spring.

The 5th is succedent, with Leo, the Sun's own sign, as its natural sign; tradition called it Good Fortune, a name for the house's mood rather than a promise of luck, and gave Venus its joy here ([planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/)). In a birth chart, the [Sun in the 5th house](/astrology/sun-in-5th-house/) is about one person at play; in synastry, [their Sun in your 5th house](/astrology/sun-in-5th-house-synastry/) is about someone who brings out your delight. In a composite, the play belongs to the pair, and two serious people can find a lightness together that neither has alone.

Tradition also gave the 5th children, and I never read it that way, in a composite or anywhere else: a 5th-house composite Sun says nothing about whether a pair will have children ([can astrology predict pregnancy?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-pregnancy/)), and the site doesn't read children's charts at all ([children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)). The real difficulty is the serious season. A bond organised around enjoyment can lose its footing when life turns heavy, and play isn't the same as avoiding things. Keeping one small pleasure going through a hard month helps more than waiting for the good times to return.

The [composite Sun in the 4th house](/astrology/composite-sun-in-4th-house/) before it keeps a bond at home, and the [composite Sun in the 6th house](/astrology/composite-sun-in-6th-house/) after it puts the bond to work; the 5th lets it play. I describe composites without drawing them in my readings, and those who use them read them with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), never as a forecast. A shared pleasure starts with both people wanting it, and so does this chart ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
