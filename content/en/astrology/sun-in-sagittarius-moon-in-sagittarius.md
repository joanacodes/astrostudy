Most pairs need some translating between the two lights. This one shares a language: your Sun and Moon both stand in Sagittarius, and you were born within a couple of days of the new moon there, the last before the December solstice. One horizon, wanted twice.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Same sign, same manner: both lights are mutable fire, so what you aim for and what you need ask for the same things. The [Sun in Sagittarius](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius/) builds an identity around a working philosophy; the [Moon in Sagittarius](/astrology/moon-in-sagittarius/) recovers its balance with a view ahead of it: a trip on the calendar, a book that opens a new subject, a long walk with no plan to come back early. Restoring yourself and becoming yourself use the same fuel. That makes you unusually wholehearted, and it also means there's no second voice inside asking you to stay.

[Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) rules both lights, and neither has a dignity in Sagittarius, so the story passes straight to Jupiter. Its sign is shared by everyone born within a year or so of you; its house, from your birth time, is yours, and with this pair it shows where purpose and comfort both go looking for meaning. Jupiter's aspects to the rest of the chart matter too: a link with Saturn, for instance, gives this double fire a structure to grow along.

You were born near the [new moon in Sagittarius](/astrology/new-moon-in-sagittarius/), within about two and a half days of it: the Moon was between 29° behind the Sun and 29° ahead. Half these births fall in the [balsamic moon](/astrology/balsamic-moon/), the last days before the conjunction, and half in the [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/) phase that follows, and on the day itself a few hours decide which. Close to 0°, the chart has a [Sun conjunct Moon](/astrology/sun-conjunct-moon/). I read a new moon birth as purpose and need starting from the same point; [your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) explains what differs either side of it.

About 1 person in 151 has both lights in Sagittarius. On a birthday when the Moon was crossing from Scorpio, or on into Capricorn, only the hour can choose between this page, the [Sun in Sagittarius, Moon in Scorpio](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius-moon-in-scorpio/) and the [Sun in Sagittarius, Moon in Capricorn](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius-moon-in-capricorn/) ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). With the lights in one sign, the rising sign, from the same hour, often decides how this fire is first seen, and none of the three is the whole chart.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You need a partner who is also a companion on the road: someone who'll talk ideas until late, laugh at the right moments and not expect you home at the same hour every night. Honesty is non-negotiable on both sides of you. The difficulty is staying when a relationship asks for the ordinary, the same kitchen, the same small problems. With nothing in the two lights pulling you home, it helps to choose that on purpose, and to treat the shared life as one more place worth exploring.

## At work {#at-work}

Teaching, travel, publishing, research, anything that keeps opening onto a larger question: both lights want work with a horizon in it. You're often the one who reminds a team why the work matters, and you can make a long project feel like an adventure. Routine with no visible point drains you twice over. Look for roles that change shape every year or two while staying true to one idea, so that the search has a direction and not only a speed.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

A single sign gives a clear voice and few counter-voices. When the Sun and Moon agree that the answer is always further on, nobody inside says *stay*, *finish*, *rest*. Plans can multiply faster than a year can hold, and a feeling can be outrun rather than felt. What helps is borrowing what the pair lacks: look for earth and water elsewhere in your chart, in Saturn, in the rising sign, in the Moon's house, and let them set the pace now and then. A horizon is better reached than endlessly moved.
