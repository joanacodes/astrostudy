The Black Moon has a white twin, though not one the sky provides. White Moon Selena was added to astrology in the 1980s, to stand opposite the Black Moon in meaning.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The Russian astrologer Pavel Globa introduced the White Moon in the 1980s as the symbolic opposite of [Black Moon Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/). Where the Black Moon is the far point of the [Moon](/astrology/moon/)'s orbit, read for what a person learned to hide, the White Moon was made to be its bright counterpart. Its name comes from Selene, the Greek goddess who personified the Moon.

It is calculated, not observed. Nothing in the sky marks it, and it is not a Lilith, whatever list it turns up in: [which Lilith is mine?](/astrology/which-lilith-is-mine/) sets it beside the Liliths only to tell them apart.

Like Lilith, it comes in two versions. Beside the White Moon there is a True White Moon, also called Arta, worked out from [true Lilith](/astrology/true-lilith/), the far point of the Moon's orbit taken at a single instant, rather than from the mean. Both have entered Unicode's astrological symbols: ⯝ for the White Moon, at U+2BDD, and ⯟ for Arta, at U+2BDF.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Its place in a chart comes from a formula. For Arta, the formula recorded with its symbol starts from three points: the Moon's node ([nodal axis](/astrology/nodal-axis/)), true Lilith, and the Sun's apogee, where the Sun appears farthest from the Earth. Because the node and the Sun's apogee enter the sum, Arta doesn't simply sit 180° from true Lilith. "Opposite", for the White Moon, describes meaning, not geometry.

That has a practical consequence. A White Moon a few degrees from the point opposite your Lilith isn't a sign that something is wrong with either; the two were never built to mirror each other degree for degree.

Two versions also mean two answers, as they do for Lilith. A chart that shows a White Moon may be using the mean version or Arta, and the label is the only way to know. Among the other names gathered with the Liliths on this site, [Priapus](/astrology/priapus/) has a page of its own too; read the word beside each glyph before deciding which point you are looking at.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The White Moon plays no part in my readings. I read one point from this family, the mean Black Moon, on its own terms, without a bright twin to balance it. Interpretations of Lilith differ widely between traditions, and adding a second, opposite point is one tradition's answer to that, not something I've tested.

My notebook is clear about which point it counted: mean Lilith, which held 75 to 90% of the time, with Saturn and Chiron ([the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)). The White Moon was never in it, so I have nothing of my own to say about how often it describes anyone ([my testing notes](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

If you come across it in someone else's reading, take it as that astrologer's choice and ask how they calculate it, mean or true. Then read your Lilith separately; nothing written about the White Moon transfers to it. Giving the most time to the placements that held best, Lilith among them, is the first rule of [how I read](/how-i-read/).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The White Moon is a real second moon.”** It's a calculated point; nothing in the sky corresponds to it.
- **“It sits exactly opposite the Black Moon.”** It's the Black Moon's opposite in meaning. Its true version mixes in the lunar node and the Sun's apogee, so it isn't simply 180° away.
- **“Selena is another name for Lilith.”** It's a separate point with its own calculation, paired with Lilith only by its meaning.
- **“The White Moon is an ancient idea.”** It was introduced in the 1980s.
