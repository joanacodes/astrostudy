Nine noviles go once round the circle: two planets 40° apart, a third of a trine. I read it as the aspect of ripening, of things that take their time to become ready.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

Forty degrees is one sign and ten. A novile from 10° Aries lands at 20° Taurus, the next sign; from 25° Aries, at 5° Gemini, two signs on. So most noviles join neighbouring signs, and the rest skip one.

What it describes, as I read it, is gestation and maturation: a connection between two planets that develops slowly, almost out of sight, until it reaches a kind of readiness. Something is incubated, and then completed. That isn't perfection, and it isn't automatic enlightenment or destiny either; maturing still takes time and the right conditions. It goes wrong in two opposite ways: rushed, the process closes before it's ready; resisted, it never quite finishes. Given time, it ripens, and the readiness, when it comes, tends to be quiet and solid.

An orb of about a degree (see [orbs](/astrology/orbs/)) keeps it apart from its close neighbours on the circle, the decile at 36° and the semi-square at 45°.

Three noviles make a [trine](/astrology/trine/). Two, at 80°, stop 10° short of the [square](/astrology/square/); four, at 160°, stop 20° short of the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/); the novile itself falls 20° short of the [sextile](/astrology/sextile/). Among [harmonic charts](/astrology/harmonic-charts/), the ninth-harmonic chart, where every position is multiplied by nine, turns each novile into a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/), and each trine too, since a trine is three ninths.

## Between which planets {#between-which-planets}

There are no novile pages for pairs of planets here. Speed matters, and so does one limit: of the two planets that never stray far from the Sun, only Venus, at up to about 47°, gets far enough away to make a novile with it; Mercury, at about 28° at most, never does.

Between two personal planets, the ripening stays close to daily life: a way of feeling and a way of speaking, say, that come together slowly as you live with them. When Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune or Pluto takes part, the maturing runs over years, and that's where I'm most patient with it. Between two slow planets it belongs to a generation, and the houses show where that ripening takes place in your life.

Beside it among the minor aspects stand the [triseptile](/astrology/triseptile/), at 154°17′, the last of the sevens, where separate strands converge, and the [binovile](/astrology/binovile/), at 80°, two noviles, where two ripenings run side by side.

## The novile in synastry {#in-synastry}

In synastry the novile is slow to show. It rarely sparks at the start; more often it's a bond that ripens with time, a friendship or partnership whose best qualities need years to appear. With the Moon or Venus involved, the ripening is often one of trust, which deepens on its own schedule. It asks for patience on both sides, and it guarantees nothing. I mention it in [synastry readings](/synastry/), read in both directions, when it's tight and touches a personal planet.

## The novile in transits {#in-transits}

Transiting noviles are among the shortest-lived contacts in a chart: within a degree, the Moon passes in a few hours and the Sun in a couple of days. From Saturn outwards they last long enough to notice, and even then they read as a quiet sign of something reaching readiness rather than as a turn of events. Weather, not a forecast: a novile transit describes a moment of readiness, never what it will bring.
