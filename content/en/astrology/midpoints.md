Two planets can be linked without touching. Halfway between them lies a degree that some astrologers treat as the place where the two meet, and a third planet standing there is read as tying them together. That degree is a midpoint.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A midpoint is written with a slash: Sun/Moon names the point midway between the Sun and the Moon. Every pair has two, because the circle can be halved two ways. The nearer midpoint lies on the shorter arc between the planets; the farther one sits exactly opposite it, on the longer arc. With the Sun at 10° Aries and the Moon at 20° Gemini, the nearer midpoint falls at 15° Taurus and the farther at 15° Scorpio.

A chart holds a lot of them. Ten planets make 45 pairs; add the Ascendant and the Midheaven and there are 66 midpoints, each with its opposite. Most of them touch nothing. The ones astrologers read are those with a third body on them, or in a hard aspect to them, and each such combination is read as three functions working as one.

The method as it is used now comes from Hamburg. The surveyor and astrologer Alfred Witte (1878–1941) revived and developed midpoints, and his Hamburg School published its rules in the *Regelwerk für Planetenbilder* in 1928 ([the Hamburg School](/astrology/hamburg-school-uranian-astrology/)). Reinhold Ebertin took the midpoints over, dropped Witte's hypothetical planets, and called his version cosmobiology; his *The Combination of Stellar Influences* (1940) became its handbook ([cosmobiology](/astrology/cosmobiology/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Midpoint astrology reads a short list of aspects, the ones that divide the circle by two, four and eight: the [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/), the [semi-square](/astrology/semi-square/) of 45°, the [square](/astrology/square/), the [sesquiquadrate](/astrology/sesquiquadrate/) of 135° and the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/). That's why the nearer and farther midpoints are read as one axis: a planet on either is in a hard aspect to both.

To see all of that at once, the Hamburg School used a dial of 90° instead of 360° ([the Uranian 90° dial](/astrology/uranian-90-dial/)). Folding the zodiac into a quarter puts every point that is 90° or 180° from another on the same spot, and every point 45° or 135° away directly across. A pointer laid across the dial then shows which planets are equally spaced around which, which is what a midpoint is.

Because every pair has a midpoint, orbs have to stay small, or every chart would look full of combinations. Speed matters too: a midpoint moves at half the combined speed of its two bodies, so one that involves the Moon moves about 6 or 7° a day, and one that involves the Ascendant or the Midheaven moves with the clock. Those need an exact birth time.

Midpoints also sit underneath a familiar technique. A [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) puts each planet of a relationship at the nearer midpoint of the two people's positions: the composite Sun is the midpoint of the two Suns.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Midpoints aren't among the techniques my readings use. My readings stay with the planets themselves, their signs, houses and aspects, and with the timing methods set out in [how I read](/how-i-read/). Nor can I offer you a tick-or-cross count for a midpoint: my notebook went placement by placement, sign by sign and house by house.

If you explore your own, two starting points are worth knowing. The [Sun/Moon midpoint](/astrology/sun-moon-midpoint/) joins the chart's two lights, and the [Venus/Mars midpoint](/astrology/venus-mars-midpoint/) joins valuing and pursuing. Keep the orbs tight, start from the personal points, and read a combination as a link between functions, not a sentence about your future. A program can print every midpoint that touches a point as a list, known as a [midpoint tree](/astrology/midpoint-trees/).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“You need the hypothetical planets to use midpoints.”** Witte's school used them; Ebertin's cosmobiology dropped them and kept the midpoints.
- **“Every midpoint in a chart matters.”** A chart has dozens. Only the few with something on them, or in hard aspect to them, are read.
- **“The nearer midpoint is the real one.”** The farther one is exactly opposite, and an opposition is one of the aspects midpoint astrology reads, so the two work as one axis.
- **“A planet on a midpoint seals a fate.”** A combination describes how three functions link up in a chart. What you do with it is yours.
