Asteroid 3811 is called Karma, a heavy name for a rock about 30 km across. It came from Indian philosophy by way of a Finnish astronomer's discovery in 1953, and it has nothing to do with what astrologers usually mean by karma.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Karma is a small asteroid in the middle part of the main belt, between Mars and Jupiter. Liisi Oterma found it at Turku, in Finland, on 13 October 1953, and it was first designated 1953 TH. It gives its name to a small family: when astronomers traced the asteroid families of the belt in 2015, they counted 59 members in the Karma family, dark bodies on similar orbits. Families like this are thought to be the pieces of collisions between asteroids.

Oterma was the first woman in Finland to earn a doctorate in astronomy, and she later directed the Tuorla Observatory. The Minor Planet Center credits her with 54 minor planets found between 1938 and 1953, and Karma is the last of them. She had first meant to study Sanskrit, the language the word comes from, but Turku didn't teach it.

The name shows how far asteroid names had travelled from those of earlier finds such as [Arachne](/astrology/arachne/), taken from Greek and Roman myth. This one honours an idea; [Hidalgo](/astrology/hidalgo/), found decades earlier, honours a man.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The naming citation gives karma as a term of Hindu philosophy. The Sanskrit word means action, work or deed, and the effect that follows from it. In the Indian traditions it became a principle of cause and effect, in which a person's intentions and actions shape their future, in this life and, in many schools, in lives to come. Hindu thought distinguishes several kinds: the karma stored up from past actions, the part of it already bearing fruit in the present life, called prarabdha, and the karma being made now. Buddhism and Jainism have their own accounts, and the schools disagree about how karma and rebirth fit together. The idea was popularised in the West by the Theosophical Society, and many in the New Age movement now treat it as a law of cause and effect that keeps the cosmos in balance.

In astrology the word usually comes up around the lunar nodes, which some astrologers, in [evolutionary astrology](/astrology/evolutionary-astrology/) especially, read in terms of past lives, with the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) as what a soul brings in. None of that involves this asteroid. Its sign, its house and its contacts can be placed like any small body's, and they come with nothing attached but a word.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I don't read karma in a chart, from this asteroid or from anywhere else. My test for a placement is whether it describes the living person in front of me, and in my notebook each one got a tick or a cross on that basis ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). There's no way to put a tick or a cross beside a past life, so a reading of mine stays in this one. I read the nodes in this life too, and I look at both, the mean node and the true: the ground a person starts from, and the direction the chart asks of them ([North Node](/astrology/north-node/)).

The asteroid adds a problem of its own: it was named after a philosophy, not discovered by one. A rock found at Turku in 1953 can't carry the weight of a word from centuries of Indian thought, and a placement of it can't tell you what you owe. If a reading ever presents a hard placement as a debt from another life, you're entitled to ask how anyone could know ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

The asteroid itself was never in my notebook, which covered the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign, each planet, Chiron and Lilith, and the system behind my readings doesn't compute it.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Karma means fate.”** In the traditions it comes from, it means action and its effects; the term closest to fate, prarabdha, names only the part already in motion.
- **“The asteroid Karma is what karmic astrology reads.”** Karmic astrology mostly works with the lunar nodes. The asteroid is a 30-km rock with no tradition of its own.
- **“A hard aspect is bad karma.”** No chart can show karma, and an aspect isn't good or bad. It describes a way two parts of a chart work together ([Square](/astrology/square/)).
