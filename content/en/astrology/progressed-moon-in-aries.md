Aries is where the progressed Moon starts each new round of the zodiac. After the soft, dissolving years of Pisces, it arrives like a morning: for two years or so, what you need is room to begin, and the patience for waiting runs shorter.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/) move your chart forward a day for each year of your life, and [the progressed Moon](/astrology/progressed-moon/), quickest of all, sets the emotional tone of a chapter. In [Aries](/astrology/aries/), cardinal fire ruled by Mars, the Moon’s work of needing, comforting and feeling takes on the sign’s directness. Feelings tend to arrive fast and ask to be acted on; comfort comes from doing something rather than from being soothed; the need for independence grows louder, and so does the wish to try things your own way. The Moon has no dignity in Aries, and nothing about this chapter is fragile: these are needs looking for a way to move.

On its way through Aries the progressed Moon also meets your natal chart: it crosses any planet you have in Aries, squares those in Cancer and Capricorn and faces those in Libra. Each contact is a short stretch inside the chapter, when that planet’s function asks to take part in the new start.

It is symbolic time, not a body in the sky, and it describes an inner season, never a sequence of events. Which part of life those needs gather in depends on the house the progressed Moon crosses at the same time, and that takes [your birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/): in Placidus, the house system I use, an exact time places the cusps, and without it I don’t read a chart. If your natal Moon is in Aries too ([Moon in Aries](/astrology/moon-in-aries/)), that is your lifelong way of feeling; the progressed Moon’s visit is a chapter, and it passes.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

In the births I counted, from 1930 to 2009, the progressed Moon took between two and two and a half years to cross Aries, usually about two years and four months: as many years as the real Moon took days to cross the sign after each birth. The real Moon moves faster at some points of its orbit than at others, so your own Aries chapter may run nearer two years or nearer two and a half. A program that calculates secondary progressions will show the month it enters Aries and the month it leaves, from your exact date, time and place of birth.

It follows the [progressed Moon in Pisces](/astrology/progressed-moon-in-pisces/) and gives way to the [progressed Moon in Taurus](/astrology/progressed-moon-in-taurus/), and because Aries is the first sign, its arrival marks a new lap: the progressed Moon takes about 27 years to go round the whole zodiac. Depending on where your natal Moon sits, the Aries chapter may come early in life, in the middle of it or later, and in a long life it can come round more than once.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Give the impatience a job. I find a chapter like this sits better when the urge to start has somewhere to go: a project of your own, a skill you’ve wanted to try, a decision you’ve been putting off. Mars rules Aries, so the house Mars occupies in your birth chart shows where the push tends to look for an outlet.

It also helps to let other people keep their own pace: needing room isn’t the same as needing to be alone, and saying which one you mean saves a good deal of friction. Then, as the chapter closes, Taurus will ask what of all this is worth keeping.
