Juno was found on 1 September 1804, the third body in the belt between Mars and Jupiter, and it took the name of the queen of the Roman gods. Of the first four asteroids it is the one whose myth is about a marriage, so astrologers who use it read commitment: what you promise, and what you need promised back.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In Rome, Juno was the protector and special counsellor of the state, called Regina, the Queen. She was the sister and wife of Jupiter and, like her Greek counterpart Hera, a goddess of marriage, honoured as Iuno Pronuba, the guardian of weddings. Astrologers who use the asteroid take the bond itself from her: commitment, loyalty, the terms two people agree on, and what happens when those terms feel broken.

I don't read Juno for anyone. The system behind my readings has no asteroids in it, so this page sets out how others use it. They look at:

- **Its sign:** the kind of commitment that feels right to you, whether spontaneous, steady, talkative or protective.
- **Its house:** where in life you make your most serious promises: to a partner, a business, a cause.
- **Its aspects:** what your promises have to make room for, such as desire, freedom or ambition.

The comparison everyone makes is with [Venus](/astrology/venus/). Venus is attraction and what you value; Juno, read this way, is the agreement that may follow, and the two don't always keep the same pace. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) adds time and responsibility to any bond it touches, and an [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) between Juno and another planet sets your commitments against that planet's needs, a seesaw to balance. Partnership as an area of life belongs to [the 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/); Juno is read as the kind of promise you bring to it, wherever the asteroid falls.

## Juno through the signs {#through-the-signs}

Juno goes round the Sun in about 4.4 years, a little faster than [Pallas](/astrology/pallas/) and Ceres, so people born in the same few months often share its sign. I have no ephemeris for it, which is why I give no dates; the house, which needs your birth time, says far more.

- **Juno in Aries:** loyalty shown in deeds, and a bond that leaves you room to act.
- **Juno in Taurus:** slow to promise and slow to leave; security and the body's comforts matter.
- **Juno in Gemini:** a bond kept alive by conversation, with a partner who's also someone to talk to.
- **Juno in Cancer:** commitment as family, protective and in need of emotional safety.
- **Juno in Leo:** loyalty given generously, and a wish to be chosen openly.
- **Juno in Virgo:** commitment shown through daily help, reliability and small attentions.
- **Juno in Libra:** partnership as an ideal of fairness, on equal terms.
- **Juno in Scorpio:** deep, exclusive commitment, where trust is everything.
- **Juno in Sagittarius:** a bond as a shared adventure, with freedom and meaning on both sides.
- **Juno in Capricorn:** commitment as a long-term structure, built on respect and responsibility.
- **Juno in Aquarius:** unconventional terms, friendship inside the bond, independence kept.
- **Juno in Pisces:** devotion and compassion, with a risk of giving yourself away.

## Juno in the houses {#in-the-houses}

The house is set by the hour of birth: without the [time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/) there is no house, and I don't read a chart without it. I use Placidus. The 8th house, which tradition tied to death, is read here as what partners share, and no line below predicts anything ([Can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)).

1. **1st house:** keeping your word is part of who you are, and people can see it.
2. **2nd house:** money and values shared as part of the bond.
3. **3rd house:** a commitment kept through daily contact, messages and talk.
4. **4th house:** a home built together, with family at the centre of the promise.
5. **5th house:** romance and play kept alive inside a long commitment.
6. **6th house:** partnership at work, and commitment shown in daily routines.
7. **7th house:** partnership itself: the terms, the contract, the question of equality.
8. **8th house:** shared resources and intimacy, where trust and power are tested.
9. **9th house:** commitment to shared beliefs, and a partner who widens your world.
10. **10th house:** a working life built alongside someone, or in public as a pair.
11. **11th house:** committed friendships, and causes you sign up to for good.
12. **12th house:** promises kept privately, and devotion that also needs solitude.

## Juno in synastry {#in-synastry}

My [synastry readings](/synastry/) leave the asteroids out and count only contacts that involve at least one personal body. Astrologers who use asteroids between two charts read Juno from both sides. When your Juno sits on someone's Sun, Moon or Venus, that person can seem to fit the terms you would commit on; from their side, they may feel chosen, or measured against a picture they never agreed to. Neither reading says whether the pair will stay together. And no comparison starts without both people saying yes: [why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/).

## Transits and the Juno return {#juno-return}

Juno comes back to its natal place about every 4.4 years, the Juno return. My forecasts follow the transits of Jupiter to Pluto, Chiron and the true node, so Juno plays no part in them. Those who follow it read Juno crossing Venus or the Descendant as a time when the terms of a commitment come up for review. It is weather, not a forecast: it can describe a conversation worth having, never a wedding or a parting. A sister goddess, [Vesta](/astrology/vesta/), gives her name to the fourth asteroid.

## What held in my tests {#what-held}

If you came here hoping my notebook would say how reliable Juno is, it can't: Juno wasn't one of the lines I [ticked or crossed](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/). ## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Juno shows who you'll marry.”** No placement names a person or dates a wedding.
- **“Juno in the 7th house means a happy marriage.”** A house describes where a theme lives, never how a relationship turns out.
- **“Juno is only about marriage.”** Any bond you commit to counts: a business partnership, a long friendship, a shared cause.
- **“Juno was always an asteroid.”** Like Ceres, Pallas and Vesta, it was counted as a planet until the 1850s.
