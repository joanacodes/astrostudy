Every chart has to start counting somewhere. The tropical zodiac counts from the March equinox, where the Sun crosses the celestial equator heading north. The draconic chart counts from the Moon's North Node, where the Moon crosses the Sun's path heading north.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) and the South Node are the two points where the Moon's tilted orbit crosses the ecliptic, the Sun's yearly path. Eclipses happen only when a new or full moon falls near them ([Why eclipses follow the nodes](/astrology/why-eclipses-follow-the-nodes/)).

That's where the dragon comes in. Medieval Arabic texts described an eighth pseudo-planet that caused eclipses, the Dragon, split into a Head and a Tail at the two nodes; Latin astronomy called them *caput* and *cauda draconis*, and in India the same points are Rahu and Ketu, two of the nine grahas ([Vedic astrology (Jyotish)](/astrology/vedic-astrology-jyotish/)). Astronomers still say *draconic* for anything measured from the nodes: the draconic month, about 27.2 days, is the time the Moon takes to come back to the same node, and the draconic year, about 347 days, the time the Sun takes.

The draconic chart applies that frame to a birth. The North Node becomes 0° Aries, the South Node 0° Libra, and every planet and angle is counted from there instead of from the equinox.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Subtract the North Node's position from every point of the chart. With the node at 15° Leo, which is 135° from 0° Aries, every position moves back by 135°: a Sun at 10° Aries becomes 25° Scorpio, a Moon at 20° Leo becomes 5° Aries. Many programs do it with one setting.

Because everything moves by the same amount, the aspects between planets are untouched, and so are the houses they occupy: the Ascendant and the house cusps move with the rest. Only the signs change. The size of the shift is simply where your node was. The nodes travel backwards round the zodiac in about 18.6 years, about 19° a year, so two people born a year apart have draconic shifts about 19° apart, and someone born with the North Node at 0° Aries has a draconic chart identical to the tropical one.

There is a choice to make first. The [mean node and the true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/) sit a degree or two apart at most, so the two draconic charts differ by that much, which matters for any planet close to the edge of a sign. In the chart I read, I look at both nodes. As with any chart, the angles and the Moon depend on the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

The geometry has its own logic. Since eclipses happen only near the nodes, within about 11½° for a lunar eclipse and 17½° for a solar one, every eclipse in a draconic frame falls near the start of Aries or Libra. And the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) always sits at 0° Libra, opposite.

What the chart offers is a second set of signs for the same planets, counted from the Moon's crossing rather than from the Sun's season. I don't draw it. I read the birth chart in the [tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/), and I read the nodes themselves in that chart, the North Node as a direction of growth and the South Node as what comes easily ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Its limits {#its-limits}

The signs of the tropical zodiac take their meaning from the seasons, counted from the equinox. Moved to the node, they keep their names but lose that anchor, so any meaning given to a draconic sign is borrowed from a frame it no longer uses.

The node chosen changes the result, and so a planet near a sign's edge can change its draconic sign from one program to the next. Other chart frames have the same problem in other forms ([The heliocentric chart](/astrology/heliocentric-chart/)).

And no frame measured from the nodes can show a past life, a fate or a soul's assignment. The nodes are where two orbits cross; what I read in them is a direction of development, never a verdict.
