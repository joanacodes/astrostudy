Mars conjunct Regulus puts the planet of effort on a star that already carries some of its nature: Ptolemy compared Regulus to Mars and Jupiter together. About 1 person in 161 born between 1930 and 2009 has Mars within a degree of it, on the Lion's heart in the last degrees of Leo.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read [Mars](/astrology/mars/) as the part of you that acts: initiative, effort, the nerve to start something and the will to protect what matters. Anger is one of its uses, never its definition. [Regulus](/astrology/regulus/), in Ptolemy's account, already has Mars in it, joined to Jupiter, and the tradition's own vocabulary shows what that mixture meant to it: Mars was its lesser malefic and Jupiter its greater benefic ([benefics and malefics](/astrology/benefics-and-malefics/)). A Mars on Regulus meets a star in which heat is tempered by breadth. Add the royal names, and the old picture is of drive with a sense of command in it.

I keep that picture at the level of tone. As in any conjunction, neither partner replaces the other: Mars still works through its own sign and house, and for everyone born from 1930 to 2009 that sign is Leo, in its final degrees, since the star stood at 28°50′ Leo in 1930 and 29°49′ in 2000 ([Mars in Leo](/astrology/mars-in-leo/)). The house shows where the effort goes. The system behind my readings places no stars, so I describe this contact rather than tell anyone what it will do ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

Mars reaches the star about every two years and usually crosses its degree within a few days. Three times in these eighty years it lingered. In the winters of 1947–48, 1979–80 and 1994–95, Mars passed the star, turned retrograde in early Virgo, came back over it, turned direct again in Leo and crossed it a third time in the spring. Births in those months can have Mars on Regulus while moving backwards ([Mars retrograde in Leo](/astrology/mars-retrograde-in-leo/)). Outside those winters, the contact belongs to a few days at a time, and the date of birth settles it far more often than the hour.

In the sky the meeting is close but never complete. Of the bodies that make contacts with this star, the Moon and Venus can hide it outright ([Moon conjunct Regulus](/astrology/moon-conjunct-regulus/), [Venus conjunct Regulus](/astrology/venus-conjunct-regulus/)); the Sun passes half a degree south of it ([Sun conjunct Regulus](/astrology/sun-conjunct-regulus/)), and Saturn a degree or so north ([Saturn conjunct Regulus](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-regulus/)). Mars, held off by the tilt of its orbit, will not cover Regulus for millennia: in all its passes from 1930 to 2009 it went by on the northern side, between about half a degree and four degrees away.

None of this asks you to expect anything. Tradition's words for Mars, heat and conflict among them, describe how the ancients saw a planet, and the conjunction adds a royal colour to them; the [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/) page explains the one-degree rule, and [fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/) the lore behind it. What I'd look at, in any chart with Mars here, is the work Mars has to do: where you start things, how you go after them, and what you're willing to defend.
