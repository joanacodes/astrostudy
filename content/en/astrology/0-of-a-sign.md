A planet at 0° has just crossed a line. A few hours or days before you were born, or a few minutes for the Ascendant, it was still in the sign before. That makes the first degree of a sign interesting for two reasons, one about meaning and one about the clock.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The first degree of a sign runs from 0°00′ to 0°59′. Since the count starts at nought, a chart that shows 0°12′ Taurus means twelve minutes of arc into Taurus, the first degree of the sign, not a planet with no position ([degrees, minutes and seconds](/astrology/degrees-minutes-and-seconds/)). Just behind it lies [the anaretic degree (29°)](/astrology/anaretic-degree-29/) of the sign before.

A planet there belongs to its new sign completely. Some astrologers read the first degree as the sign at first contact: its qualities eager, direct, not yet worn smooth. That's an interpretation; what the degree states for certain is that the change of sign is recent.

How recent depends on the body. The Moon reached the sign within the last two hours or so; the Sun within the last day; the Ascendant within the last four minutes, on average. The Sun takes about a day to cross each sign's opening degree, twelve days a year in all, so about 1 birth in 30 has the Sun at 0° of a sign, and the same share has the Moon there.

Four first degrees have names of their own: the equinoxes fall at the start of Aries and Libra, the solstices at the start of Cancer and Capricorn, and the Sun opens a season at each. Together they are called the [Aries Point](/astrology/aries-point/).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A first degree is where the sky's changes of sign happen ([planetary ingresses](/astrology/planetary-ingresses/)), and not every change is final. A planet that turns retrograde soon after entering a sign can slip back into the last one. [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) entered Gemini on 7 July 2025, went back into Taurus on 8 November, and only came back to Gemini on 26 April 2026. A slow planet at 0° in a chart may be in the middle of such a crossing, with a whole sign ahead of it or a step back still to come.

For the fast points, the degree is a question for the clock. The Moon changes sign every two and a half days or so, and the Ascendant about every two hours on average, so a time rounded to the hour can put either of them in the sign before. House cusps in Placidus, the system I use, behave the same way: a cusp at 0° of a sign is a few minutes away from the sign before ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

The Sun is the common case. If yours is at 0°, you were born within about a day of its change of sign, and the date alone can't always tell you which side you're on, because the Sun's arrival in a sign falls at a different hour each year ([born on the cusp](/astrology/born-on-the-cusp/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read a planet at 0° in the sign it has entered, as fully as one in the middle of the sign, by its house and its aspects. The degree doesn't change what the planet means; it changes how carefully I check the data. With the Moon or an angle at 0°, I ask where the birth time came from, a certificate or a family memory, and I say so when a reading leans on it.

Looking just before the moment of birth is a habit some astrologers take further. The [prenatal lunation](/astrology/prenatal-lunation/), the last new or full moon before you were born, is read by some as a kind of preface to the chart, set a few days or weeks earlier. A planet at 0° is a smaller version of the same idea: it carries the trace of a threshold crossed just before you arrived.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“0° means the planet has no position.”** It is the first degree of the sign, measured in minutes of arc from its start.
- **“A planet at 0° hasn't really arrived.”** It arrived the moment it crossed the line. If the sign before shows in you, look to your other planets for it.
- **“With your Sun at 0°, you're really the sign before.”** Your Sun sign is the one the Sun had entered, and the birth time settles it when the change fell on your birthday.
- **“Only 0° Aries matters.”** Every first degree marks a change of sign; the four cardinal ones also mark the seasons.
