Every planet in your chart was moving when you were born, some quickly, some barely at all. Speed is the measure of that movement, and in my work it matters more to timing than to temperament.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A planet's speed is the distance it covers along the zodiac in a day, as seen from Earth. The Moon is the quickest by far, at about 13° a day, faster when it is near Earth and slower when it is far. The Sun moves about a degree a day, a little faster in early January, when Earth is closest to it, and a little slower in early July.

Mercury and Venus keep pace with the Sun on average, since they never stray far from it: all three go round the zodiac in about a year. From there the planets slow down step by step. Mars takes about two years to go round, Jupiter about twelve, Saturn about 29½, Uranus about 84, Neptune about 165 and Pluto about 248, so on average the outermost planets cover less than a minute of arc a day.

The ancients ranked the seven planets they could see by that speed, from slowest to fastest: Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, the Sun, Venus, Mercury, the Moon. The ranking now goes by the name of the Chaldean order (Eviatar Zerubavel, *The Seven Day Circle*, 1985). It decides which planet rules each of the [faces](/astrology/faces/) and in what order the [planetary hours](/astrology/planetary-hours/) run, and the hours in turn gave each day of the week its planet.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

No planet keeps a steady pace, because we watch from a moving Earth. From Mercury outwards, each one speeds up, slows down, comes to a standstill at its [station](/astrology/stations/), goes backwards for a while and stops again ([Retrograde, explained](/astrology/retrograde-explained/)).

Where a planet is fastest depends on where it is in relation to us and the Sun. Mercury and Venus run quickest on the far side of the Sun, when their own motion and the Sun's apparent motion add up. Mars and the planets beyond move fastest around their conjunction with the Sun, when Earth and the planet are on opposite sides of the Sun. In both cases the planet is then close to the Sun in the sky, lost in its glare: a planet at its fastest is usually a [combust](/astrology/combustion/) one, hidden from view.

Speed sits with the conditions that describe a planet's situation rather than its sign. The main one of those is [angularity](/astrology/angularity/), where a planet stands in relation to the angles of the chart; speed describes how it was moving when you were born.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The system I built for my readings uses speed first of all for timing. In transits, a fast planet crosses a point in your chart in hours or days, while a slow one can stay near it for months. In [secondary progressions](/astrology/secondary-progressions/), each day after your birth stands for a year of your life, so the planets' speed in the days after you were born sets how far they progress: the progressed Moon moves about a degree a month, the progressed Sun about a degree a year, and a planet that stations a few weeks after your birth turns, in progression, a few decades into your life. In [solar arc directions](/astrology/solar-arc-directions/), every point moves at the progressed Sun's pace, about a degree a year: a shade more for someone born in January, a shade less for someone born in July.

In the birth chart itself, speed matters most at the extremes. A planet close to a station when you were born was barely moving, a function caught at a turning point, and I give it more weight. The Moon, at the other extreme, moves about half a degree an hour, so its exact degree depends on the birth time ([The Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

Speed says nothing about temperament. A fast Mercury doesn't make a quick talker, and a slow Saturn doesn't make a slow person; the planet's sign, house and aspects describe how its function works.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Slow planets matter less, since they barely move.”** In my forecasts it's the other way round: Jupiter, Saturn and the outer planets are the transits I follow, because a slow planet stays on a point long enough to be lived.
- **“Planets move at a steady pace.”** None does. Every planet speeds up and slows down through its cycle, and all but the Sun and Moon stop and turn back.
- **“The Sun moves exactly one degree a day.”** A little less in July, a little more in January, because Earth's orbit isn't a perfect circle.
- **“A retrograde planet is a slow one.”** In the middle of a loop a planet can move backwards at a fair pace. It is slowest at the two stations, where it turns.
