The second decan of Aries is the middle of the sign, 10° to 20°, and the only stretch of the zodiac where the Sun is honoured three ways at once: by exaltation, by face and by element. Mars keeps the sign; the Sun has the run of these ten degrees.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) holds the face, coming straight after Mars in Lilly's table of [faces](/astrology/faces/), and it holds the decan by element too: the middle decan of each fire sign goes to the ruler of the next fire sign along, and after Aries comes Leo, the Sun's own sign. The two lists part ways in most decans; they agree here, and this is the only decan where they agree on the Sun.

Old scoring makes the point in numbers. The Sun is exalted in [Aries](/astrology/aries/), and by day it also rules the whole fire triplicity ([triplicity rulers](/astrology/triplicity-rulers/)), so a daytime Sun between 10° and 20° Aries collects exaltation, triplicity and face: four points, three and one, eight in all. By night Jupiter takes the triplicity and the Sun keeps five.

Any other planet in these degrees serves two masters, and here they make a natural pair: Mars, ruler of the sign, supplies the drive, and the Sun, honoured three ways in this decan, says what the drive is for. Your Mars's sign and house answer the first question, your Sun's the second.

## Its image {#its-image}

Budge's Egyptian name for it is Qeṭ, and the next decan's, Sasaqeṭ, repeats it; the Greco-Egyptian lists tie it the other way, writing Chont-chre after the first decan's Chont-har. Firmicus Maternus calls it Senacher, or Asenter. The Latin Hermetic list gives it Sabaoth, the Hebrew word for hosts, familiar from the Lord of Sabaoth. Athanasius Kircher's list of 1652 puts Anubis here, and the list of Cosmas of Maiuma Persephone, between Aidoneus before and Eros after.

None of these is a portrait. They are names for ten degrees of sky, and which stars they first meant, nobody can now say ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

If your birthday falls between about 31 March and 9 April, your Sun is in this decan, and on about 8 April it stands on the degree of its own exaltation ([Sun in Aries](/astrology/sun-in-aries/)). The 2026 Sun spent from 30 March, 16:58 UT, to 9 April, 20:40 UT, in these degrees; the 2027 Sun runs about five and a half hours behind.

That degree is the 19th of Aries, between 18° and 19°, where the old tables put the Sun's [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/); in 2026 the Sun occupied it from 7 April at 19:50 UT to 8 April at 20:15 UT. I don't treat it as a lucky degree: it is a point the tradition marked, and a Sun on it is the same Sun, read through its house and aspects.

Births from March 1930 to April 1932 have Uranus here. Saturn's passes came in 1938–39, 1967–69 and 1997–98 ([Saturn in Aries](/astrology/saturn-in-aries/)), the latest from 9 May to 20 October 2026, with a return from 29 January to 25 April 2027. [Alpheratz](/astrology/alpheratz/), the star Andromeda shares with the Great Square of Pegasus, marks the middle of the decan at 14°40′ Aries in 2026.

In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the Sun sign held about a third of the time, like the placements overall. So I weigh the Sun's decan as a refinement of one voice among many: the edges of the decan ([Aries, first decan](/astrology/aries-first-decan/), [Aries, third decan](/astrology/aries-third-decan/)) matter less to me than the house your Sun sits in.
