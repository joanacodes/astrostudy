The second decan of Aries is the middle of the sign, 10° to 20°, and the only stretch of the zodiac where the Sun is honoured three ways at once: by exaltation, by face and by element. Mars keeps the sign; the Sun has the run of these ten degrees.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

By face, the decan belongs to the [Sun](/astrology/sun/), next after Mars in the Chaldean order that Lilly's table of [faces](/astrology/faces/) follows. By element, the modern decans hand the middle ten degrees of a fire sign to the ruler of the next fire sign along, Leo, and Leo's ruler is the Sun. The two lists part ways in most decans; they agree here, and this is the only decan where they agree on the Sun.

Old scoring makes the point in numbers. The Sun is exalted in [Aries](/astrology/aries/), and by day it also rules the whole fire triplicity ([triplicity rulers](/astrology/triplicity-rulers/)), so a daytime Sun between 10° and 20° Aries collects exaltation, triplicity and face: four points, three and one, eight in all. By night Jupiter takes the triplicity and the Sun keeps five.

Any other planet in these degrees answers to two rulers. Mars, for the sign, says how the Aries part of you acts; the Sun, for the decan, says what that drive is organised around. I look at both: the sign and house of your Mars, then those of your Sun.

## Its image {#its-image}

Budge's Egyptian name for it is Qeṭ, and the next decan's, Sasaqeṭ, repeats it; the Greco-Egyptian lists tie it the other way, writing Chont-chre after the first decan's Chont-har. Firmicus Maternus calls it Senacher, or Asenter. The Latin Hermetic list gives it Sabaoth, the Hebrew word for hosts, familiar from the Lord of Sabaoth. Athanasius Kircher's list of 1652 puts Anubis here, and the list of Cosmas of Maiuma Persephone, between Aidoneus before and Eros after.

None of these is a portrait. They are names for ten degrees of sky, and which stars they first meant, nobody can now say ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Births from about 31 March to 9 April have the Sun here ([Sun in Aries](/astrology/sun-in-aries/)). In 2026 the Sun reached 10° Aries on 30 March at 16:58 UT and 20° on 9 April at 20:40 UT; a year later each crossing comes about five and a half hours later.

Born on or around 8 April, your Sun stands in the 19th degree of Aries, the degree of its [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/) in the old tables. In 2026 the Sun was in it from 7 April at 19:50 UT to 8 April at 20:15 UT. I don't treat it as a lucky degree: it is a point the tradition marked, and a Sun on it is the same Sun, read through its house and aspects.

Slower planets put whole birth years here. Uranus crossed these degrees for births from March 1930 to April 1932, and Saturn in 1938–39, 1967–69 and 1997–98 ([Saturn in Aries](/astrology/saturn-in-aries/)). Saturn came through again from 9 May to 20 October 2026 and returns from 29 January to 25 April 2027. The fixed star [Alpheratz](/astrology/alpheratz/), α Andromedae, sits at 14°40′ Aries in 2026, in the middle of the decan.

In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the Sun sign held about a third of the time, like the placements overall. So I weigh the Sun's decan as a refinement of one voice among many: the edges of the decan ([Aries, first decan](/astrology/aries-first-decan/), [Aries, third decan](/astrology/aries-third-decan/)) matter less to me than the house your Sun sits in.
