Tradition puts the Sun's strength on one axis: its exaltation in Aries, its fall in Libra, directly across. Your chart has the Sun at the Libra end and the Moon at the Aries end, so the boldness the old books give the Sun belongs to your feelings, and the careful, weighing manner to your sense of who you are.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Libra and Aries are the two signs that begin at an equinox, Aries in March and Libra in September, and they face each other across the zodiac. Both are cardinal, and air and fire are the two active elements, so neither light is slow. What differs is the reason for moving. The [Sun in Libra](/astrology/sun-in-libra/) builds its identity on proportion, on what it judges fair between people; the [Moon in Aries](/astrology/moon-in-aries/) looks after itself by doing something, at once, without waiting for agreement. You aim for balance, and you need to move first.

That axis comes from Ptolemy's [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/) and [fall](/astrology/fall/) of the Sun, and I read it gently: not a weak self beside strong feelings, but a self whose strength works through consultation, and an inner life that is frank, warm and fast. [Venus](/astrology/venus/), ruler of Libra, shows what your Sun finds worth balancing for; [Mars](/astrology/mars/), ruler of Aries, shows where your Moon spends its first surge, and their houses say whether the two keep to one plan.

Your Moon was 151° to 208° round the zodiac from your Sun, in a lunar month 12½ to 17 days old. Half of these births come in the last days of the [waxing gibbous moon](/astrology/waxing-gibbous-moon/) and half in the [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/) phase, and the exact [Sun opposite Moon](/astrology/sun-opposite-moon/) lies in the middle of the range. With the Sun in Libra, any full moon is a [full moon in Aries](/astrology/full-moon-in-aries/), and the old English calendar names it: the [Harvest moon](/astrology/harvest-moon/) when it is the full moon closest to the September equinox, the [Hunter's moon](/astrology/hunters-moon/) when it is the one that follows. [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) shows whether you came before it or after.

About 1 person in 147 is born with the lights on the equinox axis this way round. On a birthday when the Moon arrived from Pisces or left for Taurus, the hour decides between this page, the [Sun in Libra, Moon in Pisces](/astrology/sun-in-libra-moon-in-pisces/) and the [Sun in Libra, Moon in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-libra-moon-in-taurus/) ([the Moon, the one to check](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). That hour gives the rising sign as well, and the two lights with the rising sign are only a chart's first draft. It also gives, in Placidus, the house system I use, the pair of houses the lights face each other across, which is where this pair does its weighing, and why I wait for the time.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Partnership matters to a Libra Sun, and you put real thought into being fair, attentive and good company. The Aries Moon adds a need that surprises people who know only that side: when something hurts, you want to say so now, and settle it now. Waiting for the right moment, which the Sun does so well, can leave the Moon stewing for days. A partner who would rather hear the quick, honest sentence than the polished one a week later is a gift to you, and so is some independence inside the bond.

## At work {#at-work}

You tend to do well where a fair decision has to be made quickly: refereeing, negotiating to a deadline, running a busy team, design work with a hard date at the end. The Sun knows what everyone can accept; the Moon has the nerve to put it on the table first. Committees that weigh for ever wear on the Aries Moon, and colleagues who push through without consulting anyone offend the Libra Sun. You want a process that is fair and a pace that is brisk.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The friction runs between the person you take yourself to be and the speed of your reactions. You think of yourself as even-handed, so a sharp word or a sudden decision can feel as if it came from someone else. It came from the Moon, and it is just as much yours. Treat its first reaction as information rather than a ruling: give it a minute and somewhere to go, a walk or a few lines on paper, then let the Libra Sun decide what would be fair to do with it. In that order, you lose neither.
