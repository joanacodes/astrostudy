Mars square Saturn is where *go* meets *not yet*. The part of you that wants to act and the part that wants things built properly sit a quarter of the chart apart, and each keeps testing the other.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) wants to move: it initiates, asserts, competes and spends effort to get somewhere. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) holds the line: responsibility, limits, discipline, and the integration only time brings. A [square](/astrology/square/) brings constructive friction between them. Neither replaces the other and neither lets the other off lightly: Mars pushes at Saturn's limits, and Saturn tests Mars's push.

In plain words, what you set out to do often meets resistance: a rule, a delay, a voice that wants it done properly or not at all, sometimes your own. You may know the stalled start, or the holding back that ends in pushing too hard. None of this means your efforts don't count. The square asks you to learn timing: when to press, when to wait, and where effort will actually move something.

Lived with for a while, it turns into stamina and strategy: you know what a thing costs because you've paid it. Saturn, as I read it, keeps a timetable rather than building walls, and this square is the clearest case of slower, not never ([The three that held: Saturn, Chiron and Lilith](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/#saturn)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

A square usually joins signs of one modality ([the three modalities](/astrology/three-modalities/)), and for this pair that says a lot. Every sign where tradition exalts these two or puts them in their fall is cardinal: Mars is exalted in Capricorn and falls in Cancer, Saturn is exalted in Libra and falls in Aries. A cardinal square therefore puts both in a sign of strength or strain: Mars in Capricorn square Saturn in Libra has both exalted, Mars in Cancer square Saturn in Aries both in fall. Through the houses you can see where it rubs: Mars in the 1st square Saturn in the 10th might set your own push against the slow climb of a career.

About 1 person in 17 has this square, counting within 6°. Within a degree or two, the rhythm of push and wait runs through most of what you take on; towards 6°, it comes up mainly when a goal really matters.

Mars, much the quicker, brings the square in and out over a few weeks of births, twice in each two-year cycle of the pair.

Mars and Saturn's other contacts, for comparison:

- **[Mars conjunct Saturn](/astrology/mars-conjunct-saturn/):** push and structure already fused, so the friction happens inside one gesture rather than between two.
- **[Mars sextile Saturn](/astrology/mars-sextile-saturn/):** the same two functions on easy terms, with method there for the taking.
- **[Mars trine Saturn](/astrology/mars-trine-saturn/):** patience and drive in step, with none of the square's stop and start.
- **[Mars opposite Saturn](/astrology/mars-opposite-saturn/):** the limit seems to stand across the room, often in another person, before you find it in yourself.

Between two charts, [Mars square Saturn in synastry](/astrology/mars-square-saturn-synastry/) can make one person's pace feel like a brake to the other, and the other's push like pressure. It's workable when both say what they need, and I read it from each side.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Saturn square natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-saturn-square-natal-mars/) comes twice in each 29½-year round, about seven years after Saturn crosses your Mars and about seven years before it returns there. With some two and a half years to a sign, Saturn holds the square for weeks, and its retrograde loop can bring it back to the exact angle two or three times: a season when effort meets a resistance that has something to teach.

Those born with the square get extra rounds: the [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/) and Saturn's half-turn, around 15 and 44, each repeat it, and when Saturn squares its own place in between, it lands on your Mars or opposite it.

As for Mars itself, it squares your natal Saturn on two short occasions in each two-year lap, a few days apiece: bursts of impatience with rules, or of energy for a job that's been waiting.

Like all transits, these are weather, not a forecast. They show effort being tested for a while; they don't say it will fail.
