Cancer, the sign the Moon rules, holds your Moon; Aquarius, which tradition counts as the Sun's detriment, holds your Sun; and no other pair puts those two together. The Moon was growing towards full when you arrived. To me the pair is a self that works for people in general, with feelings that need one home and the people in it.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Five signs separate Aquarius from Cancer, which makes a [quincunx](/astrology/quincunx/) by sign: fixed air and cardinal water, with no element, mode or polarity in common, so each light keeps adjusting to the other. The [Sun in Aquarius](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius/) takes its sense of self from fairness for everyone and the future of the group; the [Moon in Cancer](/astrology/moon-in-cancer/) keeps watch over its own, and settles once they are safe and fed. One light thinks in populations, the other in names. You may campaign for strangers all week and still want every evening to belong to the same five people.

Cancer is the Moon's [domicile](/astrology/domicile/), so your Moon answers to no other planet, and its own placement by house and aspect tells its story. The Sun, for its part, is in Aquarius, its [detriment](/astrology/detriment/). Of the two rulers of Aquarius I read both, [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) for where your Sun insists on doing things differently and [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), the older ruler, for the obligations it takes on. Saturn has its own detriment in Cancer: the planet that gives your Sun its structure is least comfortable in your Moon's sign. Read gently, that means principles that need translating before they feel like home, and a home that does better with a few rules of its own.

Your Moon was 121° to 179° from the Sun and still waxing, ten to almost fifteen days into the month, high in the evening and nearly round. The [waxing gibbous moon](/astrology/waxing-gibbous-moon/) phase takes the great majority of these births, seven in eight, the [first quarter moon](/astrology/first-quarter-moon/) phase the earliest, and an exact quincunx at 150° falls inside the range. The full moon that followed, within about five days, was usually a [full moon in Leo](/astrology/full-moon-in-leo/). The gibbous phase, as I read it, is needs adjusting before they show, which is the quincunx's work too; [your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) explains how it differs from the phase before.

Of every 149 people born in the years counted, about one has this pair, and the date alone can't always settle it. The Moon needs only about two and a half days to cross Cancer, from the Gemini border to the Leo one, so a birthday on a crossing could be the [Sun in Aquarius, Moon in Gemini](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius-moon-in-gemini/) or the [Sun in Aquarius, Moon in Leo](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius-moon-in-leo/) until the hour is known ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). With the Moon carrying so much here, its house matters more than usual, and, like the rising sign, it needs the hour.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You protect the people you love and give them room to be themselves, which is a rarer pair of gifts than it sounds. The Cancer Moon remembers, feeds and shelters; the Aquarius Sun insists that everyone, a partner included, stays free and equal. What can get lost is your own need. You may find it easier to defend someone's right to independence than to tell them you missed them, and a partner who asks, kindly and often, how you are reaches the Moon.

## At work {#at-work}

Work that builds care into a system suits you: housing, community services, mutual aid, public organisations, design for homes and neighbourhoods. The Sun sees how care could be made fair and available to everyone; the Moon makes sure it actually reaches someone. A cold, purely procedural workplace costs the Moon a great deal, even when the Sun agrees with every rule in it.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The quincunx asks for constant small adjustments, and here they run between the general and the personal. You may feel guilty for putting your own people first, or restless when home closes in. Neither feeling is a fault. It helps to give each light its territory and its time, the cause in its hours and the household in its own, and to keep a few friends who belong to both.
