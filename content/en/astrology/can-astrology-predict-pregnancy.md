Am I pregnant? Will I ever be? When is the best time? These questions come with so much hope, and sometimes so much hurt, that they deserve a better answer than a placement. Here's mine, as plainly and as gently as I can put it.

## The short answer {#the-short-answer}

No. A chart can't tell whether you're pregnant, whether or when you'll conceive, how many children you'll have, or whether a baby will be a girl or a boy. Pregnancy is on my list of [what I won't read](/how-i-read/), and I don't read children's charts either, before or after they're born ([children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)).

What can tell you is a pregnancy test, and then a doctor or a midwife.

## Why it matters {#why-it-matters}

Astrology has a long record of trying. Ptolemy gave children a chapter of their own (*Tetrabiblos*, book IV), and the [5th house](/astrology/5th-house/) was traditionally the house of children, its old name Good Fortune. Horary astrologers judged questions about conceiving from it: William Lilly's chapter on the 5th house opens with questions of that kind (*Christian Astrology*, 1647). Some astrologers still answer them. I describe that history; I don't take part in it.

Here's why.

- **The stakes are too high for a symbol.** A "yes" that doesn't come true can break a heart month after month. A "no" can wound someone who goes on to have a child, or close a door that was never the chart's to close.
- **Loss is real.** Pregnancies can end, early or late, and a reading that had promised one would add a cruelty to a grief. No astrologer should risk that.
- **Decisions belong to the people making them.** Whether to try, when, and with what help are choices for you, your partner if you have one, and your doctor. A chart offered as a deadline only adds pressure where you need the least.
- **It's another person's chart.** A child's chart belongs to the child, and I don't read it, in the womb or after birth.

The symbols don't say what people hope either. The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) describes needs and comfort, not motherhood; Venus describes what you value, not fertility. I read the 5th house in an adult's own life: creativity, pleasure, play and romance. Some websites call Jupiter's passage through it "a baby year". I read [Jupiter transiting the 5th house](/astrology/jupiter-transit-5th-house/) as about a year in which that part of life widens, more room for what you make and enjoy, and nothing in it decides whether a child will come. Questions about illness and about death get the same answer from me ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)), and so do questions about trials and verdicts ([can astrology predict a court case?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-a-court-case/)).

## What to do instead {#what-to-do-instead}

- **To know whether you're pregnant:** take a pregnancy test, then see a doctor or a midwife.
- **If you've been trying for a while, or you're worried about your fertility:** talk to a doctor. They can tell you what is worth checking, and when.
- **After a loss, or through the strain of trying:** your doctor, a counsellor or a support group can help you carry it. Grief over a pregnancy is grief, whatever its stage.
- **For contraception, or a pregnancy you didn't plan:** a doctor or a sexual health service can explain your options.
- **If anyone sells you rituals or "work" to make a pregnancy happen:** keep your money and your hope for people who can help ([paid "spiritual work"](/astrology/paid-spiritual-work/)).

A chart can still keep you company in other ways: how you meet big changes, how you care for people and let them care for you, what you create. Those are questions about your life, as an adult, and I'm glad to read them.
