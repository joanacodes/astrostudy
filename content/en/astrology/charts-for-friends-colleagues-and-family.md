Many of the people who shape a life aren't partners. A best friend, a co-founder, a sister, a father in his eighties: comparing charts can say as much about those bonds as about a couple, and sometimes more, because nobody expects the comparison to answer whether to marry.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

It's [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), the comparison of two birth charts, used for any bond between two adults. The method doesn't change: the aspects one person's planets make to the other's, and the [house overlays](/astrology/house-overlays/), where each person's planets fall in the other's houses, read in both directions. What changes is the question you bring, and so which parts of the charts lead.

The idea is old. Ptolemy gave friendship and enmity a chapter of their own, and judged whether a friendship would last by comparing four points of the two nativities: the Sun, the Moon, the Ascendant and the Lot of Fortune (*Tetrabiblos*, book IV). He was asking about friends, not only spouses.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Both charts are read first, each on its own, then side by side. Then the questions of the bond decide where to look closest:

- **Friends.** Mercury and Jupiter for conversation and humour, the Moon for comfort, Venus for shared pleasures, Saturn for loyalty over the years; Mars, Saturn and Chiron for the friction that keeps coming back.
- **Colleagues.** Mercury for how you communicate, Mars for pace and drive, Saturn for structure and responsibility, the Sun and the 10th house for leadership and visibility, the 6th house for everyday working life.
- **Family.** The Moon for how care was given and received, Saturn for rules and duty, the 4th house for home and roots, Mercury for how you talk or don't, Chiron for the family wound, and the slow planets for what travels between generations.

In every case I count only contacts with at least one personal body, the Sun, the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, the Ascendant or the Midheaven, and both birth times are needed, since the houses and the Moon's exact contacts depend on them ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

It shows how two people tend to meet: what flows easily, where they rub, what each brings out in the other. Between friends, why some talks run all night and some subjects always snag. Between colleagues, why one person's pace feels like pressure to the other, and how to share the load. In a family, how care was given and received, and what each of you is still carrying.

It's also a way of talking. Asking a friend or a parent for their birth time often starts the conversation the reading is for. My own synastry readings for friends, co-workers and family, as well as couples, are described on the [synastry readings](/synastry/) page.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

These comparisons are for understanding, never for selection. A chart isn't a fair basis for anyone's job, so I don't read for hiring, firing or promotion, or on an employer's behalf. A reading shouldn't be used to test whether someone is a real friend, and it won't tell you to forgive a relative or cut one off. Those choices stay yours.

Both people must be adults and know about it, and a colleague must agree ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)). I don't read about children, even inside a family ([children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)). And when a bond has become harmful, at work or at home, a chart isn't the right tool: a manager, a union, a doctor or a therapist is.

Finally, none of this can be done with Sun signs alone: a table of signs compares one placement and leaves out almost everything that makes a friendship or a family what it is ([sun-sign compatibility, and why it isn't enough](/astrology/sun-sign-compatibility-and-why-it-isnt-enough/)).
