Sabik is the second-brightest star of Ophiuchus, two white stars on a long, stretched orbit that brought them closest together in 2024. Its Arabic name, the preceding one, has lost whatever it was meant to precede.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

At magnitude 2.43, η Ophiuchi is second in Ophiuchus only to [Rasalhague](/astrology/rasalhague/), the Serpent-bearer's head. It is a pair of white stars about 88 light-years away, circling each other every 87.8 years on a very stretched orbit: at their closest, last reached in 2024, they come within about twice the distance from the Earth to the Sun. Both are hotter than the Sun, and the brighter is about two and a half times as wide.

Its degree, 17°57′ [Sagittarius](/astrology/sagittarius/) in 2000 and 18°20′ in 2026, is one the Sun crosses every December while it is in front of Ophiuchus, the constellation the "thirteenth sign" headlines are about ([Ophiuchus and the 13th sign](/astrology/ophiuchus-and-the-13th-sign/)).

Its latitude is what sets Sabik apart from Ophiuchus's head. At 7.2° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), it lies inside the band about 8° either side where the planets travel, though beyond the Moon's reach of about 5° ([celestial latitude](/astrology/celestial-latitude/)). Twenty degrees further along the zodiac, the cluster [Facies](/astrology/facies/), in Sagittarius, lies closer still, under a degree from the ecliptic.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

The Arabic al-sābiq means the preceding one, the one that goes before. Paul Kunitzsch and Tim Smart, who traced the modern star names back to their sources, call its reference uncertain: whatever came after it has dropped out of the record.

In Chinese astronomy Sabik is the eleventh star of the left-hand wall of the Heavenly Market, a wall of eleven stars named for eleven old states; Sabik stands for Song.

I find something fitting in a star whose name points at something no one can identify any more. The tradition that reaches me is a place and a word, and I leave it as that rather than fill the gap with a meaning of my own.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun meets Sabik's degree on about 9 and 10 December, a [Sun in Sagittarius](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius/) birthday about a week ahead of the Sun's arrival at [Lesath](/astrology/lesath/) and Shaula, six degrees on. Neptune held the degree, on and off, for births from late 1977 to the autumn of 1979, and Pluto in 2002 and 2003 ([Pluto in Sagittarius](/astrology/pluto-in-sagittarius/)).

Pluto's visit was unusual. Its orbit is the most tilted of all the planets read in a chart, and in October 2003 it passed about 1.6° from Sabik in the sky while sitting on the star's degree, as near to a meeting of the two lights as a contact with Sabik gets. The Moon, by contrast, never comes nearer than about 2°.

My readings leave all this aside: the system I built doesn't place stars in a chart, and I keep to what it measures ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). For anyone born in the autumn of 2003, though, it's a pleasant thing to know that Pluto and Sabik really were neighbours in the sky that season.
