Hamal is the brightest star of the Ram, the constellation that gave the zodiac its first sign, and it stands in the second sign: 8°01′ Taurus. Few stars show as plainly that the signs and the constellations have gone their separate ways.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Hamal is Alpha Arietis, an orange giant of magnitude 2.00, about 66 light-years away and some 15 times the Sun's width. Since 2011 it has been thought to have a planet of its own, at least 1.8 times the mass of Jupiter, on a 381-day orbit. Its declination is almost exactly the latitude of the Tropic of Cancer, so it passes straight overhead along that line.

On the tropical zodiac its degree has moved from 6°40′ [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/) for a birth in 1930 to 7°39′ in 2000 and 8°01′ in 2026. Its latitude is 10.0° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), about twice the Moon's furthest reach. Between Mirach, from Andromeda, at 0°46′ Taurus and [Menkar](/astrology/menkar/), from Cetus, at 14°41′, it is one of several stars from other constellations in the first half of the sign.

Hamal sits in Taurus because of the [precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/). Between about 2000 and 100 BCE, the Sun stood in the constellation of the Ram at the March equinox, which is why the zodiac, and every horoscope column, begins with [Aries](/astrology/aries/). The tropical zodiac stays tied to that equinox; the equinox has slipped back into Pisces since, and on the zodiac's scale the Ram's own stars have moved on into Taurus ([constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/)).

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

Arabic astronomers called the whole constellation al-ħamal, the ram, and this star rās al-ħamal, its head, which became Hamal. Ptolemy left it out of the figure. His Almagest reports that Hipparchus put it at the ram's muzzle, but lists it among the unformed stars as "the star over the head", and John Flamsteed's atlas later drew it there.

Another Arabic name, El Nath or Alnath, the butting, as of horns, once belonged to Hamal too, and Geoffrey Chaucer used it so. It has since passed to Beta Tauri, the tip of the Bull's horn, which this site calls [El Nath](/astrology/el-nath/). In Chinese astronomy Hamal is the third star of the Bond, one of the 28 lunar lodges ([Chinese astrology](/astrology/chinese-astrology/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The one contact the tradition recognises is a conjunction: a planet or angle within about a degree of Hamal's longitude, so 7°01′ to 9°01′ Taurus for a birth in 2026, or 5°40′ to 7°40′ for one in 1930 ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). Birthdays on about 27 and 28 April put the Sun in that span: a [Sun in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-taurus/) sitting on the Ram's brightest star. The sign and the star tell two different stories, and the sign is the one a chart is built on.

Saturn stood on Hamal's degree for births in May 1940, in parts of 1969–70 and in spring 1999. For the Moon, which takes a few hours to pass, and for the angles, which take minutes, only the birth time can say ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

I keep stars out of the readings I give ([How I read](/how-i-read/)), so Hamal stays a description here: the Ram's own star, living in Taurus on a tropical chart.
