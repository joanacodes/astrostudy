Some pairs make plans without trying. Venus sextile Mars in synastry is often behind that: one person's Venus sits about 60° from the other's Mars, so what the first would enjoy and what the second is ready to do fit together like two halves of an invitation, offered and accepted.

## What flows {#what-flows}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) weighs and chooses: what someone likes, what they find worth having, how they keep an exchange fair. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) initiates: effort, courage, getting things moving. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) pairs them through opportunity and cooperation, and in the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) the cooperation runs between two people, each bringing half.

With the Venus on your side, your suggestions get taken up without fuss. You mention a place, a project, an idea, and the other person turns it into a plan, at a pace that leaves you room to change your mind.

With the Mars on your side, your initiative is welcome. What you start is received with pleasure rather than critique, and the appreciation makes the next effort lighter. You may notice that you try more things with this person around.

Two signs usually separate them, in elements that get along. One person's Venus in Pisces with the other's Mars in Taurus: a tender, imaginative taste meets a steady, patient drive, and the Mars person makes the Venus person's dreamier plans real, without rushing them. Friends with this sextile book the trip and enjoy it; colleagues split the work naturally, one knowing what good looks like, the other getting it done; adult siblings help each other move house and stay for dinner.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

A sextile is potential that needs someone to act on it. Each person can wait for the other to make the first move, or believe a hint was enough, and the pleasant possibility stays a possibility. With an orb of only 3.6°, the contact is also easy to overrate: it is a friendly fit, not a strong pull.

Cooperation has its own blind spot. Because plans come easily, real disagreements about what each wants can be smoothed over rather than said, and harmony is not the same as avoiding conflict. Roles can also set too early: one person always suggests, the other always organises, when either could do both.

Other angles give taste and drive a different arrangement:

- **[Venus conjunct Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-conjunct-mars-synastry/):** no gap at all; interest becomes action before anyone has asked.
- **[Venus square Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-square-mars-synastry/):** a sign further, and terms and timing have to be argued out.
- **[Venus trine Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-trine-mars-synastry/):** in one shared element, where cooperation happens unasked.
- **[Venus opposite Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-opposite-mars-synastry/):** straight across the circle, each person holding the end the other lacks.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Invite rather than hint. The Venus person can say plainly what they would enjoy; the Mars person can propose rather than wait for permission; either can swap roles now and then, so that both get to choose and both get to start. Disagree out loud when you do, because this contact makes it easy to skip.

About 1 pair of people in 13 has this sextile, one way round or the other, within 3.6°. The orb is narrow, but Venus covers only about a degree a day and Mars less, so the hour of birth decides it only right at the edge; it always decides the houses, which need both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

A person whose own chart has [Venus sextile Mars](/astrology/venus-sextile-mars/) has a knack for the welcome first move. Between two people, the knack is split in half, and works only when both halves show up.
