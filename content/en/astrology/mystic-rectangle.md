Its name promises more mystery than its geometry holds. A mystic rectangle is two oppositions laid side by side and tied together with soft aspects, so that each pull across the chart has help along the sides.

## What it looks like {#what-it-looks-like}

Picture two [oppositions](/astrology/opposition/) about 60° apart, like two diameters of the circle drawn close together, and join their ends. The short sides of the rectangle are [sextiles](/astrology/sextile/), about 60°; the long sides are [trines](/astrology/trine/), about 120°; the diagonals are the two oppositions. That's six aspects among four planets, and every planet takes part in three of them: one opposition, one trine, one sextile.

The signs follow neatly. Every aspect in the figure joins signs an even number apart, two, four or six, and such signs always share a [polarity](/astrology/polarity/). So a mystic rectangle usually sits in four signs of one polarity, fire and air or earth and water. Planets in Aries, Gemini, Libra and Sagittarius make one: Aries opposite Libra and Gemini opposite Sagittarius, Aries sextile Gemini and Libra sextile Sagittarius, Gemini trine Libra and Sagittarius trine Aries.

The [kite](/astrology/kite/) has one opposition hung from a grand trine; the rectangle trades the triangle for a second opposition. Add two more planets in the right places and the rectangle becomes part of a [grand sextile](/astrology/grand-sextile/), the six-pointed figure that holds three such rectangles inside it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

About 1 person in 28 born from 1930 to 2009 has a mystic rectangle among the ten planets, in the count behind this site, counting each aspect within 8° when the Sun or Moon is involved and 6° otherwise. Four planets have to land in just the right places, so the figure is sensitive to orbs: a little more leeway finds many loose rectangles, a little less keeps only the tight ones. The count can't see a rectangle completed by an angle, Chiron, Lilith or a node.

The system I built for my readings describes the opposition as polarity, two functions made aware of each other so they can be integrated rather than fought; the trine as flow, and the sextile as an opening that has to be taken. I read a mystic rectangle as two tensions held in a frame. Each opposition pulls on its own, and the soft sides give each end an easy line to an end of the other. In a life, that can look like someone able to hold two big questions at once without coming apart. The frame has its own risk: support can keep the tension circulating instead of settling it, because the soft sides make it easy to slide from one pull to the other without ever answering either. A single figure doesn't decide the chart, though. I read its four planets in their signs first, and the rectangle as the way they talk.

Two oppositions usually mean two axes of houses, four areas of life in two pairs, and only the time of birth places them. A rectangle with the Moon at a corner can come and go with the hour. That's why I [wait for the birth time before reading](/blog/unknown-birth-time/): the rectangle's lines without its houses would be a drawing, not a chart.

## What helps {#what-helps}

- **Work the oppositions, rest on the sides.** The two diagonals carry the questions. The trines and sextiles are where you recover between them.
- **Use each soft side for what it does.** A trine lets something flow from one end to the next; a sextile offers an opening you have to step through.
- **Name the two questions.** Saying plainly what each opposition is about stops the figure from becoming a vague, constant hum.
- **Leave the name alone.** Nothing mystical is required. Four planets and six aspects are enough to read.

Two tensions with help built in around them make a good arrangement to be born with. The work is to use the help to answer the tensions, not only to carry them more comfortably.
