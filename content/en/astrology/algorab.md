Algorab is the crow's star, in Corvus, the small four-sided constellation that perches on the back of Hydra, the water snake. At magnitude 2.95 it isn't even the brightest star of its own constellation, and in 2026 it occupies the fourteenth degree of Libra, at 13°48′.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

About 87 light-years separate us from δ Corvi. The Crow's brightest star is Gienah, at magnitude 2.59 to Algorab's 2.95, and the two, with Epsilon and Beta Corvi, make the Crow's distinctive quadrilateral, which can be seen from anywhere south of 65° north. In 1823 James South and John Herschel found Algorab to be a wide double, with a faint companion that hasn't moved against it since. The star itself is a puzzle: brighter than its temperature allows for a settled star of its kind, it is either a young star of about three million years, still contracting, or one of some 260 million years that has begun to age.

Mid-[Libra](/astrology/libra/) has been its place since well before 1930, when it stood at 12°28′, and will be for a good while yet: 13°48′ in 2026, and still only 14°09′ by 2050 ([precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/)). Along the zodiac it falls between [Alkaid](/astrology/alkaid/), at 27° Virgo, and [Spica](/astrology/spica/), at 24° Libra. Ten degrees further from the Sun's path than Spica, at 12.2° south of the ecliptic, the crow is never touched by the Moon, which now and then passes in front of Spica.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

Algorab comes from the Arabic al-ghurāb, the crow; the constellation is a raven in Latin, Corvus, one of the 48 that Ptolemy listed. Greek myth tells two stories of it, both about Apollo. In one, a white bird brought him news of his lover Coronis's betrayal, and he turned its feathers black. In the other, a crow sent to fetch water dawdled over figs, lied about the delay, and was flung into the sky beside the Cup and the water snake, forever thirsty, the Cup just out of reach. Long before the Greeks, Babylonian star lists had a raven in the same place, sacred to Adad, god of rain and storm.

Other skies made something else of these stars. In China they are a chariot, Zhen, the last of the twenty-eight lunar mansions; in India the main stars of Corvus are a hand, Hasta, the thirteenth nakshatra ([The 28 lunar mansions](/astrology/28-lunar-mansions/)). To medieval magic, the crow's star was one of fifteen Behenian stars.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

A birthday around 6 October puts the Sun on the crow's degree, a [Sun in Libra](/astrology/sun-in-libra/) at mid-sign, and a planet between about 12°48′ and 14°48′ Libra stands on it just the same ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)).

Algorab's meanings come from myths and lists, a thirsty bird and a place among the fifteen Behenian stars, rather than from anything observed in charts, and since my own readings leave the stars aside, I describe the crow's star here and stop there ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).
