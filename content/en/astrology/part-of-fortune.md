The Part of Fortune isn't a planet, or anything you could see in the sky. It's a point worked out from three others, your [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), your [Moon](/astrology/moon/) and your [Sun](/astrology/sun/), and it's the best known of the lots.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A lot is a point you calculate rather than observe: you take the distance between two parts of a chart and measure it again from a third, usually the Ascendant. Ancient astrologers worked with dozens of them, for marriage, children, travel and much else, and the [doctrine of the lots](/astrology/doctrine-of-the-lots/) is how they were built and read. You'll also meet them as *Arabic parts*, after the medieval astrologers writing in Arabic who used them widely, but they're older than that: Dorotheus of Sidon and Vettius Valens were already working with them. *Lot of Fortune* comes from translations of the Greek texts, *Part of Fortune* from the Latin.

The fortune in its name is the old one: what befalls you, your lot in life, not a win at the races. The Part of Fortune is the Moon's lot, and tradition read it for what the Moon stands for in a chart: the body, and the circumstances you find yourself in. Its pair, the [Part of Spirit](/astrology/part-of-spirit/), is the Sun's lot, read for the mind and what you set out to do. One describes what comes to you; the other, what you go after. Together they're two of a set of seven, the [Hermetic lots](/astrology/hermetic-lots/), one for each of the traditional planets.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

By day, the Part of Fortune is the Ascendant plus the Moon minus the Sun. In plain words: see how far ahead of the Sun the Moon is, counting in the order of the signs, then count the same distance on from the Ascendant. If the Moon is three signs ahead of the Sun, Fortune falls three signs on from your Ascendant.

So Fortune carries the Moon's phase over to your Ascendant. Born at a [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/), with the Sun and Moon together, your Fortune sits close to your Ascendant; born at a [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/), with the two facing each other, close to your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/).

By night, many astrologers reverse it: the Ascendant plus the Sun minus the Moon, as far from the Ascendant as the Sun is from the Moon. The reason given is [sect](/astrology/sect-day-and-night-charts/). A chart is a day chart when the Sun was above the horizon at birth, in the top half of the wheel, and a night chart when it was below. By day the Sun is the light in charge, so the count runs from the Sun to the Moon; by night the Moon is, so it runs from the Moon to the Sun.

Not everyone reverses it, and software doesn't all do the same, so two programs can give you two different Parts of Fortune. Look for one of these:

- **The night formula.** For a night birth, a program that reverses and one that doesn't put Fortune at the same distance from your Ascendant, one on each side of it. If your two Fortunes mirror each other around the Ascendant, that's why. A program that never reverses is showing you, for a night birth, the point that astrologers who do reverse would call Spirit.
- **The birth time.** Fortune moves with the Ascendant, round the whole zodiac in about a day, so a few minutes either way move it, and a time rounded to the hour can put it in another sign. [Without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides), there is no Part of Fortune to read.
- **Sunrise and sunset.** If you were born with the Sun right on the horizon, a few minutes decide whether the chart counts as day or night, and so which formula applies.
- **The houses.** Its degree is the same in every house system; the house it falls in isn't.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Astrologers who work with the lots read the Part of Fortune much as they would a planet, starting with three things:

- **Its sign and house:** the area of life where circumstances gather, where things tend to arrive rather than be chased.
- **The planet that rules its sign:** where that planet sits, and how comfortably, is read as how well those circumstances are supported.
- **Planets close to it:** a planet on Fortune lends it its own colour.

Some go further and count the houses again from Fortune, as if it were a second Ascendant, and Vettius Valens timed the chapters of a life from it, with a technique now called [zodiacal releasing](/astrology/zodiacal-releasing/).

Tradition tied Fortune to the body and to the material side of life, which makes it easy to turn it into a health point or a money point. Neither is something I read: no chart can diagnose an illness or pick an investment, both are on my list of [what I won't read](/how-i-read/), and [Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/) and [Can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/) say why. Read in an adult's life, Fortune is closer to a question: what keeps arriving for you, without your choosing it, and what do you make of it?

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Part of Fortune shows where your money will come from.”** *Fortune* here means what befalls you. Material circumstances are part of what tradition read in it, but no point in a chart promises money, or warns that you'll lose it.
- **“It's an Arabic invention.”** The name *Arabic parts* comes from the medieval astrologers who used lots widely; Dorotheus of Sidon and Vettius Valens were working with the Lot of Fortune centuries earlier.
- **“Every program calculates it the same way.”** Some reverse the formula for night births and some don't. Check how yours does it before you read anything into it.
- **“Your birth date is enough.”** Fortune moves with the Ascendant, round the zodiac in about a day: without a birth time, it could be almost anywhere.
