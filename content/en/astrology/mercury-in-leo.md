Mercury in Leo thinks in scenes. An idea arrives with a beginning, a middle and a moment where everyone leans in, and you would rather say one thing with your whole heart than five things politely.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), the mind that learns and says what it thinks, takes on in [Leo](/astrology/leo/) the warmth and steadiness of fixed fire ruled by the Sun, and the wish to be seen doing things well. Here Mercury puts its heart into its ideas: you think by giving things a shape, explain by bringing them to life, and tend to stay loyal to a view once you've lent it your voice. Fixed fire is slow to go out. That makes for conviction, and for a certain reluctance to back down in public.

The classical tables give Leo to the Sun and give Mercury nothing there, high or low: what the sign adds is manner, not strength.

Leo's ruler is the [Sun](/astrology/sun/), and Mercury is never more than about 28° from it, so this Mercury always has its ruler close by: in Leo as well, or one sign away. I find that a useful image. Your thinking tends to serve what you're living for, and your words and your sense of purpose rarely drift far apart. With the Sun in Leo too, the two may sit together, and [Sun conjunct Mercury](/astrology/sun-conjunct-mercury/) describes that closeness. With the Sun in Cancer, a private, protective self speaks out warmly through this Mercury; with the Sun in Virgo, a modest, exacting self has a mind that dares to be heard.

In most years Mercury takes about 17 days to cross Leo; a retrograde turn in the sign can hold it there for up to 70, long enough for a good story to be told twice. In any crowd born between 1930 and 2009, about 1 person in 13 would share it. A few degrees further back and you'd have [Mercury in Cancer](/astrology/mercury-in-cancer/), the same story kept rather than told; a few degrees on, [Mercury in Virgo](/astrology/mercury-in-virgo/) would be editing it.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You're generous with words. You tell people what's good about them, out loud, and remember to say it again. You want to be heard to the end, and a partner who checks their phone halfway through your story can hurt more than they realise. Disagreements feel personal because your ideas are personal; a correction in front of others stings far more than the same words in private. Love letters, toasts and declarations come naturally to you. So does loyalty: once you've spoken up for someone, you keep speaking up.

## At work {#at-work}

Presenting, teaching, leading a meeting, pitching, performing, writing in a voice people recognise, creative direction: work where ideas have to be carried by a person. You explain through stories and examples, and people remember what you told them. You need your ideas credited, less from vanity than because you put yourself into them. Give this Mercury a stage, even a small one, and it often lifts everyone who shares it.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

A view, once announced, can become a matter of pride. Changing your mind in public feels like losing face, so you may go on defending a position you've privately stopped believing. Feedback on an idea can sound like feedback on you. And when you're the one telling the story, it's easy to leave less room for the other person's version of it.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Separate the idea from yourself: *this draft* is weak, you aren't. Practise saying *I've changed my mind* as if it were good news, because it usually is. Ask for feedback privately before the big presentation, so the public moment is already the second draft. Hand the floor to someone else now and then, and enjoy their story as much as you'd want them to enjoy yours. Mercury earned its tick in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) about one time in three, so a good story about your mind is still a story: I'd rather check it with you than tell it for you.
