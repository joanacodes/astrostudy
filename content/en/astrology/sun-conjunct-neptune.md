Sun conjunct Neptune means you were born in the few days of the year when the Sun passes Neptune, with Neptune hidden behind it. It describes a sense of self with soft edges: open to other people's feelings, drawn to something larger than one life, and learning to keep a centre while staying open.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is the part of a chart that tells you who you are: it gives your identity an outline and your life a direction. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) is the part that imagines, and that dissolves the outlines that aren't needed: compassion, inspiration, a feeling for what can't be measured. In a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) the two act as one while each keeps its identity, so imagination isn't somewhere you visit. It's part of the self you organise around.

In plain words, it can be hard to say who you are without saying what you love, believe in or dream of. You take in the mood of a room; you see what someone could become before they can. That openness is a gift, and it asks for one skill above the others: keeping your own outline while you let things in. Fusion isn't identity loss, and compassion isn't self-erasure. I read this conjunction at its best when the dream has a form, a craft, a practice or a way of caring, that gives it edges.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The two usually share a sign, and since Neptune stays about fourteen years in each, that sign belongs to your generation; what's yours is the Sun standing in it with Neptune. With both in Capricorn, the self you grow into is bound up with structures you hope will serve people. The house shows where the conjunction lives, and it needs a birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)): in the 10th, your public direction carries an ideal that others project onto; in the 7th, you find yourself through partners you see the best in.

About 1 person in 22 has the Sun within 8° of Neptune at birth. Neptune moves only about two degrees a year, so the Sun meets it on nearly the same dates for years on end, about two days later each year, and the two stay in orb for some sixteen days. The closer they are, the more the self and the dream speak with one voice.

After the meeting, the Sun works its way round Neptune through the year, and each step is one of the pair's other aspects:

- **[Sun sextile Neptune](/astrology/sun-sextile-neptune/):** about two months on; imagination becomes an offer you can take up rather than part of your outline.
- **[Sun square Neptune](/astrology/sun-square-neptune/):** about three months on; the dream and the self rub, and the work is giving the ideal a shape.
- **[Sun trine Neptune](/astrology/sun-trine-neptune/):** about four months on; the two flow together, easily enough to go unnoticed.
- **[Sun opposite Neptune](/astrology/sun-opposite-neptune/):** half a year on; the boundless faces you across the chart, often in someone else.

Between two charts, [Sun conjunct Neptune in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-neptune-synastry/) puts one person's sense of self on the other's imagination: tender, inspiring and quick to idealise. I read it both ways, because the one who is dreamed about and the one who dreams feel it differently.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Neptune conjunct natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-neptune-conjunct-natal-sun/) is one of the slowest transits a Sun can have. Neptune takes about 165 years to go round, so it reaches any one Sun at most once in a lifetime, and stays near it for a year or two, crossing and recrossing as it turns retrograde and direct again.

Born with the conjunction, you meet a cousin of it at about 41. That's when the [Neptune square](/astrology/neptune-square/) brings Neptune a quarter of the way round from its place at your birth, and since your Sun stands there too, it is also [transiting Neptune square natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-neptune-square-natal-sun/). Every year, too, around your birthday, the Sun in the sky crosses your natal Neptune: a reminder of the angle you were born under rather than an event. Weather, not a forecast: these describe seasons when outlines soften and something more imaginative asks for room.
