Some pairs have never done anything because *that's what people do*. Composite Mercury in Aquarius gives a relationship a mind of its own in the plainest sense: it works things out from first principles, and it would rather be right than usual.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) builds every planet from the midpoint of its two natal positions; composite [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is the relationship's way of thinking and talking. [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/), fixed air, makes that thinking independent and systematic. About 1 pair in 12 has it.

This bond questions habits, including its own. Who cooks, how money gets discussed, how often you see each other's families: each is reasoned out rather than inherited, and the answer may look nothing like anyone else's. Its conversations range over the future, technology, the group, the cause, and it often thinks best in company: three or four friends round a table, a group chat that never sleeps. Whatever else the two of you are, you tend to talk like old friends. Two conventional people can share this placement and find that together they redesign the rules. If one of you was born with [Mercury in Aquarius](/astrology/mercury-in-aquarius/), that's your own inventive mind; the composite is the pair's.

Aquarius has two rulers: [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) in the tradition, [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) in modern tropical astrology, and in a birth chart I read both ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)). In a composite they work at different scales. Composite Saturn shows where the relationship builds its structures and keeps its commitments. Uranus spends about seven years in a sign, so for two people close in age its composite sign says more about the years you were born in than about the two of you.

Systems are this Mercury's language, so here is the system behind its strangest feature. In the sky Mercury keeps within about 28° of the Sun. In the site's count of two million pairs, composites break that rule about once in 18, and about once in 26 they put the composite Sun within 8° of the point opposite composite Mercury, which for this sign means around Leo. The rest of the time, the composite Sun sits in Capricorn, Aquarius or Pisces.

The difficulty is the principle that crowds out the person. A relationship that reasons so well can discuss a hurt as a question of fairness and never quite feel it, or hold to a position because it was well argued once. Objectivity isn't detachment, and independence isn't distance, but both need a warm counterweight. Asking *how does that feel?* alongside *is that right?* helps, and so does treating a change as an experiment with an end date, so that nobody has to win the argument for good.

[Composite Mercury in Capricorn](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-capricorn/) wants to know who does what by when; [composite Mercury in Pisces](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-pisces/) understands without needing it said. Aquarius wants to know why it should be done that way at all.

Composites are among the techniques I describe rather than use: astrologers who work with them read them beside both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). Each of you is also an independent party to any chart of the two of you, which means both agree, or it isn't drawn ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
