Fairness is this relationship's rulebook: who cooked last time, whose family gets the holidays this year, whose turn it is to choose. With composite Saturn in Libra, a bond holds together by keeping its agreements even.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Composite Saturn is where a relationship draws its lines: its agreements, its duties, the standards it holds itself to. In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) in [Libra](/astrology/libra/), cardinal air ruled by Venus, makes those lines a matter of balance and reciprocity. The pair commits by keeping things fair. Chores are shared out, big decisions are taken jointly, and an unwritten contract of equals runs underneath the whole arrangement, one that both parties are expected to honour.

In Ptolemy's scheme, Libra is Saturn's [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/), the sign facing its fall in Aries, and the planet of rules sits comfortably where fairness is the subject. A relationship shows why. Its structure is strongest where both people wrote the terms, and weakest wherever one of them simply inherited them. Exaltation describes a good fit, though, not a promise: the bond still has to sit down and negotiate.

Oddly, the composite puts Saturn in Libra more often than birth does: about 1 pair in 14 in a count of two million random pairs, against about 1 person in 16 for Saturn in Libra at birth, all among people born from 1930 to 2009. Pairs of the same age carry it if both were born between November 1950 and October 1953, or between September 1980 and August 1983, give or take the months when Saturn crossed back and forth at the edges. Age gaps produce it in a stranger way. Two people born about fifteen years apart have Saturns almost opposite each other, and two points that far apart have two [midpoints](/astrology/midpoints/), one in each half of the zodiac; the composite takes the nearer. A Saturn at about 21° Cancer (January 1946) and one at about 17° Capricorn (June 1960) give a composite Saturn near 19° Libra, while about 12° Cancer (June 1945) with that same Capricorn Saturn gives one near 14° Aries. A few degrees send the relationship's Saturn to the opposite sign. That's the method's arithmetic, not a message, and it's also why the bond's sense of fairness can belong to neither person's birth chart.

For one person, [Saturn in Libra](/astrology/saturn-in-libra/) means treating commitment as a contract, even an unwritten one; in a composite, the contract is the relationship itself, and each of you may feel bound by terms neither remembers proposing. Put its neighbours on the scales and each weighs something different: [composite Saturn in Virgo](/astrology/composite-saturn-in-virgo/) weighs whether things work, [composite Saturn in Scorpio](/astrology/composite-saturn-in-scorpio/) whether trust has been earned, and Libra whether the terms are even.

What rubs is score-keeping. A bond that counts fairness carefully can start counting everything, and a disagreement can feel like a breach of contract rather than two people wanting different things. Negotiating every choice also costs energy, and some pairs with this Saturn end up with excellent procedures and very little spontaneity. It helps to measure fairness over a season rather than an evening, to rewrite the agreements as your lives change, and to let some things go unbalanced on purpose, as gifts.

Other astrologers draw composites; I don't. What you're reading is the method as its practitioners use it, next to both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and none of it says how long two people will last. Like every reading of two people, it rests on an agreement of its own: both of you know, and both said yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
