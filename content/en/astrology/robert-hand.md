Robert Hand began with handbooks for the astrology of his own day and went on to put much older astrology back into English. His shelf runs from *Planets in Transit* to Masha'allah and Paulus Alexandrinus.

## Who {#who}

He is an American astrologer, historian and writer. His early books came in quick succession: *Planets in Composite: Analyzing Human Relationships* (1975), *Planets in Transit: Life Cycles for Living* (1976), *Planets in Youth: Patterns of Early Development* (1977), *Horoscope Symbols* (1981) and *Essays on Astrology* (1982). Early in his career he also started a company that developed computer programs for astrologers. Some of his later books and translations are credited to Rob Hand.

From the 1990s his name sits on a long row of old texts. For the Golden Hind Press he edited Robert Schmidt's translations of Paulus Alexandrinus's *Introductory Matters* (second edition, 1993) and of the third book of Vettius Valens's *Anthology* (1994). Under the ARHAT imprint, the Archive for the Retrieval of Historical Astrological Texts, came his *Night and Day: Planetary Sect in Astrology* (1995), his translation of Masha'allah's *On Reception* (1998), *Whole Sign Houses: The Oldest House System* (2000), and, with Hand as editor, Dorian Gieseler Greenbaum's translation of *Late Classical Astrology: Paulus Alexandrinus and Olympiodorus* (2001).

He has kept speaking to working astrologers as well: in 2005 he gave a talk at the Astrological Association's conference in York called “Towards a Post-Modern Astrology”, and he wrote the foreword to the 2011 edition of [Liz Greene](/astrology/liz-greene/)'s *Saturn*. The Faculty of Astrological Studies in London lists him among its patrons.

## What they changed {#what-they-changed}

He wrote reference books that take techniques one contact at a time. *Planets in Transit* describes each moving planet as it reaches each part of a chart ([Transits](/astrology/transits/)). I read transits as weather, not a forecast: a season to recognise, never an event announced. *Planets in Composite* did the same for the [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), a relationship's chart made from the halfway points between two people's planets, which I describe but don't draw. *Planets in Youth* turned to children's charts, which this site leaves alone: I read adults only ([Children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)).

He helped bring the old authors back. The translations he edited for Project Hindsight and published through ARHAT belong to [the traditional revival](/astrology/traditional-revival/), which put Hellenistic and medieval texts back into English and techniques such as sect back into use ([Sect: day and night charts](/astrology/sect-day-and-night-charts/)). Valens, whose third book he edited, has a page of his own ([Vettius Valens](/astrology/vettius-valens/)). *Night and Day* (1995) took up sect, the old distinction between charts born by day and by night, and his translation of [Masha'allah](/astrology/mashaallah/)'s *On Reception* put into English a medieval treatise on reception, the host-and-guest relation between a planet and the ruler of the sign it stands in.

He has also said plainly where the tradition can't explain itself. In *Horoscope Symbols* he wrote that some of its choices remain anomalies almost impossible to explain with any consistency, such as the exaltation of vigorous Mars in cold Capricorn; the Babylonians' reasons for the exaltations are not known ([Exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/)).

He made the case for the oldest way of counting houses. *Whole Sign Houses* argues that one whole sign to each house was the system of the ancient astrologers. I use Placidus, in which the Ascendant and the Midheaven open the 1st and 10th houses. Both systems need the birth time, Whole Sign to find the rising sign and Placidus for every cusp ([Whole Sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/); [What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

A decade after *Planets in Transit*, [Demetra George](/astrology/demetra-george/) and Douglas Bloch's *Asteroid Goddesses* (1986) helped bring the asteroids into the charts astrologers read.
