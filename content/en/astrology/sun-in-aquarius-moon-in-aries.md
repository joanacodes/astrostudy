Your Sun stands where the old books say it is least at home, in Aquarius, and your Moon where they say the Sun is most honoured, in Aries. A thin crescent hung in the western sky after sunset when you were born. I read the pair as a mind that works for the long term and a heart that answers at once.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Aquarius is fixed air and Aries cardinal fire, two signs on: a [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) by sign, between elements that feed each other. The [Sun in Aquarius](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius/) builds its identity around ideas, the people it is connected to and a future it can already picture; the [Moon in Aries](/astrology/moon-in-aries/) steadies itself by doing something, and a feeling that has found an action settles. Air gives fire room and fire gives air a reason to move: you think about how things could be better, and your feelings get you out of the chair to start.

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) rules Aries, so it speaks for your Moon. Aquarius answers to two planets, and I read both: [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), its modern ruler, shows where your Sun experiments and turns down the usual way of doing things, and [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), its older ruler, where it builds something meant to outlast the first enthusiasm. Saturn has its [fall](/astrology/fall/) in Aries, so the planet that steadies your sense of self is least at ease in the sign of your feelings, and I read Saturn and Mars side by side: one works in years, the other in minutes. Aquarius is the Sun's [detriment](/astrology/detriment/) and Aries its [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/); the Moon itself has no dignity in Aries.

The Moon had moved 31° to 89° past the Sun, which puts your birth two and a half to seven days after a new moon. In the eight-phase count, about seven births in eight with this pair fall in the [waxing crescent](/astrology/waxing-crescent/) and the rest, the earliest, in the [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/) phase. Near 60° the lights make a waxing [Sun sextile Moon](/astrology/sun-sextile-moon/). I read the crescent as the month's first push away from the Sun, and an Aries Moon is made for pushing off; [your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) shows where the line between the two phases falls.

About 1 person in 147 has the Sun in its detriment and the Moon in the Sun's exaltation like this. Aries holds the Moon for two and a half days or so, between Pisces and Taurus, and a birthday on which it crossed either border needs the hour to choose between this page, the [Sun in Aquarius, Moon in Pisces](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius-moon-in-pisces/) and the [Sun in Aquarius, Moon in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius-moon-in-taurus/) ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). The same hour gives the rising sign and the house Mars works in, which is one reason I don't read a chart without it.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You tend to love as a friend first, and you say what you feel on the spot. The Aquarius Sun wants an equal who respects your freedom and your opinions; the Aries Moon wants honesty today, not a careful talk next week. A feeling flares and is done with for you in minutes, while a partner may still be carrying it an hour later; your principles, which never flared, stay put for years. Telling the two apart out loud helps the people close to you know which one they're meeting.

## At work {#at-work}

You do well where a new idea needs someone to start it: launching a project, testing a prototype, getting a campaign off the ground. The Sun sees the improvement before most people do, and the Moon can't sit still once it has seen it. Long committees and approvals that take months cost you more than the work itself, and a team that lets you move first and review afterwards suits you better than one that reviews first and never moves.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Your feelings run ahead of your plans. The Aries Moon reacts before the Aquarius Sun has finished thinking, and then the Sun may defend the reaction as if it were a principle. Or the reverse: you argue calmly for a cause and miss the hurt that started it. Give the first surge somewhere physical to go, then ask which of the two is speaking, the feeling of the moment or the conviction of years. Both deserve a hearing, each at its own speed.
