Your Sun stands in Aquarius, the sign the old books thought least suited to it, and your Moon in Aries, the sign they thought best for the Sun. On the evening of your birth a young crescent was setting in the west, a few hours behind the Sun. To me the pair is a mind working on the long term with a heart that answers at once.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

Aquarius is fixed air and Aries cardinal fire, two signs on: a [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) by sign, between elements that feed each other. The [Sun in Aquarius](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius/) builds its identity around ideas, the people it is connected to and a future it can already picture; the [Moon in Aries](/astrology/moon-in-aries/) calms down by doing something, and a feeling that has found an action settles. Air gives fire room and fire gives air a reason to move: you think about how things could be better, and your feelings get you out of the chair to start.

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) rules Aries and so has the last word on your Moon. For the Sun's sign there are two rulers to weigh, and I read both: [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), given to Aquarius by modern astrologers, shows where your Sun experiments and refuses the customary route; [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), its ruler in the older books, where it builds something meant to outlast the first enthusiasm. Aries is the sign of Saturn's [fall](/astrology/fall/), so the planet that steadies your sense of self is at its least comfortable in the sign of your feelings, and I read Saturn and Mars side by side: one works in years, the other in minutes. Aquarius is the Sun's [detriment](/astrology/detriment/) and Aries its [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/); the Moon itself has no dignity in Aries.

Your Moon was 31° to 89° clear of the Sun, between two and a half and seven days into the lunar month. Most of these births, about seven in eight, are [waxing crescent](/astrology/waxing-crescent/) births; the earliest few still belong to the [new moon](/astrology/new-moon/) phase, and around 60° the chart has a waxing [Sun sextile Moon](/astrology/sun-sextile-moon/). I read the crescent as the month's first push away from the Sun, and an Aries Moon is made for pushing off; [your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) explains how the eight phases are counted.

This combination comes up for about 1 person in 147. Aries keeps the Moon for some sixty hours, between Pisces and Taurus, so if it entered or left the sign on the day you were born, the hour chooses between this page, the [Sun in Aquarius, Moon in Pisces](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius-moon-in-pisces/) and the [Sun in Aquarius, Moon in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius-moon-in-taurus/) ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). That hour also places your rising sign and the house Mars works in, which is why a chart without a time is one I won't read.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

You tend to love as a friend first, and you say what you feel on the spot. The Aquarius Sun wants an equal who respects your freedom and your opinions; the Aries Moon wants honesty today, not a careful talk next week. A feeling flares and is done with for you in minutes, while a partner may still be carrying it an hour later; your principles, which never flared, stay put for years. Telling the two apart out loud helps the people close to you know which one they're meeting.

## At work {#at-work}

You do well where a new idea needs someone to start it: launching a project, testing a prototype, getting a campaign off the ground. The Sun sees the improvement before most people do, and the Moon can't sit still once it has seen it. Long committees and approvals that take months cost you more than the work itself, and a team that lets you move first and review afterwards suits you better than one that reviews first and never moves.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Your feelings run ahead of your plans. The Aries Moon reacts before the Aquarius Sun has finished thinking, and then the Sun may defend the reaction as if it were a principle. Or the reverse: you argue calmly for a cause and miss the hurt that started it. Give the first surge somewhere physical to go, then ask yourself whether this is the feeling of the moment or the conviction of years. Both deserve a hearing, each at its own speed.
