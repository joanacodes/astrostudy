If two apps give you two different Lilith signs, neither has necessarily made a mistake. In astrology, *Lilith* is a name shared by several different points, and programs don't all show the same one. Here they are side by side, with how to tell which one your chart is showing, and the one I read.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Three of the Liliths are one idea worked out three ways. The [Moon](/astrology/moon/)'s orbit is an ellipse, and once a month the Moon passes its farthest point from Earth, the apogee. That point slowly turns around the zodiac, and astrologers call it Lilith. The three differ in how they follow it:

- **Mean Lilith**, the [Black Moon Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/) most astrologers read: the apogee's average position, with the wobbles smoothed out. It moves forward at a steady pace, about nine months in each sign, and goes round the zodiac in about 8 years and 10 months. It's the one I use.
- **[True Lilith](/astrology/true-lilith/)**, also called osculating Lilith: the far point of the Moon's orbit as it is at that exact moment, taken from the ellipse the Moon would follow from that instant if only the Earth pulled on it. *Osculating* comes from the Latin for kissing: that ellipse just touches the Moon's real path. But the Sun pulls on it too, all the time, so the ellipse keeps changing shape and its far point wobbles widely. It can sit tens of degrees from the mean.
- **[Interpolated Lilith](/astrology/interpolated-lilith/)**: a third calculation, which looks at where the Moon actually is each time it reaches its farthest from Earth, month after month, and draws a smooth line between those positions. It moves without true Lilith's wide swings. Some programs offer it as a third choice.

The other three share the name, or a place beside it, and little else:

- **[Dark Moon Lilith](/astrology/dark-moon-lilith/)**: not a point of the Moon's orbit but a second moon of the Earth, which Waldemath reported seeing and which was never confirmed. Its place in a chart comes from an orbit that exists only on paper.
- **[Asteroid Lilith (1181)](/astrology/asteroid-lilith-1181/)**: a real body in the asteroid belt, numbered 1181, that happens to carry the name. It follows its own orbit, so its sign has nothing to do with the others', and it's read separately.
- **[White Moon Selena](/astrology/white-moon-selena/)**: not a Lilith at all, but a point some astrologers pair with the Black Moon, as its bright counterpart. If a chart shows a White Moon, it isn't telling you anything about your Lilith.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A chart won't always tell you which Lilith it shows unless you look. Two programs can both write *Lilith*, use the same symbol and mean two different points. When your charts disagree, look for one of these:

- **One shows the mean, the other the true.** They can sit tens of degrees apart, and a sign is only 30° wide, so the two can land in different signs and different houses. Compare the lunar nodes, where the same choice, [mean node or true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/), moves things by a degree or two at most: with Lilith, it can change the whole sentence.
- **One shows the asteroid.** Asteroid 1181 can be anywhere in relation to the other two. If one Lilith is nowhere near the others, check whether its label says 1181 or asteroid.
- **The houses differ, not the sign.** Even with the same Lilith, two programs set to different house systems can put it in different houses, and [which house system to use](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/) is a question of its own. The houses also need a birth time. Mean Lilith moves so slowly that without one [its sign stays readable](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-it-doesnt), but its house doesn't.

To find out which one your chart shows:

1. **Read the label.** In the chart's legend or table of positions, look for the word beside Lilith: *mean*, *true* or *osculating*, *interpolated*, *Dark Moon*, *1181*.
2. **Open the settings.** If the chart just says *Lilith*, look in the program's settings or options: that's where the choice is made, when there is one, and where you can see what's chosen.
3. **Compare.** If nothing says, enter the same birth details in a program that shows mean and true Lilith side by side, and see which one your first chart matches.

The symbol alone won't always settle it: ⚸ is used for more than one of them.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read mean Lilith, the one most astrologers read, so when I write about Lilith on this site, in a sign like [Lilith in Aries](/astrology/lilith-in-aries/) or in a house, that's the point I mean. Before you read anything into a Lilith placement, here or anywhere else, check that the chart in front of you shows the Lilith the text was written for. A description of mean Lilith in one sign says nothing about a true Lilith sitting in the next.

It matters more with Lilith than with most points. In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Lilith was one of [the three placements that held best](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/), 75 to 90% of the time, with Saturn and Chiron, and it always gets its own part in my readings. The more weight a placement carries in a reading, the more it costs to read it in the wrong sign.

Two habits help:

- **Keep to one.** Whichever Lilith you choose, use it every time. Switching between them is how one person ends up with three Lilith signs and no idea which to believe.
- **Use the same one for both charts.** When two charts are compared, in [synastry](/synastry/), a mean Lilith on one side and a true Lilith on the other aren't talking about the same thing.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“True Lilith is the real one, so the mean is wrong.”** *True* is the astronomers' word for *at this exact moment*, as in the true node; it isn't a verdict. Both follow the same invisible point, and the mean smooths out the wobble.
- **“If two apps disagree, one of them is broken.”** Both can be right, about two different points. Read the labels before you delete anything.
- **“Black Moon Lilith is a hidden moon.”** The hidden moon is Waldemath's Dark Moon, which nobody ever confirmed. The Black Moon is a point in the Moon's own orbit, not an object.
- **“Asteroid Lilith is the same Lilith, only more precise.”** It's a different thing altogether: a rock in the asteroid belt that shares the name.
