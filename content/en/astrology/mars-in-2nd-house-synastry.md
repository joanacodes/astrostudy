Their Mars in your 2nd house sends another person's drive into what you own, earn and value. Questions you'd normally settle on your own, such as what to buy, what to keep and what your work is worth, start to move faster when they're around.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) acts: it wants, it makes the effort, it defends a boundary. [The 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) holds your resources and your sense of worth: your income and the way you earn it, your belongings, the abilities that could earn you a living, the things you'd protect first. In this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their effort meets the ground you stand on, materially speaking.

They tend to get you moving on practical matters. They push you to ask for the rate your work deserves, to finally repair or sell or build the thing, to put real effort into a skill that could pay its way. Around them your sense of what's yours can firm up: you defend it more readily, and you may feel more entitled to it.

For them, your resources and values give their drive something concrete to work on. They may want to protect what you have, to help you build it, or to test it, the way some people test a fence by leaning on it. Succedent houses gather what the angle before them set going, and the 2nd keeps what the 1st began; its natural sign is [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), slow, patient and attached to what lasts. Tradition counts Taurus among the signs where Mars is in detriment, opposite one of its own, and I read the meeting in the same spirit: quick drive in a house that likes to take its time.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

The friction is usually about pace and ownership. Their urgency can make your caution look like timidity, or their spending habits sit badly with your need to keep something in reserve. You may feel your belongings handled, borrowed or rearranged without much asking, or your worth measured by how hard you push, which is a different thing altogether.

From their side, you can seem immovable. They push and you dig in; they offer to help you earn more, and you hear a criticism of how you earn now. Their help can stop feeling welcome at the very moment they're surest it's needed.

Nothing in this overlay says who will gain or lose money, and no chart can ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)). A price isn't a value, and nobody else's drive, however well meant, sets what you're worth.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Talk about money and belongings before they become a contest. Who pays, who lends, who decides what gets sold: said plainly, these stop being tests of will. In a couple, separate pots beside a shared one can give their push and your caution room each; between friends, a rule about lending can save the friendship; between colleagues, be clear whose budget, tools and ideas are whose; between adult relatives, it may be time for each person to look after their own things.

From their chart's point of view the roles reverse: with your Mars in their 2nd house, it's you urging them to value themselves properly, and you who should check you're not pushing at what isn't yours. Before it, [their Mars in your 1st house](/astrology/mars-in-1st-house-synastry/) meets your manner; after it, [their Mars in your 3rd house](/astrology/mars-in-3rd-house-synastry/) sharpens your talk and your everyday errands.

Your birth time decides where your 2nd begins: its cusp shifts with every few minutes on the clock, in Placidus, the house system I use, and a Mars close to that line can sit in your 1st or your 2nd. Mars itself covers roughly half a degree in a day, which means the date of their birth almost always settles the house, unless it lies right on the cusp. Their houses need the time anyway, for the other half of the reading, so I wait for both ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Money and belongings are private matters, and so is this overlay: it gets read with their knowledge or not at all ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#a-chart-is-personal)).
