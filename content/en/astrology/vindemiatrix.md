Vindemiatrix is the grape-gatherer of the constellation Virgo, a giant star whose name came into Latin from Greek. In the zodiac it stands at 10°18′ Libra, one of the Maiden's stars that precession has carried into the sign of the Scales.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Vindemiatrix is Epsilon Virginis, magnitude 2.83, the third brightest star of Virgo. It is a giant of spectral type G, the Sun's broad family, which gives it a yellow light. It lies about 110 light-years away, is 12 times the Sun's size and some 82 times as luminous, and has used up the hydrogen at its core; it is thought to burn helium there now.

Its tropical position was 8°57′ [Libra](/astrology/libra/) for a birth in 1930, 9°56′ in 2000 and 10°18′ in 2026: a star of Virgo in the sign of Libra, like [Spica](/astrology/spica/), the Maiden's brightest, at 24°12′ Libra ([constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/)).

It lies 16.2° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), three times the furthest the Moon can wander, so every body of the chart passes well south of it ([celestial latitude](/astrology/celestial-latitude/)). Between Vindemiatrix and [Denebola](/astrology/denebola/), in the Lion's tail, lies the Virgo Cluster, some 1,500 galaxies, beginning about 5° west of the star.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

The name came from Greek into Latin as vindemiatrix, the grape-harvestress, and also Vindemiator; medieval writers added Provindemiator, Protrygetor and Protrigetrix, Almuredin and Alaraph. In Arabic star lore it was one of five stars of Virgo, with Beta, Gamma, Delta and Eta, called Al-ʿAwwāʾ, the Barker, a group that also named one of the Arabic lunar mansions, the one after Denebola's as-Ṣarfa; three mansions further on come the Two Claws, [Zubeneschamali](/astrology/zubeneschamali/) and Zubenelgenubi ([the 28 lunar mansions](/astrology/28-lunar-mansions/)). Chinese astronomy made it the fourth star of the Left Wall of the Supreme Palace Enclosure, standing for the Second Eastern General; Richard Hinckley Allen wrote the Chinese name as Tsze Tseang, the Second General.

A grape-picker, a barker and a general: three skies, three pictures of the same star.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Within a degree either side of the star, the tradition calls a planet or angle conjunct: 9°18′ to 11°18′ Libra for a birth in 2026, 7°57′ to 9°57′ for one in 1930 ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). The Sun goes through on about 2 and 3 October, a [Sun in Libra](/astrology/sun-in-libra/) about a third of the way through the sign. Saturn stood on it for births in parts of 1951–52 and 1980–81, and the outer planets crossed it in turn: Neptune in parts of 1945–48, Uranus in parts of 1969–71, and [Pluto in Libra](/astrology/pluto-in-libra/) at intervals from late November 1974 to September 1976. The Moon's crossing takes hours, and only a birth time can place it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

With stars kept out of my readings ([How I read](/how-i-read/)), what this page can offer for Vindemiatrix is the record: the year its degree belongs to, the planets that crossed it, and the 16° that separate it in the sky from every one of them.
