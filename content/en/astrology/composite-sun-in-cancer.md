Of the twelve composite Sun signs, Cancer is the one the most pairs have, if only by a whisker. Care sits at the centre of such a relationship, and the bond the two of you make becomes, first of all, somewhere to come back to.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

When the halfway point between your two natal Suns, the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) of [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), lands in [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), it lands in the sign the most pairs share: about 1 pair in 11, in a count of two million random pairs born between 1930 and 2009, against about 1 in 12 for most signs. The reason is in the sky. The Sun takes about 31 days to cross Cancer, a day or two longer than it spends in any winter sign, so more days of the year have their Sun in the summer signs, and the midpoints follow them.

Its identity forms around protecting something: each other, a household, an old circle of friends, a family business, a shared stretch of garden. It recovers its strength somewhere familiar, and it shows itself through attention rather than display: the remembered detail, the meal kept warm, the message sent on the day that was always going to be hard. Its shelter often reaches past the two of you: the friends who come for Sunday lunch for years, the sibling who stays for a month and is never made to feel a guest.

The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) rules Cancer, so the next thing to look at is the composite Moon: its sign and house show where this bond actually goes for comfort. With the [composite Moon in Cancer](/astrology/composite-moon-in-cancer/) too, what the relationship is for and what it needs speak with one voice; elsewhere, the two can ask for different things, and the pair learns to give both. In one person's chart, the [Sun in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-cancer/) describes a self that needs to feel safe to shine. In a composite, the need for safety belongs to the relationship, and two people who have never felt at home anywhere can find that they've made a home of the bond itself.

Protection can turn into a wall, though. A relationship organised around shelter can close around itself, keeping the world outside, and sometimes change with it. Care isn't control, and belonging isn't the same as merging: the bond does best when its shelter has a door. Its neighbours open a bond to the world more readily, the [composite Sun in Gemini](/astrology/composite-sun-in-gemini/) by talking and the [composite Sun in Leo](/astrology/composite-sun-in-leo/) by showing what the two of you make; a Cancer bond keeps the world at the door, and chooses whom to let in.

Nothing here forecasts the bond's lifespan, or how it will fare. I don't use composites in my own readings; I describe the technique as those who use it do, beside both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). And a bond built on shelter would want the same care for its data: a composite is drawn only when both people know and agree ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
