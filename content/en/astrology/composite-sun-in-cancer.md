Of the twelve composite Sun signs, Cancer is the one the most pairs have, if only by a whisker. It gives a relationship a centre built around care, and the bond the two of you make becomes, first of all, somewhere to come back to.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), every planet sits halfway between its two natal places, and the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) shows what the relationship gathers itself around. When that midpoint falls in [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), cardinal water, it falls in the sign the most pairs share: about 1 pair in 11, in a count of two million random pairs born between 1930 and 2009, against about 1 in 12 for most signs. The reason is in the sky. The Sun takes about 31 days to cross Cancer, a day or two longer than any winter sign, so more days of the year have their Sun in the summer signs, and the midpoints follow them.

A bond centred here tends to know itself as a refuge. Its identity forms around protecting something: each other, a household, an old circle of friends, a family business, a shared stretch of garden. Its vitality comes back when the two of you are somewhere familiar, and it expresses itself through attention rather than display: the remembered detail, the meal kept warm, the message sent on the day that was always going to be hard. Its shelter often reaches past the two of you: the friends who come for Sunday lunch for years, the sibling who stays for a month and is never made to feel a guest.

The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) rules Cancer, so the next thing to look at is the composite Moon: its sign and house show where this bond actually goes for comfort. With the [composite Moon in Cancer](/astrology/composite-moon-in-cancer/) too, what the relationship is for and what it needs speak with one voice; elsewhere, the two can ask for different things, and the pair learns to give both. In one person's chart, the [Sun in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-cancer/) describes a self that needs to feel safe to shine. In a composite, the need for safety belongs to the relationship, and two people who have never felt at home anywhere can find that they've made a home of the bond itself.

The difficulty is that protection can turn into a wall. A relationship organised around shelter can close around itself, keeping the world outside, and sometimes change with it. Care isn't control, and belonging isn't the same as merging: the bond does best when its shelter has a door. Beside it, the [composite Sun in Gemini](/astrology/composite-sun-in-gemini/) opens a bond to the world by talking, and the [composite Sun in Leo](/astrology/composite-sun-in-leo/) by showing what the two of you make. This one keeps the world at the door, and chooses whom to let in.

None of this says how long a relationship will last or whether it will work out. I don't use composites in my own readings; I describe the technique as those who use it do, beside both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). And a bond built on shelter would want the same care for its data: a composite is drawn only when both people know and agree ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
