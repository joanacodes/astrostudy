A composite Sun in Aquarius points a relationship beyond itself. The bond the two of you make tends to gather around something larger than the pair, an idea, a circle of friends, a future worth working for, and it keeps room for two distinct people inside it.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Tradition calls [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/) the Sun's [detriment](/astrology/detriment/), the sign opposite its own, and in [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) that has a particular flavour. The composite's [Sun](/astrology/sun/), halfway between your two natal Suns, is read as what the relationship organises itself around; in fixed air, opposite Leo, that centre sits partly outside the two of you, in what you're part of together. It's an everyday placement, shared by about 1 pair in 12.

This kind of bond is often easiest to recognise in company: the pair who started the reading group, who keep a house open to friends of friends, who would rather change how something works than complain about it. It's revived by ideas and by the people who share them, and friends often meet each other through this pair: some of the bond's best moments happen with other people in the room. Its purpose often points forward, towards a better arrangement, for the two of you and for others. And it's comfortable with two separate lives running alongside the shared one; individuality, here, isn't distance.

Aquarius answers to two rulers: [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) for modern tropical astrology, [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) for the tradition. In a birth chart I read both, and a composite has its own version of each. Uranus spends about seven years in a sign, so two people of the same generation usually share it: born in 1983 and in 1986, both have Uranus in Sagittarius, and so does their composite. Saturn moves faster, about two and a half years a sign, and is likelier to land somewhere new between you. Principles that a natal [Sun in Aquarius](/astrology/sun-in-aquarius/) would hold alone belong, here, to the bond, and two private people can find that, together, they've become a fixture of their neighbourhood.

The cause can end up with the warmth the two people need. A relationship organised around the wider circle can forget that it's also two people at a kitchen table, and fairness to everyone can leave too little that's just yours. Objectivity isn't coldness, though, and the remedy is simple: time that belongs to no project and no group. Saying plainly what the two of you want for yourselves, apart from what you want for everyone, helps too. Between the [composite Sun in Capricorn](/astrology/composite-sun-in-capricorn/), which builds on what has been promised, and the [composite Sun in Pisces](/astrology/composite-sun-in-pisces/), which gathers around a shared inner world, an Aquarius bond keeps its eyes on what could come next.

A bond that values each person's independence would also respect each person's say over their own chart: a composite uses both people's birth data, and it's drawn only with both people's agreement ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)). I describe the technique without using it in my readings. Astrologers who do use it read it with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and never as a forecast of how long a relationship lasts or how it turns out.
