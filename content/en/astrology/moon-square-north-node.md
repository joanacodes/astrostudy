Moon square North Node places the Moon a quarter of the way round from both nodes at once, at the point of its orbit farthest from the Sun's path. In a chart, your needs stand across the line of growth rather than on it, and ask to be included.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) regulates feeling and need: attachment, habit, how you adjust when life shifts. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) points to a capacity you're still building, and the South Node, opposite, to what came early and easily. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates the Moon to that line through friction: needs don't replace direction, direction doesn't overrule needs, and the rubbing between them is what develops.

In plain words, your feelings don't come with you automatically, whichever way you go. Head for the new and part of you protests that it isn't safe yet; fall back on what you know and the same part protests that it isn't enough. Neither protest is a verdict; both say that growth has to be liveable, or it won't last. Over time this tends to make you good at something many people never learn: changing without abandoning yourself.

Astronomy gives the square a picture: a quarter of the way between the nodes, where it crosses the Sun's path, the Moon is as far north or south of that path as it goes. Your Moon was at the top or bottom of its swing.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Squares usually fall between signs of one modality, so your Moon shares a pace with both nodes, cardinal, fixed or mutable. The Moon's house is the area of life that keeps asking for room while you grow, and the nodes' houses are the road: with the nodes across the 4th and 10th and the Moon in the 7th, your need for close company asks for a place between home and career.

About 1 person in 11 is born with the Moon within 8° of this square, which the Moon makes twice a month for about a day each time. Which node it meets next is written in the chart too. Ninety degrees ahead of the North Node in the zodiac, the Moon is at its farthest north, heading for the South Node; ninety degrees behind, at its farthest south, heading for the North Node. I read the first as needs on their way back to familiar ground, and the second as needs on their way into the new.

My notebook has no count for the nodes ([the invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)), so I offer this square as something to test against your own life. I look at both the mean and the true node; they only disagree at the very edge of the orb.

Compared with the four other contacts:

- **[Moon conjunct North Node](/astrology/moon-conjunct-north-node/):** the Moon on the line, at its growing end, instead of across it.
- **[Moon sextile North Node](/astrology/moon-sextile-north-node/):** two signs from the node; the needs open doors to growth instead of asking for a seat.
- **[Moon trine North Node](/astrology/moon-trine-north-node/):** needs that flow towards the direction rather than standing in its way.
- **[Moon opposite North Node](/astrology/moon-opposite-north-node/):** the familiar end itself holds the Moon, so the axis runs through your needs rather than past them.

Between two charts, [Moon square North Node in synastry](/astrology/moon-square-north-node-synastry/) places one person's Moon across the other's nodal line: a need that can feel like a detour on the other's road, or like a reminder to travel it more kindly. I read it from each side.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting North Node square natal Moon](/astrology/transiting-north-node-square-natal-moon/) comes about every nine years, from one side and then the other, and lasts a month or two each time. If the square is in your chart, your [nodal return](/astrology/nodal-return/), at about 18½, 37 and 56, and your [nodal reversal](/astrology/nodal-reversal/), at about 9, 28 and 46, both bring it back: the axis squares your Moon whichever way round it lies. Weather, not a forecast, each time: a season that asks again where your needs fit in your plans.
