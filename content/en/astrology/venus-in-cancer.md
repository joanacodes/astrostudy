Venus in Cancer values what feels like home, and remembers who made it feel that way. The planet that weighs what is worth having sits here in the sign of belonging, so its choices tend to carry a memory. I read it as affection that protects, and a heart that opens wide once it is sure of its welcome.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) is what you find valuable and how you go about relating. [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), cardinal water ruled by the [Moon](/astrology/moon/), gives it a manner that gathers and shelters. What you love often comes with a story: the dish that tastes of one particular kitchen, the song from a long car journey, the friend who knew you before you were anyone in particular. Cardinal signs start things, and this one starts care: you're often the first to notice that someone needs looking after, and the first to do it.

Venus has no dignity in Cancer, so I read it through its manner and its ruler. The Moon shows what you need in order to feel safe. With Venus in the Moon's sign, what you value and what you need are tied together, and when one is hurt, the other usually feels it.

Venus usually spends about 25 days in Cancer, and up to 68 when it is retrograde there; about 1 person in 12 born from 1930 to 2009 has it. Your Sun is in Cancer or up to two signs either side, from Taurus to Virgo. Venus arrives from [Venus in Gemini](/astrology/venus-in-gemini/), where affection is mostly talk, and moves on to [Venus in Leo](/astrology/venus-in-leo/), where it wants to be seen. In between, it wants to be kept safe.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In love, a Cancer Venus looks after people. You remember the name of someone's childhood dog, the anniversary of a loss, the way they take their tea, and you show affection by making them comfortable. Safety comes first: you may need time, and a few proofs of kindness, before you open up, and a careless remark can send you back into your shell for days. Reciprocity, here, is care returned: being thought of, fed, asked how you are. The risk is looking after someone instead of telling them what you need, then feeling hurt that they never guessed. Partners who are tender without being asked, and who don't mock your attachments, get to see how deep this Venus goes.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, this Venus values belonging. A team that feels a little like family, a manager who notices when you're tired, an office with its own small rituals: these matter more than you may admit. You often make places welcoming, the one who brings cake, remembers birthdays and settles the newcomer. Hospitality, care work, food, interiors, family businesses, archives and history can suit it. Harsh, impersonal places wear it out, and criticism given coldly can sting long after the meeting is over.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Hurt gets stored rather than spoken. You may withdraw instead of saying what wounded you, and expect the other person to work it out. Loyalty to the familiar can outlast its use: an old circle, an old pattern, a way of loving learned at home that no longer fits the life you have. And caring for others can turn into a way of being needed, which feels safer, to this Venus, than simply being wanted.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Ask for care in plain words: *I'd like you to look after me tonight* is a gift to a partner, not a burden. Let people do things for you without rushing to do something back. Start one tradition of your own, rather than only keeping the ones you were given. And when you withdraw, say that you're withdrawing, and roughly when you'll be back.

Venus is one of the placements everyone reads first, and in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) it held about a third of the time. A Cancer Venus can take a description of itself very personally, so I offer this one gently: keep what rings true, and leave the rest here on the page.
