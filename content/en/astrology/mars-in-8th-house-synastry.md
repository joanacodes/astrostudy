Their Mars in your 8th house takes another person's drive into the most guarded part of your chart: desire, trust, shared money and the decisions you can't undo on your own. It is an overlay of intensity, and of nerve: closeness here asks for courage from both of you.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) carries desire and courage, the push to go after something and the nerve to stay when it gets difficult. [The 8th house](/astrology/8th-house/) is what you share once you've let someone in: intimacy of body and of mind, joint finances and obligations, the changes that come from being close. As a [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their Mars here carries that push into your inner rooms.

With them, you may find the courage to face what you usually avoid: the money conversation, the old fear, the decision that has to be made together. Desire can be strong and direct, if the bond has that side, and trust becomes something you choose actively rather than drift into. Closeness with them asks something of you, and you may want to answer.

For them, your depth is a challenge worth meeting. They may want to protect you, to back you through a hard change, or to win a trust they can tell isn't given lightly. Succedent, the 8th holds whatever a bond builds up, and its natural sign is [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/), which tradition gave to Mars and modern astrology to Pluto. I read both, and here it's the older ruler that arrives: their Mars lands in a house whose sign it once ruled.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Intensity can become pressure. You may feel pushed to open up faster than you're ready, or to merge money, plans or decisions before you've agreed the terms. Questions of control can surface: who decides about what's shared, whose need counts more, how much privacy is left to each of you.

From their side, your guardedness can look like a closed door they're tempted to force. Desire and trust may not move at the same speed in the two of you, and they can feel rejected where you simply needed time.

Tradition gave the 8th death, and called Mars a [malefic](/astrology/benefics-and-malefics/); neither label predicts anything between you. No chart can foresee a death ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)), and an overlay never says what two adults will do with desire: that is theirs to choose, and only with a yes from both.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Let the slower one set the pace, and say the terms out loud. With desire, a clear yes from both is the ground everything else stands on, and a no or a "not yet" is respected without argument. With shared money, put agreements in writing and keep some resources each. In a couple, talk about what intimacy means to each of you before assuming; between friends, decide which secrets stay secret; between colleagues sharing a budget or a risky decision, clear roles stop control from becoming the subject; between adult relatives who own something together, a written agreement spares a great deal of feeling.

Look at it from their chart and you have your Mars in their 8th: the push towards closeness is yours, and so is the care about pace. [Their Mars in your 7th house](/astrology/mars-in-7th-house-synastry/) is the meeting across the table that comes first; [their Mars in your 9th house](/astrology/mars-in-9th-house-synastry/) turns that energy towards beliefs and far horizons.

In Placidus, the house system I use, the size of your 8th depends on the latitude of your birthplace and on what was rising, and only your birth time settles it. A birth date fixes their Mars to within half a degree or so, so their hour matters only at the edges of the house, though the other half of the synastry can't do without it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Desire, trust and joint money are the last things anyone should have read without their yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/#a-chart-is-personal)).
