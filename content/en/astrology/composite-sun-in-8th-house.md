The 8th house holds what two people share and owe, the trust it takes to merge anything, and the changes that go deep. A composite Sun there makes that depth the relationship's centre.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

With only birth dates, [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) has planets but no houses at all, so there would be no [8th house](/astrology/8th-house/) to place its [Sun](/astrology/sun/) in. *No birth time, no reading*, I say of birth charts, and a composite has the same rule built in ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). With both times, there are still two ways to draw the houses, from the midpoints of your natal cusps or from the composite Midheaven at the place where you live, and the 8th's cusps can fall differently in each.

As the setting for the bond's Sun, the 8th is about trust and what is held in common: confidences, obligations, a shared home or a shared debt, the parts of each life the other has been allowed into. A relationship like this tends to know itself in depth rather than at the surface, and it often finds out what it's made of when something changes for good: a move across the world, the care of a parent, a crisis met side by side. It regenerates, and it's often more honest after the hard year than before it. It also tends to take on what each person brings without being asked, friends, habits, a family's ways, and make that inheritance its own.

The 8th is succedent, with Scorpio as its natural sign, and no planet has its joy here. In a birth chart, the [Sun in the 8th house](/astrology/sun-in-8th-house/) is about one person's relationship with depth and trust; in synastry, [their Sun in your 8th house](/astrology/sun-in-8th-house-synastry/) describes trust between two people as one of them feels it. In a composite, the trust is the bond's own substance, and two guarded people can find that, with each other, they hold nothing back.

Tradition gave the 8th death, and I never read it that way, here or in any chart: a composite Sun in the 8th predicts no death, loss or ending ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)). The difficulty is that merging can blur two people into one. Shared obligations can become the whole bond, and intensity can leave little room for anything light. A little separateness keeps the merging healthy: time, friends and decisions that stay each person's own. Talking about shared obligations before they pile up, rather than after, keeps them from becoming the bond's only subject.

Before it, the [composite Sun in the 7th house](/astrology/composite-sun-in-7th-house/) turns a bond outwards, to its alliances; after it, the [composite Sun in the 9th house](/astrology/composite-sun-in-9th-house/) turns it towards meaning and the wider world. Composites stay outside my readings, and those who use them set them beside both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). What's shared here is intimate, and that includes birth data: a composite is drawn only when both people have handed theirs over knowingly ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
