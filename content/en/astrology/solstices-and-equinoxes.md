Astronomy and astrology meet at four moments a year. At each one the Sun reaches a turning point in its yearly path, a season turns, and the tropical zodiac starts a new quarter.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

An equinox is the moment the Sun crosses the [celestial equator](/astrology/celestial-equator/): heading north in March, heading south in September. The word is the Latin *aequinoctium*, equal night, and on that day the Sun rises almost due east and sets almost due west from nearly everywhere on Earth. Day and night come close to equal without quite getting there: the Sun is a disc rather than a point, and the air bends its light over the horizon, so even at the equator the day runs about fourteen minutes longer than the night.

A solstice is the moment the Sun reaches its furthest north, in June, or its furthest south, in December, 23.44° from the equator. The Latin *solstitium* means the Sun standing still, because for a few days its noon height hardly changes before it turns back. At the June solstice it stands overhead at noon on the Tropic of Cancer; at the December one, on the Tropic of Capricorn.

Measured along the Sun's own path, the four moments are four exact numbers. Astronomers define them as the instants the Sun's [zodiacal longitude](/astrology/zodiacal-longitude/) reaches 0°, 90°, 180° and 270°, which the tropical zodiac calls 0° Aries, 0° Cancer, 0° Libra and 0° Capricorn ([tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/)). The March equinox is also the zero of the star clock astrologers use to find the angles: [sidereal time](/astrology/sidereal-time/) reads 0h when that point crosses your meridian.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The moments drift. A year doesn't divide into whole days, so each one comes nearly six hours later by the clock every year, until a leap day pulls it back ([sign dates, and why they move](/astrology/sign-dates-and-why-they-move/)). In the figures I work from, the March equinox falls at 14:45 UT in 2026, 20:24 in 2027 and 02:17 in 2028, on 20 March each time. Across this century, in Universal Time, it ranges from 21 March, in 2003, to 19 March, in 2096.

The four quarters aren't equal either. The Earth moves fastest when it is closest to the Sun, in early January, so the Sun takes longer over one half of the zodiac than the other. From the March equinox of 2026 to the September one is 186 days and 9 hours; from there to the March equinox of 2027, 178 days and 20 hours. The Sun spends about a week longer in the six signs from Aries to Virgo than in the six from Libra to Pisces.

The seasons themselves come from the tilt of the Earth's axis, not from its distance, and south of the equator they run the other way: the June solstice brings the shortest day there, and the March equinox opens autumn. Astronomers name the moments by their months, or call the March one the northward equinox, so that no hemisphere's seasons are built into the words.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

For most charts, the solstices and equinoxes stay in the background, fixing where each tropical sign begins. They come forward when you were born close to one. The Sun changes sign at an exact minute that moves from year to year, so on 20 March 2026 the time decides it: born before 14:45 UT, you have the Sun in the last degree of Pisces; born after, at 0° Aries ([born on the cusp](/astrology/born-on-the-cusp/)). It is one of the many things the hour settles, and one more reason I want the birth time before I read anything ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Planets on those four first degrees draw many astrologers' attention, since they sit exactly on the year's turning points ([Aries Point](/astrology/aries-point/), [0° of a sign](/astrology/0-of-a-sign/)). And the Sun's distance from the equator at the solstices, 23.44°, is the line that defines a planet [out of bounds](/astrology/out-of-bounds-planets/): further north or south than the Sun ever goes.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Day and night are exactly equal at the equinox.”** The day is a little longer. The day of truly equal light comes a few days towards winter: before the March equinox and after the September one, in the northern hemisphere.
- **“The solstice always falls on the 21st.”** Both solstices fall between the 20th and the 22nd of their month, and the equinoxes move in the same way.
- **“The seasons come from the Earth's distance from the Sun.”** They come from the tilt of its axis. The Earth is closest to the Sun in early January, in the middle of the northern winter.
- **“An equinox lasts a day.”** It's an instant. The March equinox of 2026, at 14:45 UT, already falls on 21 March in New Zealand ([time zones and daylight saving in charts](/astrology/time-zones-and-daylight-saving-in-charts/)).
