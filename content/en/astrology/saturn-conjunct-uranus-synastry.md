Saturn conjunct Uranus in synastry puts one person's Saturn on the other's Uranus, within a few degrees. The commitments the first person keeps lie exactly where the second wants to change things, and because both planets move slowly, the meeting is less between two people than between two generations.

## What flows {#what-flows}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) establishes structure: responsibility, boundaries, discipline, the long view. [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) enables change: it innovates, frees, reforms systems grown too tight. A [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) makes two functions one process, and among the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) this one gives the keeping to one person and the changing to the other. The two planets share a sign, too: Saturn is the traditional ruler of [Aquarius](/astrology/aquarius/) and Uranus its modern one. I read both, and in this contact each person brings one of them.

If the Saturn is yours, the other person's restlessness lands precisely where you keep your sense of what must hold. They question the rule you'd never thought to question. Sometimes they're right, and that is unsettling in a useful way.

If the Uranus is yours, this person puts a floor under your urge to change things. Your experiments get a timetable, your reforms a budget and a date. It can feel like a lid, and it can also be the first time a change of yours actually lasted.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

A conjunction blurs the line between the rule and the reform. After a while, it's hard to say whose idea the new arrangement was: the Uranus person's experiment hardens into the Saturn person's procedure, or the Saturn person's structure absorbs each change so thoroughly that nothing new is left. Reform isn't destruction, and structure isn't a cage; this pair needs to keep telling them apart.

- **[Saturn sextile Uranus in synastry](/astrology/saturn-sextile-uranus-synastry/):** a working alliance on offer, two signs apart.
- **[Saturn square Uranus in synastry](/astrology/saturn-square-uranus-synastry/):** keeping and changing collide at right angles.
- **[Saturn trine Uranus in synastry](/astrology/saturn-trine-uranus-synastry/):** the handover from old to new runs smoothly.
- **[Saturn opposite Uranus in synastry](/astrology/saturn-opposite-uranus-synastry/):** security and freedom at either end of a seesaw.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Separate the trial from the rule. Try a change for a fixed time, then decide together what becomes permanent. The Saturn person can name the commitments that aren't negotiable; the Uranus person, the ones they'd change first. A manager and a younger member of the team, an adult child and a parent sharing a house, partners merging two ways of running a home: each can turn this into reform that holds.

Someone born in mid-1965, with Uranus in Virgo, has it with anyone born between September 1978 and September 1979, whose Saturn crossed that degree. Someone born in mid-1975 has it the other way round, their Saturn on the Uranus of anyone born from May 1953 to August 1954. Everyone born in the same few years shares these contacts, so in my synastry readings I count only those with at least one personal body: the Sun, the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, the Ascendant or the Midheaven. I read this one through the houses, as a difference between two eras more than between two people: what the late-1970s generation holds steady, sitting on what the mid-1960s one wants to change.

So the houses are everything here, and they need both birth times; the hour doesn't move either planet enough to matter to the conjunction ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Your Saturn in their 4th house, say, and the rule-and-reform question moves into their home ([Their Saturn in your 4th house](/astrology/saturn-in-4th-house-synastry/)).

In one chart, [Saturn conjunct Uranus](/astrology/saturn-conjunct-uranus/) asks a person to keep and change at once. Between two, each holds one of those tasks, and both should agree before I read it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
