About three years before the North Node reaches your Saturn, and again about three years after, it stands two signs away from it. Those two sextiles frame the conjunction like a doorstep on either side, and each lasts about a month.

## What changes {#what-changes}

[Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) gives a chart its structure: what you commit to, where you set limits, how you plan for the long run. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) points to a capacity built through repeated practice. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) relates them through opportunity and cooperation, with one condition: you have to respond. Under this [transit](/astrology/transits/), the direction you are growing in and your capacity for steady work can help each other, if you let them meet.

The opening is usually modest and practical. A short course that fits your week. A routine that suddenly looks sustainable. Someone more experienced who offers an hour. Of the node's contacts with Saturn, this is one of the light ones: the new skill can borrow its discipline without being weighed down by it. Across the chart the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) trines your Saturn, so the ways you already know of being reliable stay within reach, a support rather than a pull backwards.

The signs show it plainly. With Saturn in Taurus, the node sextiles it from Cancer on its way to your Saturn and from Pisces on its way out. From July 2026 to March 2028 the node is in Aquarius, sextile every Saturn in Aries and in Sagittarius. Which houses it crosses depends on your birth time, and so does the part of life the opening concerns ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

From 1½° before the exact sextile to 1° after, the true node usually needs about a month. An eclipse season can stretch that to three months or more, as the node all but stops while the Sun is aligned with it, and about one pass in 25 brings three exact hits. The eclipses of such a season fall two signs or four signs from your Saturn, and those I don't count as eclipses on your chart.

My forecast lists the node only when one of its ends meets a point, by conjunction or opposition, because I read the nodes as an axis. A sextile touches neither end to your Saturn, so this transit isn't one of its seasons; I mention it, when it coincides with slower transits, as a light accent.

The sextiles come twice a round, so by 80 people have met eight or nine. The first depends on the node's place at birth: for someone born on 1 January 1965 it came at 2.6, for 1 January 1985 at 6.2. The one before the conjunction leads to [Transiting North Node conjunct natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-north-node-conjunct-natal-saturn/); the one after it leads to [Transiting North Node square natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-north-node-square-natal-saturn/), then to [Transiting North Node trine natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-north-node-trine-natal-saturn/) and [Transiting North Node opposite natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-north-node-opposite-natal-saturn/). With [Saturn sextile North Node](/astrology/saturn-sextile-north-node/) in your chart, each nodal return brings the node back to the same angle with your Saturn.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Say yes to the small structured thing: the four-week course, the standing appointment, the one evening a week kept for practice.

Ask for an hour of someone's experience. A sextile from the node to Saturn rewards the question more than the solitary effort.

Write down what you start this month. If this is the sextile before the conjunction, you will see what it has become when the node reaches your Saturn, about three years on.
