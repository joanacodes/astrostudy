The friends who always end up in your kitchen, the soup taken round when someone is low, the drawer of photographs nobody is allowed to clear out: composite Venus in Cancer finds the relationship's pleasures in shelter and belonging.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

Take the two Venuses of your birth charts, find the point that splits the distance between them evenly, the short way round, and you have composite [Venus](/astrology/venus/): [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/)'s account of what the relationship values, likes and keeps, and of how it handles give-and-take. In [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), cardinal water ruled by the [Moon](/astrology/moon/), Venus has neither dignity nor debility, and the sign brings protection, care and a sense of belonging. Valuing, here, means sheltering: the bond loves what it can look after, and shows affection by noticing what the other needs before it's said.

Two independent people who'd never call themselves homely can make a bond that likes nothing better than an evening in with the door shut. Its taste is shaped by memory: recipes handed down, the café from your first year, objects kept because of who gave them. A natal [Venus in Cancer](/astrology/venus-in-cancer/) describes one person's way of caring; the composite describes a care the two of you share, which often widens to friends who end up treated as family. With the Moon as ruler, the composite Moon's sign and house show what feeds this Venus, the comfort the relationship needs in order to keep giving. About 1 pair in 12 shares the placement, much as about 1 person in 12 is born with Venus in Cancer.

Usually the composite Sun keeps this Venus company somewhere between Taurus and Virgo: in about 9 pairs in 10 the two sit within 47° of each other, the way Venus and the Sun always do in the sky. The tenth pair tends to be two people born many months apart, whose Suns sit across the year from each other. Taken each the short way, their Suns' midpoint and their Venuses' midpoint can fall on opposite sides of the circle, so that in about 1 pair in 22 a Capricorn Sun, or one close to Capricorn, ends up across the chart from this Cancer Venus. The relationship's centre and its comforts end up at opposite ends of the chart through nothing more than arithmetic.

Protection has an edge. A bond that loves its shelter can start guarding it from everything: outside opinions, new people, sometimes the difficult conversation that would let some air in. Care isn't control, and belonging needn't mean closing ranks. It helps to open the door on purpose, with a guest at the table from outside the usual circle, a worry said out loud before it's tidied away, and a way for each of you to be looked after without having to ask.

[Composite Venus in Gemini](/astrology/composite-venus-in-gemini/) enjoys what passes between you; [composite Venus in Leo](/astrology/composite-venus-in-leo/) loves whatever can be celebrated in the open; Cancer keeps what it loves close. None of this comes from my own readings, which leave composites out: it is the technique as described, read with the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) and the two birth charts. A relationship that looks after its own would look after this too, since a composite is made from two births, and both people should be in on it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
