Two signatures on the same lease, a business partnership with a written agreement, a promise said out loud and then kept. Composite Saturn in the 7th house makes commitment itself the subject of the relationship.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) is where a chart meets other people as equals: partners, collaborators, the open rivals who stand in front of you. In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), it turns two ways at once. It describes the bond's own idea of partnership, and the way the pair, together, deals with others one to one: business partners, other couples, the people it signs things with. With [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) here, commitment is explicit. The pair puts agreements into words, often into writing, keeps them scrupulously, and expects the same of the people it works with. Rivals count too: a competitor the pair respects and faces openly can sharpen this bond more than any ally.

Its cusp, the composite [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), faces the composite Ascendant across the chart and shares all its uncertainty: it needs both birth times, and it moves with the method chosen, cusps averaged one by one or a frame built on the composite Midheaven for the town you share ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Libra is the house's natural sign, the one where tradition exalts Saturn. Exaltation belongs to signs, not houses, yet the fit is easy to see: agreements made between equals are this planet at its most comfortable.

Why does this differ from [Saturn in the 7th house](/astrology/saturn-in-7th-house/)? Because a birth chart describes one person who commits slowly and stays, and [their Saturn in your 7th house](/astrology/saturn-in-7th-house-synastry/) describes one person's sense of duty landing in another's idea of partnership, whereas the composite's commitment belongs to the bond itself. Two people who are both wary of promises can find that, as a pair, they make careful ones and keep them.

If the neighbours were documents, [composite Saturn in the 6th house](/astrology/composite-saturn-in-6th-house/) would be the bond's work timetable and [composite Saturn in the 8th house](/astrology/composite-saturn-in-8th-house/) its joint account; the 7th is the agreement both of you signed.

Trouble starts when the relationship is run like a contract. Every change needs renegotiating, the terms start to matter more than the warmth, and other people, friends or other couples, can feel like auditors of how well the two of you are doing. It helps to revisit the agreements together as your lives change, to keep tenderness in the room alongside the terms, and to let a few people close to you see the bond as it is rather than as it's supposed to be. None of it predicts a wedding, a separation or anything else: Saturn here describes how the bond commits, not where it ends up.

I don't draw composites. This is the method's own account, which practitioners check against both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and sometimes against the [Davison chart](/astrology/davison-chart/), cast for a real moment between the two births. In the house of open partnership, consent should be open too: a composite is drawn only when both of you know about it and have agreed ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
