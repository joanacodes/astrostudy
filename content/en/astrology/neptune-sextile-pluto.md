Neptune sextile Pluto is the background aspect of the twentieth century's second half. Most people born between 1943 and 1993 carry it, and it held so long that it can feel less like an aspect than like the weather everyone grew up in. Shared that widely, it tells you about a generation; read through your houses, it tells you about you.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) imagines and connects: it feels its way into other people's lives, dreams of something larger and softens the lines that keep us apart. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) goes to the root: it lets go of what has died, regenerates, and gives back a stronger footing. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) relates them through opportunity: the dreamer and the digger can switch each other on, though only when someone takes the opening.

In plain words, the dream has access to the depths, and the depths have access to the dream. Imagination can help you change in earnest, and real change can find an image, a story or a belief to follow. Nothing forces the two together, which is why I read it as available rather than automatic.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

About 7 people in 10 born between 1930 and 2009 have Neptune within 6° of a sextile to Pluto, and it isn't spread evenly. Everyone born from October 1943 to October 1993 has it. Before that come three short windows, in 1941, 1942 and 1943. After it, the angle hovered at the edge of the orb, and the planets' yearly retrograde loops carried it in and out: births from February to October 1994 have it, then a shorter window each year, down to June–August in 2009.

Half a century is a long time for one aspect. The reason is Pluto's uneven orbit, which keeps it anywhere from 12 to 31 years in a sign. For much of that time Pluto was on the quick part, keeping almost the same pace as Neptune, which crosses a sign in about 14 years, with Neptune always about two signs ahead. The angle barely moved.

Shared by so many, the sextile is a generation's aspect, not a personal signature ([personal, social and generational planets](/astrology/personal-social-and-generational-planets/)). What makes it yours is where it falls. A sextile usually links houses two apart: Pluto in the 3rd with Neptune in the 5th might put the deep changes into the way you think and speak, and the dreaming into play and creative work. Without the time of birth, the houses stay out of reach ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). The orb matters too: births in the late windows, near its edge, have a quieter sextile than births when it was close to exact.

The pair's other angles, none of which formed for anyone born in those eighty years:

- **[Neptune conjunct Pluto](/astrology/neptune-conjunct-pluto/):** vision and renewal fused into one movement.
- **[Neptune square Pluto](/astrology/neptune-square-pluto/):** the ideal and the clearing-out at cross purposes, a friction that asks for adjustment.
- **[Neptune trine Pluto](/astrology/neptune-trine-pluto/):** the two flowing together with little resistance.
- **[Neptune opposite Pluto](/astrology/neptune-opposite-pluto/):** the two facing each other, each showing what the other leaves out.

When two people born close together both carry the sextile, their Neptune and Pluto tend to repeat it across the two charts: [Neptune sextile Pluto in synastry](/astrology/neptune-sextile-pluto-synastry/) is then a shared background more than a personal link, though each person may still feel it differently.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Pluto sextile natal Neptune](/astrology/transiting-pluto-sextile-natal-neptune/) and [transiting Neptune sextile natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-neptune-sextile-natal-pluto/) bring the same opening from the sky. Both are slow: Neptune stays near a point of your chart for a year or more, and Pluto for two years or so, each crossing it several times. Everyone born within a year or two of you meets such a transit at about the same time; its house is what makes it yours.

Born with the sextile, you had both on the day you were born. Then the sky moves on, Neptune drawing away from your Pluto, and Pluto closing slowly on your Neptune.

As with every transit, this is weather rather than a forecast: a Neptune–Pluto sextile in transit is an opening for imagination and renewal to help each other, and, like the natal one, it asks to be taken up.
