Before 1898 every asteroid was given a female name, and the first were goddesses: Ceres, Pallas, Juno, Vesta. The eightieth was named after a woman who lived: Sappho, the poet of Lesbos, whose love poems were so admired that ancient readers called her the Tenth Muse. Some astrologers place the asteroid in the birth chart. This page says what it is, what it can never be used for, and why I leave it out.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Norman Pogson, an English astronomer, found it from Madras on 2 May 1864. It's a stony asteroid in the inner part of the main belt, between Mars and Jupiter, going round the Sun every 3.48 years on an orbit tilted by about 8½° to the Earth's. Estimates of its size run from about 66 to 83 km across, depending on the measurement; radar from Arecibo, between 1980 and 1985, gave the larger figure.

Its namesake was born around 630 BC, on Lesbos, and died around 570. She wrote lyric poetry to be sung with music, probably some ten thousand lines, of which most is lost; only one poem, the *Ode to Aphrodite*, survives complete. The scholars of Hellenistic Alexandria counted her among the nine lyric poets they esteemed most. She was best known for her love poetry, and she has become a symbol of love and desire between women: the English words *sapphic* and *lesbian* come from her name and her island.

That sets the asteroid apart from its neighbours in this encyclopaedia. [Amor](/astrology/amor/), [Cupido](/astrology/cupido-asteroid/) and [Eros](/astrology/eros/) all carry the names of one god; Sappho carries a human name, like the many asteroids since named after people, the ones behind the hunt for an asteroid with your own name ([personal-name asteroids](/astrology/personal-name-asteroids/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

A body named in 1864 has no ancient tradition behind it, so what astrologers say about Sappho starts from the poet. Her surviving lines are about love above all, with family and religion, and one fragment describes the body's response to desire so exactly that Plato seems to echo it. That is the material any meaning is built from.

The asteroid itself is followed closely by astronomers. On 16 September 2018 it passed in front of a star in Taurus; its shadow crossed the western United States, over Sacramento and Salt Lake City, and the observations were made to refine its shape. Its 3.48-year orbit means it moves through the zodiac at a steady pace; its house, as for every body, depends on the exact birth time.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I don't read Sappho. The system behind my readings calculates no asteroids, and Sappho wasn't among the placements I [checked one by one](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), so I can't tell you how often it holds. If you use it yourself, keep it to close contacts, as a detail beside the planets ([How to use asteroids without drowning the chart](/astrology/how-to-use-asteroids-without-drowning-the-chart/)).

There's a firmer line too, and it's the main reason for this page. No asteroid, planet or placement can tell anyone's sexuality, and I'd never use a chart to guess at it, yours or anyone else's. Doing it to another person, without their knowledge, would be an intrusion as well as bad astrology ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)). A person's sexuality is theirs to know and to name.

What a chart can describe is how you love and create. [Venus](/astrology/venus/) shows what you value and what draws you, whoever draws you; the [5th house](/astrology/5th-house/), the house of creativity, play and romance, shows where pleasure and self-expression find room. Venus held about a third of the time in my notebook, and I say so when a reading rests on it.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Sappho in your chart reveals your sexuality.”** No placement can, and no one needs a chart's permission to be who they are.
- **“A Sappho contact between two charts proves an attraction.”** A contact describes how two people may meet, never what either feels.
- **“Sappho was a goddess.”** She was a poet, who lived on Lesbos about 2,600 years ago.
- **“Sappho on your Mercury makes you a poet.”** A placement describes a way of working; it doesn't hand out talents. Writing does that.
