The North Node in the 4th house turns the chart's attention downwards, to the foundations. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) was in [the 4th house](/astrology/4th-house/) when you were born, at the bottom of the wheel, and it describes growth that happens out of public view: roots, home, the private self.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The 4th is an angular house. In Placidus, the house system I use, it begins at the [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), the point opposite the Midheaven, and its natural sign is Cancer. Tradition linked it to parents, land and ancestry, and to the end of things; I read it as where you come from, and what you build in order to belong. The [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) sits at the top of the chart, in [the 10th house](/astrology/10th-house/): work, standing, reputation, the life other people see. That's the familiar ground, and you may move through it with ease. The 4th-house end asks for the part of life that never appears on a CV.

The IC and the Midheaven both come from [your birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/), so this placement can't be read without it. Near a cusp, a few minutes either way can change the house, and [North Node in the 3rd house](/astrology/north-node-in-3rd-house/) or [North Node in the 5th house](/astrology/north-node-in-5th-house/) may be the page to read. I look at both the mean and the true node; with a degree or two between them they can straddle a cusp, and I say so when they do.

The angle in my [testing notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) is the rising sign, not this house's cusp, and it held about a third of the time; the three that stood out were Saturn, Chiron and Lilith.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

The 10th-house end can treat a relationship like a public role: the right partner, the right life, the plans everyone approves of. The 4th-house end wants the inside of it: a shared home that feels like yours, evenings that belong to nobody else, the private language two people build over years.

A partner may also help you understand where you come from, and what you'd like to keep or change about it. That kind of conversation belongs to this node.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, the South Node shines: you may be competent, respected, quick to take responsibility in public. The North Node doesn't ask you to stop. It asks your career to rest on something: a home life that refills you, time that isn't for show, perhaps work you can do from home, or a role that lets you put down roots in one place. Ambition grows sturdier when it has a base. If you lead people, the 4th-house end may show in how you look after them, so that the workplace feels like a base rather than a stage.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The public role can eat everything. When things get difficult, the 10th-house reflex is to work harder, be seen to cope, and leave home for later; later rarely arrives by itself.

Turning towards the 4th can feel like going backwards, especially if family history is complicated. It isn't a return to childhood. It's building foundations of your own, on your own terms, at any age.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Treat your home as a project worth your best attention, the way you'd treat work: one repair, one improvement, one corner made yours each month. Keep rituals that have nothing to do with your job: a Sunday walk, a family recipe, a call with someone who knew you young. Learn something about where your people come from, if that's possible and safe for you. Put one appointment a week in the diary that belongs only to home. If home has never felt safe, start smaller: a corner, a routine, a friend's kitchen where you can breathe out.

And let the 10th-house end keep its gifts. Your sense of responsibility can build a home as well as a career.
