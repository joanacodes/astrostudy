Libra rising puts the sign of partnership on the eastern horizon of your birth, and so at the very start of your chart. You meet the world as if it were a conversation between two: who's here, what would be fair, how can this go well for both of us? [Libra](/astrology/libra/) on the Ascendant makes courtesy your first move.

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

Your [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/) sits in a cardinal air sign: one that starts things, and starts them by turning towards someone. I read Libra rising as the other person at the door. You tend to find yourself in relation to people, and your first instinct in a new place is to read its balance: who is talking, who isn't, what would make the room easier. In Placidus, the house system I use, the Ascendant is the cusp of the [1st house](/astrology/1st-house/). Libra is the natural sign of the 7th, the house of partners, so with Libra rising the sign of the other stands where the self begins.

At London, Libra takes more than three times as long to rise as Aries, the sign opposite: about 170 minutes against 52. At Paris it takes 165, at New York 151. So it's far more common: about 1 London birth in 8 has Libra rising, against 1 in 28 for Aries. With nearly three hours on the horizon, a round hour leaves the sign in doubt only in its first or last few degrees, where [Virgo rising](/astrology/virgo-rising/) ends and [Scorpio rising](/astrology/scorpio-rising/) begins. Those edges are where I ask for the record rather than a memory ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). South of the equator Libra and Aries swap roles: signs that rise fast in the northern hemisphere rise slowly in the southern, and Libra becomes one of the quick ones.

## First impressions {#first-impressions}

People tend to find you easy to approach. You put them at ease, look for what you have in common, and notice who has been left out of the conversation. Courtesy comes first, and often a light, considered way of disagreeing that leaves everyone their dignity.

The misreading is courtesy taken for agreement. Harmony isn't the same as avoiding conflict, and being pleasant isn't the same as having no view: Libra rising often holds a firm opinion and waits for the moment when it can be heard.

Before you weigh all this, add one thing to the scales. In [my testing notes](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the rising sign is among the placements everyone reads first, and it held about a third of the time. A gracious first impression is a likely start, not the whole of you.

## Its ruler {#its-ruler}

Libra belongs to [Venus](/astrology/venus/), so Venus is your [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/): the planet of value, attraction and reciprocity speaks for the whole chart. Its sign shows how you choose and relate; its house, the part of life your relationships and your values keep drawing you into.

With this rising sign the horizon is shared by Venus and Mars. Your 1st house belongs to Venus, and your 7th, opening in Aries, to [Mars](/astrology/mars/): the planet of relating speaks for you, the planet of drive for the people you choose. Where each one sits tells you a great deal about what you offer and what you're drawn to.

In Libra, Venus is at home, and the ruler doubles the Ascendant's sense of fairness. In Pisces, its exaltation, it gives without counting; in Virgo, its fall, care shows in practical help. In Aries, on your Descendant, Venus is in detriment: courtesy borrows some of Aries' frankness, and you can be more direct than your Ascendant suggests.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Close up, Libra rising thinks in pairs. You consider the other person's view early, sometimes before your own, and you work hard to keep things fair. The work is letting a relationship hold a disagreement: a harmony that never risks a no isn't harmony, only quiet.

Your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/) is in Aries, opposite: you're drawn to people who know their own minds and act on them, frankly and fast ([Aries on the 7th house cusp](/astrology/aries-on-7th-house-cusp/)).

They give you permission to want something; you give them the pause in which two people can both be right.
