Sadalmelik belongs to Aquarius on the star map and to Pisces in the zodiac: its degree crossed from one sign into the next around 1760. Its Arabic name, sa‘d al-malik, is given as the lucky star of the king, though no one remembers which king.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Sadalmelik is α Aquarii, number 8414 in the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, the star in the water-bearer's right shoulder. An alpha is often its constellation's brightest star, as [Rasalhague](/astrology/rasalhague/) is in Ophiuchus. Not here: at magnitude 2.96, Sadalmelik comes a hair behind [Sadalsuud](/astrology/sadalsuud/), the left shoulder, at 2.91 (the lower the number, the brighter).

In the tropical zodiac it stands at 3°42′ [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/) in 2026, against 3°20′ in 2000 and 2°22′ in 1930. Count back at precession's pace, about a degree every 72 years, and its degree was still in Aquarius until around 1760, when the slow backward drift of the equinox carried it over the line. So a star of the constellation Aquarius now sits in the sign of Pisces, and will for well over a thousand years ([constellations versus signs](/astrology/constellations-versus-signs/)).

Its latitude is 10.7° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), the Sun's path, but its distance from the [celestial equator](/astrology/celestial-equator/) is almost nil: Sadalmelik lies 19′ south of that line, one of only two stars with ancient names within a degree of it. A star on the equator rises due east and sets due west wherever you stand on Earth, and spends about half of each day above the horizon.

It is a yellow supergiant about 690 light-years away, shining some 3,900 times as brightly as the Sun. Like Sadalsuud, it travels through space at right angles to the plane of the Milky Way.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

The Arabic sa‘d al-malik means the lucky star of the king, in Richard Hinckley Allen's rendering, and the trail ends there: the origin of the name is lost. Allen also records the name Rucbah for it, which it once shared with δ Cassiopeiae.

Its first word, sa‘d, luck, is the one it shares with its neighbour in the other shoulder, Sadalsuud, the luck of lucks. In Chinese astronomy Sadalmelik is the first star of Wēi, the Rooftop, a figure it makes with two stars of Pegasus.

Beyond the name, I haven't found an old meaning for Sadalmelik in a source I can check, so this page stops where the name stops: a king's luck, given as the tradition's word, with nothing added to it ([fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Within the usual degree of orb ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)), any planet from 2°42′ to 4°42′ Pisces in a 2026 chart sits on a star of Aquarius. The Sun gets there on about 21 and 22 February, so anyone born on those days has a [Sun in Pisces](/astrology/sun-in-pisces/) on Sadalmelik: the sign belongs to the season, the constellation to the stars behind the Sun.

Among the slow planets, Uranus lingered near its degree from mid-2003 to early 2005, which puts [Uranus in Pisces](/astrology/uranus-in-pisces/) on Sadalmelik for everyone born in that stretch. Saturn passed it early in 1935, in the spring and summer of 1964 and again in January 1965, and early in 1994.

Stars were never part of [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), where I ticked or crossed each placement of my chart and other people's, and the system I read with doesn't compute them. So this page describes Sadalmelik and leaves it there. Whatever a reading built on stars makes of it, the planet on that degree keeps its own meaning, with its sign and house read first.
