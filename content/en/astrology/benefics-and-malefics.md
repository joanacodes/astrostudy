A planet called a malefic sounds like bad news waiting in your chart. It isn't. Benefics and malefics are the tradition's labels for two pairs of planets, and they tell you more about how ancient astrologers saw the world than about your future.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The benefics are [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) and [Venus](/astrology/venus/); the malefics are [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) and [Mars](/astrology/mars/). Tradition ranked them as well: Jupiter the greater benefic and Venus the lesser, Saturn the greater malefic and Mars the lesser. The words simply mean doing good and doing harm, and they come from an astrology that read a chart for what fate had in store.

Ptolemy gives the reasoning in the second century, in terms of the four qualities. Warmth and moisture were thought to make things grow, so Jupiter and Venus, tempered and moist, were beneficent, and so was the Moon. Saturn was too cold and Mars too dry, so they were maleficent. The Sun and Mercury he called common: they take on the nature of whatever planet they're with. That leaves the [luminaries](/astrology/luminaries/) on either side of the line, the Moon with the benefics and the Sun in between.

The scheme was drawn for the seven bodies the eye can see. Uranus, Neptune and Pluto were discovered in 1781, 1846 and 1930, long after it was settled, and have no place in it.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Even in traditional practice, the label was never the whole story: a benefic badly placed could do little good, and a malefic in its own sign or exaltation a great deal. Sect added a layer: Ptolemy counts the Sun, Jupiter and Saturn as day planets and the Moon, Venus and Mars as night ones, and astrologers who use sect read each malefic and each benefic differently depending on whether you were born by day or by night ([benefic and malefic of the sect](/astrology/benefic-and-malefic-of-the-sect/)).

The houses carry the same vocabulary. Each of the four has a house where tradition said it rejoices, its joy: Venus in the 5th, which the old texts called Good Fortune, and Jupiter in the 11th, the Good Spirit; Mars in the 6th, Bad Fortune, and Saturn in the 12th, the Bad Spirit ([planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/)). The benefics rejoice in the houses with kind names, the malefics in those with hard ones: a tidy system, built by people who expected a chart to sort life into gifts and blows.

Aspects were sorted the same way: Ptolemy calls the trine and the sextile harmonious, the square and the opposition not. But the words benefic and malefic belong to the planets, and a square is friction, never failure ([hard and soft aspects](/astrology/hard-and-soft-aspects/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I keep the words, because they're history and you'll meet them in every older book, and I read past them. Jupiter, for me, is where you widen your understanding and grow, not where luck is waiting; Venus is how you value, choose and relate, not a promise of romance; Mars is the part of you that can act, assert and protect; Saturn is how you build, take responsibility and keep going for years. A malefic shows where life asks for effort, patience or courage. That's work, not misfortune.

My notebook is the best argument I have. [Saturn](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/#saturn), the planet tradition feared most, was one of the three placements that held 75 to 90% of the time in [my tests](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/). What it described was accurate, and it was never a curse: a subject learned slowly, often later than everyone around.

The same goes for your [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/). With Capricorn rising, Saturn is yours, and with Aquarius rising it shares the role with Uranus; Aries rising gives you Mars, and Scorpio rising gives you Mars with Pluto. That says how you tend to meet life, not how kindly it will treat you ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A malefic in your chart means bad luck.”** The word describes the planet's nature as the ancients saw it, not your fortune. Everyone has Saturn and Mars somewhere, and they show effort, limits and drive.
- **“Jupiter brings luck and money.”** Jupiter shows where you grow and look for meaning. No planet promises money, and I don't read a chart for it ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)).
- **“A chart full of benefics is a charmed life.”** A benefic shows where things tend to come more easily to you. Ease is welcome, and it can also go unnoticed or untested; it was never a promise that nothing will be hard.
- **“Uranus, Neptune and Pluto are the new malefics.”** They were discovered long after the scheme was drawn, and nothing in it covers them. Each has its own function, and none is a threat.
