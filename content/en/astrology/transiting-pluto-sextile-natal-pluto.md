Pluto never returns to its birth place within a human life: a full circle takes about 248 years. The first step of its cycle that people do meet is the sextile, 60° on, and everyone born between 1930 and 2009 meets it once, somewhere between their early twenties and mid-thirties.

## What changes {#what-changes}

Natal [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) is where you go deepest: what you hold on to, what you can't do by halves, where you can let something end and begin again. The same function in the sky, 60° on, reaches it through a [sextile](/astrology/sextile/), an opening that cooperates and asks to be used. Under this [transit](/astrology/transits/), changing something at depth becomes possible by choice rather than by necessity, often for the first time in adult life: leaving a pattern you adopted to get through childhood, taking real responsibility for your own strength, choosing what to transform rather than waiting for it to break.

Pluto's blind spot here is confusing survival adaptation with permanent identity. The habits that once kept you safe can feel like who you are; the sextile is the first season when you can test that, quietly, and keep only what still serves.

Because everyone born within a year or two of you shares it at nearly the same age, the transit describes a generation's coming of age as much as a person's. What makes it yours is the house your Pluto sits in and the planets it touches at birth. With Pluto in your 10th, the sextile comes from around your 12th or your 8th ([Pluto transiting the 8th house](/astrology/pluto-transit-8th-house/)): something reworked in private, or with someone close, prepares a deeper change in your public role. With Pluto close to your Moon or Sun at birth, the same years sextile those too. Your Pluto's house comes from your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

With my orbs, from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the sextile lasts about two years and eight months, twenty-two months at the quickest, with three exact hits in about three passes out of five and five in the rest.

Everyone born between 1930 and 2009 meets it once, between 21.5 and 36.1, and the ages trace Pluto's uneven pace. For births on 1 January it came within orb at 33.8 in 1935, 29.9 in 1945, 26.8 in 1955, 24 in 1965 and 23.1 in 1975, then 24.1 in 1985 and 27.2 in 1995, and comes at 31.3 for 2005. Pluto's orbit is so stretched that it spends about 12 years in some signs and about 31 in others, and it moved fastest around its closest approach to the Sun, in 1989. Born in the 1970s, your first 60° ran through those quick years; born in the 1930s, through slow ones ([generational placements by birth year](/astrology/generational-placements-by-birth-year/)).

Pluto's years in Aquarius, until early 2044, bring the sextile to people born from 1995 to 2008, with [Pluto in Sagittarius](/astrology/pluto-in-sagittarius/), between about 26 and 34.

The sextile opens a sequence. The square ([Transiting Pluto square natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-pluto-square-natal-pluto/)), the [Pluto square](/astrology/pluto-square/), follows between about 34½ and 59; the trine ([Transiting Pluto trine natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-pluto-trine-natal-pluto/)) from about 48½, for all but about 1 person in 14; the opposition ([Transiting Pluto opposite natal Pluto](/astrology/transiting-pluto-opposite-natal-pluto/)) after 80 for everyone, in their eighties for those born before about 1960 and beyond a lifetime for most others.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Choose one pattern to change, and change it deliberately: a way of reacting, a dependence, a fear you organised your life around.

Start where your Pluto sits. The house is where the opening is widest.

Talk to people your age. Your whole year group shares this sextile, and comparing notes makes it less private and less heavy.
