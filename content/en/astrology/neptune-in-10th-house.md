Work isn't only work for you: it's supposed to mean something, and people sense it. That is Neptune in the 10th house, the planet of dreams at the top of your chart, where everyone can see it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Your public life lives in [the 10th house](/astrology/10th-house/): work, standing, reputation, what you want to build in the world, a direction more than a job title. It's angular, its natural sign is Capricorn and its natural ruler Saturn. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) at the top of the chart turns ambition into vocation. You may want work that serves a vision, art, care, a cause, and struggle to commit to a role that doesn't. Your public image can be luminous and a little blurred: people see a healer, an artist, a dreamer, and not always the person who does the hours.

The sign Neptune was in belongs to everyone born around the same years; the 10th house is where yours goes public. In Placidus, the house system I use, the 10th begins at the Midheaven, where the zodiac crosses the meridian, which isn't the point straight overhead. It moves quickly with the birth time, so a Neptune near it can fall in [Neptune in the 9th house](/astrology/neptune-in-9th-house/) instead, depending on a few minutes ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). One house further on, [Neptune in the 11th house](/astrology/neptune-in-11th-house/) takes the dream into friendship and groups.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

The vocation can follow you home: partners may share the dream of your work, or feel they compete with it. You might idealise a partner's career or public role, or be loved for a public image that's easier to admire than your private self. Family hopes about your career can weigh here as well, since the 10th is the house of what others see you achieve. The opposite house, the 4th, is home, and a relationship does well here when it gives you somewhere private to be just a person, off duty.

## At work {#at-work}

Everything in this placement points to work. Arts and media, music, film, photography, the caring professions, charities, spiritual work, anything with a mission can suit it, as can work behind a public image, design, branding, performance. A career may change direction more than once as the vision changes. The risk is a job you idealised from outside, an institution that uses your dedication, or a path so open that you never commit to a step.

Saturn, the 10th house's natural ruler, is one of [the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/) far more often in my [notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), so with Neptune here I always look at where your Saturn sits: it shows how the dream gets a structure. Neptune itself was checked like every placement, wasn't one of the three, and overall about a third held.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The vocation you imagine and the job in front of you rarely match, and the gap is where this placement hurts. Ambition can dissolve when work feels pointless, and a reputation can blur when people pin their hopes on you. You may feel you ought to be doing something more meaningful, whatever you're doing. Comparing your path with the tidier careers around you can make a rich, winding working life look like failure, which it isn't. And a public role built on other people's dreams is tiring to keep up.

## What helps {#what-helps}

A structure for the calling helps most: a plan, a mentor, one concrete step a month. Say clearly what your work is and isn't, so that people project less. Find colleagues who share the mission, so the vision isn't carried alone. Let a steady job fund the dream if it needs to; that's not a betrayal. And measure a career by what it gives, not by how straight the line is.
