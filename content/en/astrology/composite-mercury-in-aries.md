Some pairs settle a whole weekend in the time it takes to finish a coffee. Composite Mercury in Aries writes that tempo into the relationship's own chart: a bond that thinks by speaking, decides by starting, and seldom leaves a problem unnamed for long.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) moves each planet to the halfway point between its places in two birth charts, and its [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is the relationship's mind: how the two of you take in news together, weigh it, decide, and tell other people what you've decided. In [Aries](/astrology/aries/), cardinal fire, that mind runs at the speed of a first reaction. Of two million pairs of birth dates the site drew at random from 1930 to 2009, about 1 pair in 13 has it.

Such a bond tends to think out loud. A question gets its answer the same evening; the trip is booked while it's still an idea; the awkward subject comes up in the car and is settled before the next junction. Its messages are short and practical, more *leaving now* than *how do you feel about Sunday?* Neither of you needs a quick mind for this. Two careful people can find that together they decide faster than either would alone, because this Mercury belongs to the pair. Your own [Mercury in Aries](/astrology/mercury-in-aries/), if you have it, describes one mind; the composite describes the conversation the two of you make.

[Mars](/astrology/mars/), which rules Aries, is a midpoint in the composite as well, and its sign and house show where the bond's quick decisions turn into action. Mercury itself has neither a home nor a weakness in Aries, so there's no dignity to weigh: the sign gives the relationship's thinking its manner, direct and self-starting, and nothing more.

In the sky, Mercury never strays more than about 28° from the Sun. Composites keep to that in about 17 pairs out of 18, which usually puts the composite Sun in Pisces, Aries or Taurus beside this Mercury. The rest is arithmetic: each midpoint is worked out on its own, and in about 1 pair in 26 the composite Sun lands near the opposite point, around Libra, a pairing no birth chart allows and nothing to read a message into.

The difficulty is the second conversation. A relationship that decides on the spot can skip the part where a decision is looked at again, and the slower of you may only find the objection once the tickets are bought. Speed isn't certainty. One rule helps: anything bigger than a weekend waits a night. So does letting the quieter of you speak first on the questions that matter. The bond's frankness is worth keeping; it only needs a pause built in.

Its neighbours think differently. [Composite Mercury in Pisces](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-pisces/) understands a great deal without spelling it out, and [composite Mercury in Taurus](/astrology/composite-mercury-in-taurus/) takes its time and holds to what it decides. Aries is the one that says it first.

Composites aren't part of my readings. I describe the technique as astrologers use it, beside both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), where the Mercury contacts between your charts show how each of you talks to the other. A relationship this direct would want its chart discussed the same way: openly, with both of you agreeing before anyone draws it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
