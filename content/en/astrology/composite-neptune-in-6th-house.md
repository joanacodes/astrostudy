For some pairs, the shopping list is a kind of love letter. A composite Neptune in the 6th house puts the relationship's dream into its ordinary days: the chores, the routines, the work done for others.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

[The composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) has a working week of its own: built from the midpoints of two birth charts, it describes the habits of the relationship rather than those of either person. Composite [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) is where those habits take on the colour of an ideal. [The 6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) holds the working day: routines, chores, shared tasks, service to others, and the care a pair gives its own wellbeing. With Neptune here, routine becomes ritual. The two of you may cook as if it were a ceremony, keep a shared practice of walking, yoga or gardening, volunteer together, or work side by side at something you both feel called to. Care flows easily, towards each other and towards anyone who needs it.

Its old name, Bad Fortune, and the joy Mars was said to take in it ([planetary joys](/astrology/planetary-joys/)) are labels from another age, not verdicts on a couple. Its difficulty here is roles that blur. A bond that idealises service can stop noticing who does what: one partner drifts into carrying the household, or into looking after the other full time, and neither remembers deciding it. Being needed isn't the same as being loved, and devotion that only runs one way stops being devotion. A plain division of tasks, said out loud and revisited now and then, keeps the care mutual.

The 6th was also, in the old books, the house of illness. A composite 6th predicts no illness for either of you, and I never read a chart for one ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)); if one of you is unwell, the right door is a doctor's. What this house does describe is how the relationship looks after its days, and, in this case, how gently it goes about it.

Alone in a birth chart, [Neptune in the 6th house](/astrology/neptune-in-6th-house/) describes one person who needs meaning in daily work; across two charts, [their Neptune in your 6th house](/astrology/neptune-in-6th-house-synastry/) is one partner giving the other's routine a sense of purpose. The composite 6th is the pair's shared day, and its ideal is held in common.

The arithmetic is unforgiving here. A birth time out by half an hour moves that chart's cusps by about 7½° on average, and the composite's cusps by about half that, nearly 4°: enough to carry this Neptune into the 5th, or across the composite Descendant into the 7th. The choice of method moves them again ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Both exact times, then, before anyone talks about this house.

Its neighbours look elsewhere for the ideal: [composite Neptune in the 5th house](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-5th-house/) in play and romance, [composite Neptune in the 7th house](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-7th-house/) in partnership itself. I write about composites without drawing them, and those who do draw them read this house with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/): as a description of habits, never a forecast.
