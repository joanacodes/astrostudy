Saturn conjunct Spica is the rarest contact this star receives: about 1 person in 332 born between 1930 and 2009 has it. Saturn's slow round brought it to Spica's degree, late in Libra, in only two seasons of those eighty years, and the second visit lasted barely more than a fortnight.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

I read [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) as time made useful: discipline, responsibility, and the kind of mastery that only comes from doing something for years. [Spica](/astrology/spica/), the star of the Virgin's wheat, has a long history with builders as well as farmers. R. H. Allen reported that a temple to Menat at Thebes, built around 3200 BC, was laid out to line up with Spica, and that over the centuries precession slowly moved the star away from the line its builders had chosen ([precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/)). It is a very Saturnian story: a structure set true to the sky, and time quietly changing what true means.

In the tropical zodiac Spica sits late in Libra, so this is always [Saturn in Libra](/astrology/saturn-in-libra/), the sign of Saturn's [exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/) in the tradition: the place where the old books thought its sense of measure and law worked best. Read without the star, that is a Saturn that weighs obligations carefully and keeps promises because they were fair to begin with.

The first visit was long ([Saturn retrograde](/astrology/saturn-retrograde/)). Saturn crossed the degree on 26 November 1952, came back over it on 25 April 1953 while retrograde, turned forward again on 24 June two and a half degrees short of the star, and made its last crossing on 22 August 1953. The second was a near miss followed by a quick pass. On 31 January 1982 Saturn stopped and turned back about a degree and a third before the star; when it came forward again that autumn it crossed only once, on 6 October, and was clear by the 13th. The next visit, in 2011–12, fell just outside the eighty years counted here, with crossings on 16 November 2011, 17 May 2012 and 4 August 2012.

In the sky they came fairly close. Spica lies a couple of degrees south of the ecliptic and Saturn ran a couple of degrees north of it here, so the two passed four to five degrees apart, near enough to take in at a glance.

If one part of this contact deserves your trust, it is the planet. In my notebook, Saturn, Chiron and Lilith held 75 to 90% of the time, far above everything else ([The three that held: Saturn, Chiron and Lilith](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)), and no star was ever on my list, and none is drawn in any chart I read ([fixed stars in astrology](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/)). What I'd read here is Saturn in Libra, its house and its aspects.

Saturn kept its own calendar even on this degree. In October 1982 it left the window on the 13th, three days before the [Sun conjunct Spica](/astrology/sun-conjunct-spica/) arrived and a week before [Venus conjunct Spica](/astrology/venus-conjunct-spica/), so no birth that autumn has both. The [Moon conjunct Spica](/astrology/moon-conjunct-spica/) and [Mars conjunct Spica](/astrology/mars-conjunct-spica/) come round far more often, the Moon every month and Mars every two years or so. How the tradition counts any of them is the subject of [conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/).
