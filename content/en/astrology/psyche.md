Psyche was the sixteenth asteroid ever found, and the first to be written with a number rather than a picture. Its symbol, which never caught on, was a butterfly's wing topped by a star, because in Greek *psyche* means both the soul and the butterfly. A spacecraft named after it is on its way there now.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Annibale de Gasparis found it from Naples on 17 March 1852, three years after his first discovery, [Hygiea](/astrology/hygiea/). The first asteroids had each been given a symbol, like the planets, but by then there were too many to draw. Johann Franz Encke suggested a number in a circle instead, and when the American astronomer J. Ferguson published his observations of the new asteroid in 1852, he wrote it as ⑯: the first to carry the new kind of mark.

It's a large body, about 222 km across, and the biggest of the M-type asteroids, the ones rich in metal. For years it was taken for the bare iron core of a young planet whose outer layers had been knocked away; observations reported in 2020 cast doubt on that, and its density suggests metal mixed with rock. It goes round the Sun in about five years, in the main belt. NASA launched a spacecraft named after it on 13 October 2023, due to arrive in 2029: the first visit to a metal-rich asteroid. Two large craters on it have been provisionally named after witches from the very book that tells Psyche's story.

That book is Apuleius's Latin novel *Metamorphoses*, also called *The Golden Ass*, from the 2nd century. Psyche, a mortal, becomes the wife of Cupid, the god the Greeks called [Eros](/astrology/eros/) and the Romans also [Amor](/astrology/amor/). She breaks his trust, loses him, and wins him back through trials set by his mother, Venus, until the gods make her immortal. Her name has also been translated as life, spirit and breath, and in Homer the word means the ghosts of the dead.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Astrologers who use Psyche take its meaning from that tale: the soul in her name, the trials of trust in her story. Its five-year orbit makes it a steady traveller through the zodiac, without the sudden swings of the near-Earth asteroids, so its house and its close contacts are what anyone using it would treat as personal, and the house needs an exact birth time.

Before borrowing an asteroid for the soul, it's worth knowing that old astrology had already placed it. Ptolemy gave the quality of the soul two chapters of his *Tetrabiblos* (book III), and read its reasoning part from [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) and its sensitive, unreasoning part from the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) ([Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos](/astrology/ptolemys-tetrabiblos/)). The soul wasn't missing from the chart; it was spread across two planets.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

My readings leave asteroids out, Psyche with them, and when I [checked my chart like an invoice](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), line by line, Psyche wasn't on the list. Mercury and the Moon were. In the notebook, Mercury held about a third of the time, as the Sun sign and the rising sign did; the Moon, checked like everything else, didn't stand out the way Saturn, Chiron and Lilith did. That's the honest footing for anything I say about your mind or your feelings.

A word of care, too, because of the name. A chart describes how you tend to think and feel; it doesn't assess anyone's mental health, and no placement, Psyche's included, is a symptom ([astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/)). If you'd like to try Psyche in your own chart, let Mercury and the Moon speak first, and give the asteroid a voice only where it sits close to one of them ([How to use asteroids without drowning the chart](/astrology/how-to-use-asteroids-without-drowning-the-chart/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Psyche shows your soul.”** It's a metal-rich rock named after a soul. What your chart says about your inner life comes from its planets.
- **“Psyche and Eros together mean a soulmate.”** Two asteroids from one story make a pretty coincidence in a chart, not a promise.
- **“NASA's mission will reveal what Psyche means in charts.”** It will tell us what the asteroid is made of, which is astronomy.
- **“Psyche was always a goddess.”** In the tale she's a mortal woman, made immortal only at the end.
