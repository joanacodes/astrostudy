Before the telescope, astrology's sky held seven moving lights: the Sun, the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn. Everything else, the fixed stars, turned together night after night. Those seven are what the old texts mean by the planets, and most of traditional astrology comes in sevens because of them.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The word planet comes from the Greek for wanderer. To an ancient observer, a planet was any light that changed its place among the stars, which is why the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) and the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) were counted: they wander more visibly than anything else. Today astrologers usually call those two the [luminaries](/astrology/luminaries/), and keep the word planet for the rest. Ranked from brightest to dimmest, the seven run Sun, Moon, Venus, Jupiter, Mercury, Mars and Saturn.

Ranked by speed instead, from slowest to fastest, they run Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, the Sun, Venus, Mercury and the Moon: the [Chaldean order](/astrology/chaldean-order/), which ordered the heavens for ancient astronomy and the hours of the day for astrology.

Three more came much later, with telescopes and calculation: Uranus in 1781, Neptune in 1846, Pluto in 1930. By then, Ptolemy's rules for the seven were more than fifteen centuries old.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Almost every traditional system has seven places, one per planet:

- **Rulership.** The Sun rules Leo and the Moon Cancer. Each of the five others rules two signs: Mercury Gemini and Virgo, Venus Taurus and Libra, Mars Aries and Scorpio, Jupiter Sagittarius and Pisces, Saturn Capricorn and Aquarius. Twelve signs share seven rulers ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)).
- **Exaltation.** Each of the seven has one sign where it is exalted: the Sun Aries, the Moon Taurus, Mercury Virgo, Venus Pisces, Mars Capricorn, Jupiter Cancer, Saturn Libra. The outer planets were never given one.
- **Sect.** Ptolemy counts the Sun, Jupiter and Saturn as day planets and the Moon, Venus and Mars as night ones; Mercury belongs to the day when it rises before the Sun and to the night when it rises after ([sect: day and night charts](/astrology/sect-day-and-night-charts/)). Medieval astrologers refined this: a planet of the chart's sect, in a sign that matches its sect and on the side of the horizon its sect prefers, with the Sun for a day planet and away from it for a night one, was said to be in [hayz](/astrology/hayz/).
- **Benefics and malefics.** Jupiter and Venus were the benefics, Saturn and Mars the malefics: tradition's words, and the old books' verdicts rather than mine ([benefics and malefics](/astrology/benefics-and-malefics/)).
- **Time.** Each day of the week and each hour of the old astrological day belongs to one of the seven ([planetary days](/astrology/planetary-days/)).

The system holds together because it was designed as a whole, which is also why the outer planets don't slot into it. There is no eighth day, no eighth sect, no spare exaltation.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I practise modern tropical astrology with traditional techniques, so the seven do two jobs in my readings. They are seven of the ten planets I read in a chart, each by sign, house and aspects. And they are the backbone of the old rules I keep. For Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces, I read both rulers, the traditional one and the modern one: Mars and Pluto, Saturn and Uranus, Jupiter and Neptune. Profections and zodiacal releasing, two of the timing techniques in my forecasts, come from the tradition too, and in the old texts every year or chapter they mark goes to the ruler of a sign, which means to one of the seven.

The outer three move so slowly that whole generations share their signs, so I read them mostly through the houses, which need a birth time.

My notebook has its own say. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), the slowest and the dimmest of the seven, was one of the [three placements that held best](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/), 75 to 90% of the time, with Chiron and Lilith, while overall about a third of what I checked held. That's why Saturn gets the closest reading of the seven ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Traditional astrology ignores the outer planets because it's outdated.”** It was built before they were found, and many traditional astrologers keep it that way on purpose. Modern tropical astrology adds them; I use both.
- **“The Sun and the Moon aren't planets, so the count is wrong.”** In the old sense of wanderer, they are the two most obvious planets of all.
- **“Seven is a mystical number astrologers chose.”** Seven is simply what the naked eye sees moving against the stars.
- **“Every sign has its own planet.”** In the tradition, five planets rule two signs each; only the Sun and Moon rule one.
