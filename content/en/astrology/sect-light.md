Every chart has two lights, and only one of them is in charge. Tradition hands the lead to the light whose time it is: the Sun if you were born by day, the Moon if you were born by night.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The sect light, or luminary of the sect, follows from [sect](/astrology/sect-day-and-night-charts/): it is the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) in a day chart and the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) in a night chart. It heads its sect as a captain heads a team, the Sun with Jupiter and Saturn, the Moon with Venus and Mars, while Mercury joins whichever side its phase puts it on. Each team includes one benefic and one malefic, so the sect light also settles which of tradition's two pairs is on home ground ([Benefic and malefic of the sect](/astrology/benefic-and-malefic-of-the-sect/)).

Only the Sun's position decides which light leads. In a night chart the Moon leads whether or not it is above the horizon, and whatever its phase: born at night close to a new moon, with both lights below the earth and the Moon too near the Sun to be seen, you still have the Moon as your sect light. In a day chart the Moon may be up in the sky as well, as it often is on an afternoon around the first quarter, and the Sun still leads.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The sect light is where the best-known lot begins. The [Part of Fortune](/astrology/part-of-fortune/) takes the distance from one light to the other and lays it off from the Ascendant: by day from the Sun to the Moon, and by night, for astrologers who reverse it, from the Moon to the Sun. Either way the count starts at the light in charge. The [Part of Spirit](/astrology/part-of-spirit/) runs the other way, from the other light to the sect light, and the rest of the [Hermetic lots](/astrology/hermetic-lots/) are built on those two.

Not everyone counted that way. Ptolemy measured Fortune from the Sun to the Moon in day and night births alike, and his authority carried that method through much of medieval and Renaissance astrology, while most Hellenistic astrologers reversed it at night. The two methods agree for a day birth; for a night birth they give two points mirrored either side of the Ascendant.

And through the lots, the sect light reaches into timing: [zodiacal releasing](/astrology/zodiacal-releasing/), which I use in forecasts, unfolds the chapters of a life from the Lot of Spirit and its kin.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read the two lights together ([luminaries](/astrology/luminaries/)), and the sect light tells me which one sets the key. In a day chart, the Sun's sense of purpose tends to lead and the Moon's needs follow it; in a night chart, the needs come first and the purpose works through them. The other light keeps its whole meaning, and its sign.

I'm also careful about what I claim for it. In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), the Sun sign held about a third of the time, and the Moon went through the same checks without being one of the three placements that held far more often. The notebook has no figure for day births against night births, so I can't tell you the sect light held better, and I don't pretend it did.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The sect light is whichever light is above the horizon.”** Only the Sun's position counts. At night, the Moon leads even from below the horizon.
- **“A new moon can't lead a night chart.”** The Moon leads by night in every phase, the dark one included.
- **“Every ancient astrologer reversed the lots at night.”** Ptolemy didn't, and many later astrologers followed him; chart programs still differ.
- **“Your sect light is your real sign.”** It shows which light leads. Your Sun sign and your Moon sign stay what they are.
