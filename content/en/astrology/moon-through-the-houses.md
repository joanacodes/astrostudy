The slow planets take years to cross one of your houses. The Moon crosses all twelve every month. Its round is the quickest rhythm in your chart, a change of emphasis every couple of days, and that is exactly how to take it: as a mood, not a season.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

The Moon moves about 13° a day and goes round the zodiac in about 27⅓ days. On the way, it passes through each house of your birth chart, the twelve areas of life counted from your Ascendant, and spends about two and a half days in each. The Moon, in the system I built for my readings, regulates feelings, needs, habits and the sense of being safe, so its passage through a house is a short stretch when those gather around that area of life.

Houses aren't all the same size. In Placidus, the house system I use, they stretch and shrink with the birth time and the latitude: in charts drawn at London's latitude they run from about 16° to about 61°, at New York's from about 20° to about 48°. So at London the Moon can cross a narrow house in a day and a quarter and linger nearly five days in a wide one. Where your houses begin and how wide they are comes from your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The Moon reaches your houses in their order, 1st to 12th, then starts again. Each stay touches the same kind of thing every month:

1. **[1st house](/astrology/1st-house/):** feelings close to the surface; how you come across follows your mood.
2. **[2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/):** what you own and what steadies you; comfort taken in familiar things.
3. **[3rd house](/astrology/3rd-house/):** messages, errands, neighbours and siblings; a talkative, curious couple of days.
4. **[4th house](/astrology/4th-house/):** home and family; the pull to stay in and gather yourself.
5. **[5th house](/astrology/5th-house/):** play, pleasure, making things for the joy of it.
6. **[6th house](/astrology/6th-house/):** routines, daily tasks, looking after what keeps a life running.
7. **[7th house](/astrology/7th-house/):** one-to-one relationships; other people's moods register more.
8. **[8th house](/astrology/8th-house/):** trust, intimacy, what you share and owe; feelings that run deeper.
9. **[9th house](/astrology/9th-house/):** a wish for perspective, for learning or for somewhere else.
10. **[10th house](/astrology/10th-house/):** work and your public role; feelings more on show.
11. **[11th house](/astrology/11th-house/):** friends, groups and shared plans.
12. **[12th house](/astrology/12th-house/):** rest, retreat and a quieter inner life.

Tradition gave the 5th house children, the 6th illness, the 8th death and the 12th confinement. A two-day Moon predicts none of those, and neither does any transit ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)).

Planets in a house when you were born are the first the Moon meets there: crossing your natal Venus or Saturn, it lights that planet for a few hours, and the day may carry its colour.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

The Moon's monthly round is the one transit everyone can watch: any calendar gives the Moon's sign each day, and your chart tells you which house that sign falls in. It can be a gentle way to get to know your houses, noticing over a few months which stays feel like anything at all.

It isn't something I time by. Transits, as I use them, mean the slow planets, whose stays in a house last a year or more; the Moon's passes are texture inside those seasons, the way a few days of rain sit inside a climate. Once a month the Moon also reaches the exact degree it held at your birth, and the chart for that moment is the [lunar return](/astrology/lunar-return/), which astrologers who use it read as the tone of the month.

It is weather, and the shortest kind: worth noticing, never worth rearranging a week around.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Moon in your 7th house is a bad time for relationships.”** It brings other people's moods closer for a day or two. It says nothing about how anything will go.
- **“Every house gets exactly the same time.”** Placidus houses vary in size, so the Moon may spend a day and a quarter in one and nearly five in another.
- **“You can follow it with your Sun sign.”** Houses come from the Ascendant, so a Sun-sign calendar puts the Moon in the wrong part of most people's charts.
- **“The Moon in the 12th house means trouble.”** Tradition called the 12th the Bad Spirit. In a reading it's rest and retreat, and a two-day Moon there is a quiet stretch, nothing more.
