Mars opens Scorpio as it opens Aries: the first face of each of its two signs is its own. Here the planet the tradition gave Scorpio meets Pluto, the ruler modern tropical astrology added after 1930.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

After Jupiter's face at the end of Libra ([Libra, third decan](/astrology/libra-third-decan/)), the [Chaldean order](/astrology/chaldean-order/) comes back to Mars, so the first face of [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/) is Mars's ([faces](/astrology/faces/)). From here Scorpio's faces repeat those of Aries exactly, Mars, then the Sun ([Scorpio, second decan](/astrology/scorpio-second-decan/)), then Venus: twenty-one faces separate the two signs, three full rounds of the seven planets.

The modern decans give the first ten degrees of a sign to its own ruler, and Scorpio has two, Mars in the tradition and Pluto in modern tropical astrology; as with every sign that has an old ruler and a new one, I read both ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)). Mars therefore rules this decan in either system, and [Mars in Scorpio](/astrology/mars-in-scorpio/) between 0° and 10° adds its own face to its traditional home.

Pluto brings a slower question to the same degrees. Mars asks what needs doing; Pluto asks what has to end before something else can begin.

## Its image {#its-image}

Wallis Budge's list of the Egyptian decans sets Khentet-khert against these degrees, a neighbour of the Khentet-ḥert that closes Libra. [Firmicus Maternus](/astrology/firmicus-maternus/) gives Sentacer or Asente, and the first of those names was still being copied twelve centuries later, in the list of the scholar Joseph Scaliger. Cosmas of Maiuma assigns the decan to the Nymphs, the minor goddesses of Greek folklore who each belonged to one spring, one mountain or one tree.

I like that last image for Scorpio more than I can justify: spirits bound to a single place, the way this sign binds itself to a single person or question. I keep it as a picture, not a rule.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun crosses these degrees from about 23 October to 2 November, so a [Sun in Scorpio](/astrology/sun-in-scorpio/) born in the last week of October or the first days of November belongs here. With Mars holding the decan twice over, I read Scorpio's depth here as something that acts: the feeling goes all the way down, and then it does something about it. Mars itself, placed here, stands in its traditional home and its own face, and I read it as effort that knows its ground: aimed, sustained, hard to distract.

[Scorpio rising](/astrology/scorpio-rising/) in its first decan gives the chart two rulers to follow, Mars and Pluto. These ten degrees take almost an hour to rise in Paris or London, which sounds generous, but a birth time still has to be right to within half an hour to put the Ascendant here rather than in late Libra or in the next decan ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

[Pluto in Scorpio](/astrology/pluto-in-scorpio/), at home in the modern scheme, entered these degrees in November 1983 and left them for good in the summer of 1988, after two loops back. Everyone born in between carries it, and its house is what makes it theirs. Neptune had crossed the same stretch between late 1955 and 1961, and Uranus between late 1974 and 1977.
