Secondary progressions rest on one quiet idea: the days just after your birth describe the years of your life. The sky a week after you were born speaks for your seventh year; the sky a month after, for your thirtieth. It's a symbolic clock, and astrologers use it to describe the slow changes that happen from the inside.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A progressed chart is a real chart, cast for a real day, the day as many days after your birth as you are years old, and then read as if it belonged to the present year. Because each day stands for a year, everything moves very slowly. The progressed Sun moves about a degree a year, so it changes sign roughly every 30 years. The progressed Moon moves about a degree a month: it spends two to two and a half years in each sign, usually about two years and four months, and goes round the whole zodiac in about 27 years ([the progressed Moon](/astrology/progressed-moon/)).

Mercury, Venus and Mars can move anywhere from a few degrees to a few signs over a lifetime, and one born close to a standstill may turn retrograde or direct by progression. The slow planets hardly move at all. The angles are progressed too, but astrologers do it in more than one way, so two programs can give two progressed Midheavens.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

Take your age in years, count that many days after your birth, and cast the chart for that day, at your birth time and place: at 35, the chart for 35 days after birth. In practice, a program does it from your birth data, and shows the progressed planets around your natal chart so that you can see what they touch.

Then you read three things:

- **Signs and houses.** When a progressed planet changes sign or house, the emphasis of that function shifts. The progressed Sun's change of sign is rare and slow; the progressed Moon's comes every couple of years.
- **Aspects to the natal chart.** A progressed planet reaching an aspect to a natal point, within about a degree, marks a stretch when the two functions work together. For the progressed Sun, a degree is about a year either side.
- **The progressed Sun and Moon together.** Their cycle, from one progressed new moon to the next, takes about 29½ years, and astrologers read its phases as chapters ([progressed lunar phases](/astrology/progressed-lunar-phases/)).

The birth time matters more than it seems. The Moon moves about half a degree an hour, so an hour's error in your birth time shifts the progressed Moon by about half a degree, two weeks or so of its progress, and moves the progressed angles much further ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

Progressions describe development: how your sense of purpose, your needs and your way of relating ripen, slowly, from the inside. I read a progressed Moon entering a new sign as a change in what you need to feel at home, and a progressed Sun meeting your natal Venus as what you value moving to the centre of your life for a year or two. Nothing arrives overnight. That's the method's nature: it shows gradual maturing, not sudden turns.

When I look at a year, progressions are one of the methods I use, with [transits](/astrology/transits/) from Jupiter to Pluto, Chiron and the true node, [solar arcs](/astrology/solar-arc-directions/), the [solar return](/astrology/solar-return/), profections and zodiacal releasing. Steven Forrest's *The Changing Sky* (1986) reads progressions and transits side by side, and that pairing is how I work too: transits as the weather that comes from outside, progressions as the season growing inside.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

Progressed planets don't move in the sky: the day-for-a-year is a symbol, applied to real positions, and it's worth keeping the two apart. Progressions don't predict events, and they never replace the birth chart or the transits; they add a slow layer to both.

They are also a family, not a single method. [Tertiary and minor progressions](/astrology/tertiary-and-minor-progressions/) use other units of time, [converse progressions](/astrology/converse-progressions/) count backwards from birth, and the progressed angles change with the method chosen. A progressed Midheaven depends on the method, and with it the timing of everything it touches.

And like every timing method I use, they are never read for health, death, pregnancy, money or legal outcomes ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).
