Sun sextile Neptune links the two across a sixth of the zodiac: close enough to help each other, far enough apart to stay themselves. It describes an imagination that's ready to serve your sense of purpose, and that tends to wait until you ask.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is your creative centre: the self you express, and the direction you put your energy into. [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) imagines and empathises; it widens the frame and senses what a situation could mean. Through a [sextile](/astrology/sextile/), they meet as an opportunity: cooperation that's available and responsive, and fully active only once you engage it.

In plain words, you have a way in to inspiration that doesn't cost you your footing. When you give your imagination a job, a piece of work, a cause, someone to look after, it turns up, and your own direction grows warmer and wider for it. Left unasked, it stays in the background: daydreams, music, a soft spot for people who struggle. Neither is wrong. But the opportunity in a sextile is real only when it's taken, and potential isn't a promise. I'd describe it as compassion that doesn't swallow you, and an ideal you can actually work with.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

In a chart, it tends to show as an easy relationship with the imaginative side of life: you can care without dissolving, and dream without losing the thread. A sextile usually joins signs two apart, whose elements complement each other. With Neptune in Scorpio, a Sun in Virgo or Capricorn puts careful, practical work in the service of something deeper; with Neptune in Capricorn, a Sun in Pisces or Scorpio gives a feeling nature some structure to dream into. The houses show which two areas of life the opening connects.

About 1 person in 11 is born with the Sun and Neptune within 8° of a sextile. That's twice as common as the conjunction, because the Sun sextiles Neptune twice a year: about two months after it passes Neptune, moving away from the meeting, and about two months before it passes it again. The closer to 60°, the clearer the opening.

My notebook counted placements, signs and houses, rather than aspects. Neptune was checked like the rest and wasn't one of the three that held far more often, while overall about a third of what I checked held ([the invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). So I hold this sextile lightly, and say so in a reading.

The pair's other aspects change the way the two meet:

- **[Sun conjunct Neptune](/astrology/sun-conjunct-neptune/):** no door to open; imagination and identity are fused from the start.
- **[Sun square Neptune](/astrology/sun-square-neptune/):** a step further round, the opening turns into friction between the self and the ideal.
- **[Sun trine Neptune](/astrology/sun-trine-neptune/):** the stronger soft link; the two work together without being asked.
- **[Sun opposite Neptune](/astrology/sun-opposite-neptune/):** across the chart, the ideal is often met in someone else.

Between two charts, [Sun sextile Neptune in synastry](/astrology/sun-sextile-neptune-synastry/) is one person's direction meeting the other's imagination as a friendly offer: gentle inspiration, easy to miss, and felt a little differently by each.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Neptune sextile natal Sun](/astrology/transiting-neptune-sextile-natal-sun/) is a slow, gentle opening. Neptune spends about fourteen years in a sign, so it lingers near the sextile to your Sun for a year or two, coming and going as it turns retrograde: a long stretch when imaginative or caring work comes more easily, if you go towards it. The Sun in the sky makes the same angle to your natal Neptune twice a year, for a day or so each time. Weather, not a forecast: an opening describes what's easier, never what will happen.
