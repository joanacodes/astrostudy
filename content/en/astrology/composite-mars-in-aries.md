The tent is up before either of you has finished saying *we should go camping*. With composite Mars in Aries, that speed is built into the chart drawn for the two of you: the bond moves first, wants openly and says what bothers it on the spot.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

A composite takes two birth charts and averages them, planet by planet, and its [Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the relationship's drive: how the pair starts things, competes, desires and defends what it has ([the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/) shows the arithmetic). [Aries](/astrology/aries/), cardinal fire, is Mars's own sign, its [domicile](/astrology/domicile/), so this drive works in its native manner, quick and self-starting. It's one of the scarcer composite Marses, found in about 1 pair in 15: Mars crosses Aries in about 40 days, one of its shorter stays, and the midpoints follow that rhythm.

The pace belongs to the bond. A Mars at 20° Pisces and one at 20° Taurus meet halfway at 20° Aries, so two people whose own drive is gentle or slow can still be, together, the first to volunteer and the first to leave. In one birth chart, [Mars in Aries](/astrology/mars-in-aries/) describes a person who goes first; here the relationship does, and that can surprise you both. Such a pair decides by doing: the flat gets painted the weekend it's mentioned, the game you play together turns into a race by the second round, and desire is shown rather than hinted at.

Disagreements keep the same tempo. They're voiced when they're felt, sometimes loudly, and they're usually over by dinner; a grudge doesn't come naturally to this Mars. The difficulty is the one of you who needs longer. A bond that settles everything in a single exchange can leave the slower person agreeing just to end it, and a quick truce isn't a settled question. It helps to make a second round normal: either of you can bring a quarrel back the next morning, calmly, without it counting as a new one. Competition works best aimed outwards, at a goal you chase side by side.

Heat isn't harm, though. If an argument ever leaves one of you afraid, that's beyond anything a chart can weigh: in the UK, call 999 in an emergency, or Samaritans on 116 123 to talk it through.

I don't draw composites myself; what's here is how the astrologers who do would read this one. They weigh it against the composite Venus, which shows what the pair is drawn to, and against the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/): a contact such as [Venus conjunct Mars in synastry](/astrology/venus-conjunct-mars-synastry/) tells who pursues whom, which a composite can't. Nothing in it can time a relationship. Mars moves less than a degree a day, so two birth dates place this one unless the midpoint sits within about half a degree of Pisces or Taurus; and since it describes two people, it's drawn only once both have agreed ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

Next door, the pairs differ. With [composite Mars in Pisces](/astrology/composite-mars-in-pisces/), a bond tends to take up someone else's cause before its own; with [composite Mars in Taurus](/astrology/composite-mars-in-taurus/), it builds slowly and holds its ground. In Aries it simply sets off.
