Taurus asks how something can be made to last. A composite Neptune there would give a relationship a dream of permanence, and in practice no couple alive carries it: Neptune left Taurus in 1889 and returns only in 2038.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), every planet of a relationship stands at the midpoint of its two natal positions, and the result is read as the bond's own chart, never as a portrait of either partner. Composite [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) is what that bond idealises: what the two people imagine together, the compassion that passes between them, the larger whole they feel part of. [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/), fixed earth ruled by [Venus](/astrology/venus/), would ask that ideal to take root and stay. A pair with this Neptune would dream of one life tended slowly and well: a house kept for decades, a garden worked through every season, a craft passed back and forth until neither remembers who started it. Its compassion would be the steady kind, the kind that keeps turning up.

The reason no couple alive has it is arithmetic. Neptune stays about fourteen years in a sign and needs about 165 to go round the zodiac, so a composite Neptune mostly records when two people were born ([generational placements by birth year](/astrology/generational-placements-by-birth-year/)): a pair of the same age gets its generation's sign, and an age gap sets the composite between two generations. In two million pairs drawn at random from the births of 1930 to 2009, none had it. Neptune was last in Taurus from June 1874 to March 1889, and its next stay runs from May 2038 to May 2052. The first couples to share this placement will be two people born during that stay, and none of them has been born yet.

In principle, Venus would be the planet to read beside it, since it rules Taurus: in a composite, Venus shows what a pair values and enjoys, and here it would show the shape the shared dream takes in the hands. In one person's chart, [Neptune in Taurus](/astrology/neptune-in-taurus/) holds the same dream for a single life, in a generation no longer alive and one still to come.

Taurus sits between two other signs that no adult pair has. [Composite Neptune in Aries](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-aries/) waits for two people born from 2025, babies today, and [composite Neptune in Gemini](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-gemini/) for pairs born from 2051. Between them, Taurus will belong to the couples born from 2038 to 2052.

Its shadow, on paper, is a dream that mistakes permanence for stillness: a bond so devoted to keeping things as they are that change feels like a betrayal, or that idealises its routines until nobody dares to question them. Those pairs don't exist yet, but the way they would be read can already be said: with both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), never as a promise of how long the bond will last, and only with both people's agreement ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)). Composites stay outside [how I read](/how-i-read/); I describe this one for couples whose parents may not have met yet.
