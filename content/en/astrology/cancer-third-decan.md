The third decan of Cancer is 20° to 30° of the sign, the last ten degrees of cardinal water, which the Sun crosses in the second half of July. The Moon holds both the sign and the face, and Pisces lends the decan its two rulers.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

The Chaldean order reaches the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) at the end of [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), so Lilly's [faces](/astrology/faces/) give the Moon a face inside its own sign. Of the Moon's five faces, this is the only one in a sign it rules: a [Moon in Cancer](/astrology/moon-in-cancer/) between 20° and 30° is at home twice over.

By element, the modern decans give the last ten degrees of a water sign to the ruler of the third water sign, Pisces, which has two: [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) in modern tropical astrology, [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) in the tradition. I read both. Jupiter adds a twist of its own, because it is exalted in Cancer: in the traditional reading, the decan goes to a planet the sign already honours.

So a planet here hears the Moon twice, by sign and by face, before anything else: care, protection, the need to feel safe. Then comes Pisces's pair. Through Neptune it reaches for imagination and compassion; through Jupiter, for growth and meaning. I look at your Moon first and longest, then at your Neptune and Jupiter, and only then let the decan add its shade.

## Its image {#its-image}

In Budge's list the decan is Kenmut, the middle one of three decans grouped around that name, between Ṭepā-Kenmut before it ([Cancer, second decan](/astrology/cancer-second-decan/)) and Kher-khept-Kenmut after it, at the start of Leo. The Egyptian list grouped neighbours in the same way around Saḥ, from late Taurus to the end of Gemini. The Greco-Egyptian names pair off too: Knum for this decan, Char-Knum for the next; the Greek Hermetic list writes Chnouphos.

Firmicus Maternus wrote Thiumis, or Thumus, and Joseph Scaliger Thuimis; the *Testament of Solomon* calls it Saphthorael. Cosmas of Maiuma put Hekate here, and Athanasius Kircher Mercophta ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun stands here for births from about 12 to 22 July ([Sun in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-cancer/)). In 2026 it reached 20° Cancer on 12 July at 07:46 UT and entered Leo on 22 July at 19:13 UT; in 2027, on 12 July at 13:27 UT and 23 July at 01:04 UT, so the 22nd and 23rd belong to one sign or the other depending on the year and the hour ([Leo, first decan](/astrology/leo-first-decan/)).

The two brightest stars of the constellation Gemini stand in the sign of Cancer, in this decan: [Castor](/astrology/castor/) at 20°36′ and [Pollux](/astrology/pollux/) at 23°34′ in 2026, followed by [Procyon](/astrology/procyon/), the Little Dog's star, at 26°08′.

Pluto spent most of the 1930s here, steadily from April 1932 to October 1937, with visits from 1930 and until 1939, so it is the Pluto of many people born in that decade ([generational placements by birth year](/astrology/generational-placements-by-birth-year/)). Uranus followed for births from August 1953 to August 1955, and Saturn passed in 1945–46, 1975–76 and 2004–05. The partial eclipse of the Moon on 12 January 2028 falls at 21°33′ Cancer, and the total eclipse of the Sun on 22 July 2028 at 29°50′, in the decan's last degree.

In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) the Moon, checked like every placement, wasn't one of the three that held far more often, and overall about a third held. Its double rule here makes the Moon the planet to read closely, not a certainty to lean on.
