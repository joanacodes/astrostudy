The Sun was climbing towards the top of the sky when you were born, in the most visible part of the chart. Here is a self that wants its life's work to say who it is, and that keeps having to tell purpose from position.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 10th house](/astrology/10th-house/) is public life: work, standing, reputation, what you want to build in the world. It's angular, beginning at the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) in Placidus and the other quadrant systems, and its natural sign is Capricorn. A [Sun](/astrology/sun/) here lives out its purpose in the open, through a vocation more than a job: the contribution you'd like to be known for. Two distinctions matter with this placement. Purpose isn't status, and recognition isn't fame. A 10th-house Sun wants its work acknowledged for what it is; it doesn't need a crowd. The Sun's sign tells you how the vocation sounds: an Aries Sun here wants to found something, a Pisces Sun to serve something.

In the house system I use, Placidus, a 10th-house Sun is a late-morning Sun, still climbing towards the highest point it reaches at the Midheaven. The Sun usually shares the Midheaven's sign or sits in the next one. Like every house, this one needs an exact [birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/). Its opposite is the [4th house](/astrology/4th-house/), home and roots: the private ground this public Sun stands on.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

A 10th-house Sun often comes with a calling attached, and whoever loves you meets both. What you're building is part of how you introduce yourself, and you want someone who respects the work without competing with it. The risk is that the relationship gets the hours left over, or that you bring your public self home and forget to take it off. The lesson is to let the people closest to you see the private person, doubts included, and to protect time that has nothing to do with achievement. Two callings under one roof can work very well, as long as both of you keep a little light back for home.

## At work {#at-work}

Work is the obvious setting, but think vocation rather than ladder. This Sun shines in roles where your own direction and the work's purpose line up: a profession you chose, a body of work, a public role you can stand behind. Recognition matters, and it lands best when it names what you contributed rather than the position you hold. Purpose fades in jobs that ask you to perform success instead of doing something you believe in.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

If the [Sun in the 11th house](/astrology/sun-in-11th-house/) can disappear into a group and the [Sun in the 9th house](/astrology/sun-in-9th-house/) into ideas, a 10th-house Sun can disappear into a role. Identity becomes the job title, and a setback at work feels like a verdict on who you are. Reputation can start to matter more than the work itself, while the private self goes hungry. A light set where everyone can see it needs somewhere to rest out of view.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Write down what your vocation is in your own words, without a single job title, and check your choices against it. Measure success by contribution rather than position. Keep evenings and weekends that are nobody's business. Celebrate private achievements as seriously as public ones. And read the sign on your Midheaven with the Sun's own sign: together they describe the work you're here to do, in your own manner.

Career is exactly where people want certainty from a chart, so here is my modesty clause. In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), about a third of what I checked held overall, the Sun sign included, against 75 to 90% for Saturn, Chiron and Lilith. A 10th-house Sun points a direction; what you build there is yours.
