The Sun in the 7th house was low in the west when you were born, about to set, facing the point that was rising. This is a self that finds its outline across a table: in the partner, the client, the rival, whoever stands opposite and answers back.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) holds partners and close one-to-one bonds: love and marriage, business partners, clients, and, in the old books, the open rival who faces you directly. It's angular, beginning at the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), and its natural sign is Libra, the sign of the Sun's fall. A house isn't a sign, and I don't read the Sun as weakened here, but the echo is worth hearing. A [Sun](/astrology/sun/) in the 7th does its work in encounters: the self learns its own shape from the person across from it.

I use Placidus, and in Placidus a 7th-house Sun was still above the western horizon at your birth, in the last hours of the afternoon. Because the house begins at the Descendant, the Sun usually sits in the Descendant's sign, the one opposite your rising sign, or in the sign after it. Near the cusp a few minutes decide, so the reading rests on an exact [birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/).

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Here relationships are the setting itself, not a side topic. You may feel most alive in the company of one other person, and come to know your own mind by talking it through with them. The well-known risk of a 7th-house Sun is giving your light away: choosing partners who seem to carry the direction or brightness you haven't yet claimed for yourself, then feeling dim beside them. The lesson is to keep your own light on in the room, and to choose someone who wants it on.

## At work {#at-work}

Where the [Sun in the 6th house](/astrology/sun-in-6th-house/) works beside colleagues and the [Sun in the 8th house](/astrology/sun-in-8th-house/) works with what others entrust to it, a 7th-house Sun works face to face. Consulting, counselling, negotiating, representing people, teaching one to one, any partnership or client work suits it. You often do your best thinking in dialogue. Recognition comes one person at a time, a client who returns, a partner who vouches for you, and builds into a reputation that way.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

The difficulty is needing someone opposite to know where you stand. Without a partner, a colleague or even an opponent to push against, your sense of direction can go slack, and you may stay in a relationship longer than it deserves because it keeps you defined. Compromise can harden into a habit of always meeting halfway, even when halfway isn't where you wanted to go. And because the old books put open rivals in this house too, you may find an opponent strangely clarifying, and keep one around for longer than is good for either of you.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Make some decisions alone, and live with them for a while before you discuss them. Keep a project that belongs only to you, so your purpose never depends on a partnership. Notice when you admire in someone else a quality you could grow yourself; that's a 7th-house Sun asking to be claimed. If you'd like to look at a partner's chart beside yours, ask them first: I never read a synastry without both people's consent ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

The Sun's house went through the same tick-or-cross test as everything else in [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/). Overall about a third of what I checked held, the Sun sign among it, while Saturn, Chiron and Lilith held 75 to 90%. So I read a 7th-house Sun as a strong theme in your life, never as a forecast of who you'll meet.
