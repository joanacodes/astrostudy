Gemini gathers the questions; Sagittarius goes after the answer. Your Moon and Sun sit at the two ends of that axis, and your birth came within a couple of days of a full moon, most often the full moon in Gemini, the last before the December solstice. Here is a mind that asks and a self that answers, each needing the other.

## How they get along {#how-they-get-along}

The signs are opposite, both mutable, air facing fire: an opposition by sign, which I read as a polarity to balance more than a fight. The [Sun in Sagittarius](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius/) organises itself around a few large convictions; the [Moon in Gemini](/astrology/moon-in-gemini/) steadies itself with a stream of small particulars, the remark overheard, the second opinion, the article read on the bus. Air feeds fire, and many people with the pair think best out loud, building a view from a dozen conversations and then testing it on the next.

The rulers make this axis unusual. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) rules Gemini and has its [detriment](/astrology/detriment/) in Sagittarius; [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) rules Sagittarius and has its detriment in Gemini. So each of your lights stands where the other's ruler is least at home: the Sun's planet of meaning is uneasy among the Moon's details, and the Moon's planet of words is uneasy amid the Sun's sweeping claims. As for the lights, these signs give them no dignity either way. I read the two rulers' signs and houses together, because this pair works best when Mercury and Jupiter are on speaking terms.

Your birth fell within about two and a half days of the full moon: the Moon was 151° to 209° from the Sun, twelve and a half to seventeen days after the new moon. The births split evenly: the [waxing gibbous moon](/astrology/waxing-gibbous-moon/) just before the opposition, the [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/) phase just after, so a few hours can change yours. Within a few degrees of 180°, you have a [Sun opposite Moon](/astrology/sun-opposite-moon/) as an aspect too. Most often the nearest full moon was the [full moon in Gemini](/astrology/full-moon-in-gemini/), which faces a low December Sun and so rides high through the long northern nights. [Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/) explains what turns at the full.

About 1 person in 151 is born with these two lights. If the Moon was leaving Taurus or reaching Cancer that day, your birth time chooses between this page, the [Sun in Sagittarius, Moon in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius-moon-in-taurus/) and the [Sun in Sagittarius, Moon in Cancer](/astrology/sun-in-sagittarius-moon-in-cancer/) ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). The rising sign comes from the same minute, and with the two lights it draws the outline the rest of the chart fills in.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Conversation is the first form of closeness for you, and candour the second. The Gemini Moon needs to talk things over, often and lightly; the Sagittarius Sun needs a partner whose views it can argue with and respect. A full-moon birth tends to meet itself in other people, so you may be drawn to someone who plays the half you play less: the specialist to your generalist, the planner to your improviser. It works when you swap roles now and then. What hurts is being half-listened to; say so, rather than talking faster.

## At work {#at-work}

Teaching, journalism, translation, guiding, publishing: work that moves between detail and meaning suits this pair, and so does any role that explains one world to another. The Moon gathers and connects; the Sun shapes the result into something with a point. Two mutable signs adapt quickly, which serves you in a changing field and tries your patience in a static one. Keep a project long enough to finish it, and let a colleague hold the thread on days when your attention goes wide.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Two adaptable signs looking straight at each other can see every side and settle on none. The Moon brings one more fact, the Sun builds one more theory to hold it, and the evening ends in a fine argument and no decision. Too many inputs can leave you wired rather than steadied. What helps is choosing: one question for the season, one book at a time, a notebook where the Moon's details land before the Sun draws conclusions. Some quiet, away from the news, lets both halves of the full moon rest.
