The good knives bought together after years of bad ones, a shelf of records that belongs to neither of you alone, a long-standing agreement about what deserves care and what doesn't: a relationship with composite Venus in the 2nd house knows what it values and likes to keep it close.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), the 2nd house begins at a cusp found in one of two ways: the midpoint of your two natal 2nd-house cusps, or a cusp computed from the composite Midheaven for the place where the pair lives. If both birth charts are cast in [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/), the house system I use for them, the first way averages two Placidus cusps and the second builds new ones. Either way needs both birth times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and a [Venus](/astrology/venus/) near the cusp can sit in the 1st by one method and the 2nd by the other.

[The 2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) is succedent, and Taurus, its natural sign, belongs to Venus, so the planet is on familiar ground here: resources, possessions, values, the sense of what something is worth. The bond's pleasure lies in having and keeping well: objects chosen with care, a home stocked with things you both like, skills and time treated as wealth held in common. People who quarrel about spending on their own can find that, as a pair, they agree on what deserves keeping. Born with [Venus in the 2nd house](/astrology/venus-in-2nd-house/), a person has a clear taste in what to own; [their Venus in your 2nd house](/astrology/venus-in-2nd-house-synastry/) touches your sense of worth; here the worth is jointly held.

The composite Sun is rarely far off. Venus can't stray more than about 47° from it at birth, and about 9 pairs in 10 keep that rule in the composite too, which usually leaves the Sun in this house or the next one along. The exceptions come from midpoints taken one at a time: when two birthdays are months apart, about 1 pair in 22 gets its composite Sun opposite this Venus, among the 8th house's shared resources ([composite Sun in the 8th house](/astrology/composite-sun-in-8th-house/)). The method makes that pairing; your bond doesn't.

The difficulty is letting things stand in for feeling. A bond that shows care through what it buys and keeps can start measuring affection in objects, or treat the comfort it has built as proof that all is well. Resources aren't wealth and values aren't prices, and no chart says anything about your income: I don't forecast money, and a composite can't either. It helps to name, now and then, what you value that can't be bought, and to let the things you own stay things.

[Composite Venus in the 1st house](/astrology/composite-venus-in-1st-house/) shows its warmth on arrival; [composite Venus in the 3rd house](/astrology/composite-venus-in-3rd-house/) spends it in conversation. Composites aren't drawn in my readings; their users weigh this house against both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). What two people hold in common includes their birth data, so a composite should wait until both have agreed to it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
