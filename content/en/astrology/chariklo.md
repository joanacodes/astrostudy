Chariklo is the largest centaur known, and in 2013 it gave astronomers a surprise nobody had predicted: rings, around a body only about 250 km across. In myth the name belongs to Chiron's wife.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Chariklo goes round the Sun every 62.5 years between the orbits of [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) and [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), from 13.1 to 18.4 times the Earth's distance, on an orbit close to, but not locked into, a steady rhythm with Uranus. It is a flattened body, roughly 288 by 270 by 198 km, with a dark, reddish surface of water ice, silicates, carbon and the complex organic compounds called tholins. Unlike [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/), it has never shown a comet's haze. Its stay among the giant planets is temporary, like every centaur's: simulations give it an even chance of leaving the region within the next 7 to 10 million years, and almost certainly it arrived within the last 20 million.

The Spacewatch survey picked it up at Kitt Peak, Arizona, on 15 February 1997; James V. Scotti made the observations, although the official credit goes to the survey. It was the seventh centaur found, four years after [Nessus](/astrology/nessus/), and its name was published on 28 September 1999, the same day as that of [Asbolus](/astrology/asbolus/), another Spacewatch centaur.

On 3 June 2013 Chariklo passed in front of a star, and the way the starlight dimmed revealed two narrow rings, about 386 and 400 km from its centre and some 14 km apart. Nobody had expected rings around so small a body, and nobody yet knows what stops them from spreading out within a few million years; unseen shepherd moons are one idea. The team nicknamed them Oiapoque and Chuí, after the rivers at either end of Brazil's coast. By 2025 rings had also been found around Chiron, [Haumea](/astrology/haumea/) and Quaoar.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The name belongs to a nymph, Chariclo, whose name may mean graceful spinner or graceful fame. She married the centaur Chiron and bore him children, Ocyrhoe among them, and with Chiron's mother, Philyra, she nursed the young Achilles. Some sources make her a sea nymph, or a centaur herself. Greek myth has a second Chariclo, a nymph devoted to Athena and the mother of the seer Tiresias: when Tiresias saw the goddess naked, he was struck blind, and his mother begged Athena to undo it. The goddess could not, and gave him the gift of prophecy instead.

In the late 1990s the German astrologer Robert von Heeren drew her a symbol from Chiron's key glyph, with a C where the K had been. Placed in a chart, Chariklo goes round the zodiac about once in a human lifetime, so the sign it held when you were born belongs to a run of birth years rather than to you; its house waits on the birth time ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and only a close contact with a personal planet or an angle would single it out.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

What I find most worth telling about Chariklo is what astronomers found, not what astrologers might read: rings around a 250-km body, which nobody predicted. Any meaning attached to them would be mine, not the sky's, and I'd rather not invent one.

In my readings, the couple part company. Chiron is one of the bodies I read, and in my notebook it was one of [the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/), with Saturn and Lilith, 75 to 90% of the time. Chariklo had no line there, and the system I built for my readings computes no centaur but Chiron ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). If you place her yourself, read her after the planets and only in tight contact ([How to use asteroids without drowning the chart](/astrology/how-to-use-asteroids-without-drowning-the-chart/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Only giant planets have rings.”** Chariklo was the first small body found with them, in 2013, and three more minor planets have joined it since.
- **“Chariklo was a centaur.”** In most sources she is a nymph, Chiron's wife; only some make her a centaur.
- **“Chiron's wife was the mother of Tiresias.”** Greek myth has two nymphs called Chariclo, Chiron's wife and Tiresias's mother; descriptions of the asteroid's namesake sometimes run them together.
