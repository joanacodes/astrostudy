Of all the ways Mercury and Pluto can meet, the sextile is the one most people are born with, and among the most useful. It gives an everyday mind a line down to the deep end, to be used when the moment asks for it.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) works things out and passes them on: it observes, analyses, learns and explains. [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) is the part that transforms and empowers: it clears out what's obsolete, integrates what was buried, and gives back a strength that's truly yours. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) offers them an opening: they work together as soon as something calls for it.

What this means in practice: you can go deep without living in the deep end. Most days your thinking can be light; then comes a hard conversation, a problem that won't yield or a book that asks for real attention, and you find you can get to the bottom of it. You tend to know which question unlocks a situation, and you can talk about difficult things without making them heavier. People often bring you what they can't say to anyone else, because you hear it calmly.

The sextile only works when it's engaged, though. Its depth stays unused if you keep to small talk, and potential isn't the same as practice. I read it as a tool for insight, sharper the more you use it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Pluto was in Virgo for most people born between 1958 and 1971, and a sextile to it then came from Mercury in Cancer or Scorpio, earth with water: a mind steeped in memory, or one that looks beneath the surface, with an easy line to what a generation wanted to change about work and daily life. Pluto's sign is a generation's. Mercury's sign, and the houses you need a birth time to find ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), make it yours: Mercury in the 10th sextile Pluto in the 8th, for instance, might put research or psychology to work in public.

About 1 person in 14 is born with Mercury and Pluto within 6° of a sextile, the most common of the pair's five aspects at birth. With a tight orb, insight is easy to reach for; with a wide one, it's there in the moments that ask for it.

Mercury and Pluto at other angles ask for depth in other ways:

- **[Mercury conjunct Pluto](/astrology/mercury-conjunct-pluto/):** the digging never switches off, and every thought goes down a level.
- **[Mercury square Pluto](/astrology/mercury-square-pluto/):** the depth pushes back, and insight comes through friction and hard questions.
- **[Mercury trine Pluto](/astrology/mercury-trine-pluto/):** depth that flows without being called, easy enough to take for granted.
- **[Mercury opposite Pluto](/astrology/mercury-opposite-pluto/):** the intensity belongs, at first, to the people you talk to.

For a couple or two friends, [Mercury sextile Pluto in synastry](/astrology/mercury-sextile-pluto-synastry/) is an easy channel between one person's way of talking and the other's depths: the friend you can tell anything, and who helps you put it into words. Each of you brings something different to it.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Pluto sextile natal Mercury](/astrology/transiting-pluto-sextile-natal-mercury/) is a long, workable season. Pluto's pace varies so much from sign to sign that this sextile can last a year or more. It's a time, astrologers say, to change how you think by choice: a course of study, a research project, a frank conversation you'd been putting off, an old opinion you're ready to revise.

As with the birth aspect, it rewards whoever takes it up. Weather, not a forecast: the transit describes an opening for deep thinking, never what you'll discover.
