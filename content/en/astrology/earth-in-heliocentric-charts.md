Every chart I read is drawn from the Earth, which is why the Earth itself never appears in it: you can't see the ground you stand on among the stars. Move the point of view to the centre of the Sun, as heliocentric astrology does, and the Earth takes its place among the planets.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A [heliocentric chart](/astrology/heliocentric-chart/) places the planets where they would appear from the centre of the Sun, rather than from the Earth; *helios* is Greek for the Sun. In that chart the Sun has no position, since it is the point of view, and the Earth gains one.

Where the Earth falls takes no calculation of its own. The line from the Earth to the Sun and the line from the Sun to the Earth are one line, looked at from either end, so the Earth always stands at the degree exactly opposite the [Sun](/astrology/sun/) of your ordinary chart. A Sun at 10° Taurus gives an Earth at 10° Scorpio; your heliocentric Earth is always in the sign opposite your Sun sign.

The Earth has two symbols: 🜨, a circle crossed through the middle, and ♁, a globe topped by a cross. The first may look familiar. The glyph of the [Part of Fortune](/astrology/part-of-fortune/) is the Earth's, turned through 45°.

Counted out from the Sun, the Earth is the third planet, between Venus and Mars. The planets on this site run from the Sun out to [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/), which I still read although astronomers classed it a dwarf planet in 2006; the Earth only joins the list when the chart is drawn from the Sun.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Changing the point of view changes what a chart can hold. A heliocentric chart has:

- **No houses and no angles.** The houses, the Ascendant and the Midheaven come from a birthplace on the Earth's surface, and there is no place on the Sun to draw them from.
- **No lunar nodes.** They belong to the Moon's orbit round the Earth, not to anything seen from the Sun.
- **No retrograde motion.** Seen from the Sun, every planet moves forward all the time; retrograde is an effect of watching from the moving Earth ([retrograde, explained](/astrology/retrograde-explained/)).
- **No Sun.** Its place goes to the Earth, on the far side of the wheel.

What remains are the planets' positions and the aspects between them, which is what heliocentric astrologers read. The Earth moves about a degree a day and goes round the zodiac in a year, always opposite the geocentric Sun, so it changes sign about once a month, on the same days the Sun does.

The idea is as old as Copernicus's model. Andreas Aurifaber (1514–1559) was the first astrologer to consider applying it; in 1653 Joshua Childrey published an ephemeris of the planets' places and aspects as they stand with the Sun at the centre; and Holmes Whittier Merton's *Heliocentric Astrology* came out in 1899. Even its supporters use it beside the geocentric chart, never in its place.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read the geocentric chart: the sky from the place and the minute you were born, with the houses in [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/). The angles and the houses turn with the Earth, about a degree every four minutes, and they're what the birth time pins down. A heliocentric chart has no horizon in it, so it is the same for everyone born at the same moment anywhere on Earth; it can't hold what makes a chart yours to the minute. That's also why I don't read a chart without the time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Your Sun, in my readings, is your Sun: identity and direction, read by sign, house and aspects. In a heliocentric chart that same line points the other way and becomes the Earth. I don't add it, and my notebook, which checked the Sun by sign and house and found that the Sun sign held about a third of the time, never had a separate Earth to check ([my testing notes](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). Each reading starts from that geocentric chart, prepared with a system I built myself and read by me before it reaches you ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Heliocentric astrology is the scientific version, because the planets go round the Sun.”** Both charts use real positions; they differ in point of view. A birth chart is drawn from where the birth took place.
- **“Your heliocentric Earth is a second Sun sign.”** It's your Sun's line seen from the other end: always the opposite sign, at the same degree.
- **“The Earth in a heliocentric chart is about the earth signs.”** It's the planet, not the element. An Aries Sun puts it in Libra, an air sign.
- **“Heliocentric charts show retrograde planets too.”** They have none. Retrograde is what the planets look like from the moving Earth.
