With the Moon in the 11th house, you feel most at home among friends. The people you choose, the group that meets on Thursdays, the circle that has known you for years: for this Moon, belonging is built with others rather than inherited.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[The 11th house](/astrology/11th-house/) is the house of the circle: the friends you pick, the groups and causes you join, the allies who turn up unasked, and what you hope to build with them. Succedent, with Aquarius as its natural sign, it was the Good Spirit to the old astrologers, and the house where Jupiter rejoices. With the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) here, the chart's sense of safety comes from belonging among people who know you. You tend to look after everyone in your circle, remember what each of them needs, and feel shaken when the group fractures.

When you were born, the Moon was climbing the eastern sky, some hours after rising and not yet at its highest. In Placidus, the house system I use, the edges of the 11th shift with every minute of the birth time, so a Moon near a cusp needs a precise time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#what-the-time-decides)).

The 11th is also the house of hopes, and with the Moon here, what you hope for tends to be what you need: a place among people, a future you share. That makes your hopes tender as well as warm.

The [Moon in the 10th house](/astrology/moon-in-10th-house/) carries its feelings into a public role; this one carries them into a circle. The [Moon in the 12th house](/astrology/moon-in-12th-house/), next in line, steps away from the crowd altogether.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Friendship tends to come first for you, and many of your closest bonds begin as friendships. A partner who fits into your circle, or who has one of their own you can join, makes you feel at home; one who wants to keep you to themselves can make you feel shut in. You give a lot of care to friends and may sometimes treat a partner as one more member of the group, which can leave them wanting more of you alone. Saying who matters most, and showing it, settles everyone.

## At work {#at-work}

You tend to do well in teams, networks and organisations with a shared purpose, and you're often the one who keeps a group connected: organising, welcoming newcomers, noticing who's been left out. Community work, membership organisations, campaigns and collaborative projects suit this Moon. Long stretches of working alone can weigh on you, and a team that loses its sense of shared purpose can lose your heart along with it.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Belonging can become the need. You may go along with a group to keep your place in it, or feel adrift when a circle breaks up, as if home itself had dissolved. Looking after everyone in the group can leave nobody looking after you. And because your hopes are so tied to the people around you, disappointment in a friend or a cause can hit as hard as a private hurt.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Keep a few friendships that are one to one, not only group ones, so that someone knows you outside the circle. Let your place in a group rest on who you are, not on what you do for it. When a circle changes, give yourself time to grieve it, then look for the next one; this Moon finds its people again. And let some of your hopes be your own, not only shared.

Fittingly for a house of friends, [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) went from my own chart to my family's, then to friends' and strangers', each with their consent. Through all of them, the Moon's house was checked like every placement, the strongest results went to Saturn, Chiron and Lilith, and roughly one placement in three held overall. I read an 11th-house Moon as a way you tend to belong, never a list of who your friends will be.
