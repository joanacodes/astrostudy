A birth chart looks like too much at first: a wheel of symbols, lines across the middle, figures everywhere. It isn't read at a glance. This is the order I'd follow for a first reading of your own, one layer at a time.

## Step by step {#step-by-step}

1. **Check the time the chart was drawn for.** The rising sign and the houses depend on it, and on some days the Moon too. If the time came from memory or was rounded to the hour, check it against a record first ([How to find your birth time](/astrology/how-to-find-your-birth-time/)): a chart drawn for the wrong minute shows the wrong sky ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
2. **Check the settings.** A chart is only as good as what the program was told. I read in the [tropical zodiac](/astrology/tropical-zodiac/), with Placidus houses ([Which house system should I use?](/astrology/which-house-system-should-i-use/)), both nodes, mean and true, and mean Lilith ([Which Lilith is mine?](/astrology/which-lilith-is-mine/)). Whatever you choose, keep to it.
3. **Find your way round the wheel.** The rising degree is on the left and the houses count anticlockwise from it ([Chart wheel, and how to read it](/astrology/chart-wheel-and-how-to-read-it/)); [Glyphs: the cheat sheet](/astrology/glyphs-the-cheat-sheet/) and [Abbreviations in astrology](/astrology/abbreviations-in-astrology/) decode the rest.
4. **Start with the big three.** Your [Sun](/astrology/sun/) sign, your [Moon](/astrology/moon/) sign and your rising sign, the sign of your [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), give a first sketch: what you're aiming for, what you need, how you meet the world ([The big three](/astrology/big-three/)). Not sure of your rising sign? [How to find your rising sign](/astrology/how-to-find-your-rising-sign/) takes you through it.
5. **Follow the chart ruler.** The planet that rules your rising sign is your [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/), and the house it sits in is where your life tends to gather. Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces each have two rulers, a modern and a traditional one (Pluto and Mars, Uranus and Saturn, Neptune and Jupiter), and I read both ([Traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)).
6. **Take each planet in turn: sign, then house.** The planet says what, the sign how, the house where. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is how you think and speak, [Venus](/astrology/venus/) what you love and value, [Mars](/astrology/mars/) how you go after what you want; then [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) and [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/). [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/), [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) and [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) spend years in each sign, which you share with everyone born around the same time: their houses say more about you.
7. **Read the aspects.** The lines in the middle are the aspects, the angles between planets: the [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/) at 0°, the [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) at 60°, the [square](/astrology/square/) at 90°, the [trine](/astrology/trine/) at 120° and the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) at 180°. They show which parts of you work together and which rub ([Hard and soft aspects](/astrology/hard-and-soft-aspects/)), and [the aspect grid](/astrology/aspect-grid/) lists them pair by pair.
8. **Look at the other angles.** Opposite the Ascendant sits the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/), what you look for in other people; near the top, the [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/), your direction in public life; opposite it, the [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/), your roots. Planets close to any of the four are strong in a chart ([Angular planets](/astrology/angular-planets/)).
9. **Find both nodes.** The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) and the [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) sit exactly opposite each other, on the [nodal axis](/astrology/nodal-axis/): the less familiar direction of growth, and what came early or easily. The mean node and the true node are a degree or two apart at most; I look at both, and near the edge of a sign I say when they disagree ([Mean node and true node](/astrology/mean-node-and-true-node/)).
10. **Note the retrogrades.** An R or ℞ beside a planet means that, seen from Earth, it seemed to move backwards when you were born ([Retrograde, explained](/astrology/retrograde-explained/)). I read it as a function that works inward first ([natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)).
11. **Weigh what you've read.** In [my notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), about a third of the placements I checked held overall: 30 to 40%. Saturn, [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) and [Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/) held 75 to 90% of the time, so that's where I spend the most time ([the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)). It's a notebook, not a study, but it's why I hold the rest lightly.
12. **Put it together.** Read the placements against each other: where they agree, the picture is simple; where they pull apart, it gets interesting. Keep it a description of how you tend to move, never a forecast ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common mistakes {#common-mistakes}

- **Stopping at the Sun sign.** It's one placement among dozens: a [birth chart](/astrology/birth-chart/) holds every planet's sign and house, the angles and the aspects.
- **Reading a placement on its own.** A Moon in Aries describes feelings that arrive at full strength; in the 4th house, with Saturn square to it, the sentence changes. Every line of a chart is qualified by its house and its aspects.
- **Mixing settings.** A description of mean Lilith says nothing about true Lilith, and one house system's 7th house isn't another's. Read what was written for the settings your chart uses.
- **Treating a hard aspect as a verdict.** A square is friction, an opposition two things to balance, and no placement is doomed.
- **Asking the chart what it can't answer.** No chart can tell you about illness, death, pregnancy, money or the outcome of a court case, and I don't read any of them ([Can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)).
- **Reading someone else's chart behind their back.** A chart is personal. Read your own, and another adult's only with their knowledge ([synastry and consent](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)); a child's, not at all ([Children's charts](/astrology/childrens-charts/)).
