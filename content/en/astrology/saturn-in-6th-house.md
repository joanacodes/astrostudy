The daily round is where Saturn in the 6th house lives: the alarm, the list, the tasks done properly whether or not anyone notices. It's the placement of the person who keeps everything running, and who has to learn, slowly, that stopping is allowed.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The work you actually do, as opposed to the title you hold, your habits and routines, the colleagues beside you and the care you give your body, sleep, food, movement, rest: [the 6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) is the ordinary day. It's cadent, one of the houses of adjusting and maintaining, its natural sign is Virgo, and tradition called it Bad Fortune and gave Mars its joy there, the house of toil. Here [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), the planet of discipline, takes charge of the everyday: you're reliable, thorough, hard to stop, and you hold yourself to standards nobody else would think to check.

The old books also gave this house illness. A chart can't diagnose or predict anything about your health, and I don't pretend it can ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)). With Saturn here I read something more ordinary and more useful: how you treat your own days, and whether they leave any room for you.

The 6th sits directly across from the 12th, the house where tradition says Saturn rejoices, so this Saturn works in the visible daily round while facing the quiet retreat it prefers. When you were born it had set not long before, just below the western horizon. A Saturn just under the Descendant falls in the 6th, just above it in the 7th, and in Placidus, which is how I divide the houses, a few minutes either way can decide which; the two read very differently ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Where [Saturn in the 5th house](/astrology/saturn-in-5th-house/) has to learn to play, Saturn in the 6th has to learn to stop; one house on, [Saturn in the 7th house](/astrology/saturn-in-7th-house/) carries the same seriousness into partnership.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Love, for you, often looks like practical help: making the appointment, fixing the leak, keeping the household on its feet. Shared routines matter, and a partner who lives in chaos can feel like a permanent extra task. Animals, if you share your life with them, tend to get the same faithful routine. The risk is that the relationship turns into another list. The lesson is time together with no purpose at all, which this Saturn finds strangely hard and very good for it.

## At work {#at-work}

This is where the placement does its best and its most. You're the colleague who knows how everything works, who keeps the process honest and finishes what others leave half done. Craft, quality, maintenance and any job where small things matter suit you. Supervising others can too, as long as you remember that your standards are yours, not a law everyone signed. Watch for becoming indispensable, which feels like security and works like a trap: the more you carry, the less anyone else learns to.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Never quite off duty. Guilt that arrives the moment you sit down. Routines held so rigidly that a change of plan feels like a threat. And a habit of treating your body as a machine that has to keep going, listening to tiredness only once it shouts.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Put rest on the schedule like any other task, and keep the appointment. Choose your routines instead of inheriting them, and change one now and then to prove you can. Delegate a job and let it be done differently. Take questions about your health to a doctor, and give your body the kind of steady, unglamorous care you'd give anything you valued. The 6th is where Saturn learns that maintenance includes the person doing it.
