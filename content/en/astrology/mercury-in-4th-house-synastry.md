Their Mercury in your 4th house is talk that follows you home. Another person's questions reach the room where you're most yourself, and before long you may be telling them family stories you rarely share, or arguing happily about where the bookshelves should go.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[The 4th house](/astrology/4th-house/) is your ground: the home you grew up in and the one you've made, family, belonging, and the self you keep for the people nearest you. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) asks, connects and explains. As a [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), this one puts their curiosity at your front door, and then inside it.

With them you may find words for things that were never said at home. They ask how your family did things, why a phrase of your grandmother's still makes you laugh, what your first street was like, and in answering you understand your own history a little better. Practical talk about the home goes well too: a move, a repair, a household that runs on good conversation.

For them, your private world is a place to think. They may do their best talking at your table, and your roots give their questions somewhere real to go. The 4th is angular, strong even when it works out of sight, and its natural sign is [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/), ruled by the Moon: memory, shelter, the familiar.

If it sits right after your [IC](/astrology/imum-coeli/), their Mercury is also conjunct the deepest point of your chart. I read that contact along with the house: their words reach your foundations.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Questions about where you come from can feel like probing. Not every family story is ready to be told, and a mind that wants to understand may push towards rooms you keep closed. Opinions about your home, how it's arranged, why you keep that chair, can sting more than opinions about your work.

From their side, your private life may seem fenced off. If you change the subject whenever family comes up, they can feel shut out of the part of you they most want to know. And if they explain your family to you, however kindly, it can sound as though they think they understand it better than you do.

The 4th describes your experience of home, not what your family was like; it says nothing certain about anyone's parents, and this overlay doesn't either. It shows how talk moves between you once you're indoors.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Let the stories come at your pace, and say which ones are yours to keep. Decisions about a shared home go better on paper, each voice heard before anything is bought or moved. In a couple, compare what home means to each of you before you share one; between friends, they may be the one who helped you pack and then stayed for supper; between colleagues who work from home, a glimpse of each other's kitchens can become part of the working day; between adult relatives, the one you go over the old stories with, who remembers them differently.

From the other chair it's your Mercury in their 4th house, and your questions that reach their roots. Next door, [their Mercury in your 3rd house](/astrology/mercury-in-3rd-house-synastry/) keeps the talk on the street and in your messages, while [their Mercury in your 5th house](/astrology/mercury-in-5th-house-synastry/) takes it out to play.

Your IC is opposite your Midheaven, and the two shift together with the minute of your birth, so in Placidus, the house system I use, the 4th can't be placed without your time. Their Mercury is placed by their birth date, give or take a degree or two; their time only settles it when it sits on one of your cusps, and I ask for it regardless ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Family stories belong to the people in them too, one more reason I read a pair only when both know ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
