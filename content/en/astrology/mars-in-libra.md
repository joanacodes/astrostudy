In Aries, Mars asks *what do I want?* Across the zodiac, in Libra, it asks first whether something is fair, and only later what it wants, sometimes much later. That reversal is the whole of Mars in Libra: courage that waits for the other side to have its say.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the drive to act and assert: initiative, effort, competition, the defence of a limit. [Libra](/astrology/libra/), cardinal air ruled by Venus, is the sign of balance, partnership and fairness. Here Mars works through other people and through ideas of justice. You act most readily on someone else's behalf or for a principle, you're a natural negotiator, and your competitive streak shows up as a strong sense of fair play: you want to win by the rules, or not at all.

Libra is Mars's [detriment](/astrology/detriment/), the sign opposite Aries, where Mars is at home. It describes drive that has to take another person into account before it moves, which slows the first step and often improves the second. Libra is also cardinal, so this Mars still starts things: proposals, alliances, the conversation that needed to happen.

What you'll stand up for is coloured by [Venus](/astrology/venus/), Libra's ruler, so I look at its sign and house too. Mars and Venus are the old pair of pursuing and attracting; with Mars in Venus's sign, the pursuit takes Venus's manners: charm, tact, timing.

Mars typically spends about 48 days in Libra; a retrograde can hold it there for 231, about seven and a half months. Counting everyone born from 1930 to 2009, some 1 person in 11 has it, so the sign marks a season of births more than a person, and its house says where you keep the scales. [Mars in Virgo](/astrology/mars-in-virgo/), before it, fights by fixing; [Mars in Scorpio](/astrology/mars-in-scorpio/), after it, commits all the way.

[My notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) is a useful corrective here. Mars went through it like every other placement, sign and house, and it isn't among the three placements that held far more often than the rest; across everything I checked, roughly a third held. So I never let a sign called a detriment stand as a verdict on how well someone can fight.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

Partnership is where this Mars comes alive, and also where it gets stuck. You court with charm and good timing, and you're often more comfortable acting for the relationship than in it. Disagreement is the difficult part: you may avoid an open quarrel for weeks, weighing both sides, until the backlog arrives all at once. Partners who invite your opinion, and who can hear *I want* without hearing a threat, help a great deal. Your gift is fighting fair; your lesson is fighting at all, when something matters to you.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, Mars in Libra shines wherever two sides need to meet: negotiation, mediation, design, client work, diplomacy, any role where the deal has to be good for both. You can be very persuasive without raising your voice, and you'll take on an injustice for a colleague long before you'd ask for anything for yourself. Decisions made alone and under pressure cost you the most. A trusted sounding board, even for five minutes, shortens the weighing.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Indecision, mostly: seeing every side so clearly that choosing one feels unfair. Wants you never voiced turn into a resentment you can't quite explain. You may keep the peace at your own expense, then let the anger out cold, long after the moment, in a form that surprises everyone, you included.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Say your preference first now and then, before you've heard everyone else's. Give a decision a deadline: weigh until Friday, then choose. Practise disagreeing in small, low-stakes doses, so the big ones aren't your first. Use your sense of fairness on your own behalf, since *is this fair to me?* is a fair question. And try a game with a net and rules, tennis, badminton, fencing: competition with clear rules is exactly the kind this Mars enjoys.
