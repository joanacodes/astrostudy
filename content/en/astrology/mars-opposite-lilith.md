Mars opposite Lilith sets your drive at one end of the chart and your untamed side at the other. The two keep each other in view, and the far end often turns up first in someone else: the friend who won't be managed, the rival who plays by no one's rules.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the part of you that sets out: it initiates, competes, asserts and protects your ground. [Black Moon Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/) marks the averaged far point of the Moon's orbit; astrologers take it for what in you refuses to be domesticated, what was pushed out of sight, and your own dealings with power. Readings vary between traditions, and I hold mine as symbolism. In an [opposition](/astrology/opposition/), two functions are set at the two poles of one line: each end makes the other visible, and the work is integration, not victory.

In plain words, your drive and your untamed side stand facing each other rather than side by side. You may push for things in an orderly way while the refusal you don't use shows up in people around you: a partner who won't be steered, a colleague who does exactly as they please, a friend whose bluntness both irritates and fascinates you. Or the other way round: you're the one who won't be tamed, and other people carry the plain, organised drive. An opposition isn't enmity. Those people are showing you a half of yourself.

Polarity isn't contradiction. Over time this axis tends to come home: you learn to act on instinct and to push in the ordinary way, each when it serves, and the people who once stood for your untamed side become simply people again.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Opposite signs share their modality and have elements that complete each other, so the two ends understand each other better than they first appear to. The houses name the axis. On the 1st–7th line it stretches from you to the people you pair with; on the 6th–12th, from daily work to what you keep out of sight; on the 3rd–9th, from everyday talk to what you believe. Houses need your birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Roughly 1 person in 30 has the opposition within 6°, counting with mean Lilith. Close to exact, the two ends tend to set each other off quickly; at 5° or 6°, the axis shows mostly in particular relationships. As with every placement, I read it as a tendency, not a fate ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

Mars and Lilith at the other angles:

- **[Mars conjunct Lilith](/astrology/mars-conjunct-lilith/):** the untamed side sits right beside your drive, with no one else to carry it.
- **[Mars sextile Lilith](/astrology/mars-sextile-lilith/):** an opening you take when you choose.
- **[Mars square Lilith](/astrology/mars-square-lilith/):** friction at right angles, and boundaries learned under pressure.
- **[Mars trine Lilith](/astrology/mars-trine-lilith/):** the two in easy agreement; freedom without a fight.

[Mars opposite Lilith in synastry](/astrology/mars-opposite-lilith-synastry/) sets one person's drive opposite the other's untamed point: a strong pull across a gap, intriguing at first, and kinder when neither tries to tame the other.

## In transits {#in-transits}

In [transits](/astrology/transits/), mean Lilith gives this opposition a steady rhythm. Every nine years or so, at its return, Lilith is back where it began, opposite your Mars, for a few weeks. Halfway between returns, it crosses your Mars itself, a meeting in the sky that briefly brings the untamed end right up against your drive. Mean Lilith never moves backwards, so each of these is a single passage.

Weather, not a forecast: these are seasons for noticing which half of the axis you're living, not dates on which something has to happen.
