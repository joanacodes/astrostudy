Jupiter square Saturn sets the part of you that reaches for more at right angles to the part that wants it to last. It comes back about every ten years in the sky, so each decade has its own cohort of births with it; what you do with it shows in your houses.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) is where a chart reaches for more: more understanding, a larger frame of meaning, the next possibility. [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) is where it builds and holds: responsibility, limits, patience, the long view. A [square](/astrology/square/) relates them through constructive friction: neither wins outright, and each keeps pushing the other to adapt.

In plain words, growth and caution rarely agree on the timing. You may take on a big plan and then spend months doubting it, or hold back for so long that the chance seems to pass, and then leap. Neither instinct is wrong. What the square asks is that each instinct test the other: your hopes checked against what can actually be carried, your limits stretched by what is genuinely worth the effort.

This isn't failure; it's calibration. Plans that have survived both of those questions tend to be bold and sound at once, and many people with this square become good at exactly that: judging how far to go, and how fast.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Among people born from 1930 to 2009, the square within 6° concerns about 1 in 15, grouped about ten years apart: 1935–36, 1945–47, 1955–56, 1964–66, 1975–77, 1985–87, 1994–96 and 2005–06, in two or three spells of a few months each time. In the 1980s, for instance, it ran through June 1985, March and April 1986, and October 1986 to February 1987. The spells come in twos and threes because both planets spend part of every year retrograde ([natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)).

Because whole spells of births share it, I read the square through its houses, which say where the stop and start happens. Jupiter in the 2nd square Saturn in the 11th, say, might put the tension between building your own resources and the commitments you take on with others.

The two planets don't carry the same weight in my notebook. Saturn stood there with Chiron and Lilith as the placements that kept proving right, 75 to 90% of the time, against about a third overall; Jupiter, checked like every placement, wasn't one of them ([the three that held: Saturn, Chiron and Lilith](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/)). So in this square, Saturn's half is the one I trust most.

The pair's other aspects:

- **[Jupiter conjunct Saturn](/astrology/jupiter-conjunct-saturn/):** the great conjunction of 1940–41, 1961, 1980–81 and 2000; growth and structure as one.
- **[Jupiter sextile Saturn](/astrology/jupiter-sextile-saturn/):** births of 1957–58 or 1977–78, among others; an opening the two can share.
- **[Jupiter trine Saturn](/astrology/jupiter-trine-saturn/):** eight groups of births from 1933–35 to 2007–08; growth and structure in easy agreement.
- **[Jupiter opposite Saturn](/astrology/jupiter-opposite-saturn/):** 1951–52 and 1969–71 among its years; the two at opposite ends of one line.

Between two charts, [Jupiter square Saturn in synastry](/astrology/jupiter-square-saturn-synastry/) puts one person's hopes against the other's caution: frustrating at times, and often how a shared plan gets both its ambition and its brakes.

## In transits {#in-transits}

With this square at birth, [transiting Saturn square natal Jupiter](/astrology/transiting-saturn-square-natal-jupiter/) keeps an appointment: Saturn squares your Jupiter from your Saturn's own birth place, so it comes with the [Saturn return](/astrology/saturn-return/), around 29½, and again when Saturn reaches the opposite point, around 14 or 15. [Transiting Jupiter square natal Saturn](/astrology/transiting-jupiter-square-natal-saturn/) is much quicker, a matter of days or weeks, and comes round every few years.

Weather, not a forecast: stretches when the old question of how much and how fast comes back to be answered again, never a verdict on the answer.
