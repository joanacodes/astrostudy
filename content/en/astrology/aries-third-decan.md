The third decan of Aries is where spring's first sign gets ready to hand over to Taurus: 20° to 30° Aries, ten degrees whose two decan rulers are the planets the old books called benefics.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

[Venus](/astrology/venus/) holds the last face of [Aries](/astrology/aries/), the third planet of the Chaldean count that Lilly's table of [faces](/astrology/faces/) starts with Mars. Venus is in [detriment](/astrology/detriment/) all through Aries, opposite her own sign, Libra, and the face doesn't cancel that. It gives her a foothold, the slightest dignity there is. [Venus in Aries](/astrology/venus-in-aries/) between 20° and 30° still values and wants quickly and frankly; the face lends her a little standing, not a new character.

The element scheme looks two fire signs ahead, to Sagittarius, and hands the decan to its ruler, [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/).

Whichever list you follow, then, the end of Mars's sign goes to a benefic, in the tradition's word: Venus weighing what is worth having, Jupiter widening the aim. I take that as a hint about how the Aries impulse finishes in these degrees, and I check the sign and house of your Venus or Jupiter before giving the hint any weight. Mars still rules the sign; the decan only adds a second opinion.

## Its image {#its-image}

The Egyptian names of the second and third decans of Aries make a pair: Qeṭ, then Sasaqeṭ, in Budge's transliteration. The Greco-Egyptian lists write this one Si-ket, and Firmicus Maternus Sentacher, or Asentacer; in the *Testament of Solomon* it is Artosael. Cosmas of Maiuma's list puts Eros here, the third of its Aries figures after Aidoneus and Persephone, and Athanasius Kircher's puts Horus.

Eros over the face of Venus is a coincidence I enjoy without reading anything into it: a name in one list tells me nothing about a ruler in another ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Mid-April birthdays, from about 10 to 19 April, put the Sun here, on its way out of the sign of its exaltation ([Sun in Aries](/astrology/sun-in-aries/)). Its 2026 crossing began on 9 April at 20:40 UT and ended with the move into Taurus on 20 April at 01:39 UT, so that year a birth in the first hour and a half of 20 April, UT, still had an Aries Sun. Late on the 19th or early on the 20th, only the birth time and place settle which sign it is ([born on the cusp](/astrology/born-on-the-cusp/)).

The very last degree, 29°, is the one astrologers call [the anaretic degree](/astrology/anaretic-degree-29/). I read a planet there as fully in Aries and about to move on, late rather than fated.

For births between April 1932 and March 1935, most steadily from February 1933 to June 1934, Uranus is in these degrees; Saturn's last three passes date from 1939–40, 1968–69 and 1997–99. Neptune arrives in 2033 and keeps coming back, on its loops, until 2039. Saturn's next pass is from April 2027 to April 2028. When a reading leans on a detail this fine, I say so, as I do for anything that rests on a placement that held less often in my tests ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).

Ten degrees on, the rulers change completely: Taurus opens with Venus as the sign's ruler and Mercury in the face ([Taurus, first decan](/astrology/taurus-first-decan/)), and the decan before this one belongs to the Sun ([Aries, second decan](/astrology/aries-second-decan/)). Between the three, Aries runs from Mars alone, through the Sun, to the benefics: one sign, three shades, all of them still Aries.
