Their Moon in your 1st house means someone's feelings register on you before a word is said. The part of them that reacts, soothes and needs comfort meets the part of you that walks in first, and both of you tend to notice the effect in your bodies before you can explain it.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) is the part of a chart that regulates feeling: needs, attachment, the comfort a person reaches for, the reactions that come before thought. [The 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) covers how you arrive: the body you live in, your bearing, the first move you make. In this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their emotional life meets your presence directly.

You may feel read without having to explain. They notice when you're tired, cold or hurt; they adjust to your pace; they may fuss a little over how you are, offering a jumper or a cup of tea at the right moment. Being met in your body rather than in your ideas is restful when it's welcome.

For them, simply being near you can settle something. Your voice, your way of moving, the speed at which you start things act on their feelings directly, and they may feel safer, or more stirred, around you than they could say. The 1st is angular, so this overlay shows, and its natural sign, [Aries](/astrology/aries/), is quick and direct, like the first reactions their feelings pick up. If their Moon sits on your [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), the very degree your chart begins from, it becomes a contact with that angle as well, and an intimate one.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Being read so closely has a cost. On a bad day there's nowhere to hide it, and their feelings may answer your mood before you've decided what it is. You can start to feel responsible for their comfort just by walking into a room, or adjust your manner to keep them settled.

From their side, your presence can stir as much as it soothes. Your directness or your speed may unsettle their need for safety, and their own feelings show around you more than they'd like. Their care can also overshoot: what they offer as comfort for your body, food, rest, warmth, can feel like supervision if you didn't ask.

Feelings here are information, not instructions. Neither of you has to manage the other's.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Name feelings early and simply: "I'm tired, it's not about you" saves a great deal. Accept the care you like and decline the rest kindly. In a couple, small physical rituals of comfort often matter more than big gestures; between friends, this is the friend who can tell how you are from across the room, and it's worth telling them they're right; between colleagues, keep the reading of each other's faces from turning into managing each other's moods.

The reverse placement, your Moon in their 1st house, works the same way with you as the one whose feelings land on their presence, and it asks for the same limits. Next to it, [their Moon in your 12th house](/astrology/moon-in-12th-house-synastry/) works in private, and [their Moon in your 2nd house](/astrology/moon-in-2nd-house-synastry/) around what makes you feel secure.

Their Moon covers about 13° a day, half a degree an hour, so whether it lands in your 12th or your 1st, either side of your Ascendant, can depend on the hour they were born. Your own time matters just as much: your Ascendant moves through a whole sign in roughly two hours on average, and in Placidus, the house system I use, every house moves with it. That's why I start a synastry with both times, never without ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)). Reading how someone responds to you, feeling by feeling, needs their agreement too ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
