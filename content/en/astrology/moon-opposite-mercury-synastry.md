Moon opposite Mercury in synastry is one person's Moon facing the other's Mercury from across the chart. Feeling and naming face each other here: one person tends to carry the mood, the other the words, and each sees the other's side very clearly from where they stand.

## What flows {#what-flows}

The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) is responsiveness: how a person adapts, what they need, what makes them feel safe. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is interpretation: how they reason, name and explain. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) holds two functions at opposite ends, each lighting up the other, and of the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) these two can form, it's the one that works like a mirror.

What flows is awareness. If the Moon is yours, you hear your feelings come back in the other person's words, and that outside view can tell you what you feel more clearly than you could alone. If Mercury is yours, you see in the other person what your words do to someone's feelings, a lesson no amount of thinking teaches. Between you, both halves of a conversation are present.

The signs give it its subject. With a Moon in Pisces and Mercury in Virgo, a feeling without edges meets a mind that sorts; the Moon person may feel dissected, the Mercury person adrift, and each holds exactly what the other lacks.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

This seesaw hands out labels: the emotional one and the rational one. The labels flatten both people. The Moon person's feelings get dismissed as irrational; the Mercury person's reasoning gets treated as cold, or as the final word. Each begins to argue from their end, and the conversation turns into a contest between feeling and fact. Arguments tend to replay one script, "you're overreacting" against "you don't care", and both lines describe the speaker's own end of the seesaw. Emotion isn't irrationality, and reasoning isn't certainty.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Swap ends on purpose. If Mercury is yours, say what you feel before you say what you think; if the Moon is yours, say what you think about what you feel. Use the mirror deliberately: "here's what I heard", "here's how that landed". Between adult relatives, it can mean retiring the roles of the sensitive one and the sensible one that a family handed out long ago; at work, letting the person who usually takes the minutes say how the meeting felt; in a couple or a friendship, noticing who always explains. A chart is personal, though, however well someone's words seem to explain you: I read a synastry only with both people's consent ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

The count is roughly one pair of people in 11, at an orb of 8°, the Moon being on either side. Half a day moves the Moon about six or seven degrees, most of this orb on one side, so whether the opposition exists can depend on whether the Moon person was born in the morning or the evening. Both birth times are needed for any synastry I read, and they also give the houses ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

In one chart, this polarity is [Moon opposite Mercury](/astrology/moon-opposite-mercury/). [Moon conjunct Mercury in synastry](/astrology/moon-conjunct-mercury-synastry/) puts the same two functions on one spot instead of opposite ends; [Moon square Mercury in synastry](/astrology/moon-square-mercury-synastry/) keeps them at cross-purposes, while [Moon sextile Mercury in synastry](/astrology/moon-sextile-mercury-synastry/) and [Moon trine Mercury in synastry](/astrology/moon-trine-mercury-synastry/) let them talk with less effort.
