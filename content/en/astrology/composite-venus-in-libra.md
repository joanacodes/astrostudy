Some pairs keep an unwritten ledger of turns: who chose the film, who drove last time, whose family gets the holidays this year. With composite Venus in Libra, keeping things even isn't bookkeeping; it's what the relationship enjoys and values most.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), a relationship gets one [Venus](/astrology/venus/), the midpoint of yours, and it describes what the bond values, likes and wants to keep, and how it exchanges. In [Libra](/astrology/libra/), cardinal air, that Venus stands in its [domicile](/astrology/domicile/), one of the two signs it rules, and the sign's manner is its own: balance, proportion, mutual adjustment. The bond likes an arrangement that suits both of you and tends to be good at making one: a home with each person's taste in a corner of it, a joint project whose roles were settled kindly and early.

The method's oddity shows clearly with this Venus. Take someone born in early January with Venus at the very start of Sagittarius, and someone born in late June with Venus in the middle of Leo. Their Suns' midpoint, the short way round, is about 8° Aries ([composite Sun in Aries](/astrology/composite-sun-in-aries/)); their Venuses' midpoint, the short way round, is about 8° Libra. Each calculation is right, and together they put the relationship's Venus exactly across the chart from its Sun, which no sky allows: at birth, the two are never more than about 47° apart. In the site's count, about 1 pair in 10 sees its composite Venus pass that limit, and about 1 pair in 22 lands within 8° of opposite. The other 9 in 10 keep the familiar pattern, a Libra Venus whose composite Sun sits in Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio or Sagittarius.

The relationship's manners can be better than either person's. Two people who interrupt, argue and forget whose turn it is may find that with each other they listen, wait and take turns, and that friends bring their own disputes to the two of you. [Venus in Libra](/astrology/venus-in-libra/) at birth describes one person's sense of fairness; the composite's is a habit the two of you keep up together. Venus's own sign is among its rarer composite placements: about 1 pair in 13, close to the share of people born with Venus in Libra, about 1 in 14.

Harmony can be bought too cheaply. A bond that values balance may smooth a disagreement over instead of having it, and fairness can harden into a running tally nobody enjoys. Balance isn't the same as never deciding, and harmony isn't the same as avoiding conflict. It helps to treat a frank disagreement as part of the balance rather than a threat to it, and to let one of you decide outright now and then, by turns.

[Composite Venus in Virgo](/astrology/composite-venus-in-virgo/), before it, loves by improving; [composite Venus in Scorpio](/astrology/composite-venus-in-scorpio/), after it, by going deeper. Composites stay outside my readings, so take Libra here as the technique described: its users set it beside the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) and both natal charts, and it promises no marriage. Fairness starts before the chart is drawn: both people know about it, and both agreed ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
