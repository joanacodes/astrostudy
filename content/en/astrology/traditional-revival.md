Some astrologers in the late twentieth century decided that the way forward lay in the oldest books. They reprinted them, translated them and began to practise what they found: the traditional revival.

## What it was {#what-it-was}

It began with a single book. In 1980 the British astrologer Olivia Barclay obtained an original copy of [William Lilly](/astrology/william-lilly/)'s *[Christian Astrology](/astrology/william-lillys-christian-astrology-1647/)* of 1647, and she arranged for it to be reprinted in facsimile in 1985. The astrologer and historian James Holden credits that reprint with spurring a revival of interest in traditional astrology. Barclay taught [horary astrology](/astrology/horary-astrology/), wrote *Horary Astrology Rediscovered* (1990), which received the Professional Astrologers Inc. award for an outstanding contribution to astrology in 1991, and, as Holden notes, advocated [Regiomontanus](/astrology/regiomontanus/) houses for horary, apparently on the grounds that what was good enough for Lilly was good enough for her students.

Then came the translators. In the 1990s, the group known as Project Hindsight published English versions of ancient and medieval texts. Robert Schmidt translated Ptolemy's *[Tetrabiblos](/astrology/ptolemys-tetrabiblos/)* (1994 to 1998) and the third book of the *[Anthology](/astrology/vettius-valens-anthology/)* of Vettius Valens (1994), the latter edited by [Robert Hand](/astrology/robert-hand/), and the same press published Robert Zoller's translation of the Latin *Liber Hermetis* (1993). Benjamin Dykes later did the same for the Persian and Arabic authors, with Sahl and Masha'allah in 2008 and Dorotheus, from the Arabic, in 2017, while scholars such as Keiji Yamamoto and Charles Burnett edited Abu Ma'shar, his book on the great conjunctions in 2000 and his *Great Introduction*, in English, in 2019.

The translations did not stop at English. David Pingree had published the Arabic Dorotheus with an English translation in 1976; J.-F. Bara put the first book of Valens into French in 1989; Firmicus Maternus's *Mathesis*, translated by Jean Rhys Bram in 1975, found a new English translator in James Herschel Holden in 2011; and in 2023 Fabien Waibel published a complete French translation of Dorotheus. The historian Nicholas Campion has written a first, preliminary history of the whole movement.

Practice followed the texts. Robert Hand's *Whole Sign Houses: The Oldest House System* (2000) made the case for the way the Hellenistic astrologers counted houses, one whole sign to each, and techniques long out of use came back into readings.

## What it gave astrology {#what-it-gave-astrology}

The old techniques, in working order. [Whole Sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/), the day and night charts of sect, the lots and the old timing methods are read again from these translations, and horary astrology, the main subject of Lilly's book, came back with it.

A clearer view of where everything comes from. Astrologers can now check a rule against its source instead of repeating it at third hand, and that habit of checking is one I value.

For my own practice, it means the "traditional techniques" half of what I do. I keep the traditional rulers in play: for Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces I read the old ruler and the new together, Mars beside Pluto, Saturn beside Uranus, Jupiter beside Neptune ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)). For houses I stay with Placidus, a quadrant system, rather than Whole Sign houses or the Regiomontanus houses Lilly used for horary, and every one of them needs the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). I describe the other systems on this site rather than dismiss them, and [how I read](/how-i-read/) says why I work the way I do.

The revival looked back as far as it could go. More than half a century earlier, in Hamburg, another school had looked the other way entirely, forward to new points in the sky and a new way of reading the chart: [the Hamburg School (Uranian astrology)](/astrology/hamburg-school-uranian-astrology/). The same years also saw [evolutionary astrology](/astrology/evolutionary-astrology/) push in the opposite direction, towards the soul.
