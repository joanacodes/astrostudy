The Sun takes a year to go round the zodiac, and gets twelve signs, about one a month. The Moon goes round in a little over 27 days, and several astronomies gave it a station for each night. Those stations are the lunar mansions.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A lunar mansion, or lunar station, is a stretch of the sky the Moon crosses in about a day. Strung together, 27 or 28 of them make a ring round the zodiac matched to the Moon's sidereal month of 27⅓ days, the time it takes to come back to the same stars. "Mansion" is an over-literal translation: the Latin *mansio* was a stopping place on a road, a night's lodging, nothing grand.

Several traditions built such a ring. Arabic astronomy had 28 manāzil al-qamar, each named after a group of stars: ath-Thurayya is the Pleiades, ad-Dabaran is [Aldebaran](/astrology/aldebaran/), al-Qalb, the Heart of the Scorpion, is [Antares](/astrology/antares/). Indian astronomy has the nakshatras, usually 27 and sometimes 28 ([Vedic astrology (Jyotish)](/astrology/vedic-astrology-jyotish/)). Chinese astronomy has 28 xiù, in four groups of seven under the Azure Dragon of the east, the Black Tortoise of the north, the White Tiger of the west and the Vermilion Bird of the south ([Chinese astrology](/astrology/chinese-astrology/)).

Historians generally trace the idea to Babylonian astronomy. Jim Tester, in his history of Western astrology, notes that the Babylonians had well-established lunar groupings by the 6th century BC, and finds the stations in Hellenistic astrology in a 2nd-century list of stars by the astrologer Maximus, then in Arabic lists. He thought the 28-station scheme itself came by way of Egyptian magic, which linked lucky and unlucky days of the lunar month to the zodiac.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

There are two ways to draw the mansions. The older follows the stars: each mansion is a star group near the Moon's path, and the stretches between them are unequal, as the original Indian and Chinese mansions were. The later way cuts the zodiac into equal parts. Twenty-eight equal mansions are 12°51′ each; India settled on 27 of 13°20′, which divide the circle more cleanly, and left one nakshatra, Abhijit, without a portion.

Counted from 0° Aries, the first equal mansion runs to 12°51′ Aries, the second to 25°43′ Aries, the third on into Taurus: every sign holds pieces of two or three mansions, and their edges never line up with the signs. The Moon spends about a day in each.

What the mansions were for was mostly timing. In the Arabic calendar of the seasons, the manāzil were grouped into anwā', each tied to its weather, so the rains and winds of the year were dated by them. In the *Picatrix*, an Arabic book of astral magic put into Spanish at the court of Alfonso X between 1256 and 1258 and then into Latin, the Moon's passage through the 28 mansions decides when to make images and perform rituals, and what each is for. Its electional astrology, the choosing of a moment to begin something, rests on them too. Calendars tied to the Sun used another marker, the morning a star first reappears at dawn ([Heliacal rising](/astrology/heliacal-rising/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) by its sign, its house, its aspects and its phase at birth ([Your birth lunar phase](/astrology/your-birth-lunar-phase/)); I don't add its mansion. The system I built for my readings doesn't use them, and they weren't part of what I tested: my notebook checked the Moon by sign and house, like every planet, and the Moon wasn't one of the three placements that held far more often than the rest; overall, about a third held ([The invoice method, and what held overall](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

The mansions do show, very plainly, how fast the Moon moves. A day moves it a whole mansion, which is why the Moon is the body whose sign a birth time can change, and why I won't guess it ([What changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

If you work with a Vedic astrologer, your Moon's nakshatra will carry real weight. In the astrology I practise it belongs to another tradition's toolkit, best read by someone trained in it.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The lunar mansions are the Moon's houses in my chart.”** They are stretches of the zodiac the Moon travels through, the same for everyone born that day; houses depend on the birth time and place.
- **“Every tradition has 28.”** India usually counts 27; Abhijit lost its portion when the circle was cut into equal parts.
- **“The mansions are an Arabic invention.”** Arabic astronomy used them widely, but India and China had their own, and historians look to Babylon for the idea.
- **“Each mansion is a constellation.”** At first they were star groups. Once the circle was cut equally, a mansion became a stretch of degrees, named after the stars in or near it.
