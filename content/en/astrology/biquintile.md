The biquintile is two quintiles laid end to end: two planets 144° apart, two-fifths of the circle. Draw a five-pointed star without lifting your pen and every stroke joins two points 144° apart; I read the aspect in that spirit, as a pattern too intricate to take in at a glance.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

One hundred and forty-four degrees is four signs and 24°, so a biquintile usually lands five signs on: from 10° Aries, it reaches 4° Virgo. Only a planet in the first six degrees of its sign makes it four signs on, as from 3° Aries to 27° Leo.

Where the quintile organises two planets into a pattern, the biquintile, as I read it, integrates them into a more complex one: higher-order synthesis, systems inside systems, the kind of craft that comes from refining a combination until it works. It isn't genius, perfection or automatic success. Early on it can even overcomplicate things, or strain after originality, before it learns which parts belong together.

Kepler's *Harmonices Mundi* (Linz, 1619) is where it joins Ptolemy's aspects, along with the [quintile](/astrology/quintile/) and the sesquiquadrate. With the quintile it belongs to the fifth harmonic: among [harmonic charts](/astrology/harmonic-charts/), the fifth-harmonic chart turns biquintiles and quintiles alike into [conjunctions](/astrology/conjunction/), which is why astrologers who use them read the two as one family.

Its orb is a degree or two, no wider (see [orbs](/astrology/orbs/)), and here tightness matters more than usual: the biquintile sits only 6° short of the quincunx, and with a loose orb the two would blur.

It runs 24° past the [trine](/astrology/trine/), twice the quintile's 12° lead over the [sextile](/astrology/sextile/), and stops 36° short of the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/). In between, the [square](/astrology/square/)'s family already has the sesquiquadrate at 135°; the biquintile, 9° further on, belongs to another family altogether.

## Between which planets {#between-which-planets}

A biquintile between two particular planets has no page of its own here. What I watch is which of the two moves faster: the quicker one tends to do the experimenting, the slower one to hold the frame.

With two personal planets, the Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus or Mars, it's hands-on: Mars and Mercury, for instance, combining effort and thought until complicated tasks become absorbing rather than draining. When one of the pair is Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune or Pluto, the personal planet is drawn into a long apprenticeship to something larger. And when both are slow, the aspect belongs to a generation; the houses show where its intricate way of combining things lands in your life.

On one side among the minor aspects is the [quintile](/astrology/quintile/), at 72°, the simpler form of the same fifth; on the other, the [septile](/astrology/septile/), at 51°26′, a seventh of the circle, where pattern stops being something you build and becomes something you recognise as it comes round again.

## The biquintile in synastry {#in-synastry}

Two people whose planets are 144° apart may never notice it among the louder major aspects. Where it shows is in complicated things undertaken together: a renovation, a long piece of research, a shared craft with many parts, where each one's way of thinking completes a piece of the other's. It guarantees nothing. With personal planets and a tight orb, I name it in [synastry readings](/synastry/), read from both sides, as the place where the two of you might build something intricate.

## The biquintile in transits {#in-transits}

A transiting biquintile is a brief alignment. Within a degree's orb, it lasts hours from the Moon and a couple of days from the faster planets, and even Saturn's runs only to weeks unless a retrograde brings it back. It often goes unnoticed, and I'd rather not make it carry more than it does. If you notice anything, it may be a moment when separate pieces of a project click together. Weather, not a forecast: a biquintile transit describes a moment that suits synthesis, not a result you're owed.
