Sometimes one sign opens two houses in a row: it's on the cusp of the 6th, say, and again on the cusp of the 7th. That sign is duplicated, and it means one planet answers for two neighbouring parts of life.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Every house takes the sign on its cusp, and the ruler of that sign rules the house ([house rulers](/astrology/house-rulers/)). A house narrower than a sign can begin and end inside it, so the next cusp lands in the same sign: that sign is duplicated.

It never happens alone. There are twelve cusps for twelve signs, so a sign with two cusps means another sign with none, an [intercepted sign](/astrology/intercepted-signs/). And because opposite cusps mirror each other, duplications come in opposite pairs, like interceptions. In a quadrant system such as [Placidus](/astrology/placidus/), it's the same event seen from two sides: some houses grow wider than a sign, others shrink narrower than one.

Take a birth in London with 19° Sagittarius rising and 22° Libra on the Midheaven. In Placidus, the 12th house begins at 3° Sagittarius and the 1st at 19° Sagittarius, so Sagittarius opens both; opposite, Gemini opens the 6th and the 7th. Two signs pay for it: Aquarius sits wholly inside the 2nd house and Leo inside the 8th.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

One ruler for two houses ties two parts of life together. In the London chart, [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) rules both the 12th and the 1st, and through Pisces on the 3rd cusp, which it shares with Neptune as Pisces's modern ruler, a third house as well. [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) rules the 6th and 7th through Gemini, and the 9th through Virgo. Wherever Jupiter and Mercury sit in that chart, by sign, house and aspect, colours every house they rule ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)).

The sign also stops being a reliable guide to the house. In that chart, a planet at 10° Sagittarius falls in the 12th house, and one at 25° Sagittarius in the 1st: same sign, different houses. The degree decides, and the degree of a cusp depends on the birth time, which moves every cusp about a degree in four minutes ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

And something is left out. With Leo intercepted, the Sun rules no cusp in that chart. That changes the bookkeeping of rulers, not the Sun: it still has its sign, its house and its aspects.

Duplication isn't the only way houses are linked. Tradition also reads one house counted from another, the 8th as the 2nd from the 7th, for instance ([derived houses](/astrology/derived-houses/)). Duplication links houses that sit side by side; derived houses link houses by counting.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read the two houses of a duplicated sign as neighbours who share a landlord. They keep their own topics, but the same planet speaks for both, so what helps one often helps the other. With Gemini on the 6th and 7th, the daily work and the partners in a life share Mercury's way of doing things; with the rising sign on the 12th cusp as well, the chart ruler governs both how you meet the world and the time you keep to yourself ([chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/)).

I name the link when it matters and let the rest of the chart decide how much weight it carries. A duplicated sign is a structure, not a message ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“A duplicated sign is twice as strong.”** It covers more ground, not more force. A sign's weight comes from the planets in it and the state of its ruler.
- **“Duplicated and intercepted signs are two separate oddities.”** They're one count seen from two ends: a sign with two cusps always means a sign with none.
- **“If Leo has no cusp, the Sun is weak.”** Ruling no house by the cusp changes which houses answer to the Sun, not what the Sun does in the chart.
