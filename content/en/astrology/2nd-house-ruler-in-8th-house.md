What's yours and what's shared sit at opposite ends of the chart, and here one reaches across to the other. With the ruler of the 2nd in the 8th house, your money and your sense of worth get bound up with other people's: joint accounts, loans, trust.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The ruler of the [2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/), the planet of the sign on its cusp ([house rulers](/astrology/house-rulers/)), shows where your resources come from and where they go, and here it stands in the [8th house](/astrology/8th-house/), directly opposite: the house of intimacy and trust, of what you share with other people and what you owe them. In the old counting its money is the partner's money: the 8th is the 2nd house counted from the 7th ([derived houses](/astrology/derived-houses/)), so it carries joint accounts, debts and anything held in common.

Your resources rarely stay only yours. Money may come to you through other people, a partner's income, a business with shared capital, a loan, a household budget pooled with someone else, and it often goes back out the same way. Your sense of worth gets tested in closeness: what you'll share, what you'll keep, whom you'll trust with your accounts and your secrets. Depth is what you value, in money as in people. Clarity keeps it easy: shared money stays simple only while everyone knows whose it is and what's owed.

The two houses mirror each other, and so do their signs: the 8th begins in the sign opposite the one on your 2nd cusp. If the ruler stands in that sign, it's in its [detriment](/astrology/detriment/): Venus in Scorpio when Taurus rules your 2nd, Mars in Libra when Aries does. Money then gets handled in a manner that isn't the ruler's own, and you may pick up your financial habits from the people you share them with. Mars rules an Aries 2nd, and if it has reached Scorpio, further into the 8th, it stands in its other sign, the one the natural zodiac pairs with this house: shared resources are familiar ground, and you'll defend your share.

Tradition gave the 8th death and legacies, and horary astrologers used it for other people's money. Death is not something I read in a chart, and inheritances, lifespans and anyone's legacy don't show up in this placement either ([can astrology predict death?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-death/)). Nor does it forecast debts, gains or losses, or say what to borrow or where to invest ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)).

Your money's ruler had already passed the top of the sky when you were born and was coming down in the west, between the Midheaven and the setting point; Placidus, the house system I use, calls that stretch the 8th. Close to the 8th cusp, a slightly later birth would drop it into the 7th, where your money would pass through partners and clients rather than pooled accounts ([Ruler of the 2nd in the 7th house](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-7th-house/)); close to the 9th, a slightly earlier one would lift it back into the 9th, towards study, travel and belief ([Ruler of the 2nd in the 9th house](/astrology/2nd-house-ruler-in-9th-house/)). With so much riding on the clock, an approximate time won't do ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Pisces on the 2nd cusp brings in Jupiter and Neptune together, and I read both ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)): Jupiter in the 8th trusts generously and pools without fuss, while Neptune, in its own part of the chart, can blur where your money ends and someone else's begins.
