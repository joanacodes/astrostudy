The quintile divides the circle by five: two planets 72° apart, as far apart as two neighbouring points of a five-pointed star. I read it as the aspect of craft, of arranging things until they make a pattern.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

Seventy-two degrees is two signs and twelve degrees, so a quintile can join Aries to Gemini or Aries to Cancer, depending on where in Aries the first planet sits. Each of the five major aspects divides the circle by a number that also divides the twelve signs evenly; five doesn't, so a quintile never lines up with whole signs, and you can't spot one by counting them.

Its meaning, as I read it, is creative organisation. Two planets that, together, can take raw material and give it a form: words into a sentence, sounds into a tune, a mess into a system. It's about pattern and craft, the way a skill grows through practice and arrangement, more than about inspiration. It isn't talent, genius or mastery handed over at birth, but a potential: it shows what two parts of you could make together, given time.

In *Harmonices Mundi*, published in Linz in 1619, Johannes Kepler added it to Ptolemy's aspects, together with the biquintile and the [sesquiquadrate](/astrology/sesquiquadrate/); the quintile gives its name to the family of fives. Those who use it allow a degree or two, rarely more (see [orbs](/astrology/orbs/)).

Five is also one of the numbers [harmonic charts](/astrology/harmonic-charts/) are built on. In the chart of the fifth harmonic, where every position is multiplied by five, each quintile becomes a [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/), so the whole web of fifths in a chart comes into view at once.

On the circle it sits 12° past the [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) and 18° short of the [square](/astrology/square/), and its tone is neither the sextile's opening nor the square's friction: it's making. Two quintiles take you 24° past the [trine](/astrology/trine/), and it would take two and a half to reach the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/), so the fives never land on a major aspect.

## Between which planets {#between-which-planets}

I don't give quintiles a page for each pair of planets; who's involved, and how fast they move, says more.

With two quick planets, a quintile tends to show in the hands and the habits: Venus and Mars, say, giving shape to what you love through what you do. With one slow planet, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune or Pluto, the personal one learns over years to give form to something larger, and that's the quintile I look at first. Two slow planets in quintile mark everyone born over a few years; the houses show where that generation's way of making things takes root in your life.

Next to it among the minor aspects, the [quincunx](/astrology/quincunx/), at 150°, adjusts two planets that speak different languages, where the quintile organises two that can build something together. The [biquintile](/astrology/biquintile/), at 144°, is the quintile doubled, read as a more complex synthesis.

## The quintile in synastry {#in-synastry}

A quintile between your chart and someone else's rarely shows as chemistry or conflict. It shows when the two of you make something: a garden, a meal, a piece of music, a plan. One person's way of organising seems to give shape to what the other brings. Nothing guarantees that anything gets made. With personal planets and a tight orb, though, it's one of the few minor aspects I mention in [synastry readings](/synastry/), in both directions, as a resource for shared work.

## The quintile in transits {#in-transits}

Transiting quintiles are brief: within a degree, the Sun's lasts about two days and the Moon's a few hours, and only Jupiter and the slower planets stay long enough to colour a season. If one shows at all, it's as a good moment to give form to something you're already working on: to arrange, to edit, to finish. Weather, not a forecast: a quintile transit describes a moment that suits shaping things, never a result.
