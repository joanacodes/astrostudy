Mars sextile Uranus is a quiet talent for doing things a new way. The part of you that acts and the part that wants change are on friendly terms, two signs apart, and they cooperate whenever a problem asks for a fresh approach.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) gets things moving: initiative, assertion, effort, the push to compete and to finish. [Uranus](/astrology/uranus/) brings innovation and liberation: it experiments, differentiates and reforms systems that no longer fit. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) sets the two up as collaborators. Neither takes over; the angle opens a channel between them, and the channel carries most when you use it.

In plain words, you can change course without losing momentum. When the usual method fails, you're likely to try another rather than push harder at the first, and you may enjoy the problem-solving more than the routine that follows. Small reforms come naturally: a better tool, a faster route, a rule nobody remembers the reason for, quietly dropped. None of this happens unless you engage; the sextile is a readiness, not a habit.

It also makes independence easier to live with. You can do things your own way without needing to fight anyone about it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

A sextile usually joins two signs two apart, in elements that get on. In fire and air, the cooperation shows as ideas acted on quickly; in earth and water, as practical fixes found by trial. The houses show where the knack is used: Mars in the 3rd sextile Uranus in the 5th, say, might bring a fresh way of talking about, or teaching, whatever you love doing.

The sextile is common: about 1 person in 15 has it within 6°. Close to exact, inventiveness becomes part of how you work; at the wider end, it's something you reach for when you're stuck.

Mars, quick, makes the sextile twice in each two-year cycle with Uranus, while Uranus, some seven years to a sign, barely moves: the aspect belongs to a few weeks of births each time. When I read a sextile like this one, I ask where it has already served you, because a resource that's been used says more than one that's merely there ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

Mars and Uranus, compared across their aspects:

- **[Mars conjunct Uranus](/astrology/mars-conjunct-uranus/):** acting and changing in one charge, where the sextile keeps them on separate but friendly ground.
- **[Mars square Uranus](/astrology/mars-square-uranus/):** the same two urges rub at a right angle, so change comes through friction rather than invitation.
- **[Mars trine Uranus](/astrology/mars-trine-uranus/):** a fuller ease in one element, where inventive action is closer to temperament.
- **[Mars opposite Uranus](/astrology/mars-opposite-uranus/):** effort and freedom pull from opposite ends, often through other people.

Between two charts, [Mars sextile Uranus in synastry](/astrology/mars-sextile-uranus-synastry/) tends to feel like a useful jolt: one person's push meets the other's fresh angle. I read it both ways round.

## In transits {#in-transits}

[Transiting Uranus sextile natal Mars](/astrology/transiting-uranus-sextile-natal-mars/) comes from each side once in Uranus's 84-year round, so twice at most in a life. With seven years to a sign, Uranus keeps it within reach for a year or so, usually over several passes: a season when a new way of working, or a chance to act independently, is there to be taken. Born with the sextile, you meet a different angle at the [Uranus opposition](/astrology/uranus-opposition/), around 42: Uranus in the sky then trines your Mars.

Mars in the sky makes the sextile to your natal Uranus twice every two years, for a couple of days: brief openings for a quick fix or an unplanned start.

Like weather, not a forecast, these transits show when invention is within reach, never what it will produce.
