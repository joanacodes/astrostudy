Menkalinan is the Charioteer's shoulder. In 2026 it stands at 0°16′ Cancer, twenty years after crossing over from Gemini, so the Sun reaches its degree at the June solstice. It also lies far enough north of the Sun's path that any conjunction with it is a matter of numbers rather than of sky.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Menkalinan is Beta Aurigae, magnitude 1.90, the second star of the Charioteer after [Capella](/astrology/capella/), about 79 light-years away. To the eye it is one white star; in fact it is two similar stars circling each other every 3.96 days, and every 47.5 hours one passes partly in front of the other, dimming the pair from 1.89 to 1.94. Antonia Maury found the pair in its spectrum in 1890, the second binary ever discovered that way, and Joel Stebbins caught the eclipses some twenty years later. [Algenib](/astrology/algenib/), in Pegasus, flickers for another reason: it pulses, every 3.6 hours.

Its tropical position was 28°55′ [Gemini](/astrology/gemini/) for a birth in 1930 and 29°54′ in 2000; it crossed into [Cancer](/astrology/cancer/) in 2006 and stands at 0°16′ Cancer in 2026. Anyone born before 2006 has it in the last degree of Gemini ([the anaretic degree (29°)](/astrology/anaretic-degree-29/)); anyone born since, in the first degree of Cancer ([0° of a sign](/astrology/0-of-a-sign/)).

Its latitude, 21.5° north of the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), is the number to keep in mind. The Sun is always on the ecliptic and the Moon and planets keep within a few degrees of it, so none of them ever comes close to Menkalinan in the sky. A planet "conjunct" Menkalinan shares its longitude, the degree on the zodiac's scale, and nothing else ([celestial latitude](/astrology/celestial-latitude/)).

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

The name is Arabic, mankib dhī al-ʿinān, the shoulder of the rein-holder, after the star's place in the Charioteer's figure. Chinese astronomy counts it as the third of the Five Chariots, an asterism it shares with Capella and [El Nath](/astrology/el-nath/). The Inuit set it with Capella, Castor and Pollux in a figure called Quturjuuk, the collar-bones, whose rising warned that Aagjuuk, the stars of the first dawn after the winter solstice, would soon follow.

Its future is a quieter fact than its lore: it and the Sun are drawing closer, and in about a million years Menkalinan should be the brightest star of the night sky.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The tradition's single test is a conjunction within about a degree of longitude ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). For Menkalinan that span runs from 29°16′ Gemini to 1°16′ Cancer for a birth in 2026, and from 27°55′ to 29°55′ Gemini for one in 1930; since the mid-1930s it has reached across the line into Cancer. The Sun crosses it on about 20 and 21 June, as it reaches its furthest north and enters Cancer ([solstices and equinoxes](/astrology/solstices-and-equinoxes/)). Saturn passed over it for births in June 1944, in parts of 1973–74 and in 2002–03. The Moon and the angles move too fast for anything but an exact birth time to settle ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

I don't add stars to readings ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). With Menkalinan in particular, I'd want anyone who meets it in a chart to know that the planet and the star stand 21.5° apart: whatever the tradition reads into that meeting, the sky doesn't show it.
