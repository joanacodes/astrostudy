The quincunx is the aspect between two signs that have nothing in common. At 150°, five signs apart, the two planets neither clash nor flow; they work in different languages, and the chart asks you to keep translating.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

A quincunx is an angle of 150°, five-twelfths of the circle: five signs apart. Count five signs on from Aries and you reach Virgo; five back, Scorpio. Neither shares anything with Aries: not its element, not its modality, not its polarity. That's true of every quincunx, since signs five apart always differ in all three, and it's why the aspect feels the way it does. It's also called the inconjunct.

A quincunx relates two planets through adjustment. Each keeps its own job, but they can't do it the same way, so one keeps changing its approach to make room for the other: you recalibrate expectations, reorder priorities, find a third way. It isn't conflict and it isn't failure. It's the sense that two parts of you need different conditions, and that arranging those conditions is your job, again and again. Nor does it resolve on its own. You rarely solve a quincunx once; you get better at it, until the adjusting becomes almost second nature.

Astrologers keep it tight, a degree or two either side of exact (see [orbs](/astrology/orbs/)), and I'd rather miss a wide quincunx than over-read one: once you look for a mismatch, you can find it anywhere. Near the end of a sign, the angle can fall between signs four or six apart, and then the two signs do share an element or a modality after all: an [out-of-sign aspect](/astrology/out-of-sign-aspects/), a little less foreign.

It sits 30° past the [trine](/astrology/trine/) and 30° short of the [opposition](/astrology/opposition/), and it isn't one of the five major aspects: the [conjunction](/astrology/conjunction/), the [sextile](/astrology/sextile/), the [square](/astrology/square/), the trine and the opposition. Nor is it one of Ptolemy's four. In the *Tetrabiblos*, signs five apart are among those he leaves out, because they divide the circle into unequal parts. The difference from its neighbours, as I read it: a square is friction between signs that share a way of moving; an opposition is a polarity between signs that mirror each other; a quincunx joins signs with no common ground, so there's nothing to fight over and nothing to mirror, only something to arrange. Its counterpart near the conjunction is the [semi-sextile](/astrology/semi-sextile/), at 30°, which also joins two signs with nothing in common, but side by side.

## Between which planets {#between-which-planets}

There are no pages here for a quincunx between two particular planets: it's a minor aspect, and I read it in context rather than as a set piece. What changes it most is how fast the two bodies move.

- **Between two fast bodies** (the Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars), it's personal and daily: a mismatch between, say, what you need and how you act, so the adjusting happens in ordinary life — when you rest, how you ask, what you do with an evening.
- **Between a fast body and a slow one** (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto), it's where I look most closely: a personal need that keeps adapting to a larger demand, often over years.
- **Between two slow bodies**, the mismatch belongs to everyone born over months or years, so I read it through the houses: where in your life that generation's adjusting has to happen.

Its neighbours among the minor aspects ask for different work. The [sesquiquadrate](/astrology/sesquiquadrate/), at 135°, a square and a half, is a compound tension that asks you to restructure rather than adjust; the [quintile](/astrology/quintile/), at 72°, a fifth of the circle, is read as creative organisation, a gift for pattern. Both are among the aspects Johannes Kepler added to Ptolemy's, in *Harmonices Mundi* (Linz, 1619).

Two planets in sextile that both quincunx a third draw a long, narrow triangle: the [Yod (finger of God)](/astrology/yod-finger-of-god/). I read the planet at its point as a part of you that keeps adjusting to two others at once. It's demanding, and with practice it becomes a real skill. It isn't fate.

## The quincunx in synastry {#in-synastry}

Between two charts, a quincunx means one person's planet sits 150° from the other's. It rarely shows as a fight or a spark. More often it's a small, persistent mismatch: the way one of you rests doesn't suit the way the other works, or the way one shows affection lands slightly off for the other. Nobody is wrong, and both keep adjusting. With personal planets and a tight orb, it can matter more than it looks, though the major aspects between you usually speak first. I read it in both directions, as I do everything in [synastry readings](/synastry/), and never as a reason to stay or to leave: a mismatch two people keep adjusting to becomes a skill they share.

## The quincunx in transits {#in-transits}

A quincunx [transit](/astrology/transits/) asks for an adjustment: a planet in today's sky reaches 150° from one of yours. With the tight orb a quincunx gets, it doesn't last. The Moon's is over in a few hours, the Sun's in a few days; Mercury, Venus and Mars take from a few days to a week; only the slow planets stay for weeks or months, and they can cross the same degree up to three times as they turn retrograde. It tends to feel like a change of plan rather than an event: a schedule to rearrange, an expectation to recalibrate, a habit that needs adjusting. Weather, not a forecast: it shows where something wants adjusting, not what will happen.
