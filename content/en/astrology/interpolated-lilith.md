Between the mean Lilith I read and the restless true Lilith, there is a third way of placing the Black Moon, and it is the one that keeps closest to what the Moon actually does: interpolated Lilith.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Once each orbit, about every 27½ days, the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) reaches its farthest point from the Earth. Astronomers call the time from one such passage to the next the anomalistic month, 27.55 days on average. Interpolated Lilith takes those real passages, notes where in the zodiac the Moon stood at each one, and joins the points into a smooth path; to interpolate is simply to fill in between measured points.

That makes it a cousin of the other two calculations, not a different object. Mean Lilith, the [Black Moon Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/) most astrologers read, is the far end of the Moon's orbit averaged out, turning steadily forward once round the zodiac in 8.85 years. [True Lilith](/astrology/true-lilith/) is the far end of the ellipse fitted to a single instant. In the lists of the Black Moon's variants, the interpolated orbit sits beside those two, along with rarer choices such as charting the empty focus of the Moon's orbit.

It has nothing to do with the [Dark Moon Lilith](/astrology/dark-moon-lilith/), the second moon of the Earth that was announced and never found, though the names travel together in chart settings.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Set against mean Lilith, the Moon's actual farthest points from 2000 to 2040, 544 of them, all fell within about 5½° of the mean, and half of them within about 3¼°. So interpolated Lilith, which follows those points, keeps to a narrow band either side of mean Lilith, where true Lilith can wander tens of degrees away.

The points don't advance evenly. From one month's farthest point to the next, the Moon's apogee usually moves on by a few degrees, but about one month in five the new point falls slightly behind the last, by up to about 3°. A path drawn through them dips back now and then, by degrees rather than by tens of degrees.

What that means in a chart:

- **Its sign is usually mean Lilith's sign.** Only near the edge of a sign can a few degrees put the two on either side of the line.
- **Its transits come within weeks of mean Lilith's,** where true Lilith's can come months apart.
- **Its house needs the birth time,** like any Lilith's; without the time, there is no house to give it ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read mean Lilith. It's the steady point most astrologers use, and everything I write about Lilith on this site, in a sign like [Lilith in Aries](/astrology/lilith-in-aries/) or in a house, is written for it. If a program gives you interpolated Lilith, it will usually land in the same sign; when it doesn't, it's because the mean sits close to a sign's edge, and then I'd still read the mean.

Lilith carries weight in my readings. In my notebook, the mean Black Moon was one of [the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/) 75 to 90% of the time, with Saturn and Chiron, while overall about a third of what I checked held. That count was made with mean Lilith, so it says nothing of its own about the interpolated one ([my testing notes](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)).

If you're comparing charts, settle on one Lilith for all of them, and check each chart's label before you compare anything ([which Lilith is mine?](/astrology/which-lilith-is-mine/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Interpolated Lilith is true Lilith, smoothed.”** It's drawn from the Moon's actual farthest points, not from the ellipse fitted to an instant.
- **“Interpolated means made up.”** To interpolate is to fill in between measured points: here, the Moon's real passages at its farthest.
- **“It can be anywhere relative to mean Lilith.”** From 2000 to 2040 it never strays more than about 5½° from it.
- **“Interpolated Lilith is the Dark Moon.”** The Dark Moon is a hypothetical second moon; interpolated Lilith is a point of the real Moon's orbit.
