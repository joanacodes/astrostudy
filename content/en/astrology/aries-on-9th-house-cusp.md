With Aries on the 9th house cusp, the house of the far horizon opens in the sign of the first step. Long journeys, other cultures, higher study and what you believe: here each of them begins with a decision rather than a plan.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

What you believe, and how far you'll go to understand more, takes its manner from the sign on the [9th house](/astrology/9th-house/) cusp ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)), and [Aries](/astrology/aries/) makes the search a practical one. A belief has to survive contact with your own life before you'll call it yours, and a philosophy interests you most when you can put it into practice on Monday. You tend to choose your own teachers and subjects, to sign up for the course before you've finished reading about it, and to say in the seminar what everyone else was thinking. When it's your turn to teach, you tend to show first and explain afterwards. Abroad, a first visit can turn into a long attachment because you went to find out instead of wondering. Sagittarius, the 9th's natural sign, is fire like Aries, so this is fire opening a fire house. The risk isn't too little conviction but conviction reached too fast, and the remedy sits opposite, in the Libra on your 3rd: everyday conversation, where big ideas get weighed.

Tradition gave the Sun its joy in the 9th, the house it called the God, and its exaltation in Aries, so with this cusp a house where the old books saw the Sun at its happiest opens in the sign where they saw it at its strongest. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) is the ruler, though: Aries is its sign, and its house shows where your convictions are tested. Scorpio usually sits on your IC in these charts, so Mars also shares the rule of your 4th with Pluto, and I read both. What you believe and where you feel rooted tend to shape each other, in the home you grew up in or the one you choose far from where you started. The house that holds your Mars is where your beliefs get their test: the [1st](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-1st-house/), [2nd](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-2nd-house/), [3rd](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/), [4th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-4th-house/), [5th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-5th-house/), [6th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-6th-house/), [7th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-7th-house/), [8th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-8th-house/), [9th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-9th-house/), [10th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-10th-house/), [11th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-11th-house/) or [12th](/astrology/9th-house-ruler-in-12th-house/) house.

London's count is about 1 birth in 11, New York's 1 in 12, in Placidus, the system I use: the same shares as Aries on the 5th, the 9th's mirror below the horizon. Leo or Virgo rises with it, about evenly at London and more often Leo at New York, and the Midheaven is usually in Taurus. Aries never opens the 10th as well, so your career cusp lies in another sign ([Aries on the 10th house cusp](/astrology/aries-on-10th-house-cusp/) describes other charts). Now and then, though, it has already opened the 8th, and Mars then rules both what you share and what you believe ([Aries on the 8th house cusp](/astrology/aries-on-8th-house-cusp/); [duplicated signs](/astrology/duplicated-signs/)). And about a sixth of London births have no Aries cusp at all ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)).

Every one of these cusps comes from the birth time and place; until both are known, I can't say which house Aries opens, so I don't begin the reading ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
