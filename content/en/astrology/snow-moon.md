The Snow moon is February's full moon, under a name that comes from almanacs and the weather of the north-eastern woods rather than from astrology. In a chart it is simply that month's full moon, and I read it by the sign it falls in.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Each month the Moon comes round to face the Sun, and for a night it shows its whole lit side: the [full moon](/astrology/full-moon/). Almanacs give each of the year's full moons a name, and February's is the Snow moon, between the [Wolf moon](/astrology/wolf-moon/) of January and the [Worm moon](/astrology/worm-moon/) of March. These are its dates from 2026 to 2030, in Universal Time, with the Moon's sign and degree:

- **2026:** 1 February, 22:09 UT, at 13°04′ Leo
- **2027:** 20 February, 23:24 UT, at 2°05′ Virgo, on the night of a penumbral lunar eclipse
- **2028:** 10 February, 15:04 UT, at 21°24′ Leo
- **2029:** 28 February, 17:10 UT, at 10°24′ Virgo
- **2030:** 18 February, 06:20 UT, at 29°38′ Leo

Universal Time is London's winter clock: in February, Paris is an hour ahead and New York five hours behind, and in Asia and Australia a full moon late in the UT day falls on the next date.

Only two signs ever hold it. A full moon puts the Moon opposite the Sun, and the Sun leaves Aquarius for Pisces around the 19th of February, so the Snow moon lands in Leo when it comes early in the month and in Virgo when it comes late. The 2030 one sits at 29°38′ Leo, less than half a degree short of Virgo.

The 2027 Snow moon is also close enough to the Moon's node for the Earth's shadow to reach it. It's a penumbral eclipse, the faintest kind: the Moon passes through the pale outer part of the shadow and only dims a little ([lunar eclipses](/astrology/lunar-eclipses/) explains the three kinds).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

The name reaches us through two books. Jonathan Carver, a colonial-era explorer, printed a list of twelve moons in his *Travels Through the Interior Parts of North-America* (1778), the account of his journeys of 1766 to 1768. His year begins in March with the Worm Month and ends with the Snow Moon. He gives the names as those of “the Indians” in general, and doesn't say which people he heard them from.

The second is the Maine Farmer's Almanac, which began printing what it called “Indian” names for every month's full moon in the 1930s, as NASA's monthly full moon guide recounts. The almanac's reason for this one is the plainest of the year: February brings heavy snow to the north-east of North America. It offers two more names for the same moon: the Storm Moon, and the Hunger Moon, because storms and deep snow made hunting hard. NASA's guide adds a caution worth keeping: across North America, peoples speaking different languages had different names for the moons, and no single list stands for all of them.

The name follows the calendar, not the sky. Whichever full moon falls in February is the Snow moon, whatever its sign. February is also the only month shorter than the Moon's cycle of about 29½ days from one full moon to the next, so in some years it has no full moon at all. Every February from 2026 to 2030 has one.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

To be plain about it: the Snow moon is a folk and almanac name, and it isn't astrology. Snow in Maine says nothing about your chart. What I read is the full moon underneath the name, which in February is either the [full moon in Leo](/astrology/full-moon-in-leo/), with the Sun in Aquarius, or the [full moon in Virgo](/astrology/full-moon-in-virgo/), with the Sun in Pisces.

Even that full moon gets a modest place. I treat a lunation as a short monthly marker. It counts when it falls within one degree of a planet or angle in your chart, at either end of its line: on the Moon's degree, or on the Sun's opposite it. And it counts as a trigger inside something already under way, a transit or a progression on the same point, never as a forecast of its own. The 2028 Snow moon at 21°24′ Leo would matter in a chart with a planet near 21° Leo or 21° Aquarius; for most charts it is a bright night and a pleasant name.

Where in your life it falls depends on which houses Leo and Virgo occupy in your chart, and that takes [your birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“The Snow moon is the Native American name for February's full moon.”** There was never one name: different peoples had their own. This one came to us through an explorer's list of 1778 and an almanac of the 1930s.
- **“The Snow moon brings snow.”** It's named after the season, not the other way round, and south of the equator February's full moon rises on summer nights.
- **“A Snow moon has its own astrological meaning.”** Astrology reads the sign it falls in, Leo or Virgo, and the house that sign occupies in your chart.
- **“It always comes in mid-February.”** It moves with the Moon's cycle: from the 1st of the month in 2026 to the 28th in 2029.
