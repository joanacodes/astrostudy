Twice in each round, the North Node trines your Midheaven, and in most charts it does so from below the horizon, out of the half of the chart where private life is lived. The Midheaven itself, the degree at the top of the chart, comes from your birth time, and so do the houses this trine is read through ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

## What changes {#what-changes}

The [Midheaven](/astrology/midheaven/) gives your capacities a public form: a role, a responsibility, a contribution with your name on it. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) points to what you've had least practice in. A [trine](/astrology/trine/) relates them by ease, and neither has to give way for the other to work, so under this [transit](/astrology/transits/) a newer skill can find a public use without a fight: the course you took turns out to fit the project, the habit you built at home makes the work steadier.

Ease isn't inevitability, and a trine is quiet: nothing in it forces a step, and it promises neither recognition nor a result.

The other node completes the picture. The [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) sextiles your Midheaven and trines your [IC](/astrology/imum-coeli/), so in these weeks the old ways of resting and belonging cost nothing, and the standing you already have can introduce the newer work.

Counted in Placidus houses, which is what I use, the first trine of a round usually reaches you from your 5th or 6th house ([North Node transiting the 5th house](/astrology/north-node-transit-5th-house/)), the second from your 2nd or 1st ([North Node transiting the 2nd house](/astrology/north-node-transit-2nd-house/)). Both, in most charts, sit below the horizon, in the half of the chart that holds the self, resources, home and daily work: where the practice gets done before it shows.

## How long it lasts {#how-long-it-lasts}

Counting from 1½° before exact to 1° after, the trine usually lasts about a month, and all but one pass in twenty make a single exact hit. One pass in ten is quicker, 25 days or less, while one in ten outlasts 100 days; roughly one in twenty-five goes over the exact degree three times. The slow passes are those that meet an eclipse season, when the Sun sits close to the nodes and the true node nearly halts. An eclipse in those weeks falls well away from your Midheaven and IC, so if it touches anything, it is a planet near one of the nodes, not the angle.

The forecast I prepare readings from counts the nodes by conjunction and opposition alone, so this trine isn't in it.

The first trine of a round arrives some six years on from the node's crossing of your Midheaven ([Transiting North Node conjunct natal Midheaven](/astrology/transiting-north-node-conjunct-natal-midheaven/)); three years later the North Node stands on your IC ([Transiting North Node opposite natal Midheaven](/astrology/transiting-north-node-opposite-natal-midheaven/)), and three years after that comes the second trine. Between each trine and the nearest crossing of your Midheaven lie the square ([Transiting North Node square natal Midheaven](/astrology/transiting-north-node-square-natal-midheaven/)) and the sextile ([Transiting North Node sextile natal Midheaven](/astrology/transiting-north-node-sextile-natal-midheaven/)). By 80, everyone has met the trine eight or nine times.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Take one thing you've been practising in private and give it a public outing this month: a skill into a meeting, a draft to a reader.

Use the ease to finish rather than to start. A trine is good at carrying things over the line.

Rest the way you know how. The South Node's trine to your IC makes old comforts restore you quickly; they're part of the support, not a distraction from it.

Tell someone at work what you've been learning. A trine shows its value faster when somebody else can see it.
