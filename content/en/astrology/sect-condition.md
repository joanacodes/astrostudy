Sect sorts whole charts into day and night. Sect condition turns the same question on each planet: how well does this one fit the side its chart was born on? The answer comes in steps, from a planet at ease on every count to one that is out of place on all of them.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Every chart has a sect, day if the Sun was above the horizon at birth, night if it was below ([Sect: day and night charts](/astrology/sect-day-and-night-charts/)). Every traditional planet has one too: the Sun, [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) and [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/) belong to the day, the Moon, [Venus](/astrology/venus/) and [Mars](/astrology/mars/) to the night. A planet whose sect matches its chart's is in sect; one whose sect doesn't is contrary to it. That first match is the oldest part of the doctrine.

The astrologers who wrote in Arabic in the Middle Ages turned it into a graded one, with two further tests. The sign test asks for a masculine sign for a day planet and a feminine sign for a night planet. The hemisphere test asks a day planet to stand in the same half of the sky as the Sun, above or below the horizon, and a night planet in the half opposite. The tests weren't equal. Belonging to the chart's sect came first; a planet that failed it could still claim some sect from its sign, and a last shred of it from its hemisphere. A planet that passed all three was in [hayz](/astrology/hayz/).

## How it works {#how-it-works}

Take a night chart with the Sun in late Sagittarius, just under the western horizon, and grade four of its planets.

- **Mars at 18° Pisces, above the horizon:** a night planet in a night chart, in a feminine sign, in the half of the sky away from the Sun. All three: hayz.
- **Venus at 20° Sagittarius, below the horizon:** in sect by its chart, but in a masculine sign and in the Sun's half of the sky. One test of three.
- **Jupiter at 10° Leo, below the horizon:** a day planet in a night chart, so contrary to the sect, yet in a masculine sign and in the Sun's half. Two tests, without the first.
- **Saturn at 8° Taurus, above the horizon:** a day planet at night, in a feminine sign, in the half away from the Sun. None of the three.

Mercury goes through the same tests once its phase has given it a side ([Oriental and occidental](/astrology/oriental-and-occidental/)): a Mercury that rose before the Sun is tested as a day planet, one that set after it as a night planet.

The result is a profile, not a ranking. In that chart Venus is the benefic of the sect and passes only the first test, while Jupiter, the benefic contrary to the sect, passes the other two ([Benefic and malefic of the sect](/astrology/benefic-and-malefic-of-the-sect/)). Sect condition is also only one line of a planet's report, beside its sign, house, motion and aspects ([The chart ruler's condition](/astrology/chart-rulers-condition/)), and one of several old tests of a planet's state, among them [feral planets](/astrology/feral-planets/).

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

I read sect condition as grain: whether a planet's function runs along the time of day its chart was born into, or has to work against it. In the chart above, Mars works with little resistance from its surroundings, while Saturn has to work in conditions that weren't made for it. That doesn't make Saturn weaker at what Saturn does: it still structures, slowly and carefully, and asks more time of you to do it. I'd say so plainly rather than call it a problem.

The tests hang on the horizon, so they hang on the birth time. The chart's sect, and every planet's half of the sky, depend on it, and near sunrise or sunset a few minutes decide ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

[My notebook](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) never tested sect, of the chart or of a planet. When a planet's sect condition comes up in a reading, I present it as the tradition's view, and I don't let it outweigh what the planet's sign, house and aspects say ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Sect condition is all or nothing.”** It comes in steps: three tests, passed in any combination, with the chart's sect counting most.
- **“The benefic of the sect is always at ease.”** By its chart, yes; its sign and its side of the horizon can still go against it, as Venus does above.
- **“A planet at home in its sign is in good sect condition.”** Dignity and sect are separate measures. Mars in Aries, above the horizon in a day chart, is at home and fails every test.
- **“Sect condition can be read from the birth date.”** Only its sign test can. The other two need the time of birth.
