The first decan of Taurus is 0° to 10° of the sign, the first ten degrees of fixed earth, crossed by the Sun in the last days of April. Its face goes to Mercury, its element to Venus, and the Moon's exaltation degree lies inside it.

## Its rulers {#its-rulers}

The Chaldean order takes no notice of the border between two signs. Lilly's table of [faces](/astrology/faces/) runs straight on from Venus, who held the last face of Aries, to [Mercury](/astrology/mercury/), so Taurus opens under Mercury. The modern decans start each sign again from its own ruler, and give these degrees to Venus, who rules [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/). The two lists part company from the first degree.

I find the split useful. Venus, by sign and in the modern list by decan too, says what the planet in these degrees wants to keep and enjoy; Mercury, by face, adds a mind that sorts, names and compares what it keeps. With a planet here I read Venus first and Mercury second, each through its own sign and house.

The Moon has a stake here as well. It is exalted in Taurus, and the degree the old tables give that exaltation, the 3rd of the sign, falls in this decan ([exaltation](/astrology/exaltation/)). By night the Moon also rules the earth triplicity, so a night-born [Moon in Taurus](/astrology/moon-in-taurus/) between 2° and 3° stands on its exaltation degree with the triplicity behind it. Only the face is missing: the Moon's comes ten degrees later ([Taurus, second decan](/astrology/taurus-second-decan/)).

## Its image {#its-image}

None of the old names marks the change of sign. To the Egyptian count this was simply the thirty-first decan after Sothis: Ārt in Budge's list, Chau in the Greco-Egyptian ones, Suo or Asicat for Firmicus Maternus, Soou in the Greek Hermetic list and Horopel in the *Testament of Solomon*. Two later lists gave it figures. Cosmas of Maiuma put Charis here, grace in person; Athanasius Kircher, in 1652, put Serapis, a god of Ptolemaic Egypt. I treat them as part of the decan's history, not its meaning ([the Egyptian decans](/astrology/egyptian-decans/)).

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

The Sun stands here for births from about 20 to 29 April ([Sun in Taurus](/astrology/sun-in-taurus/)). In 2026 it entered Taurus on 20 April at 01:39 UT and crossed 10° on 30 April at 08:04 UT; in 2027 at 07:17 and 13:44 UT, so 30 April belongs to this decan in some years and to the next in others. At the other edge, a birth in the first hours of 20 April, UT, can still have the Sun in the last degree of Aries ([Aries, third decan](/astrology/aries-third-decan/)).

An Ascendant here moves on quickly. Seen from the latitude of London, these ten degrees take about twenty minutes to rise, so the decan of a [Taurus rising](/astrology/taurus-rising/) chart depends on a birth time accurate to the quarter of an hour ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Three fixed stars stand in the decan in 2026. [Mirach](/astrology/mirach/), in Andromeda, crossed into Taurus in 1970 and sits at 0°46′; [Mira](/astrology/mira/), the variable star of Cetus, at 1°53′; and [Hamal](/astrology/hamal/), the brightest star of the constellation Aries, at 8°01′. A star of the Ram in the sign of the Bull is precession at work: the tropical signs follow the seasons, not the constellations ([precession of the equinoxes](/astrology/precession-of-the-equinoxes/)).

Uranus crossed these degrees for births from 1935 to 1937, after a first visit in 1934 and before a last one early in 1938, and again from 2018 to 2021. Saturn passed in 1939–41, 1969–70 and 1998–99.
