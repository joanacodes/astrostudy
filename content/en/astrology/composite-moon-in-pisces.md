Some relationships are where two people go to rest from the world: music in the evening, long silences that feel full, a tenderness that doesn't need explaining. A composite Moon in Pisces gives the bond that kind of need, porous, imaginative and kind.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In a [composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), the [Moon](/astrology/moon/) sits at the nearer midpoint of your two natal Moons, describing how the relationship takes in and digests what it feels. [Pisces](/astrology/pisces/), mutable water, brings compassion, imagination and a sense of everything being connected. The bond picks up moods quickly, its own and other people's, and settles through quiet and closeness: a film watched in silence, a walk by the water, the kind of care that notices sadness before it is named.

That sensitivity can belong to the relationship even when neither person is especially dreamy. Two practical people, neither born with the [Moon in Pisces](/astrology/moon-in-pisces/), may find that together they become gentler, more intuitive, more forgiving; the relationship softens what each of them brings to it. It often doubles as a refuge, a place where the ordinary demands of the day are allowed to fall away for a while.

The risk is that kindness blurs into avoidance. A bond that absorbs every mood can lose track of whose feeling is whose, put off a hard practical conversation because it might hurt, or idealise itself instead of looking. Empathy isn't the absence of boundaries, and imagination isn't deception. This Moon stays well when the pair keeps a few plain facts in view, and lets each person have time alone to find their own feelings again.

For Pisces I read both rulers, Neptune and Jupiter. Composite [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) shows where the relationship dreams, forgives and loosens its edges; composite [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/), where it finds meaning and room to be generous.

Pisces is also where composite arithmetic catches people out. Take one Moon at 20° Aquarius and one at 10° Aries: as numbers on the circle, 320° and 10°, their plain average is 165°, which is 15° Virgo, yet the nearer [midpoint](/astrology/midpoints/), the one a composite uses, is the opposite point, 15° Pisces. About 1 pair in 12 has its composite Moon in this last sign of the zodiac. At the end of the sign, a later birth time in either chart can carry it over into the [composite Moon in Aries](/astrology/composite-moon-in-aries/), quick where Pisces is slow to react; at its start, an earlier one leaves it with the [composite Moon in Aquarius](/astrology/composite-moon-in-aquarius/), which wants space more than tenderness ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/#the-moon)).

A Pisces composite Moon can make a relationship feel fated, as if a third presence held the two of you together. The composite is arithmetic, each planet's midpoint found on its own: the feeling is real, the third person isn't. The technique is described here, not practised in my readings, and the astrologers who use it never take it apart from the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/) and the charts of the two people themselves, nor as a promise that a relationship is meant to be, or meant to last.
