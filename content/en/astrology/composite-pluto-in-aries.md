Composite Pluto in Aries belongs to couples who are either long gone or not yet born. Pluto last crossed Aries from 1822 to 1853 and won't be back until 2066, so this page gives the sign's meaning in a relationship chart, plainly labelled as a description no living pair can test.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) marks where the relationship itself transforms: what it strips away, what it rebuilds, where its intensity gathers. [Aries](/astrology/aries/), cardinal fire ruled by Mars, would give that work the manner of a first move. A bond like this would renew itself by beginning again rather than by mending: a new home chosen in a weekend, a shared project dropped and replaced by a bolder one, a way of being together rewritten from the first line. Its depth would show as nerve, the readiness to say *this isn't working* and act on it the same day. Its trap would be the one Pluto always has with fresh starts: clearing everything away and calling the empty space renewal.

Mars would be the planet to read beside it. In a composite, Mars shows how the pair acts, argues and goes after what it wants, and with an Aries Pluto those questions would sit close to the bond's core. Aries supplies initiative and independence, not aggression: two people could share this placement and never raise their voices, as long as each kept room to act on their own.

No couple can have it now, because of how slowly Pluto moves. It spends 12 to 31 years in a sign, so two people of about the same age carry it in the same sign, and their composite Pluto sits there too; with an age gap, the composite goes to the midpoint between two generations' Plutos. For that midpoint to fall in Aries, the births have to sit in or around Pluto's stays in the sign: April 1822 to February 1853, or June 2066 to March 2097. In two million pairs drawn from the births of 1930 to 2009, it never came up once. People born during the last stay carry [Pluto in Aries](/astrology/pluto-in-aries/) in their own charts, and their relationships belong to history now.

The neighbouring pages show the same gap from either side of the circle: [composite Pluto in Pisces](/astrology/composite-pluto-in-pisces/) waits for people born from 2043, and [composite Pluto in Taurus](/astrology/composite-pluto-in-taurus/) needs at least one partner born before 1884. In practice, the composite Plutos of living couples run from Cancer to Capricorn, and which one a pair gets depends on when the two were born ([generational placements by birth year](/astrology/generational-placements-by-birth-year/)).

When couples born after 2066 come to have it, the sign will be shared by most pairs of their age, and what makes it particular to one relationship will be its house, which needs both birth times. Until then, I'd rather say plainly that this page describes no one alive than dress a guess up as a portrait ([how I read](/how-i-read/)).
