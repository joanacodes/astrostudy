Tradition gave Mars one house where it is said to rejoice, and it isn't a house of glory. It's the 6th, the house of the daily grind. Put the planet of effort among the tasks, routines and lists of an ordinary day, and it comes alive.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is effort, initiative and the will to get things done. [The 6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) is the shape of an ordinary day: the work you actually do, your routines, the people you work alongside, the care you give your body. Tradition called it Bad Fortune, the house of toil, and said that Mars has its [joy](/astrology/planetary-joys/) there: the planet of effort is happy where there's effort to be made. With Mars here, you tend to work hard, quickly and practically, to tackle problems as they come, and to feel best with something in your hands.

It's a cadent house, one of the houses of adjusting and maintaining, and its natural sign is Virgo. Opposite it is the 12th, where Saturn rejoices, so this Mars faces a house of retreat and rest across the chart: a hint about what it most often forgets.

The old books also gave the 6th illness. A chart can't diagnose or predict anything about your health ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/)), and questions about it belong with a doctor. What I read with Mars here is how you spend your energy from day to day, and whether your body gets a share of the care you give your work.

At your birth, Mars had dropped below the western horizon within the last couple of hours or so. Placidus, my house system, puts the Descendant at the start of the 7th, so a Mars that had set only minutes earlier sits at the very end of the 6th, and with an uncertain time it could as easily be in the 7th ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). [Mars in the 5th house](/astrology/mars-in-5th-house/) plays to win; [Mars in the 7th house](/astrology/mars-in-7th-house/) meets its match in a partner. Between them, the 6th simply gets on with the job.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

In close relationships, love often looks like help that arrives before anyone asks: the shelf put up, the form filled in, the car taken to the garage. Shared chores can turn into a quiet battleground if you and a partner work at different speeds, and impatience with someone's slowness is the thing to watch. Working on something together, a garden, a renovation, a training plan, can bring you closer than any long talk.

## At work {#at-work}

This is the placement's home ground. You're energetic, efficient and practical, quick to fix what's broken and good at keeping a process moving. Trades, crafts, operations, logistics, emergency work, any job with a clear task and a visible result, suit it well. You can also become the colleague who's irritated when others drag their feet, or who quietly takes on everyone's share. A workplace that values speed and competence brings out your best; one that runs on meetings and delay, your worst.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Overwork. Rest can feel like laziness, and a free day turns into a list. Irritation with colleagues, especially slow ones, can leak into the atmosphere. And you may treat your body like a tool that should keep running, pushing through tiredness instead of listening to it.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Build movement into the day, a walk, a training session, a physical job, so that effort has somewhere to go besides work. Plan rest with the same firmness as tasks. Turn irritation into improvement: fix the process instead of fighting the person. Take questions about your health to a doctor, and give your body the same steady attention you'd give a machine you relied on. The 6th is Mars's joy; let the joy include stopping when the work is done.
