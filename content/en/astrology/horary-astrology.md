Most astrology starts from a birth. Horary starts from a question: the chart is cast for the minute the question reaches the astrologer and is understood, and the answer is read from that chart.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

A horary chart belongs to a question, not to a person. Where is the ring I lost? Will the sale go through? Should I take the job? Once the astrologer has understood what is being asked, they draw the chart for that minute and place, and read the matter from it, as if the sky of that moment held the question in miniature.

The method is old, and its classic text in English is [William Lilly’s Christian Astrology (1647)](/astrology/william-lillys-christian-astrology-1647/). [William Lilly](/astrology/william-lilly/) printed it in English rather than Latin, the first compendium of its kind to be, and gave its second book to resolving questions, with worked examples on illness, wealth, marriage and travel.

## How it is done {#how-it-is-done}

The astrologer notes the time and place at which the question is understood, and casts the chart. The person asking, the querent, takes the 1st house and the planet that rules its sign, with the Moon as a second voice. The matter asked about, the quesited, takes the house that covers it and that house's ruler: the 10th for a job, the 7th for a partner or an opponent, the 2nd for money or a lost possession. A question about someone else's affairs is counted on from that person's own house ([Derived houses](/astrology/derived-houses/)).

Then the two rulers, the significators, are watched. An aspect forming between them, still [applying](/astrology/applying-and-separating-aspects/), is read as the two coming together; one that has already passed, as something done. The tradition names the ways a meeting can be helped or blocked, such as [translation of light](/astrology/translation-of-light/), when a third planet carries the connection from one to the other. A Moon that will make no further aspect before leaving its sign, the [void-of-course Moon](/astrology/void-of-course-moon/), is read as a matter that will come to little.

Some horary astrologers first check whether a chart is fit to be judged at all, and set it aside if not; others, the horary astrologer John Frawley among them, do without those rules.

## What it shows {#what-it-shows}

Judged by its own standards, a good horary reading gives more than a yes or a no: the state of the matter, what the people involved want, the options open to the one asking. That is its appeal. A question, a minute, an answer.

It's also why I don't practise it. A horary chart answers the question as it was put, and many of the questions people bring to it are ones I won't read: an illness, a pregnancy, a court case, an investment, whether a relationship will last ([How I read](/how-i-read/)). Lilly's second book judged questions of that kind, and some horary astrologers still do ([Can astrology predict a court case?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-a-court-case/)). My readings are of a birth chart, read for tendencies and seasons, and "what will happen" isn't a question I answer from any chart.

## Its limits {#its-limits}

Everything rests on one minute, and that minute can be hard to pin down. Is it when you typed the question, when the astrologer read it, or when they understood it? A message written at night and opened in the morning gives two different charts.

An answer can also make itself true. Told that a sale will go through, you may push harder; told that it won't, you may stop trying, and the prediction then looks right for reasons that have nothing to do with the sky ([Self-fulfilling prophecy](/astrology/self-fulfilling-prophecy/)).

And a yes or a no, from any source, can quietly take over a decision that's yours. If you're torn about a job, a move or a relationship, the people and the facts involved will tell you more than a chart of the minute you asked. Horary's sister technique works the other way round, choosing a moment in advance rather than reading one: [Electional astrology](/astrology/electional-astrology/).
