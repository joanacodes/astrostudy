Their Mercury in your 1st house puts another person's thinking right where you meet the world. What they notice first is you: your pace, your manner, the look on your face as you start something. And they rarely keep the observation to themselves.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is how a person takes things in, makes sense of them and passes them on: noticing, reasoning, talking, learning. [The 1st house](/astrology/1st-house/) is the part of your chart people meet before they know you: your body, your manner, the way you begin. In this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), their mind turns towards your person.

For you, it can feel like being described properly for the first time. They put into words how you walk into a room or tackle a problem, ask you direct questions about yourself, and may have a nickname for you within a week. You may think faster beside them, answer before you've rehearsed, and leave a conversation knowing a little more about how you look from outside.

For them, you're interesting to think about. Your manner raises questions they want to answer, and their ideas tend to arrive more quickly when you're in the room. The 1st is angular, the most visible kind of house, and its natural sign is [Aries](/astrology/aries/), quick off the mark. Tradition gave Mercury its joy here: in a birth chart, the mind at home in the house of the self. Between two charts that joy is only borrowed, yet it helps explain why their thoughts settle on you so readily.

Within a few degrees of your [Ascendant](/astrology/ascendant/), their Mercury is also conjunct it, and their words land on the very point your chart rises from.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Being read aloud gets tiring. A running commentary on your clothes, your tone or the way you phrased something can start to sound like a review, and a label given in fun, "the organised one", "the dreamer", can follow you about long after it stopped fitting.

From their side, the risk is that thinking aloud lands as judgement. They meant an observation; you heard a verdict, and they watched it register on your face before they'd finished the sentence. If you react to their remarks quickly and visibly, they may start editing themselves around you, which is a loss for you both.

Mercury describes the habits of a mind, not anyone's intentions: this overlay says where their attention tends to go, and nothing more.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Ask for the observations you'd find useful, and say which ones you'd rather not hear. A simple "what made you think that?" turns a remark into a conversation. In a couple, enjoy being noticed and keep comments on appearance kind and rare; between friends, they may be the frank voice you check a first impression with before an interview; between colleagues, the one who tells you clearly how your presentation came across; between adult siblings, the one who still narrates you, and can be asked to update the story.

Turn the charts round and you are the one who can't stop putting them into words: that's your Mercury in their 1st house. Move their Mercury back a house and it lands among the things you leave unsaid ([their Mercury in your 12th house](/astrology/mercury-in-12th-house-synastry/)); move it forward and it starts weighing what you own and value ([their Mercury in your 2nd house](/astrology/mercury-in-2nd-house-synastry/)).

Your Ascendant moves about a degree every four minutes, which is why, in Placidus, the house system I use, your birth time decides whether a Mercury close to it falls in your 12th house or your 1st. Theirs matters less: Mercury takes two or three weeks to cross a sign, a degree or two a day, so their birth date places it unless it sits right on one of your cusps. Even so, I open a synastry only once I have both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)), and only when you both know about it ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
