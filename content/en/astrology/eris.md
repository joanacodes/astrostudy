Eris is the reason astronomers had to say what a planet is. Found in 2005 and thought at first to be bigger than [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/), it forced a definition, and Pluto came out of it a dwarf planet. Astrologers who adopted Eris took its name at its word: discord. The goddess behind the name is more interesting than that.

## What it is {#what-it-is}

Eris is a dwarf planet in the scattered disc, the population of icy bodies that [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/)'s pull flung onto distant, unusual orbits while the Solar System was forming. It is 2,326 km across, a little smaller than Pluto, but about a quarter more massive, and its surface is almost white. Its orbit takes about 558 years and swings from 38.4 times the Earth's distance from the Sun to 97.7 times. It was at its farthest around 1977, and won't be at its closest again until December 2257. The orbit is tilted about 44° to the Sun's path, which is one reason searches that kept to the plane of the planets passed it by.

Mike Brown, Chad Trujillo and David Rabinowitz photographed it at Palomar Observatory in October 2003, but their software ignored anything moving that slowly. Going back over the old images, they found it on 5 January 2005. Seeming larger than Pluto, it was called the tenth planet in the news, and in August 2006 the International Astronomical Union settled the question with its first definition of a planet: Eris, Pluto and [Ceres](/astrology/ceres/) became dwarf planets. The name came a few weeks later. Brown chose the goddess of strife and discord, and said she had earned it: the object had caused quarrels too. Its moon is Dysnomia, Lawlessness, one of her daughters.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

As the story came to be told, Eris was the one god left off the guest list for the wedding of Peleus and Thetis. She came anyway and threw a golden apple among the guests, meant for the fairest; Hera, Athena and Aphrodite each claimed it, Paris was asked to judge, and his choice led to the Trojan War. Homer mentions the judgement with neither Eris nor an apple: the apple belongs to later versions, such as Hyginus's.

The far older poet Hesiod offers another Eris. At the opening of his *Works and Days* he says there are two kinds of strife on earth: one fosters war and conflict, and no one loves it; the other is "much better for men", because it "rouses even the helpless man to work" when he sees a neighbour hurrying to plough and plant. Read through both, Eris carries rivalry in two directions: the quarrel that splits people, and the competition that makes them work.

Two features of the body itself matter for anyone who places it. It is slow: at 558 years a circuit, it spends decades in each sign, longest near its farthest point, where it has been in our lifetimes, so its sign is shared by everyone born over several decades, the mark of a generation rather than of a person ([personal, social and generational planets](/astrology/personal-social-and-generational-planets/)). And it is steeply tilted: much of the time it stands far above or below the Sun's path, and a chart records only its position along the zodiac, projected down onto it. Two points in conjunction by zodiac degree can be far apart in the actual sky, a caution astrologers also apply to [fixed stars](/astrology/fixed-stars-in-astrology/) far from that path.

## In a reading {#in-a-reading}

Eris isn't part of the system behind my readings, and I don't add it. When the astronomers reclassified Pluto in 2006, nothing changed in how I read Pluto; when they named Eris, nothing changed either. Whether a body counts as a planet is their question; whether it belongs in a reading is mine, and I answer it with what I've tested.

Eris had no line in that test. My notebook covered the Sun, the Moon, the rising sign, each planet, Chiron and Lilith, and the placements that held far more often than the rest, 75 to 90% of the time, were [the three that held](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/): Saturn, Chiron and Lilith. A body that shares its sign with people born decades apart would have to earn its place through its house or a tight contact, and I have no evidence either way ([How I read](/how-i-read/)).

Among the same team's other finds are [Makemake](/astrology/makemake/), announced the same day as Eris, and [Sedna](/astrology/sedna/), found in 2003, whose orbit never brings it nearer than 76 times the Earth's distance from the Sun.

## Common myths {#myths}

- **“Eris is the tenth planet.”** The news called it that in 2005. Since 2006 it has been a dwarf planet, like Pluto and Ceres.
- **“Eris is bigger than Pluto.”** It's more massive, by about a quarter, but a little smaller across: a stellar occultation in 2010 and the New Horizons flyby of Pluto in 2015 settled it.
- **“A strong Eris makes you a troublemaker.”** Even the myth knew two kinds of strife, and no placement fixes a temperament.
