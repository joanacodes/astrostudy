At the base of every chart is the IC, the point where the 4th house begins and the rest of the chart finds its footing. With Taurus on the 4th house cusp, that footing is in the sign of continuity: what you keep, mend and hand on.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

The sign on the [Imum Coeli](/astrology/imum-coeli/) describes the ground you need under you ([house cusps](/astrology/house-cusps/)), and [Taurus](/astrology/taurus/) makes it ground you tend. You tend to root yourself by staying with a place long enough to improve it: the kitchen redone over three summers, the tree planted for later, the shelves that fill up slowly. Things handed down matter, and so do the things you'll hand on. Change at home isn't the problem it's made out to be; what you need is for it to add to what's there rather than wipe it clean. Nothing in the [4th house](/astrology/4th-house/) describes your parents as people; it describes the private self, and with Taurus here, that self regenerates through routine: the same morning walk, the same chair, an evening that ends the way it always does.

Tradition saw a particular fit here. The Moon rules Cancer, the 4th's natural sign, and the Moon is exalted in Taurus, so with this cusp your house of roots opens in the sign where the natural ruler of every 4th house was said to be strongest. Above it, on the Midheaven, sits Scorpio: a public life that asks for depth, nerve and intensity. The steady ground of the IC is often what makes that possible, the place you come back to after the work has taken everything.

With Taurus on the IC, the 4th belongs to [Venus](/astrology/venus/). Libra, its other sign, opens your 9th in most of these charts, so home and the far horizon of study, travel and belief answer to the same planet: a home full of books, or one made far from where you started. In the rest, Libra has no cusp and lies wholly inside the 8th or 9th ([intercepted signs](/astrology/intercepted-signs/)). Venus's house tells you what home is for, wherever it falls: the [1st](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-1st-house/), [2nd](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-2nd-house/), [3rd](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/), [4th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-4th-house/), [5th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-5th-house/), [6th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-6th-house/), [7th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-7th-house/), [8th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-8th-house/), [9th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-9th-house/), [10th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-10th-house/), [11th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-11th-house/) or [12th](/astrology/4th-house-ruler-in-12th-house/).

About 1 birth in 12 has a Taurus IC, at London as at New York. In Placidus, the house system I use, the IC is the 4th cusp, and its sign follows the time of day, not the latitude; Taurus crosses the meridian in almost exactly two hours, the average for a sign. The rising sign is then usually Capricorn, a patient manner on top of patient roots. In nearly half of these London charts Taurus opens a second house as well ([duplicated signs](/astrology/duplicated-signs/)): the 5th, so that Venus rules home and pleasure together ([Taurus on the 5th house cusp](/astrology/taurus-on-5th-house-cusp/)), or the 3rd, joining home to your everyday talk and your street ([Taurus on the 3rd house cusp](/astrology/taurus-on-3rd-house-cusp/)). Like every angle, the IC turns with the sky, so your birth time places it, and there's no reading without one ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
