Scorpio is where the zodiac goes underground. A composite Neptune there gives a relationship a longing for depth, and it is the commonest composite Neptune sign of all: about 1 pair in 3 among people born from 1930 to 2009 has it.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

In [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/), the relationship gets a chart of its own, each planet at the midpoint between two natal positions, and astrologers read it as the bond's character rather than either partner's. Its [Neptune](/astrology/neptune/) holds the bond's ideal: what the pair imagines, where its compassion runs, where the line between the two people grows thin. [Scorpio](/astrology/scorpio/), fixed water, takes all of that down to the roots. A pair with this Neptune dreams of being fully known: no masks, no small talk, a closeness that has seen the worst of each other and stayed. Its compassion is the kind that sits with someone through a crisis without trying to hurry it, and its imagination is drawn to what lies under the surface, in people, in stories, in itself.

Its frequency is a lesson in how midpoints behave. Two people born between December 1955 and November 1970 both have [Neptune in Scorpio](/astrology/neptune-in-scorpio/), and their composite does too. But Scorpio also catches nearly every pair with one partner born in the Libra years, 1942 to 1957, and the other in the Sagittarius years, 1970 to 1984, because their midpoint can land nowhere else. Midpoints gather in the middle of a range, and Scorpio sits in the middle of the Neptune signs of people born from 1930 to 2009: in two million pairs drawn at random from those births, about 1 in 3 had it, with Sagittarius a close second. For so many couples the sign is close to a given; the house, which needs both birth times, says what is theirs.

Scorpio has two rulers: [Pluto](/astrology/pluto/) for modern tropical astrology and [Mars](/astrology/mars/) for the tradition. I read both, in any chart, and a composite reading would follow suit: composite Pluto shows where the bond is changed by what it goes through, composite Mars how it acts on what it feels. With Neptune in Scorpio, those two are the planets that turn a longing for depth into a life.

Beside it, [composite Neptune in Libra](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-libra/) idealises fairness and peace, and [composite Neptune in Sagittarius](/astrology/composite-neptune-in-sagittarius/) meaning and the far horizon; Scorpio's ideal looks inward, and down.

The difficulty is merging so far that the outlines go. A bond that idealises depth can mistake intensity for truth, feel most real in a crisis, or assume that because it feels everything the other feels, it knows what the other thinks. Depth isn't secrecy, and closeness isn't fusion: some air, a few separate friendships and a plain conversation about money and boundaries keep this depth from turning into fog.

The composite is a technique I describe on this site; I don't draw composites in my readings, and those who do never read one without both natal charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/). A placement that dreams of being fully known also asks the most of consent: it should only be looked at by two people who have both chosen to be seen ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
