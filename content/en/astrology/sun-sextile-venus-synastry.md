Venus never gets more than about 47° from the Sun, so nobody's own chart can hold the two at 60°. Between two people the sextile is common, and sociable: one person's taste, their sense of what makes a good evening or a good life, keeps offering the other small pleasures and open doors.

## What flows {#what-flows}

The [Sun](/astrology/sun/) is a person's way of shining: self-expression, vitality, a creative centre. [Venus](/astrology/venus/) is taste and liking: what someone enjoys, values and wants more of. A [sextile](/astrology/sextile/) opens a door between two functions that someone has to walk through, and few of the [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/) are friendlier.

The signs are usually two apart, fire beside air or earth beside water. An Aquarius Venus with a Sagittarius Sun: a liking for unusual people, offered to someone who wants the whole wide world. A Virgo Venus with a Scorpio Sun: an eye for what's well made, offered to someone who values depth over show.

If it's your Sun, life gets pleasanter around them. They suggest the exhibition, the walk, the person you ought to meet, and their evident liking for you makes it easy to say yes. If yours is the Venus, giving them things to enjoy is a pleasure of its own: the way they take up what you offer confirms your taste, and liking them stays easy and light.

It's the contact of friends who introduce each other to things, of colleagues where one has the address book and the other the project, of flatmates who cook for each other. A couple with it may simply have fun together; that says nothing about how long they'll last, only about what's on offer.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

A sextile asks for engagement, and liking can stay polite. Two people can enjoy each other's company for years without saying anything that matters, because nothing here forces it. The offers can also run one way: the Venus person organising the good times, the Sun person enjoying them, and neither noticing who does the giving. As I read Venus, pleasure is not indulgence: the point of a shared pleasure is the sharing.

Turn the angle and the liking changes shape:

- **[Sun conjunct Venus in synastry](/astrology/sun-conjunct-venus-synastry/):** birthdays about a month apart; taste lands right on the self, warmer and easier to confuse with identity.
- **[Sun square Venus in synastry](/astrology/sun-square-venus-synastry/):** a sign further round; liking comes with conditions, and taste rubs against identity.
- **[Sun trine Venus in synastry](/astrology/sun-trine-venus-synastry/):** in signs of one element as a rule; appreciation that arrives uninvited.
- **[Sun opposite Venus in synastry](/astrology/sun-opposite-venus-synastry/):** six signs apart; giving and receiving become a matter of balance.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Take the invitations, and issue some of your own. Say directly what you enjoy about each other; light liking deepens when it's named. Now and then, steer a pleasant evening towards something more personal, a question you'd usually skip. At work, tell the colleague with the contacts what their introductions made possible; between friends or in a family, notice who usually does the planning, and swap.

Of every ten pairs of people, about one has this sextile, one way round or the other, within 4.8°. Venus wanders further from its own Sun than Mercury does, so the birthdays spread widely here: usually about two months apart, but anywhere from under two weeks to nearly four months, whatever the years.

The hour of birth hardly moves the Sun or Venus, so it rarely settles whether the sextile exists; what it settles is the houses: where in each life the shared pleasures take place, and that's one reason a synastry starts with both times ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).
