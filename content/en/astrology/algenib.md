Algenib is the corner of the Great Square of Pegasus that lies closest to the March equinox point in the sky: its right ascension, the astronomers' measure along the celestial equator, is barely past zero. Along the zodiac, though, it reads 9°31′ Aries, and the gap between those two numbers says a good deal about how a star's degree is found.

## Where it is {#where-it-is}

Algenib is Gamma Pegasi, the fourth brightest star of Pegasus, at the south-east corner of the Square. It is a hot blue-white star some 470 light-years away, and its light isn't quite steady: it pulses every 3.6 hours, moving between magnitude 2.78 and 2.89 (the catalogue gives 2.83). Its neighbour on this site, [Menkalinan](/astrology/menkalinan/), dims for another reason, the eclipse of one star by its partner.

In the catalogue its [right ascension](/astrology/right-ascension/) is 0h 13m: about 3° east of the circle that runs from the celestial pole through the March equinox. On the zodiac's own scale, measured along the [ecliptic](/astrology/ecliptic/), the same star stood at 8°10′ [Aries](/astrology/aries/) for a birth in 1930, 9°09′ in 2000 and 9°31′ in 2026. The two scales lean against each other, and Algenib lies well north of both circles, so its zodiac degree runs ahead of its place on the equator.

Its latitude is 12.6° north of the ecliptic. The Moon never strays more than about 5° from that line, so it never covers Algenib; [Zubenelgenubi](/astrology/zubenelgenubi/), which sits almost on the line, is the opposite case, a star the Moon and even the planets can pass in front of.

## Its tradition {#its-tradition}

Its Arabic name means the side, and for a long time it was shared: Alpha Persei carried it too, until the International Astronomical Union settled Algenib on Gamma Pegasi in 2016 and called the Perseus star Mirfak. A star book that speaks of Algenib may therefore mean either.

With [Alpheratz](/astrology/alpheratz/), Algenib forms the eastern side of the Square, and the lunar calendars treated that side as one station, the counterpart of the western side that [Markab](/astrology/markab/) and Scheat make: Uttara Bhadrapada, the 26th Indian nakshatra, whose lord is Saturn; the Second Spout of the Arabic mansions; and the Wall of the Chinese lodges, with Algenib as its first star ([the 28 lunar mansions](/astrology/28-lunar-mansions/)). Older star lore also counted it, with Alpheratz and Beta Cassiopeiae, among the Three Guides that mark the prime meridian of the heavens, the line its right ascension sits beside.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Measured a degree either side, Algenib's window takes in 8°31′ to 10°31′ Aries for a birth in 2026 and 7°10′ to 9°10′ for one in 1930; within it, the tradition calls a planet or angle conjunct the star ([conjunctions to fixed stars](/astrology/conjunctions-to-fixed-stars/)). The Sun passes through on about 29 and 30 March. Saturn did so for births in spring 1938, in parts of 1967–68 and in March 1997. For the Moon and the angles, the answer lies in the birth time ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Fixed stars aren't part of the readings I give ([How I read](/how-i-read/)); what I'd want you to take from Algenib is the lesson in its numbers: a star's zodiac degree is a projection onto the Sun's path, worked out for the year of birth, and it can sit some way from where the star appears on any other scale.
