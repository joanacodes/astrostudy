Their North Node in your 6th house is the overlay of the ordinary day. The direction the other person is growing in falls among your routines, your tasks and the way you look after yourself, and their South Node falls opposite, in your 12th, in the quiet hours nobody sees.

## What it brings {#what-it-brings}

[The 6th house](/astrology/6th-house/) is the shape of your ordinary day: the work itself rather than the title, habits and schedules, colleagues, the care you give your body. The [North Node](/astrology/north-node/) is a capacity that grows only with repetition, which suits this house well. Through this [house overlay](/astrology/house-overlays/), the routine you keep becomes their practice.

For you, they may ask how you plan your week, borrow your lists, or start turning up for the early swim. Being someone's example of a working day can be oddly moving: habits you never thought about suddenly look like skills.

For them, a steady, useful routine is new ground, and keeping one beside you seems possible. The 6th is cadent, a house that keeps things running; [Virgo](/astrology/virgo/), its natural sign, is one of Mercury's two signs, and tradition called the house Bad Fortune and gave Mars, the planet of effort, its joy here. Near the end of your 6th, their North Node reaches your [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/) and their South Node your Ascendant.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

Their [South Node](/astrology/south-node/) in [the 12th house](/astrology/12th-house/) makes your private world their easy ground. They're comfortable in your silences and your retreats, and when the chores pile up, they may drift there: a long bath, a novel, a day that dissolves. You can end up keeping the timetable for two.

From their side, your routines can feel like a test they keep failing. Practice is clumsy, and to someone at home in drift, a schedule may feel like a cage; it takes them a while to find it holds them up.

Tradition gave the 6th illness and the 12th confinement. Neither end of their line says anything about anyone's health or freedom ([can astrology predict health problems?](/astrology/can-astrology-predict-health-problems/), [astrology and mental health](/astrology/astrology-and-mental-health/)): I read daily work and care at one end, the inner life at the other.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Build the routine together and let part of it be theirs. In a couple, share the weekly chores on a list you both wrote; between friends, a regular walk or class keeps the practice light; between colleagues, give them a process to own rather than one to follow; between adult relatives, help with a task without taking it over.

In their chart, the contact is your North Node in their 6th house, and their daily habits would be what you learn from. Around it, [their North Node in your 5th house](/astrology/north-node-in-5th-house-synastry/) is about play and [their North Node in your 7th house](/astrology/north-node-in-7th-house-synastry/) about partnership.

In Placidus, the house system I use, your 6th ends at your Descendant, opposite your Ascendant, and both shift with your birth time minute by minute ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)). Their birth date places their node, which takes about 18 months to cross a sign, but not which side of your Descendant it falls. There, the mean node and the true node can disagree, one keeping them at work on your routines, the other sitting them across the table in your 7th, with the South Node moving from your 12th to your 1st; I look at both. Daily habits and private hours are both close to the bone, so I read this pair only with both birth times and both people's agreement ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
