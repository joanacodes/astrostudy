Money, belongings and the sense of your own worth: that's the territory your chart ruler occupies when the planet of your rising sign sits in the 2nd house. The self you lead with goes looking for solid ground, and finds it in what you can make, earn and keep.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

As ruler of the [1st house](/astrology/1st-house/), your [chart ruler](/astrology/chart-ruler/) carries the business of the self with it ([house rulers](/astrology/house-rulers/)), and in the [2nd house](/astrology/2nd-house/) that business meets money and belongings, the skills you could live from, and the sense of your own worth underneath them. So the questions of the 1st, who am I and how do I go about things, get answered in the 2nd's terms: what can I do, what is mine, what am I worth.

This is a self that wants something to stand on. You tend to know yourself through competence: the trade you learned, the thing you made, the savings you put by, the possessions you chose with care. Your manner and your values usually match, and people can often tell what matters to you from the way you live. On a good day that gives you a steady, practical confidence. On a harder one, worth can feel like something to be earned again every morning, as if you were only as good as what you produce or own. The 2nd house deals in values, not prices, and keeping the two apart is the work here.

It can take work, and the old astrologers had an explanation. Counted by sign, as the Hellenistic astrologers counted with [whole sign houses](/astrology/whole-sign-houses/), the 2nd is the sign next to the rising sign, and neighbouring signs make no aspect to each other: tradition called them averse, with no easy way for what's in one to work with what's in the other ([aversion](/astrology/aversion/)). In Placidus, the house system I use, houses and signs fit more loosely, but when your ruler does stand in the sign after your Ascendant, I read the aversion as a self-worth built by doing, step by step, rather than taken for granted.

Which planet it is sets the kind of security you build. Saturn, ruling a Capricorn Ascendant from Aquarius in the 2nd, stands in the second sign tradition gave it, and builds security patiently and on its own terms. Jupiter, ruling Sagittarius rising, can land in Capricorn, the sign of its [fall](/astrology/fall/), and has to learn thrift against its own generous grain. Same house, two very different ideas of enough. With Aquarius rising, Saturn shares the chart with Uranus, and I read both ([traditional and modern rulers](/astrology/traditional-and-modern-rulers/)): a Saturn in Pisces in the 2nd might describe a careful, slow-built sense of your worth, while Uranus, wherever it sits in your chart, asks whether security is really what you're after.

In the sky, a ruler in the 2nd was below the eastern horizon when you were born, still some hours from rising. If it sits near the edge of the house, the minutes matter: an earlier birth time would leave it lower, in the 3rd, where your story would run through talk, learning and the people close by ([Ruler of the 1st in the 3rd house](/astrology/1st-house-ruler-in-3rd-house/)), and a later one would bring it up into the 1st, with your life gathered around yourself ([Ruler of the 1st in the 1st house](/astrology/1st-house-ruler-in-1st-house/)). Below the horizon, a rounded time can hide which of those three houses holds it, so I don't read it from one ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

One thing a chart ruler in the 2nd never does is forecast money. The house shows how you handle what you have, not how much will come, and no chart can tell anyone when to buy or what to back ([can astrology pick investments?](/astrology/can-astrology-pick-investments/)). What it does show is that, for you, feeling like yourself and feeling secure tend to arrive together.
