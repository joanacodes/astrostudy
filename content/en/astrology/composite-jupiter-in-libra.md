Whose turn it is to choose the film, whether the favour has been returned, what's fair to the friend who always hosts: in some relationships, every decision gets weighed by two. Under composite Jupiter in Libra, a bond grows through fairness, and believes most in what it has agreed.

## What it says about the two of you {#what-it-says}

The relationship's growth, its understanding, its principles, the room it opens for the two of you and for others: that is what [Jupiter](/astrology/jupiter/) describes in [the composite chart](/astrology/composite-chart/). [Libra](/astrology/libra/), cardinal air ruled by [Venus](/astrology/venus/), brings a manner of weighing, balancing and meeting halfway, which suits a chart made of halfway points. Jupiter has no dignity in Libra, which tradition gives to Venus as a home and to Saturn as an exaltation. Composite Venus shows what the pair values and finds beautiful, and so what its sense of fairness serves.

This is the commonest composite Jupiter in the count: about 1 pair in 10, slightly more than any other sign, among two million random pairs born between 1930 and 2009. Same-age pairs share it when both were born while Jupiter crossed Libra, as from September 1933 to October 1934, from late August 1945 to September 1946, from late October 1980 to November 1981, or from mid-October 1992 to early November 1993.

Libra is also a good place to see how much the method matters. Take a pair born on 1 June 1983 and 1 June 1991: Jupiter in early Sagittarius for one, in Leo for the other, and a composite Jupiter at about 7° Libra. [The Davison chart](/astrology/davison-chart/), cast for the moment halfway between their births, puts Jupiter where it really stood in June 1987: about 21° Aries, across the zodiac. Born less than about six years apart, a pair's two relationship charts usually put Jupiter in the same part of the sky; between about six and eighteen years apart, almost always on opposite sides. Neither method is the more accurate, and anyone reading a Libra Jupiter should say which chart it came from.

Whichever chart is used, this placement describes growth through the relationship itself. The bond's beliefs form in dialogue, each of you hearing the other side before deciding; its principles are about fairness, to each other and to everyone around you; its generosity is reciprocal: the dinner returned, the favour remembered, a friend's quarrel mediated with patience. A natal [Jupiter in Libra](/astrology/jupiter-in-libra/) understands through the other side; here both sides are inside the bond, and each of you can find yourself fairer for it.

Its difficulty is fairness turned into bookkeeping: who did the washing-up, who chose last time, who owes whom. Growth can also wait for the other's agreement until neither of you moves. Harmony isn't the same as avoiding conflict, and balance can be found over a year rather than in every exchange. What helps is letting the scales settle slowly, and welcoming the disagreements that keep the balance honest.

Virgo and Scorpio frame it: [composite Jupiter in Virgo](/astrology/composite-jupiter-in-virgo/) grows by improving, [composite Jupiter in Scorpio](/astrology/composite-jupiter-in-scorpio/) by going deep. Composites are outside my own readings; astrologers who use them set this Jupiter beside both birth charts and the [synastry](/astrology/synastry/), and no composite says whether a match is a good one. Fairness starts before the chart: both people's data, given with both people's consent ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).
