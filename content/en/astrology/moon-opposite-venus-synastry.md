In Moon opposite Venus in synastry, one person's Moon faces the other's Venus from across the chart. Needs on one side meet affection and taste on the other: each person seems to carry what the other is missing, which is both the pull of this contact and its work.

## What flows {#what-flows}

A person's [Moon](/astrology/moon/) is the part of them that needs: closeness, routine, the comfort of the familiar. Their [Venus](/astrology/venus/) is the part that values: what they find lovely, how they share, the balance they like between people. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) sets two functions face to face, so that each is seen most clearly in the other; it's the most polarised of this pair's [synastry aspects](/astrology/synastry-aspects/).

What flows is complementarity. From the Moon's side, the other person brings grace, pleasure and lightness to what you need: they make comfort lovely. From Venus's side, they bring depth and belonging to what you enjoy: with them, pleasure starts to feel like home. Each learns from the far end what their own end leaves out.

The signs give it a subject. With a Moon in Leo and Venus in Aquarius, a need for warmth and attention faces an affection that values freedom and friendship; with a Moon in Cancer and Venus in Capricorn, a need for closeness faces a care that shows itself in reliability and effort.

## Where it rubs {#where-it-rubs}

This seesaw likes to set the roles: one person always giving comfort, the other always making things pleasant; or one always needing, the other always pleasing. The Moon person can come to feel that the other cares more about how things look than how they feel, and the Venus person that the other's needs weigh on every pleasure. Couples often find it in the sharing-out of an ordinary day: who plans the pleasant things, who checks that everyone is all right. Neither job is lesser, but each can start to resent the other's.

Polarity isn't contradiction. The two ends need each other to make sense.

## How to meet it {#how-to-meet-it}

Take turns at each end. If Venus is yours, ask for comfort sometimes instead of only offering charm; if the Moon is yours, offer appreciation, not only need. Between co-workers, it can be the pairing where one keeps the team steady and the other keeps it pleasant, and crossing over helps both; among friends and adult relatives, the same holds. Because this contact so often comes with a pull, people sometimes want to look at the other person's chart early on. I'd rather wait until you've known each other a while and both of you agree ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

My count puts it at about 1 pair of people in 12, within 8°, whichever chart holds the Moon. The Moon's speed makes the time decisive: wrong by a few hours, a chart can show this opposition where there is none, or miss one that's there. That's a reason to insist on both birth times; the houses are another ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

One person can carry both ends too: that's [Moon opposite Venus](/astrology/moon-opposite-venus/). [Moon conjunct Venus in synastry](/astrology/moon-conjunct-venus-synastry/) puts the two on the same spot; [Moon square Venus in synastry](/astrology/moon-square-venus-synastry/) makes them misread each other; [Moon sextile Venus in synastry](/astrology/moon-sextile-venus-synastry/) and [Moon trine Venus in synastry](/astrology/moon-trine-venus-synastry/) let them meet with less work.
