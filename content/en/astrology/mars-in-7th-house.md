With Mars in the 7th house, you often meet your own drive first in someone else: the partner who says what they want, the rival who pushes you to be better, the colleague whose energy matches yours. The person across the table carries a good share of your fire.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

[Mars](/astrology/mars/) is initiative, desire and the will to stand your ground. [The 7th house](/astrology/7th-house/) is the person across the table: partners in love and in work, close one-to-one bonds, and the open rivals you can see coming. With Mars here, relationships are where your drive comes alive. You're drawn to strong, direct people, you energise the people you pair with, and partnership is where you learn how to assert yourself with someone who matters.

Angular, it begins at the [Descendant](/astrology/descendant/). When you were born, Mars stood above the western horizon, getting ready to set. The 7th begins at the Descendant in Placidus, the system I read houses in, and a Mars just below that line belongs to the 6th instead, so the birth time decides ([what changes without a birth time](/blog/unknown-birth-time/)).

Its natural sign is Libra, Mars's detriment, which makes a fitting parallel: in both, Mars has to act with another person in mind. Tradition also gave the 7th open enemies and contracts. I don't forecast disputes, marriages or how they end; what I read is how you meet another person's will with your own. [Mars in the 6th house](/astrology/mars-in-6th-house/) pours its effort into the daily round; [Mars in the 8th house](/astrology/mars-in-8th-house/) carries it into intimacy and what's shared.

## In close relationships {#in-close-relationships}

This is the heart of the placement. Attraction often comes with a spark of challenge: you like people who push back, and a relationship without any friction can feel strangely flat. At its best, the energy is mutual, two people who encourage each other's courage. At its harder edge, you may hand your own assertiveness to a partner, then resent them for using it, or keep finding yourself in arguments you didn't start. Learning to fight fair, and to own your wants as yours, changes the pattern. If you'd like to compare charts with a partner, do it together; for me, a synastry starts with both people's yes ([why I won't read a synastry behind someone's back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/)).

## At work {#at-work}

At work, your best results often come in pairs: a business partner, a client relationship, a negotiation across the table, a sparring partner who sharpens your ideas. Consulting, sales, coaching, mediation, any role built on one-to-one dealings, suit this Mars. Competition with a single rival can motivate you hugely. Business partnerships can be very productive, provided the roles are clear: two people who both want to lead need a map of who decides what. Watch that a colleague's success doesn't turn into a contest you never agreed to enter.

## The hard part {#the-hard-part}

Meeting your own anger in other people: partners who seem to start every fight, rivals who appear at every turn. Choosing relationships for their intensity rather than their kindness. Wanting to win against the person you love. And giving up your own initiative so completely that it only comes back as resentment.

## What helps {#what-helps}

Agree on rules for arguments while you're both calm: no scoring points, no dragging up the past, a break when it gets heated. Put shared energy into a project or a sport you both enjoy, so competition has a friendly target. Say *I want* plainly, so your partner doesn't have to guess. Notice when someone attracts you mainly because they're a challenge. And remember that a strong partner is a gift when you're strong too.
