Mercury opposite Mars is the rarest meeting of these two planets, and the most particular. Mercury never strays far from the Sun, so it can face Mars only when Mars is on the far side of the sky, during its retrograde: everyone with this opposition was born with Mars retrograde.

## What it means {#what-it-means}

[Mercury](/astrology/mercury/) is where a chart listens, learns and puts things into words. [Mars](/astrology/mars/) is where it takes a stand: wanting, starting, pushing through, defending a line. An [opposition](/astrology/opposition/) sets them as two poles: neither gives way, and each is easier to see against the other.

In plain words, thinking and doing live at opposite ends of your chart. You may be a reflective talker in one part of life and a decisive actor in another, and feel the two don't always know each other. Or you may meet one end through other people: the friend who pushes you to act when you'd rather discuss, the colleague who argues when you'd rather get on with it. None of this is hostility. It's how an opposition builds awareness, by contrast.

The retrograde Mars that comes with it adds its own note. I read a retrograde Mars as drive that turns inward first, that reconsiders and returns rather than charging straight out ([natal retrograde planets](/blog/retrograde-planets/)). Set opposite Mercury, it often belongs to someone who acts after thinking a thing through twice, and who argues best when allowed to come back to it.

## In a birth chart {#in-a-birth-chart}

Most often the two sit in opposite signs, alike in modality and complementary in element: Mercury in Gemini opposite Mars in Sagittarius, say, sets quick questions against big, restless convictions. The opposition spans two areas of life, one for each planet: Mercury in the 3rd with Mars in the 9th puts words close to home and action far afield; the 1st and 7th set your own voice against other people's push.

About 1 person in 81 is born with Mercury and Mars within 6° of an opposition, by far the rarest of the pair's aspects. It can only happen around the time Mars stands opposite the Sun, about every two years, and only on the days Mercury lines up across from it. Mercury moves forward while Mars drifts back, so the angle between them changes from both ends at once, and a day or two of birth date can decide whether you have it.

How the other four stand against it:

- **[Mercury conjunct Mars](/astrology/mercury-conjunct-mars/):** the reverse of this sky, Mars near the Sun and moving forward, thought and action fused in one gesture.
- **[Mercury sextile Mars](/astrology/mercury-sextile-mars/):** the pair's most common aspect, an easy opening where this one is a polarity to hold.
- **[Mercury square Mars](/astrology/mercury-square-mars/):** friction at right angles rather than tension across the chart; the push is inside you more than between you and others.
- **[Mercury trine Mars](/astrology/mercury-trine-mars/):** the smooth version, thought and action keeping step instead of facing each other.

Between two charts, [Mercury opposite Mars in synastry](/astrology/mercury-opposite-mars-synastry/) sets one person's words across from the other's drive: a lively, sometimes provoking exchange, and each person lives a different half of it. I read it both ways.

## In transits {#in-transits}

Mars faces your natal Mercury once on each two-year lap, holding the opposition for a few days, and for longer when it turns retrograde near that point. Mercury, for its part, faces your natal Mars roughly once a year, for a day. Weather, not a forecast: for a few days, someone else's push meets your view, and one conversation can settle what weeks of thinking haven't.
