If you’ve read [how I tested my own chart](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), you know three placements kept coming back with a tick when the rest scored about a third. Here’s what they are, and how I read them.

## Saturn: the hardest teacher {#saturn}

Where Saturn sits, the person’s hardest lesson sits. Not a punishment: a subject they’ll be asked to learn slowly, often later than everyone around them — and then, very often, to teach.

I read Saturn as a timetable more than a wall. *This will take longer for you* is a very different sentence from *this isn’t for you*, and it’s the one I believe.

## Chiron: the wound you keep circling {#chiron}

Chiron shows the wound a person keeps coming back to — the thing that still stings when someone touches it, sometimes decades later. It’s also, often, where they’re best at caring for other people.

This is the placement I read most carefully. Naming a wound accurately is a kind of intimacy, and it has to be offered, not imposed. It’s one reason I [never read a chart behind someone’s back](/blog/synastry-behind-their-back/).

## Lilith: what you were told to hide {#lilith}

Lilith points to the part of someone they learned to hide — too much, too loud, too strange, too hungry. In a reading, it’s usually where people go quiet, and then say *how did you know*.

I don’t read Lilith as something to fix. I read it as something that was taken off the table early, and might be worth putting back.

## How I read them gently {#gently}

These are the [hardest places](/birth-chart/) in a chart, so they’re where words matter most. Soft words, clear meaning: I say what I see in plain language, the way I’d want it said to me. No doomed placements, no verdicts — only parts of you that ask for more patience than others.

## Why not the Sun? {#why-not-the-sun}

Your Sun sign still matters, and I read it. But in my tests it held about as often as a flattering horoscope, so it doesn’t get the most time. The placements that earned their place do.
