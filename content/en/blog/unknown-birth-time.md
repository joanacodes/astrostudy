Plenty of people don’t know the minute they were born. It’s on no certificate, nobody wrote it down, or the family stories disagree. It’s fine. Here’s what it changes.

## What the time decides {#what-the-time-decides}

The sky turns a full circle every day, so the time of birth decides what was rising on the horizon — your **rising sign** — and how the twelve **houses** fall: the areas of life each planet speaks to (work, home, love, and so on). Four minutes is enough to move the horizon by a degree.

## What it doesn’t {#what-it-doesnt}

The planets move far more slowly than the horizon. Over one day, most of them stay in the same sign — Saturn, Chiron and Lilith included, the [three placements I read most closely](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/). So a great deal of a chart is still there without the time.

## The Moon, the one to check {#the-moon}

The Moon is the quick one: it changes sign roughly every two and a half days. On most birthdays it stays in one sign from midnight to midnight. On the day it changes, your Moon could be either — and I’ll tell you so, and read both if they matter.

## How I read without it {#how-i-read-without-it}

The order form has a box for *I don’t know the time*. Tick it, and I read what holds without one: your planets in their signs and the aspects between them. Wherever something would need the time, I say so in the reading itself — never a guess presented as a fact.

## Finding your time {#finding-your-time}

If you’d like to try: a parent’s or grandparent’s memory, a baby book, a hospital record if your country keeps one. Even “just after lunch” narrows things down. And if it turns up after your reading, [write to me](/contact/).
