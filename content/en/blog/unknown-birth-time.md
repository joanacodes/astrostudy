Plenty of people don’t know the minute they were born. It isn’t on every birth certificate, nobody wrote it down, or the family stories disagree. But I don’t read a chart without it. Here’s why, and where to look.

## What the time decides {#what-the-time-decides}

The sky turns a full circle every day, so the time of birth decides what was rising on the horizon — your **[rising sign](/astrology/ascendant/)** — and how the twelve **houses** fall: the areas of life each planet speaks to (work, home, love, and so on). Four minutes is enough to move the horizon by a degree.

## What it doesn’t {#what-it-doesnt}

The planets move far more slowly than the horizon. Over one day, most of them stay in the same sign — [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) and [Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/) included, the [three placements I read most closely](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/). Their signs are there without the time. What’s missing is the house each one speaks from, and the house is where a placement becomes yours.

## The Moon, the one to check {#the-moon}

The [Moon](/astrology/moon/) is the quick one: it changes sign roughly every two and a half days. On most birthdays it stays in one sign from midnight to midnight. On the day it changes, only the time can tell which one is yours.

## Why I wait for the time {#why-i-wait-for-the-time}

A reading is the whole chart: the planets, their signs, and the houses they speak from, with the rising sign at the start of the first. Without the time, the rising sign and the houses are missing, and a reading would rest on the half that’s left. I’d rather not [present a guess as a fact](/how-i-read/). So [the order form](/order/) asks for the time, and I start once we have it.

## Finding your time {#finding-your-time}

Start with your birth record. In France, Scotland, Spain, Germany and Brazil it states the time: ask for the full copy (in France, the *copie intégrale* of your *acte de naissance*). English and Welsh certificates show it only for twins and other multiple births; in the US, it depends on the state and on the copy.

Otherwise, try a parent’s or grandparent’s memory, a baby book, or a hospital record. Even “just after lunch” is a start. If the time can’t be found, [write to me](/contact/) before you order.
