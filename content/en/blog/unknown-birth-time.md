Plenty of people don’t know the minute they were born. It isn’t on every birth certificate, nobody wrote it down, or the family stories disagree. It’s fine. Here’s what it changes.

## What the time decides {#what-the-time-decides}

The sky turns a full circle every day, so the time of birth decides what was rising on the horizon — your **rising sign** — and how the twelve **houses** fall: the areas of life each planet speaks to (work, home, love, and so on). Four minutes is enough to move the horizon by a degree.

## What it doesn’t {#what-it-doesnt}

The planets move far more slowly than the horizon. Over one day, most of them stay in the same sign — [Saturn](/astrology/saturn/), [Chiron](/astrology/chiron/) and [Lilith](/astrology/black-moon-lilith/) included, the [three placements I read most closely](/blog/saturn-chiron-lilith/). So a great deal of a chart is still there without the time.

## The Moon, the one to check {#the-moon}

The Moon is the quick one: it changes sign roughly every two and a half days. On most birthdays it stays in one sign from midnight to midnight. On the day it changes, your Moon could be either — and I’ll tell you so, and read both if they matter.

## How I read without it {#how-i-read-without-it}

[The order form](/order/) has a box for *I don’t know the time*. Tick it, and I read what holds without one: your planets in their signs and the aspects between them. Wherever something would need the time, I say so in the reading itself — [never a guess presented as a fact](/how-i-read/).

## Finding your time {#finding-your-time}

Start with your birth record. In France, Scotland, Spain, Germany and Brazil it states the time: ask for the full copy (in France, the *copie intégrale* of your *acte de naissance*). English and Welsh certificates show it only for twins and other multiple births; in the US, it depends on the state and on the copy.

Otherwise, try a parent’s or grandparent’s memory, a baby book, or a hospital record. Even “just after lunch” narrows things down. And if it turns up after your reading, [write to me](/contact/).
